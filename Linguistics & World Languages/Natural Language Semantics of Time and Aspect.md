# Natural Language Semantics of Time and Aspect

## Overview
How do languages situate events in time? Beyond familiar past, present, and future *tense*, languages use *aspect* to view an event from inside or outside — distinguishing completed wholes from ongoing processes — and *modality* to express necessity, obligation, and counterfactual possibility.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Bernard Comrie defined tense as 'grammaticalized location in time' relative to the moment of speaking, whereas aspect captures 'the internal temporal constituency of a situation.' English pairs tense with progressive (*she was writing*) and perfect (*she has written*) aspects. Slavic languages like Russian bake aspect directly into verb pairs (imperfective *pisat'* 'to be writing' vs. perfective *napisat'* 'to write to completion'), while Mandarin uses no obligatory tense at all, relying on aspect particles (*le*, *zhe*, *guo*) and time adverbs (*yesterday*).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Modality divides into epistemic ('she *must* be home' — speaker's certainty given evidence) and deontic ('she *must* leave now' — obligation or permission). Some languages even mark 'remote past' vs. 'recent past' (metrical tense in Bantu and Amazonian tongues) distinguishing what happened this morning from years ago.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Mapping tense, aspect, and modality (TAM) systems is critical for cross-lingual NLP, legal contract precision, and understanding how speakers frame causality and intention.

## Key Terminology
- **Tense vs. Aspect**: Locating an event in time relative to speech versus describing its internal completion or ongoing flow.
- **Perfective vs. Imperfective**: Viewing an action as a single completed whole versus an ongoing, habitual, or incomplete process.
- **Epistemic vs. Deontic modality**: Expressing logical likelihood/belief versus moral obligation or permission.
- **TAM system**: Integrated grammatical system of Tense, Aspect, and Modality markers in a language.
- **Counterfactual conditional**: Grammatical construction describing unreal hypothetical alternatives ('If he had left earlier...').
- **Aktionsart (Lexical aspect)**: Inherent temporal contour of a verb's meaning (e.g., punctual *cough* vs. durative *sleep*).
