# Language Endangerment and Revitalization

## Overview
Of roughly 7,000 languages spoken on Earth today, linguists estimate nearly half face endangerment this century as globalization, urbanization, and historical assimilation interrupt intergenerational transmission. When a language vanishes, humanity loses irreplaceable oral history, ecological taxonomy, and unique grammatical design.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Linguists assess vitality using UNESCO's scales and Ethnologue's EGIDS framework: a language is 'safe' when children learn it natively at home, 'definitely endangered' when children no longer speak it as a mother tongue, and 'critically endangered' when only elderly grandparents remain. Dominant drivers include economic pressure toward global lingua francas (English, Mandarin, Spanish), forced residential schooling legacies, and displacement.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Yet language loss is not inevitable. Pioneered by Māori *Kōhanga Reo* ('language nests') in New Zealand in 1982 and Hawaiian *Pūnana Leo* preschools, community-led immersion pairs fluent elders directly with toddlers, successfully raising new generations of native speakers. Other strategies include 'Master-Apprentice' adult pairing (California Indigenous languages), bilingual signage, smartphone keyboards, and archival documentation (ELAR, DoBeS) recording natural conversation, songs, and botanical knowledge.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Hebrew's revival proves dormant written languages can return to vibrant daily speech, while Wampanoag (Wôpanâak) in Massachusetts demonstrates how meticulous historical archival reconstruction can reawaken a sleeping tongue.

## Key Terminology
- **Intergenerational transmission**: Natural passing of a language from parents to young children at home — the primary determinant of vitality.
- **Language nest**: Early-childhood immersion preschool pairing fluent elders with toddlers (pioneered by Māori *Kōhanga Reo*).
- **Master-Apprentice program**: One-on-one adult immersion method pairing a fluent elder speaker with a dedicated learner.
- **Language documentation**: Creating lasting, ethically archived audio-video recordings and annotated texts of endangered speech.
- **Sleeping language**: Language with no living native speakers that remains documented in archives and open to reclamation.
- **Linguistic diversity**: Variety of distinct languages and structural systems across human populations, paralleling biodiversity.
