# Lexical Borrowing, Purism, and Neologisms

## Overview
No living language is hermetically sealed; trade, conquest, religion, and technology trigger constant lexical borrowing. While some language academies resist foreign loanwords through purist coinages, borrowing enriches expressive nuance and records centuries of global exchange.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Languages borrow words along predictable paths: 'cultural borrowings' import new inventions or foods (*coffee, chocolate, internet, sushi*), whereas 'core borrowings' replace native terms under intense prestige pressure (English borrowing *sky* and *they* from Old Norse). When native spelling or sound norms resist raw imports, languages use 'calques' (loan translations: German *Wolkenkratzer* literally 'cloud-scraper' for *skyscraper*, Chinese *diàn-nǎo* 'electric brain' for *computer*).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Linguistic purism periodically pushes back: Icelandic famously coins native Old Norse roots for every modern invention (*tölva* 'number-prophetess' for computer), and France's *Académie française* promotes *courriel* over *email* and *logiciel* over *software*. Conversely, English and Japanese (*gairaigo*) absorb foreign roots voraciously, adapting them seamlessly into native grammar.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Studying loanwords maps ancient Silk Road, maritime spice-trade, and modern digital networks, proving borrowing strengthens rather than corrupts living tongues.

## Key Terminology
- **Cultural vs. Core borrowing**: Adopting words for novel foreign items versus replacing existing native vocabulary due to prestige.
- **Calque (Loan translation)**: Translating a foreign compound word's components literally into native morphemes.
- **Wasei-eigo**: Japanese-coined pseudo-English compounds (*salaryman, anime*) built from borrowed English elements.
- **Neologism**: Newly invented word or phrase entering active circulation (e.g., *podcast, meme*).
- **Portmanteau (Blend)**: New word fusing parts of two existing words (*smog = smoke + fog, brunch*).
- **Inkhorn term**: 16th-century English critique of pretentious Latin/Greek scholarly loanwords.
