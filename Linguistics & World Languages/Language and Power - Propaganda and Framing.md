# Language and Power: Propaganda and Framing

## Overview
Language never merely mirrors neutral reality; subtle lexical framing, euphemisms, and presuppositions steer moral judgment and public policy. Studying political framing and propaganda equips citizens to spot covert manipulation in headlines, advertising, and civic debate.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Cognitive linguist George Lakoff demonstrated how single framing words evoke entire moral storylines: calling taxation 'tax relief' casts taxes as an affliction and cutters as heroes, whereas 'public investment' frames them as shared infrastructure. Similarly, passive agentless syntax ('mistakes were made' or 'a pedestrian was struck') quietly erases human responsibility compared to active verbs.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Political language deploys euphemisms ('collateral damage,' 'enhanced interrogation,' 'rightsizing') to anesthetize moral shock, while 'dog whistles' embed coded in-group signals innocent to outsiders yet unmistakable to target audiences. Psychological experiments confirm even subtle grammatical shifts — describing immigrants as a 'flood' (natural disaster metaphor) versus 'neighbors' — measurably alter policy support.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Critical media literacy teaches readers to check agency, question loaded presuppositions, and recognize emotional metaphor framing across all political perspectives.

## Key Terminology
- **Framing (Linguistic)**: Selecting specific words and metaphors that activate particular moral schemas and causal interpretations.
- **Euphemism**: Mild or indirect expression substituted for harsh, taboo, or politically embarrassing reality.
- **Dog whistle**: Coded political phrasing carrying neutral surface meaning alongside a resonant subtext for a target group.
- **Agentless passive**: Grammatical construction omitting the doer of an action ('shots were fired'), obscuring accountability.
- **Loaded presupposition**: Framing a disputed claim as an unquestioned background fact inside a question or headline.
- **Orwellian doublespeak**: Deliberately ambiguous or inverted language designed to obscure truth and evade responsibility.
