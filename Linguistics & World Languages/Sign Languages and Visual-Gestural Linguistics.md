# Sign Languages and Visual-Gestural Linguistics

## Overview
Sign languages — including American Sign Language (ASL), British Sign Language (BSL), and Nicaraguan Sign Language — are full, natural human languages utilizing hands, facial expressions, and 3D space rather than speech. Proving language is independent of vocal chords, sign linguistics revolutionized cognitive science.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Until 1960, even linguists mistakenly dismissed signing as crude pantomime. William Stokoe shattered that myth by proving ASL signs decompose into meaningless combinatorial parameters analogous to phonemes: handshape, location, movement, orientation, and non-manual markers (eyebrow raises, head tilts, mouth shapes encoding questions and relative clauses). Sign languages are *not* universal nor mere manual English: ASL is genetically related to French Sign Language (LSF) and mutually unintelligible with BSL.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
In syntax, signers exploit 3D signing space for pronoun reference and verb agreement — placing absent people at spatial loci and directing verbs between them. Crucially, Nicaraguan Sign Language (documented from 1977 onward by Judy Kegl and Ann Senghas) gave scientists an unprecedented natural experiment: deaf children brought together in Managua schools spontaneously forged a new, grammatically systematic language from disjointed home signs within two cohorts.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Neuroimaging confirms left-hemisphere Broca's and Wernicke's areas process sign language identically to spoken speech, underscoring that human language is modality-independent and validating early bilingual sign-and-written education for deaf children.

## Key Terminology
- **Sign phonology (Parameters)**: Five contrastive building blocks of signs: handshape, location, movement, palm orientation, and non-manual markers.
- **Non-manual markers**: Grammatical facial expressions, eyebrow shifts, and head movements marking questions, negation, and clauses.
- **Spatial indexing**: Assigning referents to fixed points in 3D signing space for unambiguous pronoun and verb tracking.
- **Nicaraguan Sign Language**: Newly emerged natural sign language created spontaneously by deaf schoolchildren in Nicaragua since the late 1970s.
- **Home sign**: Idiosyncratic gestural communication invented by an isolated deaf child lacking access to a signing community.
- **Classifiers (Sign)**: Pronominal handshapes representing object shapes, categories, and spatial movement trajectories.
