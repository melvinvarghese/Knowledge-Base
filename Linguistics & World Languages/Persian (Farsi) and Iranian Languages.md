# Persian (Farsi) and Iranian Languages

## Overview
Persian (known natively as Farsi in Iran, Dari in Afghanistan, and Tajik in Tajikistan) is the premier representative of the Iranian branch of Indo-European. Despite adopting Arabic script and rich Arabic loanwords after Islam, Persian remains structurally Indo-European — boasting genderless pronouns, SOV syntax, and a legendary poetic tradition.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Spanning Old Persian (cuneiform inscriptions of Darius the Great, c. 500 BCE), Middle Persian (Pahlavi of the Sasanian Empire), and New Persian emerging in the 9th–10th centuries, Persian served for centuries as the courtly, administrative, and literary lingua franca from Ottoman Anatolia and Central Asia across Mughal India — shaping Urdu, Turkish, and Bengali vocabularies.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Grammatically, New Persian streamlined ancient inflections into remarkable regularity: nouns lack grammatical gender (even third-person pronoun *u* covers both 'he' and 'she') and link modifiers backward via the elegant *ezāfe* vowel particle (*ketāb-e bozorg* 'book-of big'). While Iran and Afghanistan write Persian in Perso-Arabic script (adding four letters *p, ch, zh, g*), Tajikistan uses Cyrillic.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Ferdowsi's epic *Shahnameh*, Hafez, Rumi, and Saadi forged a poetic continuity so enduring that modern Iranian speakers still read 1,000-year-old verse with ease.

## Key Terminology
- **Iranian branch**: Eastern wing of Indo-Iranian uniting Persian, Kurdish, Pashto, Balochi, and Ossetian.
- **Ezāfe**: Unstressed *-e/-ye* linking vowel connecting Persian nouns to following adjectives or possessors.
- **Farsi / Dari / Tajik**: Three closely mutually intelligible national standards of New Persian across Iran, Afghanistan, and Tajikistan.
- **Indo-European gender loss**: Persian's complete elimination of grammatical gender distinctions even in third-person pronouns.
- **Perso-Arabic script**: Arabic abjad expanded with four modified letters (*پ چ ژ گ*) to represent Persian sounds.
- **Compound light verbs**: Dominant Persian verb strategy pairing nouns/adjectives with general verbs like *kardan* ('to do').
