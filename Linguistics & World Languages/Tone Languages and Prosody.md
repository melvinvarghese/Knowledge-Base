# Tone Languages and Prosody

## Overview
Over half of the world's languages use pitch variation not merely for emotional expression, but as lexical tone distinguishing core word meanings. Alongside stress, rhythm, and sentence intonation, prosody weaves melody and timing into grammar and pragmatics.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
In tonal languages, identical consonants and vowels carry completely different dictionary meanings depending on fundamental frequency (F0) contour or register. Mandarin Chinese contrasts four main contour tones (*mā* 'mother,' *má* 'hemp,' *mǎ* 'horse,' *mà* 'scold') plus a neutral tone; Cantonese contrasts six; and West African languages like Yoruba and Igbo use register level tones (High, Mid, Low) that can also mark grammatical tense or negation.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Prosody operates across larger phrases even in non-tonal languages via stress, rhythm, and intonation. Languages vary rhythmically between stress-timed (English, German — compressing unstressed syllables between roughly regular stressed beats) and syllable-timed (Spanish, French — giving syllables steadier duration), while Japanese uses moraic timing and pitch accent. Sentence intonation distinguishes statements from yes/no questions, signals focus contrast, and expresses irony or politeness.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Prosody research advances clinical autism and Parkinson's speech therapy, natural emotional speech synthesis, and forensic voice analysis — while revealing higher rates of absolute pitch among native tonal-language musicians.

## Key Terminology
- **Lexical tone**: Use of pitch height or contour on a syllable to distinguish basic dictionary word meanings.
- **Contour vs. Register tone**: Gliding pitch trajectories (rising, falling) versus steady relative pitch levels (high, mid, low).
- **Pitch accent**: Prosodic system marking one prominent syllable per word with a pitch drop or rise (e.g., Japanese, Swedish).
- **Tone sandhi**: Contextual tonal change triggered when adjacent tones interact (e.g., Mandarin third-tone sandhi).
- **Stress-timed vs. Syllable-timed**: Rhythmic tendencies spacing stressed beats evenly versus giving all syllables relatively uniform duration.
- **Mora**: Sub-syllabic timing unit governing rhythm and poetic meter in languages like Japanese and Ancient Greek.
