# Discourse Analysis and Conversation

## Overview
Discourse analysis looks beyond isolated sentences to examine how extended conversations, narratives, courtroom testimonies, and news texts achieve coherence, manage turn-taking, and negotiate social power.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Conversation Analysis (CA), founded by Harvey Sacks, Emanuel Schegloff, and Gail Jefferson in the 1960s, revealed that spontaneous everyday talk is extraordinarily orderly. Speakers coordinate seamless 'turn-taking' with ~200-millisecond gaps worldwide using grammatical, prosodic, and gaze cues at 'Transition Relevance Places.' Conversations organize into paired 'adjacency pairs' (greeting-greeting, question-answer, invitation-acceptance/refusal), where dispreferred refusals are predictably cushioned with pauses, hedges ('well...'), and excuses.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
At broader levels, Discourse Analysis tracks cohesion devices (anaphora, discourse markers like *however*, *so*, *actually*) and narrative structure (William Labov's oral story arc: abstract, orientation, complicating action, evaluation, resolution). Critical Discourse Analysis (Norman Fairclough, Teun van Dijk) interrogates how passive voice, nominalization, and lexical framing subtly conceal agency or naturalize political ideology in media and law.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Conversation and discourse science directly improves doctor-patient diagnostic interviews, 911 emergency call protocols, courtroom cross-examination fairness, and conversational UX design in voice assistants.

## Key Terminology
- **Turn-taking**: Systematic coordination of speaker transitions in conversation minimizing prolonged silence and overlap.
- **Adjacency pair**: Two-part conversational exchange where a first turn (question, greeting) creates expectation for a matching second.
- **Preference organization**: Structural asymmetry cushioning socially dispreferred responses (refusals, disagreements) with delays and hedges.
- **Repair (conversation)**: Self- or other-initiated mechanism for fixing misunderstandings, slips, or hearing errors mid-talk.
- **Critical Discourse Analysis**: Interdisciplinary method examining how language choices reinforce or challenge social power and ideology.
- **Discourse markers**: Words or phrases (*well*, *anyway*, *you know*) signaling structural transitions and speaker stance rather than literal content.
