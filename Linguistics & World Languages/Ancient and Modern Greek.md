# Ancient and Modern Greek

## Overview
Spanning over 3,400 years of continuous written records — from Mycenaean Linear B tablets to Homeric epics, New Testament Koine, Byzantine scholarship, and modern Athens — Greek is one of the longest-documented branches of Indo-European and the cradle of Western scientific vocabulary.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Mycenaean Greek (c. 1450–1200 BCE), preserved on clay tablets in the syllabic Linear B script deciphered by Michael Ventris in 1952, predates Homer by centuries. Around 800 BCE, Greeks adapted the Phoenician abjad to invent the first true vowel-and-consonant alphabet. Classical Attic Greek (Athens, 5th–4th century BCE) became the vehicle for Plato, Aristotle, Sophocles, and Thucydides, featuring pitch accent, three voices (active, middle, passive), and aspect-rich verbs.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Alexander the Great's conquests spread simplified *Koine* ('common') Greek across the eastern Mediterranean as the lingua franca of the Hellenistic world and early Christianity. Through the medieval Byzantine era into modern Greece, Greek experienced sharp diglossia between archaic *Katharevousa* ('purified') and everyday spoken *Dimotiki* until Dimotiki became official in 1976.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Greek roots (*bio-, geo-, psycho-, tele-, morph-*) supply the international backbone of modern medicine, physics, and linguistics.

## Key Terminology
- **Linear B**: Bronze Age Mycenaean syllabic script deciphered by Michael Ventris in 1952 as early Greek.
- **Koine Greek**: Simplified Hellenistic common Greek spoken across the eastern Mediterranean and used in the New Testament.
- **Attic Greek**: Classical prestige dialect of 5th-century BCE Athens central to Greek philosophy and drama.
- **Middle voice**: Greek verbal voice indicating the subject acts upon or for itself.
- **Katharevousa vs. Dimotiki**: Historical Greek diglossia between purist archaizing Greek and modern vernacular speech.
- **Hellenic branch**: Independent primary branch of the Indo-European family consisting of Greek varieties.
