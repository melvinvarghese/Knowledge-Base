# Austronesian Language Family

## Overview
Spanning half the globe from Madagascar off Africa's east coast to Rapa Nui (Easter Island) in the eastern Pacific, the Austronesian family (~1,200 languages) chronicles humanity's greatest prehistoric maritime expansion — uniting Malagasy, Indonesian, Malay, Tagalog, Hawaiian, and Māori.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Linguistic diversity pinpoints Taiwan as the Austronesian homeland: nine of the family's ten primary branches are indigenous Formosan languages spoken exclusively on Taiwan, while every single language outside Taiwan (Malayo-Polynesian) belongs to just one branch — mirroring a rapid outrigger-canoe expansion ~4,000 years ago through the Philippines, Indonesia, Oceania, and across the Indian Ocean to Madagascar.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Typologically, Philippine and Formosan languages (such as Tagalog) are famous for 'Austronesian alignment' (symmetrical voice): verbs carry affixes elevating either the actor, patient, location, or instrument to grammatical subject without demoting other arguments as passive voice does in English. Polynesian languages (Hawaiian, Māori, Samoan) dramatically simplified syllable structures into open CV patterns with tiny consonant inventories (Hawaiian uses just 8 consonants) and rich reduplication (*wiki-wiki* 'very fast').

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Malay/Indonesian serves today as a vital unifying national lingua franca for ~270+ million speakers across thousands of islands, while Māori and Hawaiian lead inspiring indigenous immersion revitalization.

## Key Terminology
- **Out-of-Taiwan model**: Consensus linguistic and genetic theory tracing Austronesian origins to Neolithic Taiwan.
- **Austronesian alignment**: Symmetrical voice system using verbal affixes to promote agent, patient, or location to grammatical pivot.
- **Reduplication**: Repeating all or part of a word root to mark plurality, intensity, or continuous action.
- **Inclusive vs. Exclusive 'we'**: Pronoun distinction separating 'we including you' from 'we excluding you,' pervasive across Austronesian.
- **Proto-Polynesian**: Reconstructed ancestor of Hawaiian, Māori, Samoan, and Tahitian spoken by Lapita-descended navigators.
- **Open syllable (CV)**: Syllable ending in a vowel without trailing consonants, characteristic of Polynesian languages.
