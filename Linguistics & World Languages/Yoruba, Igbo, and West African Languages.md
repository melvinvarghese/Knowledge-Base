# Yoruba, Igbo, and West African Languages

## Overview
Spoken by tens of millions across Nigeria, Benin, and Togo, Yoruba and Igbo exemplify West African Volta-Niger languages — celebrated for three-level lexical and grammatical tone, serial verb constructions, and artistic fusion with talking-drum prosody.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Yoruba contrasts three crisp register tones (High, Mid, Low) marked with acute/grave accents in standard orthography, where tonal melody alone distinguishes *igbá* ('calabash'), *ìgbá* ('locust bean'), *igba* ('two hundred'), and *ìgbà* ('time'). Igbo similarly contrasts High, Low, and grammatical 'downstep,' using vowel harmony (*ATR* — Advanced Tongue Root) to align prefix and root vowels.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Both languages lack English-style prepositions for many spatial paths, instead chaining 'serial verbs' together (*ó mú ìwé wá* 'he took book came' = 'he brought the book'). Crucially, Yoruba's *dùndún* talking drum literally mimics spoken tone contours and vowel rhythm to recite proverbs.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Yoruba liturgical vocabulary also survived the Middle Passage intact in Cuban *Lucumí* and Brazilian *Candomblé*, linking West Africa directly to Atlantic diaspora culture.

## Key Terminology
- **Three-level register tone**: Contrastive High, Mid, and Low syllable pitches distinguishing Yoruba words and grammar.
- **Serial verb construction**: Stringing multiple finite verbs in one clause without conjunctions to express complex trajectories.
- **ATR vowel harmony**: Igbo phonological harmony dividing vowels by Advanced versus Retracted Tongue Root.
- **Orthographic tone marks**: Diacritic accents (´, `) and underdots (ẹ, ọ, ṣ) disambiguating Yoruba words in print.
- **Lucumí**: Yoruba-derived liturgical language preserved in Afro-Cuban Santería traditions.
- **Proverbial speech**: Central role of tonal proverbs (*òwe*) as rhetorical authority in West African discourse.
