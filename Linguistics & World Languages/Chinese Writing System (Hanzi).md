# Chinese Writing System (Hanzi)

## Overview
Chinese characters (*hanzi*) constitute the world's oldest continuously used writing system, spanning over 3,300 years from Shang-dynasty oracle bones to digital smartphones. Far from purely 'picture writing,' roughly 85% of modern characters are systematic phono-semantic compounds combining a meaning radical with a sound hint.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Earliest attested on c. 1250 BCE ox-scapula oracle bones at Anyang, early Chinese characters combined direct pictographs (*rì* 'sun', *shān* 'mountain'), simple ideographs (*shàng* 'up'), semantic compounds, and — overwhelmingly — *xingsheng* (phono-semantic) compounds: pairing a semantic 'radical' (e.g., 氵 'water') with a phonetic component suggesting pronunciation. Because spoken Chinese varieties diverged widely over millennia while sharing Classical literary roots, characters enabled mutual written comprehension across mutually unintelligible dialects.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
In 221 BCE, Qin Shi Huang standardized seal script across China, evolving into clerical and regular (*kaishu*) forms. In the 1950s, Mainland China introduced Simplified Chinese characters to boost mass literacy while Taiwan and Hong Kong retained Traditional forms, alongside *Pinyin* Romanization for typing and early education.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Modern smartphone input uses Pinyin phonetic typing to select characters instantly, fueling ongoing 'character amnesia' (forgetting handwritten stroke order) even as digital Chinese literacy thrives globally.

## Key Terminology
- **Phono-semantic compound**: Dominant Chinese character type pairing a semantic meaning radical with a phonetic sound component.
- **Radical (Bushou)**: Graphically recurring semantic component used to classify and index Chinese characters in dictionaries.
- **Oracle bone script**: Oldest known Chinese writing (c. 1250 BCE) carved onto turtle plastrons and ox bones for divination.
- **Traditional vs. Simplified**: Classical multi-stroke Chinese character forms versus 1950s Mainland stroke-reduced reforms.
- **Pinyin**: Official Romanization system encoding Mandarin sounds and tones for teaching and keyboard input.
- **Morpheme-syllabic script**: Accurate linguistic label for hanzi, where each character typically maps to one syllable and one morpheme.
