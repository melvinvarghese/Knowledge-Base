# Sino-Tibetan Language Family

## Overview
The Sino-Tibetan language family is the world's second-largest by native speakers (~1.4 billion), uniting Sinitic (Chinese) varieties with over 400 Tibeto-Burman languages spoken across East Asia, the Himalayas, and Mainland Southeast Asia — including Tibetan and Burmese.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Although Sinitic languages like Mandarin are famously isolating and SVO, most Tibeto-Burman relatives (Tibetan, Burmese, Karen, Jingpho) are SOV and preserve conservative consonant clusters and verb agreement prefixes lost in modern Chinese. Phylogenetic studies trace Proto-Sino-Tibetan back roughly 6,000–7,000 years to Neolithic millet farmers along China's Yellow River basin before southward and Himalayan dispersals.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Within Sinitic, Old Chinese originally lacked lexical tones; as initial and final consonant clusters eroded over millennia (documented through Tang-dynasty *Qieyun* rhyme dictionaries), lost voicing contrasts evolved into modern tonal distinctions — a process called 'tonogenesis.' Meanwhile, Classical Tibetan preserved ancient 7th-century consonant clusters in orthography even as spoken Lhasa Tibetan simplified them into modern tones.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Sino-Tibetan languages illustrate dramatic typological drift within a single family — from agglutinative Himalayan prefixes to isolating Chinese compound words and iconic logographic literacy.

## Key Terminology
- **Sinitic vs. Tibeto-Burman**: The two primary divisions of the Sino-Tibetan language family.
- **Tonogenesis**: Historical emergence of contrastive lexical tones from the loss of neighboring voiced or glottal consonants.
- **Isolating typology**: Grammatical profile relying on word order and particles rather than inflectional prefixes or suffixes.
- **Measure words (Classifiers)**: Obligatory grammatical counters paired with numerals before nouns across East and Southeast Asian languages.
- **Rhyme dictionary**: Classical Chinese phonological reference organizing characters by tone and final rhyme, vital for historical reconstruction.
- **Serial verb construction**: Stringing multiple verbs together without conjunctions to express sequential or directional actions.
