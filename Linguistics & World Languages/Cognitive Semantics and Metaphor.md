# Cognitive Semantics and Metaphor

## Overview
Cognitive semantics rejects the strict separation between dictionary meaning and encyclopedic world knowledge, arguing that grammar and vocabulary grow directly out of embodied human experience, spatial image schemas, and pervasive conceptual metaphors.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
In *Metaphors We Live By* (1980), George Lakoff and Mark Johnson overturned the view of metaphor as mere poetic ornament. They showed abstract thought relies systematically on 'conceptual metaphors' mapping concrete bodily domains onto abstract targets: ARGUMENT IS WAR ('attack a claim,' 'defend a position'), TIME IS MONEY ('spend time,' 'waste hours'), and MORE IS UP ('prices rose,' 'spirits sank').

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Ronald Langacker's Cognitive Grammar and Leonard Talmy's force dynamics further demonstrated how grammar encodes visual perspective — such as foregrounding figure vs. ground ('the bike is near the house' vs. '*the house is near the bike*') and fictive motion ('the road runs along the coast'). Gilles Fauconnier and Mark Turner extended this into Conceptual Blending, showing how novel idioms and jokes merge elements from distinct mental spaces.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Cognitive semantics reshaped political framing analysis (how metaphors like 'tax relief' or 'war on crime' steer policy intuition), language teaching, and human-centered UI metaphors ('desktop,' 'trash,' 'scrolling').

## Key Terminology
- **Conceptual metaphor**: Systematic cognitive mapping understanding an abstract target domain in terms of a concrete bodily source domain.
- **Image schema**: Pre-linguistic recurring bodily pattern (CONTAINMENT, PATH, BALANCE) grounding abstract grammatical meaning.
- **Figure-Ground alignment**: Cognitive framing selecting a smaller, movable focal entity relative to a larger static landmark.
- **Prototype semantics**: Modeling word meanings around central typical examples with fuzzy boundaries rather than rigid checklists.
- **Metonymy**: Referring to an entity via a closely associated part or context (e.g., 'the White House announced').
- **Conceptual blending**: Combining structures from two mental input spaces into a novel emergent meaning.
