# Second Language Acquisition (SLA)

## Overview
Second Language Acquisition (SLA) investigates how adolescents and adults learn additional languages after early childhood. Navigating 'interlanguage' fossils, comprehensible input, and corrective feedback, SLA theory reshaped modern communicative classrooms.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
In the 1970s, Larry Selinker introduced 'interlanguage' — recognizing that learners do not simply make random errors, but build an evolving systematic mental grammar bridging native transfer and target rules (occasionally stalling in 'fossilization'). Stephen Krashen's influential Input Hypothesis argued acquisition happens subconsciously through 'comprehensible input' (*i+1*, messages slightly above current level) when anxiety ('affective filter') is low, rather than memorizing dry grammar charts.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Later SLA researchers (Merrill Swain, Michael Long) countered that passive listening isn't enough: learners need 'pushed output' (forced to produce precise grammar) and 'interaction' (negotiating misunderstandings with partners) alongside explicit 'noticing' of forms (Richard Schmidt). While adult learners rarely match native childhood phonology due to neural commitment, adults often acquire vocabulary and explicit grammar faster than children.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Modern SLA blends communicative immersion, spaced-repetition flashcards, and task-based instruction — powering global language apps, bilingual immersion schools, and adult migrant literacy programs.

## Key Terminology
- **Interlanguage**: Systematic, evolving transitional mental grammar constructed by a second-language learner.
- **Comprehensible input (i+1)**: Krashen's concept of meaningful target-language input just slightly beyond a learner's current level.
- **Affective filter**: Emotional barrier (anxiety, embarrassment) impeding natural language intake.
- **Fossilization**: Persistence of non-target grammatical or pronunciation habits despite years of fluent exposure.
- **Language transfer**: Influence of a learner's native language phonology or syntax on their second language.
- **Communicative competence**: Dell Hymes's broader goal combining grammatical accuracy with socially appropriate pragmatic use.
