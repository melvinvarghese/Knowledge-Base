# Natural Language Processing: Benchmarks and Ethics

## Overview
As Large Language Models and speech tools scale globally, computational linguists confront urgent ethical bottlenecks: English-centric 'resource inequality' leaving 95% of world languages behind, encoded social stereotypes in embeddings, and the difference between fluent mimicry and genuine understanding.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Because web text overwhelmingly favors English, Mandarin, Spanish, and European tongues, roughly 6,500+ 'low-resource' languages lack digitized corpora, leaving speakers locked out of voice search, medical translation, and accessibility tools. Initiatives like Masakhane (African NLP) and Bloom/NLLB build community-curated multilingual corpora to close this digital divide while respecting indigenous data sovereignty.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Equally critical is representational bias: word embeddings and language models absorb historical gender, racial, and occupational stereotypes from training text (associating *doctor* with male pronouns and *nurse* with female ones). Furthermore, Emily Bender and Timnit Gebru's 'Stochastic Parrots' critique warned that massive energy-intensive models generate plausible-sounding fluent prose without grounded communicative intent or factual accountability.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Modern NLP ethics pairs linguists with computer scientists to design debiased evaluation benchmarks, transparent model cards, and human-centered multilingual tools.

## Key Terminology
- **Low-resource language**: Language lacking large digitized text and parallel corpora needed for standard machine learning.
- **Stochastic parrot**: Bender and Gebru's metaphor for LMs stitching probabilistic word sequences without grounded meaning.
- **Representational bias**: Algorithmic reproduction of societal stereotypes learned from unbalanced historical training corpora.
- **Data sovereignty**: Right of Indigenous and local communities to govern collection, ownership, and commercial use of their language data.
- **Zero-shot cross-lingual transfer**: Applying an NLP model trained on high-resource languages to perform tasks in unseen languages.
- **Hallucination (NLP)**: Fluent, grammatically confident generation of fabricated facts by probabilistic language models.
