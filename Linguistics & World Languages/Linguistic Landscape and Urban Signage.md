# Linguistic Landscape and Urban Signage

## Overview
Linguistic landscape research turns city streets into living sociolinguistic data — photographing and mapping shopfronts, street signs, graffiti, and transit warnings to reveal which languages hold official power, commercial prestige, or grassroots neighborhood vitality.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Pioneered by Rodrigue Landry and Richard Bourhis (1997), linguistic landscape studies distinguish 'top-down' official government signage (street plaques, court signs enforcing state policy) from 'bottom-up' commercial and grassroots signs (ethnic grocery banners, restaurant menus, protest murals). Often, official monolingual street signs mask vibrant multilingual neighborhoods thriving just beneath them.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Researchers analyze script hierarchy (which language sits larger or on top in bilingual signs), symbolic prestige (e.g., French or English boutique names in non-English cities signaling luxury), and contested language politics — such as bilingual Irish/English signs in Northern Ireland, Catalan vs. Spanish storefronts in Barcelona, or disappearing Chinatowns.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Linguistic landscape mapping informs inclusive municipal wayfinding, tourism design, and urban sociology.

## Key Terminology
- **Linguistic landscape**: Visibility and salience of languages on public and commercial signs within a given geographic territory.
- **Top-down vs. Bottom-up signage**: Official state-mandated public signs versus private commercial and community-created signage.
- **Script hierarchy**: Visual placement, font size, and ordering of languages on multilingual signs signaling relative status.
- **Symbolic vs. Informational function**: Signage conveying cultural identity or prestige versus literal practical directions.
- **Geosemiotics**: Study of how physical placement in space shapes the social meaning of signs and symbols.
- **Visual multilingualism**: Coexistence of multiple scripts and languages across shared urban streetscapes.
