# Linguistic Typology and Universals

## Overview
Linguistic typology compares structural diversity across thousands of the world's languages to discover which grammatical patterns vary freely and which obey universal cognitive or communicative constraints.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Joseph Greenberg launched modern typology in 1963 by surveying basic declarative word orders across 30+ diverse languages, finding that Subject-Object-Verb (SOV, ~45% — Japanese, Hindi, Turkish) and Subject-Verb-Object (SVO, ~42% — English, Mandarin, Swahili) overwhelmingly dominate, while VSO accounts for ~9% and object-initial orders are extraordinarily rare. Crucially, Greenberg uncovered 'implicational universals': SOV languages strongly tend to use postpositions and place adjectives before nouns, whereas VSO languages favor prepositions and post-nominal adjectives — reflecting harmonic head-directionality.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Typologists also map alignment systems: while nominative-accusative languages (English, Spanish) treat intransitive subjects (*She* sleeps) identically to transitive agents (*She* saw him), ergative-absolutive languages (Basque, Georgian, Dyirbal) group intransitive subjects with transitive objects (*absolutive*) while marking transitive agents distinctly (*ergative*). Other striking typological dimensions include evidentiality (obligatory grammar marking *how* you know a fact — seen firsthand vs. hearsay) and grammatical number (dual, trial, paucal).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Large open databases like WALS (World Atlas of Structural Structures) empower computational typology, revealing how ecology, population size, and human processing limits sculpt language diversity.

## Key Terminology
- **Word order typology**: Classification of languages by default ordering of Subject, Verb, and Object (SOV, SVO, VSO, etc.).
- **Implicational universal**: Statistical or absolute rule stating that if a language has feature X, it reliably exhibits feature Y.
- **Ergative-Absolutive alignment**: Grammatical system grouping intransitive subjects with transitive objects against distinct transitive agents.
- **Evidentiality**: Obligatory grammatical marking specifying the source of information (witnessed, inferred, reported).
- **Head-initial vs. Head-final**: Consistent placement of phrase heads before complements (English VP) or after complements (Japanese VP).
- **WALS**: World Atlas of Language Structures, a foundational global database mapping phonological and grammatical features.
