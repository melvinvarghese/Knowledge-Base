# Arabic Language and Diglossia

## Overview
Arabic is a Central Semitic language spoken across North Africa and the Middle East by over 350 million people. Defined by its classic diglossia — uniting a shared sacred and literary Modern Standard Arabic alongside diverse regional spoken dialects — Arabic also gave rise to one of the world's preeminent calligraphic traditions.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Classical Arabic, preserved in the 7th-century Qur'an and pre-Islamic poetry, spread rapidly alongside the Islamic conquests, absorbing Persian, Greek, and Syriac scholarship during the Islamic Golden Age to serve as Eurasia's premier scientific language (gifting English *algebra, algorithm, chemistry, zenith*). Modern Standard Arabic (*al-Fuṣḥā*) updates Classical grammar and vocabulary for news broadcasts, pan-Arab literature, law, and education.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Simultaneously, everyday conversation uses regional *'Āmmiyya* dialects — Egyptian, Levantine, Gulf, Iraqi, and Maghrebi (Moroccan/Algerian, heavily influenced by Amazigh/Berber and French) — which differ from *Fuṣḥā* and from each other as much as Romance languages do. Speakers navigate this fluidly, mixing formal vocabulary into dialectal syntax ('Educated Spoken Arabic') while Egyptian cinema and Levantine media foster wide passive comprehension.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Written right-to-left in a flowing cursive 28-letter abjad, Arabic script also adapted to write Persian, Urdu, Ottoman Turkish (until 1928), and Swahili historically.

## Key Terminology
- **Al-Fuṣḥā (MSA)**: Modern Standard Arabic used in formal media, books, speeches, and pan-Arab education.
- **'Āmmiyya (Colloquial dialects)**: Native regional spoken Arabic varieties acquired at home across Cairo, Beirut, Baghdad, and Casablanca.
- **Arabic abjad**: 28-letter cursive right-to-left consonantal script featuring distinct initial, medial, final, and isolated letter shapes.
- **Tashkīl (Harakat)**: Optional short-vowel diacritic marks added to Arabic script in the Qur'an, poetry, and children's primers.
- **Sun and Moon letters**: Phonological rule assimilating the definite article *al-* to coronal consonants (e.g., *ash-shams* 'the sun').
- **Maghrebi Arabic**: North African Arabic dialects noted for Berber substrates and rapid consonant-cluster rhythms.
