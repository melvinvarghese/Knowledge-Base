# Computational Linguistics and NLP

## Overview
Computational linguistics and Natural Language Processing (NLP) bridge formal linguistics and computer science to enable machines to parse, translate, summarize, and generate human language. Evolving from brittle hand-coded grammar rules to self-supervised Transformer architectures, NLP transformed daily global computing.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Early NLP (1950s–80s) attempted symbolic rule-based parsing and dictionary translation, stumbling over massive lexical and syntactic ambiguity ('Time flies like an arrow; fruit flies like a banana'). In the 1990s–2000s, Statistical NLP leveraged large corpora, Hidden Markov Models, and Support Vector Machines to learn probabilistic part-of-speech tags and phrase-based translation directly from data.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The 2010s neural revolution introduced dense 'word embeddings' (Word2Vec, capturing semantic analogies like *king - man + woman ≈ queen* in vector geometry), recurrent LSTM networks, and finally the 2017 Transformer architecture ('Attention Is All You Need'). By computing parallel self-attention across subword tokens, modern Large Language Models (LLMs) internalize rich syntactic, semantic, and pragmatic patterns purely from predicting masked or next tokens at planetary scale.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Today's NLP powers instant voice assistants, cross-lingual search, clinical note summarization, and low-resource language tools — though linguists emphasize remaining challenges in factual grounding, cultural nuance, and long-tail endangered languages.

## Key Terminology
- **Tokenization**: Splitting raw text into words or frequent subword units (BPE) for computational processing.
- **Word embeddings**: Dense numerical vector representations encoding semantic similarity through geometric proximity.
- **Self-attention**: Transformer mechanism weighing contextual relevance between every token pair across a sequence simultaneously.
- **Dependency parsing**: Automated syntactic analysis identifying grammatical head-dependent relations in sentences.
- **Named Entity Recognition (NER)**: NLP task extracting people, organizations, dates, and locations from unstructured text.
- **Large Language Model (LLM)**: Neural Transformer trained on vast corpora to model and generate coherent human-like text.
