# Morphology and Word Formation

## Overview
Morphology is the study of internal word structure and how languages combine minimal meaning-bearing units (morphemes) to build new words and encode grammatical relationships like tense, case, and number.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Morphemes divide into free morphemes capable of standing alone (*book*, *run*) and bound morphemes requiring attachment (*un-*, *-ness*, *-ed*). Linguists distinguish derivational morphology — creating new dictionary words or changing part of speech (*happy -> unhappiness*) — from inflectional morphology, which marks grammatical agreement, tense, or case without altering core lexical category (*walk -> walked*). Languages also expand vocabulary via compounding, reduplication, and non-concatenative root-and-pattern templates (as in Arabic *k-t-b* generating *kitāb* 'book' and *kātib* 'writer').

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Morphological typology classifies languages along a spectrum: isolating languages (Mandarin, Vietnamese) favor one-morpheme-per-word; agglutinative languages (Turkish, Japanese, Swahili) stack transparent, single-function affixes cleanly like beads on a string; fusional languages (Latin, Russian, Spanish) pack multiple grammatical categories into single fused endings; and polysynthetic languages (Inuktitut, Mohawk) pack entire sentence-worth meanings into single complex verb words.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Morphology bridges phonology (morphophonemics) and syntax, guiding dictionary lemmatization, search-engine stemming, and second-language grammar pedagogy.

## Key Terminology
- **Morpheme**: Smallest linguistic unit carrying lexical meaning or grammatical function.
- **Inflection vs. Derivation**: Grammatical marking of tense/case/number versus creation of new lexemes or word classes.
- **Agglutinative language**: Language stacking transparent, one-meaning-per-affix morphemes sequentially onto roots.
- **Fusional language**: Language blending multiple grammatical meanings (case, number, gender) into single unsegmentable affixes.
- **Polysynthetic language**: Language incorporating nouns and multiple modifiers into single highly complex verb words.
- **Allomorph**: Variant phonological realization of the same morpheme (e.g., English plural /-s/, /-z/, /-ɪz/).
