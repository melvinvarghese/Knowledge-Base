# Decipherment of Ancient Scripts

## Overview
Deciphering forgotten writing systems ranks among humanity's greatest intellectual detective stories. Combining bilingual monuments, frequency statistics, and structural linguistics, scholars unlocked Egyptian hieroglyphs, cuneiform, Linear B, and Maya glyphs — while Linear A and the Indus script remain tantalizingly mute.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Successful decipherment typically requires knowing either the underlying *language* or having a *bilingual parallel text*. Jean-François Champollion cracked Egyptian hieroglyphs (1822) by matching royal cartouches (*Ptolemy, Cleopatra*) on the Rosetta Stone and recognizing Coptic as living late Egyptian. Henry Rawlinson decoded Old Persian cuneiform from the towering trilingual Behistun Rock inscription, unlocking Akkadian and Sumerian.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Even without a bilingual text, Michael Ventris deciphered Mycenaean Linear B (1952) using Alice Kober's brilliant grid analysis of inflectional endings — proving shockingly that Bronze Age Knossos spoke archaic Greek. Similarly, Yuri Knorozov cracked Maya hieroglyphs in the 1950s by recognizing they functioned logosyllabically (not as pure ideographs) alongside living Mayan languages — transforming Maya stelae from astronomical calendars into vivid dynastic histories.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Today, machine learning assists epigraphers in restoring damaged Greek inscriptions and scanning carbonized Herculaneum papyrus scrolls via X-ray tomography without unrolling them.

## Key Terminology
- **Bilingual inscription**: Parallel text in two or more scripts (e.g., Rosetta Stone, Behistun) providing a key for decipherment.
- **Cartouche**: Oval frame enclosing royal Egyptian names in hieroglyphs, crucial to Champollion's phonetic breakthrough.
- **Kober's grids**: Tabulation of recurring stem-and-suffix patterns enabling Ventris to deduce Linear B vowel/consonant relationships.
- **Undeciphered scripts**: Ancient writing systems (Minoan Linear A, Indus Valley, Rongorongo) lacking bilingual keys or sufficient corpus length.
- **Epigraphy**: Scholarly study of inscriptions engraved on stone, metal, or clay.
- **Syllabogram**: Written sign representing a phonetic syllable rather than a whole word or single phoneme.
