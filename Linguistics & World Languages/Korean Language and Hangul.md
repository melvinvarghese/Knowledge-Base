# Korean Language and Hangul

## Overview
Spoken by ~80 million people across the Korean Peninsula and diaspora, Korean is a Koreanic language famous both for its rich agglutinative verb endings and for *Hangul* — the world's only major alphabet intentionally engineered from phonetic principles by a reigning monarch.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Before the 15th century, Koreans wrote laboriously in Classical Chinese (*Hanja*), leaving ordinary citizens illiterate. In 1443, King Sejong the Great and royal scholars promulgated *Hunminjeongeum* ('The Proper Sounds for the Instruction of the People'): Hangul. Remarkably, Hangul consonant shapes visually diagram speech-organ anatomy — ㄱ /k/ traces the tongue root blocking the velum, ㄴ /n/ the tongue tip touching the alveolar ridge, ㅁ /m/ closed lips, and ㅇ /ŋ/ the open throat — while letters cluster neatly into syllabic square blocks.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Grammatically, Korean shares striking typological parallels with Japanese (SOV word order, topic/subject particles, agglutinative suffixes, and multi-tiered speech-level honorifics distinguishing formal polite, casual polite, and intimate plain endings), though linguists treat Koreanic and Japonic as distinct families with no proven common ancestor. Like Japanese and Vietnamese, Korean absorbed a vast Sino-Korean academic vocabulary alongside native roots.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Celebrated annually on Hangul Day, Hangul's featural design achieved near-universal literacy in modern Korea while powering effortless mobile thumb-typing.

## Key Terminology
- **Hangul**: Featural Korean alphabet promulgated by King Sejong in 1443–1446, grouping letters into syllable blocks.
- **Featural writing system**: Script whose letter shapes systematically encode physical articulatory features of the vocal tract.
- **Hunminjeongeum**: 1446 royal promulgation document explaining Hangul's philosophical and phonetic design.
- **Speech levels (Korean)**: Mandatory verb-final endings calibrated to listener age, social rank, and intimacy.
- **Tense consonants**: Three-way Korean stop contrast among plain, aspirated, and stiff 'fortis' consonants (/p, pʰ, p͈/).
- **Hanja**: Traditional Chinese characters historically used in Korea and still encountered in academic or legal contexts.
