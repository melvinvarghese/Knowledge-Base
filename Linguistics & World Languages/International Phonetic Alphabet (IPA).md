# International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)

## Overview
Created in 1888 by French and British language teachers led by Paul Passy, the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA) provides a standardized one-symbol-per-sound notation capable of transcribing any spoken human language accurately.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Traditional alphabets like English spelling are notoriously inconsistent (*though, through, tough, cough* share *-ough* yet pronounce four different rhymes). The IPA eliminates ambiguity by assigning one unique symbol to every distinct speech sound across pulmonic consonants, non-pulmonic clicks/implosives, vowels, tones, and diacritics.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Linguists use two levels of IPA transcription: 'broad' (phonemic) transcription inside slashes `/pɪn/` capturing only meaning-distinguishing contrasts, and 'narrow' (phonetic) transcription inside square brackets `[pʰɪ̃n]` recording exact aspiration, nasalization, and vowel length. The IPA chart itself doubles as an anatomical map: consonant columns move left-to-right from lips (bilabial) down to throat (glottal), while rows move top-to-bottom by constriction manner.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Today, the IPA is indispensable across field linguistics, speech-language pathology, opera and classical vocal coaching, dialect coaching in cinema, and pronunciation keys in major dictionaries.

## Key Terminology
- **IPA (International Phonetic Alphabet)**: Standardized global notation assigning one unambiguous symbol to each distinct human speech sound.
- **Broad vs. Narrow transcription**: Phonemic notation capturing contrastive sounds (/.../) versus detailed phonetic notation ([...]).
- **Diacritic**: Small mark added to an IPA symbol indicating subtle modifications like aspiration ([ʰ]) or nasalization ([̃]).
- **Pulmonic egressive**: Standard airstream mechanism pushing air outward from the lungs used in most human speech sounds.
- **Suprasegmentals**: IPA markings for prosodic features spanning syllables: stress, length, tone, and intonation.
- **Vowel quadrilateral**: Trapezoidal IPA diagram mapping vowel tongue height and front-back position.
