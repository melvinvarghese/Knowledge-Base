# Abjad and Alphabetic Writing Systems

## Overview
The invention of the alphabet — reducing hundreds of cumbersome syllabic or word signs down to roughly 20–30 sound symbols — democratized literacy across the ancient Mediterranean and Eurasia. Along the way, scripts diverged into consonantal *abjads*, vowel-marked *abugidas*, and full *alphabets*.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Around 1800 BCE, Semitic miners in Egypt's Sinai adapted Egyptian hieroglyphs acrophonically — taking the first sound of each pictured word (*'aleph* 'ox' -> /ʔ/, *bet* 'house' -> /b/) to create Proto-Sinaitic, ancestor of the Phoenician script. Because Semitic roots rely on consonants while vowels fill predictable templates, Phoenician, Hebrew, and Arabic evolved as *abjads*: scripts writing primarily consonants (with optional diacritic vowel dots for learners or sacred texts).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
When Greeks borrowed the Phoenician abjad (~800 BCE), they repurposed unused Semitic consonant letters (*'aleph, he, yod, 'ayin*) to write obligatory vowels (*A, E, I, O*), creating the first true full *alphabet* — later spawning Latin and Cyrillic scripts. Meanwhile, South and Southeast Asian scripts descending from ancient Brahmi (Devanagari, Tamil, Thai, Tibetan) developed as *abugidas* (alphasyllabaries): every consonant letter carries an inherent default vowel (/ka/) modified by attached vowel strokes (/ki, ku/).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Understanding abjads, abugidas, and true alphabets explains reading acquisition differences globally and guides Unicode digital text rendering.

## Key Terminology
- **Abjad**: Consonant-focused writing system (e.g., Arabic, Hebrew, Phoenician) where vowels are omitted or marked optionally.
- **Abugida (Alphasyllabary)**: Script (e.g., Devanagari, Ethiopic, Thai) where consonant symbols carry a default vowel altered by diacritics.
- **True alphabet**: Writing system giving equal, independent letter status to both consonants and vowels (Greek, Latin, Cyrillic).
- **Acrophony**: Naming or deriving a letter's phonetic value from the initial sound of its depicted object.
- **Orthographic depth**: Consistency of spelling-to-sound rules: shallow (Finnish, Spanish) versus deep (English, French).
- **Syllabary**: Writing system where each distinct symbol represents a whole CV syllable (e.g., Cherokee, Japanese Kana).
