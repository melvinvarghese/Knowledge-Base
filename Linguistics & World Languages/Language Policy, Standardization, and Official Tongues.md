# Language Policy, Standardization, and Official Tongues

## Overview
How does one regional dialect become a prestigious national language while neighboring varieties are dismissed as 'mere dialects'? Language policy studies how states, academies, and schools select, codify, and regulate official languages — famously captured in Max Weinreich's quip: 'A language is a dialect with an army and a navy.'

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Einar Haugen modeled language standardization as four steps: selection of a prestige variety (often the capital's dialect, like Parisian French or Castilian Spanish), codification via dictionaries and grammars, elaboration for science and law, and institutional acceptance through schooling and printing. Some nations enforce top-down academies — such as France's *Académie française* (1635) or Spain's *Real Academia Española* — while English relies on decentralized dictionaries and style guides.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Postcolonial and multilingual states navigate high-stakes policy trade-offs: India's 'Three-Language Formula' balances Hindi, English, and regional state tongues; Switzerland grants equal federal status to German, French, Italian, and Romansh; and South Africa recognizes twelve official languages including South African Sign Language.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Linguistic human rights advocates champion mother-tongue early primary education, proving children learn literacy and even dominant global languages faster when first taught to read in the language they speak at home.

## Key Terminology
- **Standard language**: Codified prestige variety taught in schools and used in law, media, and formal administration.
- **Language planning**: Deliberate governmental or institutional efforts to influence language status, spelling, or vocabulary.
- **Académie française**: Prestigious 17th-century French institution charged with guarding standard French grammar and purism.
- **Linguistic purism**: Ideology resisting foreign loanwords and dialectal shifts to preserve an idealized 'pure' language.
- **Mother-tongue education**: Pedagogical policy teaching early literacy first in a child's native home language.
- **Official vs. National language**: Language designated for government and courts versus a language symbolizing shared cultural identity.
