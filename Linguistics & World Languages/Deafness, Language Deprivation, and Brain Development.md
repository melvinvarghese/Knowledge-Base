# Deafness, Language Deprivation, and Brain Development

## Overview
Over 90% of deaf children are born to hearing parents, creating an urgent medical and educational intersection: when children receive neither accessible spoken speech nor early sign language during early childhood, 'language deprivation' can permanently impair syntax, working memory, and Theory of Mind.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Rachel Mayberry's landmark studies of deaf adults who first encountered ASL at birth versus age 5, 9, or 14 proved that early language *modality* matters far less than *timing*. Adults who acquired ASL from birth achieved native mastery and effortless English reading later, whereas late-first-language learners showed lifelong deficits in complex syntax, phonological processing, and even non-linguistic executive tasks.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Historically, misguided 19th–20th-century 'oralism' banned sign languages in deaf schools, forcing children to rely solely on lip-reading (which visually obscures over 60% of English consonants). Even with modern cochlear implants, outcomes vary widely — making early bimodal bilingualism (access to both sign language and spoken/written language from infancy) a powerful protective safeguard.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Recognizing language deprivation as a preventable public-health issue is transforming pediatric audiology, early-intervention policy, and deaf children's educational rights globally.

## Key Terminology
- **Language deprivation**: Neurodevelopmental harm resulting from lack of accessible natural language input during early childhood.
- **Bimodal bilingualism**: Simultaneous acquisition of both a visual sign language and a spoken/written language.
- **Oralism**: Historical educational philosophy forbidding sign language in favor of exclusive speech and lip-reading training.
- **Critical period (SLA/L1)**: Maturational window in early childhood essential for full native first-language syntactic wiring.
- **Visual-spatial working memory**: Short-term memory buffers supporting sign language comprehension and spatial reasoning.
- **Deaf Culture**: Linguistic and cultural community centered on natural sign languages, shared history, and visual norms.
