# African and Diaspora Languages

## Overview
Africa hosts roughly one-third of the world's living languages (~2,000+ tongues) across four broad groupings — Niger-Congo, Afroasiatic, Nilo-Saharan, and Khoisan click families — alongside vibrant Atlantic and Indian Ocean diaspora creoles.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Across West and East Africa, major regional languages serve tens of millions: Hausa (Afroasiatic Chadic, northern Nigeria/Niger), Yoruba and Igbo (Niger-Congo, Nigeria), Amharic (South Semitic, Ethiopia, written in the ancient Ge'ez abugida), Oromo and Somali (Cushitic), and Swahili (Bantu). Southern Africa's Khoisan languages (such as !Xóõ/Taa) possess the largest consonant inventories on Earth — over 100 phonemes including dozens of distinct click releases.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
During the transatlantic slave trade, enslaved Africans carried grammatical templates, tonal prosody, and discourse practices (call-and-response, proverbs) to the Americas, seeding Haitian Creole, Jamaican Patois, Gullah (coastal Georgia/South Carolina), Papiamento, and Saramaccan (Suriname, preserving African lexical tones).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Today, African urban youth forge dynamic multilingual hybrids like Nairobi's *Sheng* (Swahili-English-Luo) and Johannesburg's *Tsotsitaal*, while African tech hubs build voice AI for low-resource African tongues.

## Key Terminology
- **Ge'ez (Ethiopic) script**: Ancient South Semitic abugida still used to write Amharic, Tigrinya, and liturgical Ge'ez.
- **Khoisan click languages**: Non-Bantu Southern African families possessing unmatched inventories of click consonants.
- **Haitian Creole (Kreyòl)**: French-lexifier creole with West African Fongbe/Gbe substrate grammar spoken by ~12 million people.
- **Gullah (Geechee)**: English-lexifier creole preserved in the Sea Islands of South Carolina and Georgia.
- **Sheng**: Vibrant Kenyan urban youth sociolect blending Swahili grammar with English and local Kenyan vocabulary.
- **Oral literature**: Rich African tradition of proverbs, praise poetry, and griot epics anchoring linguistic artistry.
