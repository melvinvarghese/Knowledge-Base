# Sign Language Interpreting and Accessibility

## Overview
Sign language interpreting performs simultaneous multimodal translation across completely different sensory channels — converting linear spoken English into three-dimensional visual-spatial ASL (and vice versa) in real time under intense cognitive load.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Unlike spoken conference interpreters translating between two vocal languages, bimodal sign interpreters must bridge differing word orders, spatial indexing, facial grammar, and cultural norms simultaneously without visual interference. Certified Deaf Interpreters (CDIs) — native deaf signers working alongside hearing interpreters — provide vital cultural and linguistic nuance in high-stakes courtroom trials, medical emergencies, and diverse signing registers.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Legally anchored by disability rights laws like the 1990 Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA), Video Relay Service (VRS) and live concert/emergency press interpreting expanded public access dramatically — though persistent shortages and inconsistent medical/legal certification remain challenges.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Sign interpreting exemplifies real-time cognitive multitasking and affirms linguistic accessibility as a fundamental civic right.

## Key Terminology
- **Bimodal interpreting**: Simultaneous translation between an auditory-spoken language and a visual-spatial sign language.
- **Certified Deaf Interpreter (CDI)**: Deaf specialist working alongside hearing interpreters to ensure accurate, culturally fluent communication.
- **Video Relay Service (VRS)**: Telecommunications service connecting deaf signers and hearing callers via live video interpreters.
- **Lag time (Décalage)**: Brief processing buffer (typically 2–4 seconds) interpreters maintain to grasp full clause meaning before signing.
- **Team interpreting**: Two interpreters alternating every 20–30 minutes during intense assignments to prevent cognitive fatigue.
- **Deaf Gain**: Concept celebrating the unique visual, spatial, and community perspectives of Deaf experience.
