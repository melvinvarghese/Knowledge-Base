# Forensic Linguistics and Authorship

## Overview
Forensic linguistics applies phonetic, grammatical, and statistical corpus methods to legal disputes — from attributing anonymous ransom notes and unmasking pseudonymous authors to auditing police Miranda warnings and trademark confusion.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Coined by Jan Svartvik in 1968, forensic linguistics divides into analyzing the language of the legal process (comprehension of jury instructions, coercive police questioning) and using language as physical evidence. Famously, Roger Shuy analyzed FBI covert recordings to distinguish genuine criminal intent from conversational entrapment, while Malcolm Coulthard exposed fabricated police confessions by showing identical phrasing across supposedly independent witness statements.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
In authorship attribution ('stylometry'), linguists rely less on flashy vocabulary than on unconscious, high-frequency 'function words' (*of, the, while, upon*), punctuation habits, and sentence-length distributions — famously identifying J.K. Rowling as the pseudonymous author of *The Cuckoo's Calling* (2013) and resolving disputed Federalist Papers essays. In trademark disputes, phoneticians and semanticists assess whether two brand names sound or mean confusingly alike.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Today, forensic linguists also detect AI-generated plagiarism, audit emergency 911 calls, and push courts to rewrite opaque legalese into plain, accessible civic language.

## Key Terminology
- **Stylometry**: Statistical analysis of subtle linguistic habits (function words, syntax) to determine authorship.
- **Idiolect**: An individual speaker's unique personal dialect shaped by region, background, and stylistic habits.
- **Function words**: Closed-class grammatical words (*the, by, however*) whose unconscious frequencies serve as reliable authorial fingerprints.
- **Miranda comprehension**: Psycholinguistic testing showing juveniles and non-native speakers frequently misunderstand legal rights wording.
- **Trademark confusion**: Forensic phonetic and semantic assessment of whether competing brand names mislead consumers.
- **Plagiarism detection**: Computational and stylistic comparison identifying unattributed copying or paraphrasing.
