# Stenography, Shorthand, and Rapid Writing

## Overview
From ancient Roman Tironian notes and Isaac Pitman's phonetic shorthand to modern chorded stenotype machines, rapid writing systems bypass slow orthographic spelling to capture live human speech at 200+ words per minute.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Traditional pen shorthand systems — Pitman (1837, encoding voiced/voiceless pairs via thick vs. thin strokes) and Gregg (1888, cursive elliptical loops) — abandon inconsistent English spelling for pure phonetic sounds paired with brief arbitrary outlines for frequent words. In the 20th century, mechanical and electronic stenotype machines replaced pens with a 22-key 'chorded' keyboard: court reporters press multiple keys simultaneously like piano chords (left hand initial consonants, thumbs vowels, right hand final consonants) to output entire phonetic syllables per stroke.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
While Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) assists routine transcription, live human stenographers remain unmatched in noisy courtrooms, overlapping multi-speaker depositions, and real-time broadcast closed-captioning (CART).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Stenography illustrates how stripping redundant spelling unlocks real-time human-speed text entry.

## Key Terminology
- **Phonetic shorthand**: Rapid writing system encoding spoken sounds rather than conventional spelling (Pitman, Gregg).
- **Chorded keyboard**: Stenotype input method pressing multiple keys simultaneously to record whole syllables in one stroke.
- **Tironian notes**: Ancient Roman shorthand system invented by Cicero's scribe Tiro, surviving in the ampersand/et symbol.
- **Briefs (Steno)**: Compressed single-chord outlines representing high-frequency words or legal phrases.
- **CART (Communication Access Realtime Translation)**: Live verbatim captioning service providing instant text access for deaf and hard-of-hearing audiences.
- **Words per minute (WPM)**: Speed benchmark where certified court stenographers routinely exceed 225 WPM with 98%+ accuracy.
