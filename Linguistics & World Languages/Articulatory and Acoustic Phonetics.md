# Articulatory and Acoustic Phonetics

## Overview
Phonetics is the empirical study of human speech sounds — how they are physically produced by the vocal tract (articulatory), transmitted as sound waves (acoustic), and decoded by the ear and brain (auditory). Grounded in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA), phonetics provides a universal framework for describing every possible human speech sound.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Articulatory phonetics classifies consonants along three core parameters: voicing (whether the vocal folds vibrate, separating /z/ from /s/), place of articulation (bilabial, alveolar, velar, glottal, etc.), and manner of articulation (stops/plosives, fricatives, affricates, nasals, approximants). Vowels are mapped inside a two-dimensional acoustic space defined by tongue height (high to low), tongue backness (front to back), and lip rounding. Beyond pulmonic egressive airstream sounds used in English, languages worldwide exploit implosives, ejectives, and Khoisan clicks.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Acoustic phonetics visualizes speech using spectrograms plotting frequency against time and intensity. Vowels appear as dark horizontal bands called formants (F1 tracking tongue height inversely, F2 tracking frontness), while fricatives appear as high-frequency turbulent noise. Crucially, coarticulation means sounds overlap smoothly in running speech rather than sitting like discrete typed letters.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Phonetics underpins speech-language pathology, forensic voice comparison, second-language pronunciation coaching, and neural text-to-speech synthesis.

## Key Terminology
- **Place and Manner of Articulation**: Physical location of vocal tract constriction and how airflow is modified to form consonants.
- **Formants (F1, F2)**: Resonant frequency peaks of the vocal tract that acoustically distinguish vowel qualities.
- **Spectrogram**: Visual acoustic graph displaying time, frequency, and amplitude of speech waves.
- **Coarticulation**: Articulatory overlap where neighboring speech sounds influence each other's physical production.
- **Voice Onset Time (VOT)**: Millisecond interval between stop release and vocal-fold vibration distinguishing voiced and voiceless stops.
- **Click consonants**: Ingressive velaric sounds produced by suction in the mouth, prominent in Southern African Khoisan and Bantu languages.
