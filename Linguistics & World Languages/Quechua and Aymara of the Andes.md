# Quechua and Aymara of the Andes

## Overview
Spoken across Peru, Bolivia, Ecuador, and northern Chile by ~10 million people, Quechua (*Runasimi*, 'people's speech') and neighboring Aymara preserve profound Andean linguistic traditions — famous for obligatory evidential suffixes and a reversed spatial metaphor of time.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Adopted as the administrative lingua franca of the Inca Empire (*Tawantinsuyu*), Quechua is strictly agglutinative and SOV, stacking suffixes effortlessly to build nuance. Crucially, every declarative Quechua and Aymara sentence obligates an 'evidential' suffix signaling *how* the speaker knows the claim: *-mi/-n* (witnessed firsthand), *-shi/-s* (heard secondhand/folklore), or *-chá* (inferred conjecture) — treating epistemic honesty as mandatory grammar.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Strikingly, Rafael Núñez and Eve Sweetser proved Aymara speakers reverse English's time metaphor: *nayra* ('eye/front') refers to the **past** (because history is already *seen* before our eyes), while *qhipa* ('back/behind') refers to the **future** (unseen behind our shoulders) — confirmed by unconscious backward-pointing gestures!

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Co-official in Peru and Bolivia, Quechua and Aymara thrive in Andean radio, bilingual schooling, and contemporary hip-hop.

## Key Terminology
- **Runasimi**: Native name for Quechua meaning 'human/people's speech,' spread widely as the Inca lingua franca.
- **Obligatory evidentials**: Quechua/Aymara suffixes encoding whether a claim was directly witnessed, reported, or inferred.
- **Past-in-front metaphor**: Aymara conceptual mapping placing the known past visibly ahead and unknown future behind.
- **Quipu (Khipu)**: Inca knotted-cord recording system used for census, tribute, and potentially narrative encoding.
- **Inclusive vs. Exclusive 'we'**: Pronoun contrast separating *ñuqanchik* (including listener) from *ñuqayku* (excluding listener).
- **Andean Sprachbund**: Intensive phonological convergence between unrelated Quechua and Aymara (ejective/aspirated stops).
