# Turkish and Turkic Languages

## Overview
Spoken from the Balkans and Anatolia across Central Asia to Siberia by ~200 million people, the Turkic family — led by Turkish, Azerbaijani, Uzbek, Kazakh, and Kyrgyz — showcases remarkably transparent agglutinative suffixation, strict vowel harmony, and grammatical evidentiality.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Earliest attested in 8th-century Orkhon Old Turkic runic inscriptions in Mongolia, Turkic languages share strikingly close core grammar and vocabulary across thousands of kilometers. Words build cleanly rightward from an invariable root through ordered chains of suffixes: Turkish *ev-ler-imiz-de* ('house-plural-our-in' = 'in our houses'), governed by front/back and rounding vowel harmony.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Typologically SOV and genderless, Turkish verbs obligatorily distinguish witnessed past (*-dı*: 'I saw it happen') from non-witnessed/reported past (*-mış*: 'apparently / I heard it happened'). In 1928, Atatürk replaced Ottoman Perso-Arabic script with a crisp phonetic Latin alphabet while purging many Arabic/Persian loans.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Turkic languages provide textbook case studies for morphological parsing in NLP and comparative Eurasian migration history.

## Key Terminology
- **Orkhon inscriptions**: Oldest surviving Turkic texts (8th century CE) carved in runiform script in Mongolia.
- **Evidentiality (-mış)**: Obligatory Turkish past-tense suffix marking hearsay, inference, or surprise versus direct observation.
- **Agglutinative chaining**: Sequential stacking of single-function suffixes onto Turkic noun and verb stems.
- **Ottoman Turkish**: Historical administrative register blending Turkic grammar with extensive Persian and Arabic vocabulary.
- **Oghuz branch**: Southwestern Turkic subgroup comprising Turkish, Azerbaijani, and Turkmen.
- **Front-back harmony**: Rule matching suffix vowels to the frontness or backness of the preceding stem vowel.
