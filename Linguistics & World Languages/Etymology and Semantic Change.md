# Etymology and Semantic Change

## Overview
Etymology traces individual words back through centuries of sound shifts, cultural borrowing, and metaphorical drift. Semantic change reveals how concrete physical actions slowly morph into abstract concepts — turning Latin *comprehendere* ('to grasp physically') into *comprehend* ('to understand').

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Meanings shift along surprisingly predictable pathways: *broadening* (widening a specific term like *dog*, once a specific breed, or *holiday* 'holy day'), *narrowing* (restricting *meat* from 'all food' to animal flesh, or *deer* from 'any wild animal'), *amelioration* (elevating *knight* from 'servant boy' and *nice* from 'ignorant'), and *pejoration* (degrading *silly* from 'blessed/happy' and *villain* from 'farmhand').

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Everyday vocabulary also preserves fossilized history through loanwords (English's Norman French culinary split: Anglo-Saxon *cow/pig/sheep* in the barn vs. French *beef/pork/mutton* on the noble table) and 'folk etymology,' where speakers reshape unfamiliar words to look meaningful (turning Algonquian *otchek* into *woodchuck*, or French *écrevisse* into *crayfish*).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Etymology cautions against the 'etymological fallacy' — mistakenly insisting a word's true meaning today must equal its ancient root — while illuminating deep cultural contact across trade routes.

## Key Terminology
- **Amelioration vs. Pejoration**: Historical shift of a word's connotation toward more positive prestige versus more negative stigma.
- **Broadening vs. Narrowing**: Expansion of a word's scope to cover more referents versus restriction to a narrower subset.
- **Folk etymology**: Popular reinterpretation altering an unfamiliar word's spelling or sound to resemble familiar words.
- **Etymological fallacy**: Logical error claiming a word's current valid meaning is fixed by its oldest historical root.
- **Doublet**: Two distinct words in the same language derived from the same ancestral root via different borrowing paths (e.g., *shirt/skirt*).
- **Loanword**: Word adopted from one language into another and adapted to fit native pronunciation.
