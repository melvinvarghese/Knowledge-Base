# Onomastics: Names of People and Places

## Overview
Onomastics studies the origins, cultural patterns, and linguistic evolution of proper names — dividing into *anthroponymy* (personal names) and *toponymy* (place names). Because place names outlast empires, rivers and mountains act as linguistic fossils preserving vanished languages.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Across Europe and the Americas, toponymy reads like a layered archaeological dig. In Britain, river names (*Thames, Avon*) remain Celtic, towns ending in *-chester/-caster* (*Manchester, Lancaster*) preserve Roman *castra* forts, *-by* and *-thorpe* (*Derby, Scunthorpe*) mark Viking Norse settlements, and *-ton/-ham* reflect Anglo-Saxon farms. Across North America, nearly half of US state names (*Massachusetts, Mississippi, Dakota, Utah*) preserve Indigenous Algonquian, Siouan, and Uto-Aztecan words.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Personal naming systems vary strikingly across cultures: Roman *tria nomina*, Slavic patronymics (*Ivanovich* 'son of Ivan'), Spanish dual maternal-paternal surnames, Icelandic patronymic/matronymic naming (*Jónsdóttir* — meaning phonebooks sort by first name!), and Chinese lineage-poem generation names. Sociolinguists also track how given names rise and fall in rapid fashion waves mimicking cultural taste.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Onomastics informs historical migration research, legal trademark naming, and Indigenous place-name restoration (such as restoring *Denali* and *Uluru*).

## Key Terminology
- **Onomastics**: Linguistic study of the etymology, history, and social usage of proper names.
- **Toponymy**: Study of geographic place names, preserving traces of ancient migrations and extinct tongues.
- **Anthroponymy**: Study of personal given names, surnames, patronymics, and naming customs.
- **Hydronymy**: Study of river and water-body names, typically the oldest surviving linguistic stratum in any region.
- **Patronymic / Matronymic**: Name component derived from a father's or mother's given name (e.g., *Johnson, Ólafsdóttir*).
- **Exonym vs. Endonym**: External foreign name for a place (*Germany, Munich*) versus the local native name (*Deutschland, München*).
