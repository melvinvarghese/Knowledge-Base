# Sanskrit and Indo-Aryan Languages

## Overview
Sanskrit is the classical sacred and scholarly language of South Asia, preserving archaic Indo-European morphology and Pāṇini's astonishing 4th-century BCE generative grammar. Its Indo-Aryan descendants — including Hindi-Urdu, Bengali, Marathi, Punjabi, and Gujarati — are spoken by over 1 billion people today.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Attested first in the Rigveda (~1500–1200 BCE) as Vedic Sanskrit, the language was codified into Classical Sanskrit around the 4th century BCE by grammarian Pāṇini in the *Aṣṭādhyāyī* — ~4,000 algebraic meta-rules generating every valid Sanskrit word form with mathematical economy unmatched until modern formal computer science. Sanskrit features eight noun cases, three genders, three numbers (including dual), and rich verbal roots.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
While Sanskrit remained a Pan-Indian elite literary and philosophical medium (akin to Latin in Europe), spoken vernacular *Prakrits* evolved into modern Indo-Aryan tongues. Hindi and Urdu share virtually identical spoken grammar and core Prakrit vocabulary ('Hindustani'), diverging primarily in formal register and script: Hindi writes in Devanagari abugida drawing on Sanskrit loans, whereas Urdu writes in Perso-Arabic Nastaliq drawing on Persian and Arabic vocabulary.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Pāṇini's formal rule ordering and zero-morpheme notation directly inspired 19th-century European comparative philology and modern formal-language theory in computer science.

## Key Terminology
- **Pāṇini's Aṣṭādhyāyī**: 4th-century BCE formal generative grammar of Sanskrit using algebraic meta-rules.
- **Prakrits**: Middle Indo-Aryan regional spoken vernaculars from which modern Hindi, Bengali, and Marathi descended.
- **Devanagari**: Abugida script used for Sanskrit, Hindi, Marathi, and Nepali, hanging letters from a horizontal top bar.
- **Hindustani (Hindi-Urdu)**: Shared spoken Indo-Aryan core underlying both standard Hindi and standard Urdu.
- **Sandhi**: Systematic phonological sound adjustments occurring when morphemes or words meet in speech.
- **Vedic vs. Classical Sanskrit**: Archaic liturgical language of the Vedas versus Pāṇini-standardized literary Sanskrit.
