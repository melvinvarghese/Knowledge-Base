# Polynesian Languages (Hawaiian, Māori, Samoan)

## Overview
Descending from Proto-Polynesian voyagers who settled the vast Polynesian Triangle from Hawai'i to Aotearoa (New Zealand) and Rapa Nui, Polynesian languages exhibit extraordinary structural closeness, tiny melodious sound inventories, and world-leading indigenous revitalization.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Polynesian languages are famous for open CV syllables and compact consonant sets — Hawaiian uses only eight consonants (*h, k, l, m, n, p, w, '* glottal stop *ʻokina*) alongside five short and five long vowels marked with *kahakō* macrons. Because consonant changes across islands followed regular sound correspondences (Samoan /t/ -> Hawaiian /k/, /ŋ/ -> /n/: Samoan *tangata* = Māori *tangata* = Hawaiian *kanaka* 'person'), speakers readily recognize cognates across 4,000 miles of ocean.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Grammatically VSO and isolating, Polynesian languages distinguish inclusive vs. exclusive 'we,' singular/dual/plural pronouns, and subtle *a/o* possessive classes (alienable controlled items like food vs. inalienable inherited ties like parents or land).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Nearly driven to extinction by English-only laws in Hawai'i and New Zealand, Māori *Kōhanga Reo* and Hawaiian *Pūnana Leo* immersion schools created a global blueprint for reclaiming endangered languages.

## Key Terminology
- **Polynesian Triangle**: Vast Pacific region bounded by Hawai'i, Aotearoa (New Zealand), and Rapa Nui (Easter Island).
- **ʻOkina and Kahakō**: Hawaiian glottal stop consonant mark (ʻ) and vowel-length macron (ā), both phonemic.
- **A/O possessive contrast**: Polynesian distinction between acquired/controlled possessions (*a*) and innate/inherited relations (*o*).
- **Dual number**: Dedicated grammatical pronouns for pairs ('we two,' 'you two') alongside singular and plural.
- **Kōhanga Reo / Pūnana Leo**: Pioneering Māori and Hawaiian early-childhood total-immersion 'language nests.'
- **Mana / Tapu**: Pan-Polynesian cultural concepts carried across shared ancestral vocabulary.
