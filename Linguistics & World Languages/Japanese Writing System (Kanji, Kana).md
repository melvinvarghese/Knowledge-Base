# Japanese Writing System (Kanji, Kana)

## Overview
The Japanese writing system is widely considered one of the world's most intricate orthographies, seamlessly weaving three distinct scripts — borrowed logographic *Kanji* alongside two native phonetic syllabaries, *Hiragana* and *Katakana* — within a single sentence.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Because Japanese had no native script before contact with China (4th–5th centuries CE), scholars first wrote in Classical Chinese before adapting *Kanji* phonetically (*man'yōgana*) to transcribe unrelated, agglutinative Japanese grammar. Around the 9th century, two streamlined phonetic syllabaries (46 basic morae each) emerged: cursive *Hiragana* (famed in Heian women's literature like Murasaki Shikibu's *Tale of Genji*) for native grammatical endings and particles, and angular *Katakana* (derived from kanji fragments by Buddhist monks) now used for foreign loanwords, onomatopoeia, and emphasis.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Kanji itself carries dual pronunciation layers: *on'yomi* (Sino-Japanese approximations of ancient Chinese readings used in compounds) and *kun'yomi* (native Japanese words mapped onto the same character). Thus, a single sentence visually separates content nouns/verb stems (Kanji), grammatical inflections (Hiragana), and modern loanwords (Katakana), eliminating the need for word spaces.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Despite 20th-century debates over abolishing Kanji, post-WWII reforms settled on ~2,136 standard *Jōyō Kanji*, and predictive digital input makes complex mixed-script Japanese effortless to type.

## Key Terminology
- **Kanji**: Adapted Chinese logographic characters used in Japanese for lexical nouns, verb stems, and adjective roots.
- **Hiragana**: Cursive Japanese phonetic syllabary encoding native words, particles, and grammatical inflections.
- **Katakana**: Angular Japanese syllabary used primarily for foreign loanwords, scientific names, and stylistic emphasis.
- **On'yomi vs. Kun'yomi**: Chinese-derived compound readings versus native Japanese word readings attached to the same Kanji.
- **Furigana**: Small Hiragana pronunciation guides printed above or beside uncommon Kanji for young or foreign readers.
- **Jōyō Kanji**: Official Japanese Ministry of Education list of 2,136 standard characters for general literacy.
