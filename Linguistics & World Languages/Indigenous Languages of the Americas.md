# Indigenous Languages of the Americas

## Overview
Prior to 1492, over 1,000 distinct languages across dozens of unrelated families were spoken across North, Central, and South America. Exhibiting extraordinary typological diversity — from polysynthetic Mohawk and tonal Mixtec to evidential Quechua — Indigenous American languages transformed modern linguistic theory.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Major surviving families and state tongues include Uto-Aztecan (Nahuatl, spoken by the Mexica/Aztecs and ~1.7 million people today, gifting English *chocolate*, *tomato*, and *avocado*), Mayan languages (over 30 languages spoken by ~6 million in Mesoamerica), Quechuan (lingua franca of the Inca Empire, still spoken by ~8–10 million across Peru, Bolivia, and Ecuador), Guaraní (co-official alongside Spanish in Paraguay), Algonquian (Cree, Ojibwe), Iroquoian (Cherokee, Mohawk), and Na-Dene (Navajo/Diné).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Studying Indigenous American languages shattered Eurocentric assumptions about grammar. Algonquian languages feature 'animacy hierarchies' and obviative third-person marking ('proximate vs. obviative' tracking who is central in a story), while Iroquoian and Eskimo-Aleut (Inuktitut) languages showcase breathtaking polysynthesis, packing subject, object, tense, and adverbials inside a single verb. Navajo's intricate verb morphology even served as an unbreakable WWII military cipher ('Navajo Code Talkers').

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Colonial boarding schools and forced assimilation pushed hundreds of Indigenous languages to extinction or critical endangerment; today, Cherokee, Hawaiian, Māori, and Navajo immersion nests lead global revitalization.

## Key Terminology
- **Polysynthesis**: Morphological architecture combining multiple lexical roots and grammatical affixes into single sentence-length words.
- **Animacy hierarchy**: Grammatical ranking of humans, animals, and objects governing verb agreement in Algonquian and Navajo.
- **Obviative (Fourth person)**: Grammatical distinction tracking a secondary third-person character to prevent pronoun ambiguity.
- **Navajo Code Talkers**: WWII US Marine communicators who used Navajo-based codes never broken by enemy cryptanalysts.
- **Quechua**: Andean language family of the Inca Empire still spoken by millions across South America.
- **Guaraní**: Indigenous Tupian language spoken natively by the majority of Paraguay alongside Spanish.
