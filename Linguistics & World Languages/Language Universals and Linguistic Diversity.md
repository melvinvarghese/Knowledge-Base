# Language Universals and Linguistic Diversity

## Overview
Do all ~7,000 human languages share a deep universal grammatical blueprint, or do they vary almost endlessly across cultures? Debates between Chomskyan generative universals and Nicholas Evans and Stephen Levinson's radical diversity thesis sit at the heart of modern linguistics.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Following Noam Chomsky's Universal Grammar, generativists argued all languages share core hierarchical principles (nouns/verbs, recursion, c-command) beneath superficial parameter settings. In 2009, Evans and Levinson challenged 'the myth of language universals,' cataloging dramatic exceptions: Riau Indonesian lacking strict noun/verb divisions, Pirahã reportedly lacking embedded recursion, Straits Salish collapsing nouns into predicates, and Kayardild inflecting tense onto *nouns*.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Reconciling both camps, functional typologists (Martin Haspelmath, Adele Goldberg) emphasize 'statistical universals' and shared communicative pressures: every human community solves shared problems — referring to people, negating claims, asking questions, tracking time — using converging cognitive and vocal constraints even without rigid innate templates.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Preserving global linguistic diversity ensures cognitive science never mistakes a handful of European languages for the limits of human thought.

## Key Terminology
- **Absolute vs. Statistical universals**: Traits claimed to hold in 100% of languages versus strong cross-linguistic tendencies.
- **Evans-Levinson thesis**: 2009 critique arguing structural diversity across world languages undermines rigid innate Universal Grammar.
- **Functional explanation**: Accounting for cross-linguistic commonalities via shared memory limits, pragmatics, and learnability.
- **Greenbergian universals**: Empirical implicational correlations across word order and morphology.
- **Rarissima**: Extraordinary grammatical features attested in only one or a handful of languages worldwide.
- **Communicative efficiency**: Information-theoretic principle balancing speaker effort against listener clarity across all languages.
