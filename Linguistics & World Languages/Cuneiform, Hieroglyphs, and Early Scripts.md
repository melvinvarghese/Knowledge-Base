# Cuneiform, Hieroglyphs, and Early Scripts

## Overview
Writing was independently invented in only a handful of cradles — notably Mesopotamia (c. 3400 BCE), Egypt (c. 3200 BCE), China (c. 1250 BCE), and Mesoamerica (c. 500 BCE). Early scripts evolved from concrete accounting pictographs into flexible logosyllabic systems capable of recording poetry, law, and history.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
In Sumer (southern Iraq), clay accounting tokens used to track grain and sheep evolved into wedge-shaped *cuneiform* impressed onto wet clay tablets with a reed stylus. Crucially, scribes discovered the 'rebus principle': using a picture's *sound* rather than its literal object meaning (akin to drawing an 'eye' + 'can' for 'I can'), unlocking abstract grammar and names. Sumerian cuneiform was later adapted to write unrelated Semitic Akkadian and Indo-European Hittite.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Simultaneously in Egypt, monumental *hieroglyphs* combined logograms (word signs), phonograms (1-, 2-, and 3-consonant sound signs), and silent semantic determinatives, complemented in daily bureaucracy by cursive hieratic and demotic scripts. Deciphered in 1822 by Jean-François Champollion using the trilingual Rosetta Stone, hieroglyphs preserved three millennia of Egyptian literature, medicine, and statecraft.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Both cuneiform and hieroglyphs demonstrate how writing shifts from specialized priestly-administrative bookkeeping toward universal phonetic flexibility.

## Key Terminology
- **Cuneiform**: Ancient Mesopotamian wedge-shaped writing impressed on clay tablets, originally for Sumerian accounting.
- **Hieroglyphs**: Ancient Egyptian logosyllabic script blending phonetic consonant signs, word symbols, and determinatives.
- **Rebus principle**: Crucial breakthrough using a picture symbol purely for its spoken phonetic value rather than depicted object.
- **Determinative**: Unpronounced semantic classifier glyph placed beside a word to disambiguate meaning.
- **Rosetta Stone**: 196 BCE decree in hieroglyphs, demotic, and Ancient Greek enabling Champollion's 1822 decipherment.
- **Logogram**: Written character representing an entire word or morpheme rather than an individual sound.
