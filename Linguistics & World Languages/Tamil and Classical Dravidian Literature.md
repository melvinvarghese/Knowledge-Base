# Tamil and Classical Dravidian Literature

## Overview
Spoken by ~80+ million people across Tamil Nadu, Sri Lanka, Singapore, and global diasporas, Tamil stands alongside Sanskrit, Greek, and Chinese as one of the world's oldest continuously attested classical traditions — preserving an independent non-Indo-European literary lineage over 2,200 years old.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Recorded first in Tamil-Brahmi cave inscriptions (c. 3rd century BCE) and the *Tolkāppiyam* grammar treatise, early Sangam poetry (c. 300 BCE–300 CE) codified five ecological landscapes (*tiṇai*) symbolizing human love and war with stark restraint. Unlike neighboring Malayalam, Telugu, and Kannada which absorbed heavy Sanskrit vocabulary, formal Tamil actively preserved pure Dravidian roots through the 20th-century *Tanittamil Iyakkam* purist movement.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Modern Tamil exhibits sharp diglossia between formal literary *Centamiḻ* (news, speeches, books) and colloquial *Koṭuntamiḻ*, while its rounded Tamil abugida omits voiced/aspirated stop distinctions in spelling — relying on predictable intervocalic voicing rules.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Recognized as India's first official 'Classical Language' (2004) and an official language of Singapore and Sri Lanka, Tamil thrives across cinema, literature, and digital media.

## Key Terminology
- **Tolkāppiyam**: Earliest surviving Tamil grammar and poetics treatise (c. 1st century BCE–early CE).
- **Centamiḻ vs. Koṭuntamiḻ**: Formal literary Tamil versus everyday spoken colloquial Tamil varieties.
- **Tiṇai**: Classical Sangam poetic framework pairing five ecological landscapes with emotional moods.
- **Rational vs. Non-rational gender**: Tamil noun classification separating gods/humans (*uyartiṇai*) from animals/objects (*ahṟiṇai*).
- **Tamil-Brahmi**: Southern adaptation of ancient Brahmi script used in early Tamil inscriptions.
- **Pure Tamil movement**: 20th-century literary movement replacing Sanskrit loans with native Dravidian vocabulary.
