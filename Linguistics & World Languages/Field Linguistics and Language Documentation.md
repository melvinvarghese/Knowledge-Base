# Field Linguistics and Language Documentation

## Overview
Field linguists travel directly to remote communities to record, transcribe, and analyze undocumented languages alongside native speakers. Descending from Franz Boas's salvage fieldwork, modern collaborative documentation builds dictionaries, audio archives, and practical literacy tools controlled by the community.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Fieldwork blends detective work with deep human trust. Starting often with a 'monolingual demonstration' (or bilingual contact language), linguists elicit basic Swadesh wordlists, record natural storytelling, rituals, and daily conversation, and use Leipzig Glossing Rules to align IPA transcription, morpheme-by-morpheme grammatical tags, and idiomatic translations into a 'Boasian trilogy': grammar, dictionary, and text corpus.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Modern documentary linguistics (Nikolaus Himmelmann) shifted away from extractive 'helicopter research' toward equal community partnership — training local speakers in audio engineering and ELAN time-aligned annotation so archives serve future grandchildren's language-revitalization classrooms directly rather than sitting locked in distant university vaults.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Fieldwork continually uncovers structures previously thought impossible — from Pirahã's unusual prosody and Amazonian evidentiality to whistling languages like Silbo Gomero.

## Key Terminology
- **Boasian trilogy**: Classic documentary standard comprising a reference grammar, comprehensive dictionary, and annotated text corpus.
- **Leipzig Glossing Rules**: International convention for aligning morpheme-by-morpheme grammatical labels beneath transcribed sentences.
- **Elicitation**: Structured field-interview technique testing grammaticality judgments and paradigm variations with native consultants.
- **Swadesh list**: Standardized ~100–200 basic universal core vocabulary items used for initial fieldwork and lexicostatistics.
- **Community-based documentation**: Ethical fieldwork model co-designing goals, ownership, and outputs directly with speaker communities.
- **Whistled speech**: Acoustic transposition of spoken tones or formants into long-distance whistles (e.g., Silbo Gomero, Mazatec).
