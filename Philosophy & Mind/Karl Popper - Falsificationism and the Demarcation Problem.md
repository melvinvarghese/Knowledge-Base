# Karl Popper - Falsificationism and the Demarcation Problem

## Overview
Karl Popper's *The Logic of Scientific Discovery* (1934) addressed the Demarcation Problem by establishing that a scientific theory must be inherently **falsifiable** rather than verifiable.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
The Asymmetry of Falsification: No number of white swan observations can logically prove that 'all swans are white' (induction failure); however, observing a single black swan deductively disproves the claim via modus tollens:

$$P \rightarrow Q, \quad \neg Q \vdash \neg P$$

---

## 2. Structural Analysis & Mathematical Modeling
Demarcating Science from Pseudoscience: Theories that immunize themselves against potential empirical refutation by explaining all possible outcomes (e.g., Freudian psychoanalysis, Marxist historical inevitability) are unscientific dogmas.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Scientific Impact
Standard scientific experimental methodology, clinical trial null hypothesis testing, open society political theory.

---

## Key Terms
- **Falsifiability**: Property of a scientific proposition that it can be contradicted by an empirical observation.
- **Demarcation Problem**: Challenge of defining boundary criteria separating empirical science from metaphysics and pseudoscience.
- **Modus Tollens**: Valid deductive rule of inference: If P then Q; Not Q; therefore Not P.
