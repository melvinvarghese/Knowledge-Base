# Hara Hachi Bu (腹八分目)

> **The Big Idea:** Stop eating when your stomach is 80% full. Leaving a little room at the end of every meal preserves energy, sharpens focus, and dramatically extends healthspan.

---

## 🍚 What Does It Mean?

*Hara Hachi Bu* translates to *"eat until you are 8 parts out of 10 full."* 

This Confucian teaching has been practiced for centuries in Okinawa, Japan—a famous "Blue Zone" known for having the highest concentration of healthy centenarians (people who live past 100) on earth.

Okinawan elders rarely suffer from heart disease, obesity, or diabetes. One of their biggest secrets isn't just *what* they eat, but *how much* they eat.

---

## 🧬 The Biology Behind the 20-Minute Delay

When you eat, your stomach stretches and releases hormones (like leptin) to tell your brain you are satisfied. 

However, this biological communication has a **15 to 20 minute lag**:
- If you eat until you *feel* 100% full at the table, you have actually overeaten by 20%.
- By the time you stand up, you feel bloated, lethargic, and sleepy (a "food coma").
- If you stop when you feel about **80% full** (no longer hungry, but not stuffed), you will feel perfectly satisfied and energized 20 minutes later.

---

## 🛠️ How to Practice Hara Hachi Bu

1. **Eat slowly:** Put your fork down between bites. Chew thoroughly. Give your brain time to register the food.
2. **Use smaller plates:** A full small plate tricks your visual perception into feeling abundant satisfaction without oversized portions.
3. **Ask the 80% question halfway through:** *"If I stopped right now, would I still be starving?"* If the answer is no, stop and pack the rest for later.
4. **Resist mindless seconds:** Don't refill your plate out of habit or boredom. Wait 10 minutes; if you are still genuinely hungry, eat a little more.
