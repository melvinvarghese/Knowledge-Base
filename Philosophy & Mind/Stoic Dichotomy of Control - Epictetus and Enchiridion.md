# Stoic Dichotomy of Control - Epictetus and Enchiridion

## Overview
The Stoic Dichotomy of Control, articulated by Epictetus in the *Enchiridion*, is the foundational principle of Stoic ethics asserting that human flourishing (**Eudaemonia**) requires distinguishing what is up to us from what is not.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
The Fundamental Division: Things in our control (opinions, desires, aversions, moral character) versus things not in our control (body, property, reputation, external outcomes). Suffering stems not from events, but from false judgments about external events.

---

## 2. Structural Analysis & Mathematical Modeling
Apatheia and Amor Fati: Cultivating freedom from irrational passions (**Apatheia**) and joyous acceptance of fate (**Amor Fati**), maintaining internal virtuous agency regardless of external turmoil.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Scientific Impact
Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) foundational framework, emotional resilience, psychological stress mitigation.

---

## Key Terms
- **Dichotomy of Control**: Stoic principle partitioning all reality into factors within our agency versus external factors beyond it.
- **Apatheia**: State of psychological tranquility achieved by liberation from destructive irrational passions.
- **Eudaimonia**: Greek philosophical state of human flourishing, living in accordance with virtuous reason.
