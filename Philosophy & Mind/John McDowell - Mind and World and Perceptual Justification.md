# John McDowell - Mind and World and Perceptual Justification

## Overview
In *Mind and World* (1994), John McDowell resolved the tension between sensory experience and rational conceptual thought, escaping the 'Myth of the Given' through a neo-Kantian synthesis.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
The Myth of the Given vs Coherentism: Sellars showed sensory raw feels cannot rationally justify beliefs without concepts; yet pure coherentism leaves thought spinning in a frictionless conceptual void disconnected from reality.

---

## 2. Structural Analysis & Mathematical Modeling
Conceptual Perceptual Experience: Sensory experiences are themselves already fundamentally permeated by conceptual capacities; perception draws our rational conceptual faculties into passive play, directly tethering human thought to the world.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Scientific Impact
Epistemological justification debates, contemporary philosophy of perception, post-analytic Hegelianism.

---

## Key Terms
- **Myth of the Given**: Fallacy that bare, non-conceptual sensory data can rationally justify propositional beliefs.
- **Space of Reasons**: The normative rational realm where beliefs are justified, evaluated, and criticized by evidence.
- **Frictionless Spinning**: McDowell's metaphor for coherentist epistemologies disconnected from external reality.
