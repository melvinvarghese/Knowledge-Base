# Epicureanism - Ataraxia, Aponia, and Pleasure Calculus

## Overview
Epicureanism, founded by Epicurus (341–270 BCE), is an atomistic philosophy positing that the highest human good is **Ataraxia** (tranquility of soul) and **Aponia** (absence of physical pain) achieved through rational living.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
The Hierarchy of Desires: 1. Natural and Necessary (basic food, water, philosophical friendship: satisfy easily); 2. Natural and Unnecessary (luxury cuisine, fine wine: indulge sparingly); 3. Unnatural and Empty (fame, political power, infinite wealth: avoid entirely as sources of anxiety).

---

## 2. Structural Analysis & Mathematical Modeling
The Tetrapharmakos (The Four-Part Remedy): 1. Don't fear God; 2. Don't worry about death (when we exist, death is not; when death exists, we are not); 3. What is good is easy to obtain; 4. What is terrible is easy to endure.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Scientific Impact
Early philosophical atomism, secular psychological peace, ethical critiques of consumerism.

---

## Key Terms
- **Ataraxia**: Lucid state of robust psychological tranquility characterized by freedom from mental fear and worry.
- **Aponia**: The total absence of bodily physical pain, considered the ultimate physical pleasure by Epicureans.
- **Tetrapharmakos**: The four-part Hellenistic philosophical summary of Epicurean ethical therapy.
