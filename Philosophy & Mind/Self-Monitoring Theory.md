# Self-Monitoring Theory

> **The Big Idea:** Psychologist Mark Snyder’s concept of how much you regulate your self-presentation: **High Self-Monitors** are social chameleons who adjust their behavior to fit any room; **Low Self-Monitors** act authentically true to their internal beliefs regardless of the social setting.

---

## 🦎 The Two Personality Orientations

```
┌─────────────────────────────────┐       ┌─────────────────────────────────┐
│       HIGH SELF-MONITOR         │       │        LOW SELF-MONITOR         │
├─────────────────────────────────┤       ├─────────────────────────────────┤
│ "Who does this situation want   │  vs.  │ "Who am I, and how do I         │
│  me to be?"                     │       │  honestly feel right now?"      │
└─────────────────────────────────┘       └─────────────────────────────────┘
```

---

## 🔍 Detailed Comparison

| Feature | High Self-Monitor (Social Chameleon) | Low Self-Monitor (Authentic Core) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Social Navigation** | Reads social cues instantly; adapts tone, humor, and dress. | Consistent behavior whether at a boardroom meeting or a dive bar. |
| **Friendship Style** | Different friend groups for different activities (golf buddies vs. book club). | Single close group of friends who share core values. |
| **Greatest Strength** | Diplomatic, persuasive, charming, highly successful in politics and sales. | Deeply trustworthy, genuine, principled; never plays fake games. |
| **Greatest Vulnerability** | Risk of feeling empty, fake, or losing touch with who they really are. | Can be socially awkward, stubborn, blunt, or unintentionally offensive. |

---

## 🛠️ Practical Application

* **If you are a High Self-Monitor:** Regularly practice solitude and check in with your core values. Ask: *"What do I genuinely think when nobody is watching?"*
* **If you are a Low Self-Monitor:** Practice empathy and social tact. Being honest does not require being needlessly blunt or disregarding social harmony.
