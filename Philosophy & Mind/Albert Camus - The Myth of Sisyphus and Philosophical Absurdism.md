# Albert Camus - The Myth of Sisyphus and Philosophical Absurdism

## Overview
Albert Camus formulated the philosophy of **Absurdism** in *The Myth of Sisyphus* (1942), exploring the conflict between humanity's desperate longing for inherent meaning and the cold, silent, meaningless universe.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
The Absurd Triad & Rejecting Suicide: The Absurd is born from the collision of human demand for purpose with the irrational world. Camus rejects physical suicide (cowardly surrender) and philosophical suicide (religious leaps of faith), demanding defiant revolt.

---

## 2. Structural Analysis & Mathematical Modeling
Sisyphus the Absurd Hero: Condemned for eternity to roll a boulder up a mountain only to watch it roll back down, Sisyphus triumphs through conscious defiance: 'One must imagine Sisyphus happy.'

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Scientific Impact
Existential literature, coping psychology with chronic illness and tragedy, humanistic rebellion.

---

## Key Terms
- **The Absurd**: The irreconcilable conflict between the human search for meaning and the indifferent universe.
- **Philosophical Suicide**: Adopting religious or ideological dogmas to evade the anxiety of the Absurd.
- **Revolt**: Camus's ethical answer to the Absurd: living passionately in lucid, defiant consciousness without illusions.
