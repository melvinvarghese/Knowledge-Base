# Utilitarianism - Bentham and Mill

> **The Big Idea:** The most prominent consequentialist moral theory. An action is morally right if and only if it produces **the greatest happiness for the greatest number of people**. Morality is measured by results, not good intentions.

---

## ⚖️ The Core Principle: Utility

* **Utility:** Defined as the balance of pleasure over pain, or well-being over suffering.
* **The Utilitarian Calculus:** Before making any moral or political decision, calculate the net happiness:
  $$\text{Net Utility} = (\text{Total Happiness Produced}) - (\text{Total Suffering Caused})$$
* **Radical Equality:** In utilitarian calculations, everyone’s happiness counts equally. The pain of a billionaire or a president is no more important than the pain of a beggar or an animal.

---

## 🏛️ Bentham vs. Mill: Quantity vs. Quality

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────┐       ┌──────────────────────────────────────┐
│           JEREMY BENTHAM             │       │          JOHN STUART MILL            │
│         (Act Utilitarianism)         │       │         (Rule Utilitarianism)        │
├──────────────────────────────────────┤       ├──────────────────────────────────────┤
│ "Pleasure is pleasure."              │  vs.  │ "Some pleasures are higher quality." │
│ If push-pin (a simple pub game)      │       │ Reading poetry or solving a math     │
│ brings more pleasure than Mozart,    │       │ puzzle is superior to eating donuts  │
│ push-pin is morally superior.        │       │ all day.                             │
└──────────────────────────────────────┘       └──────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Mill famously wrote:
> *"It is better to be a human being dissatisfied than a pig satisfied; better to be Socrates dissatisfied than a fool satisfied."*

---

## ⚠️ The Great Criticisms of Utilitarianism

1. **The Tyranny of the Majority:** What if 95% of a population gains immense pleasure from enslaving the other 5%? Pure utilitarianism risks trampling individual human rights.
2. **The Organ Transplant Dilemma:** A healthy traveler walks into a hospital for a checkup. Five dying patients in the ICU each need a heart, liver, two kidneys, and lungs. Should the doctor secretly kill the healthy traveler to save five lives?
   * *Mill’s Fix (Rule Utilitarianism):* We must follow general rules that maximize long-term social trust. A society where doctors kill patients for organs would collapse in terror; therefore, the rule *"Do not murder innocent patients"* produces higher long-term utility.

---

## 🛠️ Practical Takeaway

* In public policy, economics, and healthcare allocation (triage), utilitarian reasoning is the dominant framework: maximizing net benefits and saving the greatest number of lives with limited resources.
