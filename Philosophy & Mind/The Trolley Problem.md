# The Trolley Problem

> **The Big Idea:** Philosophy's most famous moral dilemma (introduced by Philippa Foot). It pits two opposing ethical worldviews against each other: Do the ends justify the means (**Consequentialism / Utilitarianism**), or are certain actions fundamentally forbidden regardless of the outcome (**Deontology**)?

---

## 🚋 Scenario 1: The Classic Switch

A runaway trolley is speeding down the tracks toward **5 workers** who cannot hear it coming. You are standing next to a lever:
* If you pull the lever, the trolley diverts onto a side track where only **1 worker** is standing.
* **The Question:** Do you pull the lever, killing 1 person to save 5?

```
Runaway Trolley ══════╦══════► [5 Workers] (Will die if you do nothing)
                      ║
                      ╚══════► [1 Worker]  (Will die if you pull the lever)
```

* **The Common Response:** Over **90% of people** say: *"Yes, pull the lever. Saving 5 lives at the cost of 1 is the better outcome."*

---

## 🌉 Scenario 2: The Footbridge (The Twist)

The same runaway trolley is speeding toward 5 workers. This time, there is no switch. You are standing on a footbridge above the tracks next to a very heavy stranger:
* If you shove the stranger off the bridge, his body will jam the wheels and stop the train, saving the 5 workers.
* **The Question:** Do you physically push the stranger to his death?

```
                     You & Heavy Stranger
                             [ 🧍‍♂️ 🧍 ]
                        ═══════════
Runaway Trolley ═════════════════════════► [5 Workers]
```

* **The Common Response:** Over **85% of people** suddenly say: *"No! Pushing him is murder; it is morally wrong!"*

---

## 🧠 Why Does Our Intuition Flip?

Mathematically, both scenarios are identical: **1 person dies so 5 live**. Why do we feel so differently?

1. **Utilitarianism (John Stuart Mill):** Only consequences matter. A true utilitarian says: *Pull the switch, and push the stranger.* $5 > 1$ in both cases.
2. **Kantian Deontology (Immanuel Kant):** Humans have dignity and cannot be used merely as tools. In Scenario 2, you are actively using a human being as a physical doorstop.
3. **Doctrine of Double Effect:** In Scenario 1, the death of the 1 worker is an unfortunate *foreseen side effect* of diverting the train. In Scenario 2, the death of the stranger is the *direct intentional means* used to stop the train.

---

## 🚗 Modern Real-World Application: Autonomous Vehicles

This is no longer just an abstract classroom debate:
* Engineers programming **self-driving cars** must decide: If brakes fail, should the car swerve into a wall (killing the passenger) to avoid hitting a crosswalk of five school children?
* Artificial Intelligence forces programmers to encode ethical philosophy into computer software.
