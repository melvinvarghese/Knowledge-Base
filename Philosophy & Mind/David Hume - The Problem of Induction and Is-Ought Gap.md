# David Hume - The Problem of Induction and Is-Ought Gap

## Overview
David Hume's radical empiricism challenged foundational pillars of knowledge, introducing the **Problem of Induction** and the ethical **Is-Ought Gap**.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
The Problem of Induction: We cannot logically justify the assumption that the future will resemble the past (e.g., the sun rising tomorrow). Induction cannot be proven deductively (not logically necessary) nor inductively (circular argument); it is a psychological habit of mind, not rational certainty.

---

## 2. Structural Analysis & Mathematical Modeling
Hume's Guillotine (The Is-Ought Gap): One cannot logically derive an ethical normative statement of what *ought* to be from purely descriptive factual premises of what *is*, decoupling objective science from subjective moral value.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Scientific Impact
Foundational challenge awakening Kant from 'dogmatic slumbers', modern scientific falsificationism, meta-ethics.

---

## Key Terms
- **Inductive Reasoning**: Deriving generalized universal principles from specific repeated empirical observations.
- **Hume's Fork**: Division of all knowledge into Relations of Ideas (analytic/a priori) and Matters of Fact (synthetic/a posteriori).
- **Is-Ought Gap**: Philosophical principle that prescriptive moral statements cannot be logically derived from descriptive facts.
