# David Chalmers - The Hard Problem of Consciousness and Philosophical Zombies

## Overview
David Chalmers formulated the famous distinction between the 'Easy Problems' of cognitive science and the **'Hard Problem of Consciousness'**, defended via the **Philosophical Zombie** thought experiment.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
Easy vs Hard Problems: Easy problems concern functional computational mechanisms (sensory processing, reportability, attention); the Hard Problem is: *Why should physical neural information processing feel like anything from the inside?* Why does subjective experience exist at all?

---

## 2. Structural Analysis & Mathematical Modeling
The Philosophical Zombie Argument: It is logically conceivable that an atom-for-atom physical duplicate of a human could exist who behaves identically in every scenario but possesses zero subjective conscious experience (a zombie), arguing that physicalism is incomplete.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Scientific Impact
Naturalistic dualism, panpsychism debates in analytic philosophy of mind, scientific neuroscience of qualia.

---

## Key Terms
- **The Hard Problem**: The question of why and how physical brain processes give rise to subjective conscious experience.
- **Philosophical Zombie (p-zombie)**: Hypothetical being physically identical to a normal human but lacking conscious inner experience.
- **Qualia**: The raw, subjective, phenomenal qualitative feels of conscious experiences (e.g., the redness of red).
