# Soren Kierkegaard - Knight of Faith and Leap into the Absurd

## Overview
Søren Kierkegaard, the father of Christian existentialism, examined the anxiety (**Angst**) of radical human freedom and the paradoxical **Leap of Faith** into the absurd.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
The Three Stages on Life's Way: 1. Aesthetic Stage (pursuit of sensory pleasure leading to despair); 2. Ethical Stage (acceptance of societal moral duty and universal law); 3. Religious Stage (transcending universal ethics in radical individual obedience to God).

---

## 2. Structural Analysis & Mathematical Modeling
Abraham and the Knight of Faith (*Fear and Trembling*): Abraham's willingness to sacrifice Isaac demonstrates the 'teleological suspension of the ethical', embracing a transcendent paradox that cannot be rationalized through human ethical systems.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Scientific Impact
Modern existential philosophy, phenomenology of anxiety, radical individual responsibility.

---

## Key Terms
- **Leap of Faith**: Kierkegaard's concept of choosing belief in the absurd without rational or empirical guarantee.
- **Angst**: Existential dread and anxiety born from the dizzying realization of radical human freedom.
- **Knight of Faith**: Individual who embraces the paradoxical demands of faith while remaining engaged in everyday life.
