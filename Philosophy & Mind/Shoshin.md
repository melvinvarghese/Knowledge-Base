# Shoshin (初心)

> **The Big Idea:** Keeping a "beginner's mind." Approaching problems, people, and topics with childlike curiosity, fresh eyes, and zero arrogant assumptions.

---

## 🧠 What Does It Mean?

*Sho* means "first" or "beginning," and *Shin* means "mind" or "heart." 

As Zen master Shunryu Suzuki famously wrote:
> *"In the beginner's mind there are many possibilities, but in the expert's mind there are few."*

When you consider yourself an "expert," your brain closes up. You think you already know how things work, so you stop asking questions, stop listening to other viewpoints, and miss innovative solutions. 

A person with *Shoshin* drops their ego and looks at familiar things as if seeing them for the very first time.

---

## 💡 The Pitfall of the "Expert Trap"

| The Know-It-All Mind | The Beginner's Mind (*Shoshin*) |
| :--- | :--- |
| Defends prior opinions aggressively | Asks *"What can I learn from this?"* |
| Listens to answer or correct others | Listens deeply to genuinely understand |
| Rigid; terrified of looking foolish | Curious; comfortable saying *"I don't know"* |
| Stuck in old, habitual solutions | Open to unexpected, creative breakthroughs |

---

## 🛠️ How to Practice Shoshin Daily

1. **Say *"I don't know, tell me more"***: Don't pretend to understand something just to save face. Admitting ignorance is the start of true mastery.
2. **Re-examine routine tasks:** Take a process you do on autopilot (cooking, coding, commuting) and ask: *"If I were doing this for the first time today, how would I do it?"*
3. **Listen to beginners:** Novices often ask the simplest questions—and the simplest questions often reveal the deepest flaws in existing systems.
