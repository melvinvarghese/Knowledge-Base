# Justified True Belief and the Gettier Problem

> **The Big Idea:** For over 2,000 years since Plato, philosophers defined **Knowledge** as "Justified True Belief" (JTB). In 1963, a three-page paper by Edmund Gettier blew this definition apart by proving you can have a belief that is both justified and true, yet is still just a lucky guess.

---

## 🏛️ The Classic JTB Definition of Knowledge

According to traditional epistemology, you **know** a proposition $P$ if and only if:
1. **Belief:** You actually believe $P$ is true.
2. **Truth:** $P$ is factually true in reality.
3. **Justification:** You have solid, credible evidence/reason to believe $P$.

```
         [ BELIEF ] ───┐
         [ TRUTH ]  ───┼──► EQUALS "KNOWLEDGE" (Pre-1963)
         [ EVIDENCE ] ─┘
```

If any one is missing, it isn't knowledge:
* If you guess a lottery number and win: it was *true* and you *believed* it, but you had no *justification* (pure luck).
* If you believe the earth is flat: you have *belief*, but it is not *true*.

---

## 💥 The Gettier Counter-Example (1963)

Edmund Gettier showed that justified true belief can still happen by pure coincidence:

### The Broken Clock Example:
* You walk into a room, look up at a clock on the wall, and see that it reads **12:00 PM**.
* You form the belief: *"It is 12:00 PM."*
* **Is it justified?** Yes, looking at a reliable wall clock is standard, reasonable justification.
* **Is it true?** By sheer coincidence, it happens to be **exactly 12:00 PM** at that second.
* **The Catch:** The clock died and broke 3 days ago at 12:00.
* **The Verdict:** Did you *know* it was 12:00? **No.** You were just remarkably lucky!

---

## 🧠 Why Does This Matter?

The Gettier Problem forced philosophers and AI researchers to rethink what it means to "know" something:
* Is knowledge JTB plus a "no false assumptions" clause?
* Or does knowledge require a direct causal connection between your brain and the reality?
* It teaches intellectual humility: even our best-justified beliefs can rest on hidden flukes.
