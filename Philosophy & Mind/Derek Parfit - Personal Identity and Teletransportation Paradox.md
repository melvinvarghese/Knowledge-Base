# Derek Parfit - Personal Identity and Teletransportation Paradox

## Overview
In *Reasons and Persons* (1984), Derek Parfit challenged traditional concepts of **Personal Identity**, arguing that identity is not what matters; rather, psychological continuity and connectedness ('Relation R') are what count.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
The Teletransportation Paradox: A sci-fi teletransporter scans your atomic structure, destroys your body on Earth, and replicates an atom-for-atom copy on Mars. What if the machine malfunctions and creates the Mars copy without destroying you on Earth? Which one is the 'real' you?

---

## 2. Structural Analysis & Mathematical Modeling
Reductionism of the Self (Bundle Theory): Echoing the Buddha and David Hume, Parfit proves there is no persistent, indivisible 'Cartesian Ego'; our survival consists purely of overlapping chains of memory, personality, and intention.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Scientific Impact
Bioethics of end-of-life care, moral responsibilities toward future generations, artificial mind-uploading ethics.

---

## Key Terms
- **Relation R**: Psychological connectedness and continuity through memory, character, and intentions.
- **Reductionism of Personal Identity**: View that personal identity consists purely in physical and psychological relations, not an indivisible soul.
- **Teletransportation Paradox**: Thought experiment demonstrating that physical duplication challenges simple notions of individual identity.
