# Simone de Beauvoir - The Ethics of Ambiguity and Existential Feminism

## Overview
Simone de Beauvoir grounded existential ethics in *The Ethics of Ambiguity* and pioneered modern feminist philosophy in *The Second Sex* (1949), coining the famous aphorism: 'One is not born, but rather becomes, a woman.'

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
The Social Construction of the Other: Men established the masculine self as the universal neutral Subject, defining woman as the inessential **Other**, trapped in passive immanence rather than active existential transcendence.

---

## 2. Structural Analysis & Mathematical Modeling
The Ethics of Ambiguity: Rejects moral nihilism; authentic freedom can only be willed by willing the reciprocal freedom of other human beings, demanding active political resistance against oppressive social hierarchies.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Scientific Impact
Foundation of second-wave feminist theory, gender studies, existentialist social ethics.

---

## Key Terms
- **The Other**: Subordinated social identity defined strictly in relation to a dominant cultural subject.
- **Immanence**: Stagnant, passive confinement to predefined biological and social roles.
- **Transcendence**: Active existential projection of oneself into the future through creative projects and freedom.
