# W.V.O. Quine - Two Dogmas of Empiricism and Holism

## Overview
W.V.O. Quine's seminal 1951 paper *Two Dogmas of Empiricism* dismantled logical positivism, rejecting the analytic-synthetic distinction and proposing **Epistemological Holism**.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
The First Dogma (Analytic vs Synthetic): Quine showed that defining 'analytic' statements (true by meaning: 'all bachelors are unmarried') relies on circular notions of synonymy, meaning no sharp boundary exists between analytic definitions and empirical facts.

---

## 2. Structural Analysis & Mathematical Modeling
The Web of Belief (Epistemological Holism): Our statements about the external world face the tribunal of sense experience not as isolated sentences, but as an interconnected **Web of Belief**. When conflicting empirical anomalies arise, we can choose which peripheral or central beliefs to revise.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Scientific Impact
Naturalized epistemology, holistic scientific modeling, fallibilist philosophy of science.

---

## Key Terms
- **Web of Belief**: Holistic model of knowledge where beliefs are interconnected and tested against experience as a totality.
- **Analytic-Synthetic Distinction**: Supposed distinction between truths of pure definition versus truths of empirical fact.
- **Epistemological Holism**: Doctrine that individual claims cannot be validated in isolation from the broader system of knowledge.
