# Saul Kripke - Naming and Necessity and Rigid Designators

## Overview
Saul Kripke's *Naming and Necessity* (1972) dismantled Russell and Frege's descriptivist theories of language, introducing **Rigid Designators** and **Necessary A Posteriori** truths.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
Rigid Designators & Causal Theory of Reference: A proper name (e.g., 'Aristotle') is not a bundle of descriptions ('the teacher of Alexander'); it is a **rigid designator** referring to the identical individual across all possible worlds where they exist, established through an initial baptismal naming event and passed along a historical causal chain.

---

## 2. Structural Analysis & Mathematical Modeling
The Necessary A Posteriori: Identity statements involving natural kinds (e.g., 'Water is $H_2O$') are discovered empirically (*a posteriori*), yet are metaphysically necessary truths holding in every possible world.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Scientific Impact
Modal logic semantics (Kripke frames), philosophy of language, metaphysics of identity and essence.

---

## Key Terms
- **Rigid Designator**: An expression referring to the exact same object in every possible world where that object exists.
- **Causal Theory of Reference**: Theory that names refer to objects via historical chains of communication traced to an initial naming.
- **Necessary A Posteriori**: A proposition discovered through empirical experience that nonetheless holds true in all possible worlds.
