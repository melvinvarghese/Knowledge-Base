# Frank Jackson - Mary's Room Knowledge Argument and Qualia

## Overview
Frank Jackson's **Knowledge Argument** (1982) uses the thought experiment of 'Mary the Color Scientist' to demonstrate that physicalism is false and that **qualia** are real non-physical facts.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
Mary's Room Thought Experiment: Brilliant neuroscientist Mary is raised in a black-and-white room, learning every physical fact about the optics, neuroscience, and physics of color vision. Jackson asks: When Mary steps outside and sees a ripe red apple for the first time, *does she learn anything new?*

---

## 2. Structural Analysis & Mathematical Modeling
The Anti-Physicalist Conclusion: If Mary learns what red looks like, she gained new knowledge. Because she already possessed all physical facts, the new knowledge must be non-physical (**qualia**), refuting complete physicalism.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Scientific Impact
The Ability Hypothesis counter-arguments, debates over phenomenal concepts and physicalism in philosophy of mind.

---

## Key Terms
- **Knowledge Argument**: Thought experiment arguing physicalism is false because complete physical knowledge omits qualia.
- **Epiphenomenalism**: Philosophical view that conscious mental states are caused by physical events but have no causal effects themselves.
- **Ability Hypothesis**: Physicalist counter-argument asserting Mary learns a new practical skill rather than a new proposition.
