# Sartre's Existentialism

> **The Big Idea:** You are not born with a pre-set purpose or destiny. You exist first, and then spend your whole life deciding who you are through your actions. Because you have total freedom to choose, you also carry 100% responsibility for your life.

---

## 💡 1. "Existence Precedes Essence"

This is Jean-Paul Sartre's most famous rule:

* **Inanimate objects:** Think of a kitchen knife. The maker first invents the idea (the *essence* = something sharp to slice vegetables), and then manufactures it (its *existence*). The knife cannot choose to be anything else.
* **Human beings:** We work in reverse. We are born into the world with no instruction manual, no pre-programmed fate, and no fixed "human nature." Our **existence** happens first. Our **essence** (who we become, our values, our character) is built day by day through what we actually do.

> You are not a "lazy person" or a "successful person" by birth. You are simply the sum of your daily choices.

---

## ⛓️ 2. "Condemned to Be Free"

Sartre famously argued that humans are *"condemned to be free."* 

Why "condemned"? Because you did not choose to exist, but now that you are here, you are forced to make choices every single day. Even deciding *not* to choose is still a choice.

Because you are completely free:
* **No excuses:** You cannot blame your parents, your past, your zodiac sign, or society for your personal character.
* **Radical responsibility:** Whatever you do, you chose it. That thought is both thrilling and terrifying.

---

## 🎭 3. "Bad Faith" (*Mauvaise Foi*)

**Bad Faith** is when you lie to yourself and pretend you have no choice.

Sartre used the example of a café waiter who moves a bit too mechanically, balances his tray with exaggerated precision, and treats customers with an overly scripted smile:
* The waiter is playing the role of "a waiter" like an actor in a play.
* He tells himself: *"I have to do this; I have no choice; I am just a waiter."*

In reality, he is a free human being choosing to wake up, put on the apron, and serve coffee every morning. When we tell ourselves *"I had no choice"* or *"That's just the way I am,"* we are living in Bad Faith—treating ourselves like lifeless objects rather than free agents.

---

## ⚡ 4. Anxiety and Anguish (*L'angoisse*)

When you truly realize that you are free and that no one else is going to write your life story for you, you experience existential **anguish**.

Sartre compared this to standing near the edge of a tall cliff:
* You aren't just scared of slipping.
* You are scared because you realize that **nothing is stopping you** from jumping off or stepping back except your own conscious will.

Anxiety is simply the feeling of recognizing your own absolute freedom.

---

## 🛠️ How to Apply This in Daily Life

1. **Stop waiting for a "sign" or pre-set purpose:** Meaning isn't hiding under a rock waiting to be discovered. Meaning is created by committing to things that matter to you.
2. **Drop the excuses:** When you feel stuck in a bad habit, job, or relationship, stop saying *"I can't."* Be honest: *"I am currently choosing to stay because of X fear."* Owning the choice gives you the power to change it.
3. **Define yourself by deeds, not intentions:** Thinking about writing a book doesn't make you a writer; writing pages does. You are what you do.
