# Learned Helplessness

> **The Big Idea:** Martin Seligman’s discovery that when humans or animals endure repeated painful situations that they cannot control, they eventually stop trying to escape—even after real escape opportunities become available.

---

## 🐕 The Original Experiment

In the late 1960s, Martin Seligman and Steven Maier tested dogs in a shuttle box:
* Dogs were subjected to mild shocks that they could not turn off.
* Later, the dogs were put into a box where they could easily avoid the shock simply by jumping over a low partition.
* Instead of jumping, the dogs simply laid down, whimpered, and endured the shock. They had learned: *"Nothing I do makes a difference."*

## 💡 The Antidote: Learned Optimism

Helplessness is not an innate genetic curse; it is **learned behavior**. Because it is learned, it can be **unlearned**:

1. **Spot the 3 P's:** When something goes wrong, catch yourself saying *"I always mess up"* (Permanent) or *"I'm bad at everything"* (Pervasive).
2. **Find the micro-control:** Identify one tiny thing you can actually change right now.
3. **Take one small action:** Taking action shatters the illusion of powerlessness.
