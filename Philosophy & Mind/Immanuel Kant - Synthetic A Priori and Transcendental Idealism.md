# Immanuel Kant - Synthetic A Priori and Transcendental Idealism

## Overview
Immanuel Kant's *Critique of Pure Reason* (1781) executed a 'Copernican Revolution' in philosophy, uniting rationalism and empiricism through **Transcendental Idealism** and the **Synthetic A Priori**.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
The Copernican Revolution: Objects do not dictate human cognition; rather, human cognitive faculties structure experienced reality. Space and time are not external physical containers, but subjective a priori forms of human sensory intuition.

---

## 2. Structural Analysis & Mathematical Modeling
Phenomena vs Noumena: We can only ever know **Phenomena** (appearances structured by human conceptual categories like causality); the **Noumenon** (the 'Thing-in-Itself', *Ding an sich*) remains fundamentally unknowable to sensory beings.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Scientific Impact
Epistemological foundation of modern philosophy, cognitive science frameworks, deontological moral philosophy.

---

## Key Terms
- **Synthetic A Priori**: Knowledge that is informative and non-tautological (synthetic) yet known prior to experience (a priori).
- **Phenomenon**: An object of perception structured by human cognitive faculties, contrasted with the noumenon.
- **Ding an sich**: The 'thing-in-itself' representing reality as it exists independently of human observation.
