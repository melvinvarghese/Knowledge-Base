# Michel Foucault - Panopticism, Biopolitics, and Power-Knowledge

## Overview
Michel Foucault analyzed the historical evolution of institutional power, introducing **Power-Knowledge**, the surveillance metaphor of **Panopticism**, and population regulation (**Biopolitics**).

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
The Panopticon & Internalized Surveillance: Jeremy Bentham's prison design—a central guard tower observing circular cells—forces prisoners into perpetual self-policing; modern institutions (schools, hospitals, prisons) discipline human bodies through continuous institutional surveillance.

---

## 2. Structural Analysis & Mathematical Modeling
Biopolitics & Governmentality: Historical shift from classical sovereign power ('to let live and make die') to modern regulatory biopower ('to make live and let die'), managing populations through public health metrics, birth rates, and demographics.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Scientific Impact
Criminology and surveillance society critiques, critical medical sociology, psychiatric power analysis.

---

## Key Terms
- **Panopticism**: Form of social control based on the permanent visibility and internalized surveillance of subjects.
- **Biopower**: Techniques used by modern states to manage, regulate, and control human populations biological life.
- **Episteme**: Unconscious historical framework of knowledge defining what is thinkable within a specific era.
