# Allport's Trait Theory

> **The Big Idea:** Gordon Allport was the pioneer of trait psychology. He organized human personality traits into three tiers: **Cardinal Traits** (rare and all-consuming), **Central Traits** (your core everyday traits), and **Secondary Traits** (situational quirks).

---

## 🏛️ The 3 Tiers of Traits

```
            ┌────────────────────────────────────────┐
            │        CARDINAL TRAIT (Rare)           │
            │ Single obsession defining an identity  │
            └───────────────────┬────────────────────┘
                                │
            ┌───────────────────┴────────────────────┐
            │       CENTRAL TRAITS (5 to 10)         │
            │ Core building blocks of personality    │
            └───────────────────┬────────────────────┘
                                │
            ┌───────────────────┴────────────────────┐
            │      SECONDARY TRAITS (Situational)    │
            │ Preferences, moods, specific reactions │
            └────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 1. Cardinal Traits
* A trait so overpowering that a person's entire life and reputation revolve around it.
* Often, historical figures become synonymous with their cardinal trait:
  * *Machiavellian* (ruthless pursuit of power)
  * *Narcissistic* (self-obsession)
  * *Christ-like* (supreme compassion and sacrifice)
* Most normal people do **not** have a cardinal trait.

### 2. Central Traits
* The 5 to 10 basic building blocks you would use to write a recommendation letter or describe your best friend to a stranger.
* Examples: Honest, anxious, humorous, ambitious, shy, reliable, generous.

### 3. Secondary Traits
* Preferences and behaviors that only appear under very specific conditions.
* Examples: Getting irritated only when standing in long lines, feeling stage fright only when speaking to executives, loving spicy food.

---

## 🛠️ Practical Takeaway

* When describing yourself or evaluating employees, ignore situational quirks (**Secondary**) and focus on the 5 core habits (**Central**).
* Beware of letting a single pursuit or flaw become your **Cardinal Trait**—balance creates resilience.
