# The Social Contract - Hobbes, Locke, and Rousseau

> **The Big Idea:** Why do governments have legitimate authority over free individuals? The Social Contract theory argues that we voluntarily surrender some of our natural freedoms to the state in exchange for safety, property rights, and social order.

## 🔍 Detailed Breakdown of Each Thinker

### 1. Thomas Hobbes (*Leviathan*, 1651)
* **View of Humanity:** Naturally selfish, violent, and fearful. Without a strong ruler, life is a constant war of *"all against all."*
* **The Social Contract:** People surrender almost all their freedom to an absolute monarch (**The Leviathan**) in exchange for one thing: **physical security**.
* **Key Idea:** Any government—even a harsh dictatorship—is better than the chaos of anarchy.

### 2. John Locke (*Second Treatise of Government*, 1689)
* **View of Humanity:** Rational and moral. We possess natural inalienable rights to **Life, Liberty, and Property**.
* **The Social Contract:** We form a government solely to act as an impartial referee to protect our property and rights.
* **The Right to Revolution:** If a government fails to protect your natural rights or becomes tyrannical, **citizens have a moral right and duty to overthrow it**. (This formed the intellectual bedrock of the American Revolution).

### 3. Jean-Jacques Rousseau (*The Social Contract*, 1762)
* **View of Humanity:** Humans are born pure, cooperative, and compassionate. Civilization and the invention of private property introduced greed, inequality, and social chains.
* **The Social Contract:** True democracy where citizens come together to obey the **General Will** (what is best for the common good of all, not just selfish individual interests).

---

## 🛠️ Practical Takeaway

* Every modern political debate—from taxes and gun control to privacy vs. national security—is fundamentally an argument between Hobbes (trading freedom for security) and Locke (protecting individual freedom from government overreach).
