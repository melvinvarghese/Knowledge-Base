# Friedrich Nietzsche - Will to Power and Eternal Recurrence

## Overview
Friedrich Nietzsche critiqued Western morality, announced the 'Death of God', and articulated the psychological concept of the **Will to Power**, the **Übermensch**, and the thought experiment of **Eternal Recurrence**.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
The Will to Power & Master-Slave Morality: Reality is an ongoing clash of competing drives to express strength, master obstacles, and self-overcome. Judeo-Christian 'slave morality' inverted classical aristocratic virtues (strength, beauty) into vices, celebrating meekness.

---

## 2. Structural Analysis & Mathematical Modeling
The Greatest Weight (Eternal Recurrence): Nietzsche proposes the psychological test: If a demon told you this exact life would repeat infinitely with every pain and joy, would you curse the demon or embrace it with total affirmation (**Amor Fati**)?

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Scientific Impact
Paved the way for 20th-century existentialism, post-modern philosophy, psychoanalysis.

---

## Key Terms
- **Will to Power**: Fundamental psychological drive toward expansion, mastery, and continuous self-overcoming.
- **Übermensch**: The ideal higher human who creates personal values independently following the death of shared dogmas.
- **Eternal Recurrence**: Philosophical concept imagining that identical events repeat cyclically throughout infinite time.
