# Growth Mindset vs Fixed Mindset

> **The Big Idea:** Stanford psychologist Carol Dweck’s framework. If you believe intelligence and talent are carved in stone (**Fixed Mindset**), you avoid challenges to protect your ego. If you believe abilities can be developed through effort (**Growth Mindset**), failure becomes fuel.

---

## ⚖️ The Two Mindsets Contrasted

| Situation | Fixed Mindset | Growth Mindset |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Core Belief** | *"Intelligence and talent are innate gifts."* | *"Abilities can be developed with effort and strategy."* |
| **Challenges** | Avoids them (fear of looking stupid). | Embraces them (opportunity to stretch brain). |
| **Obstacles** | Gives up easily when things get hard. | Persists; treats roadblocks as puzzles. |
| **Effort** | Seen as proof you lack natural talent. | Seen as the necessary path to mastery. |
| **Criticism** | Takes feedback as a personal attack. | Learns from critiques and adjusts method. |
| **Others' Success** | Feels threatened, jealous, or envious. | Finds inspiration and lessons to apply. |

---

## 🧠 The Power of the Word "YET"

Carol Dweck tells the story of a high school that gave students who didn't pass a course the grade **"Not Yet"** instead of an "F":
* Getting an "F" tells your brain: *"You failed; you are done."*
* Getting "Not Yet" tells your brain: *"You are on a learning curve; keep going."*

```
"I don't understand how coding works." 
                       ▼
"I don't understand how coding works... YET."
```

---

## 🛠️ How to Build a Growth Mindset

1. **Praise the process, not raw talent:**
   * Stop praising kids (or yourself) with: *"You're so smart!"* (builds fixed mindset fear).
   * Start praising: *"You worked hard on this puzzle and found a creative way through!"*
2. **Reframe failure as data:** An error is not a verdict on your worth; it is simply neutral information about what technique didn't work.
