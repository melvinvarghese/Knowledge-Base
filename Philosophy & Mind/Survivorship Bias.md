# Survivorship Bias

> **The Big Idea:** Survivorship bias is a common thinking mistake where we only look at the things or people that succeeded (or survived), while completely ignoring the ones that failed. Because of this, we end up drawing the wrong conclusions.

---

## 🛩️ The World War II Plane Story

During World War II, the military wanted to add heavy armor to their bomber planes to protect them from enemy fire. 

However, armor is heavy. If you put too much armor on a plane, it becomes too slow and uses too much fuel. They had to choose only the most critical spots to reinforce.

### The Mistake
Researchers inspected the bombers returning from battle and mapped out all the bullet holes:
- Lots of holes in the **wings**
- Lots of holes in the **fuselage (main body)**
- Lots of holes in the **tail**

The military's initial reaction was obvious:  
*"Put the extra armor on the wings, body, and tail because that's where the bullets are hitting!"*

### The Breakthrough (Abraham Wald)
A mathematician named **Abraham Wald** stopped them and said they had it completely backwards.

He pointed out a simple fact: **They were only looking at the planes that made it back.**

- The planes with bullet holes in the wings survived and returned home safely. That meant planes can take hits in the wings and still fly.
- Where were there **no** bullet holes on returning planes? In the **engines** and the **cockpit**.
- Why were there no bullet holes there? Because when a plane was hit in the engine or cockpit, **it crashed and never came back.**

The correct answer was to put armor where there were **no bullet holes**.

---

## 💡 Why This Matters in Daily Life

We make this exact mistake all the time:

1. **Success Stories:** We hear about successful college dropouts like Steve Jobs or Mark Zuckerberg and think, *"Dropping out makes you successful!"* We forget the millions of people who dropped out and struggled.
2. **Business Advice:** Books interview successful companies to find out what they do, ignoring all the bankrupt companies that did the exact same things.
3. **Old Products ("They don't make them like they used to"):** We think 50-year-old cars or appliances were built better because a few are still working today. We ignore the 99% that broke and ended up in a junkyard decades ago.

---

## 🔑 The Golden Rule
Whenever you make a decision, always ask:

> **"What am I not seeing? Where are the missing failures?"**
