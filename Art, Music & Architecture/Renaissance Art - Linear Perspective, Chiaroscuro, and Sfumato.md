# Renaissance Art - Linear Perspective, Chiaroscuro, and Sfumato

## Overview
Renaissance Art (14th–16th centuries) revolutionized visual representation through mathematical spatial perspective, classical anatomical realism, and optics.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Mathematical Linear Perspective: Filippo Brunelleschi formulated the mathematical rules of one-point linear perspective (c. 1415), codified by Leon Battista Alberti (*De pictura*). Parallel lines (orthogonals) converge at a single vanishing point on the horizon line, creating a scientifically accurate illusion of three-dimensional depth on a flat two-dimensional plane.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Chiaroscuro & Sfumato: Artists mastered light and shadow (*chiaroscuro*) to model volumetric three-dimensional forms. Leonardo da Vinci pioneered *sfumato* ('evaporated like smoke')—the subtle blending of colors and tones without harsh outlines, epitomized in the enigmatic smile of the *Mona Lisa*.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
High Renaissance masters: Leonardo da Vinci, Michelangelo (Sistine Chapel ceiling), Raphael (*The School of Athens*).

## Key Terminology
- **Linear Perspective**: Mathematical system for creating the illusion of spatial depth on a two-dimensional surface using vanishing points.
- **Chiaroscuro**: Treatment of light and shade in drawing and painting to give an illusion of three-dimensional volume.
- **Sfumato**: Painting technique of applying multiple translucent glazes to create soft, imperceptible transitions between colors and tones.
