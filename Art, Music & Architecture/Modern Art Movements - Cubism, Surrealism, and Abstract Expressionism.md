# Modern Art Movements - Cubism, Surrealism, and Abstract Expressionism

## Overview
20th-century Modern Art shattered representational fidelity, reflecting the psychological, technological, and existential upheavals of global industrialization and world wars.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Cubism (Picasso and Braque, 1907–1914): Discarded five centuries of Renaissance linear perspective. Pablo Picasso and Georges Braque fragmented subjects into geometric facets, depicting multiple viewpoints simultaneously on a single flat plane (*Les Demoiselles d'Avignon*).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Surrealism & Abstract Expressionism: Surrealism (Salvador Dalí, René Magritte), inspired by Freudian psychoanalysis, sought to liberate the unconscious mind through dream imagery and psychic automatism. Following World War II, Abstract Expressionism (Jackson Pollock's spontaneous drip action paintings) shifted the epicenter of the art world from Paris to New York.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Reconceptualization of art from mimetic illustration to conceptual inquiry, autonomy of color, form, and material.

## Key Terminology
- **Cubism**: Early 20th-century avant-garde art movement that revolutionized European painting and sculpture by depicting objects from multiple viewpoints simultaneously.
- **Surrealism**: Artistic and literary movement that sought to release the creative potential of the unconscious mind.
- **Action Painting**: Style of painting in which paint is spontaneously dribbled, splashed, or smeared onto the canvas rather than carefully applied.
