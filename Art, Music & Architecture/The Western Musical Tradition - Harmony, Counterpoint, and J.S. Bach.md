# The Western Musical Tradition - Harmony, Counterpoint, and J.S. Bach

## Overview
The Western polyphonic musical tradition developed mathematical harmonic counterpoint and equal temperament, reaching its intellectual summit in Johann Sebastian Bach.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Polyphony & Counterpoint: Polyphony interweaves multiple independent melodic lines simultaneously. Counterpoint ('point against point') establishes strict rules for combining melodic voices harmoniously while maintaining their rhythmic and melodic independence. Bach perfected the Fugue—a contrapuntal composition based on a main thematic subject introduced successively across all voices.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Equal Temperament Tuning: In *The Well-Tempered Clavier* (1722, 1744), Bach championed equal temperament tuning—dividing an octave into twelve logarithmically equal semitone frequency ratios ($f_{n} = f_0 \cdot 2^{n/12}$), allowing musicians to play in all 24 major and minor keys without dissonant acoustic acoustic wolf intervals.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Foundational harmonic syntax for all Western classical, jazz, and modern popular musical composition.

## Key Terminology
- **Counterpoint**: Relationship between two or more musical voices that are harmonically interdependent yet independent in rhythm and contour.
- **Fugue**: Contrapuntal musical composition based on a short melody or phrase that is introduced by one part and successively taken up by others.
- **Equal Temperament**: Musical tuning system in which the octave is divided into 12 semitones of equal frequency ratios.
