# Impressionism and Post-Impressionism - Monet, Cézanne, and Van Gogh

## Overview
Impressionism and Post-Impressionism shattered classical academic art conventions in late 19th-century France, capturing subjective light perceptions and structural forms.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Impressionism (*En Plein Air*): Claude Monet, Pierre-Auguste Renoir, and Edgar Degas abandoned dark academic studios to paint outdoors (*en plein air*), using portable metal paint tubes. They employed rapid, broken brushstrokes of unmixed complementary colors to capture fleeting optical impressions of natural sunlight and atmospheric conditions (*Monet's Impression, Sunrise*, 1872).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Post-Impressionist Divergence: Paul Cézanne deconstructed landscapes into geometric underlying volumes (cylinders, spheres, cones), laying the intellectual groundwork for Cubism. Vincent van Gogh deployed thick, expressive impasto brushwork and emotional subjective color (*The Starry Night*), fathering Expressionism.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Paved the way for modern 20th-century avant-garde art, legitimizing artistic subjectivity over photographic mimesis.

## Key Terminology
- **En Plein Air**: French expression meaning 'in the open air', referring to the act of painting outdoor scenes on location.
- **Impasto**: Technique where paint is laid on canvas thickly in visible brushstrokes or palette knife textures.
- **Broken Color**: Technique of applying separate dabs of pure color side by side so the viewer's eye optically mixes them.
