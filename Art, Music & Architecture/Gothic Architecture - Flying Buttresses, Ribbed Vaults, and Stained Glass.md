# Gothic Architecture - Flying Buttresses, Ribbed Vaults, and Stained Glass

## Overview
Gothic Architecture (12th–16th centuries) transformed medieval European cathedral construction, dematerializing heavy stone walls to let in divine light (*Lux Nova*).

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Engineering Triad: Originated at the Basilica of Saint-Denis by Abbot Suger (c. 1144). Replaced heavy Romanesque semicircular arches with three engineering innovations: 1. The Pointed Arch (reduces lateral outward thrust); 2. The Ribbed Vault (channels ceiling weight along structural skeletal stone ribs); 3. The Flying Buttress (transfers lateral outward roof thrust outside the building onto exterior masonry piers).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Stained Glass & Verticality: By freeing walls from bearing roof weight, architects installed vast multi-story stained-glass rose windows, flooding soaring cathedral naves with ethereal colored light intended to transport worshippers toward the divine (Chartres, Notre-Dame de Paris, Sainte-Chapelle).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Engineering triumph of medieval master masons, structural precursor to modern skeletal steel-frame skyscrapers.

## Key Terminology
- **Flying Buttress**: Exterior inclined masonry arch transferring lateral thrust from a high wall or vault to an external buttress pier.
- **Ribbed Vault**: Vault supported by a framework of intersecting arched stone ribs that direct ceiling weight to supporting columns.
- **Rose Window**: Large, circular stained-glass window with radiating tracery characteristic of Gothic cathedrals.
