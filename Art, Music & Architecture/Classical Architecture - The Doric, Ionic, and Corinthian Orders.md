# Classical Architecture - The Doric, Ionic, and Corinthian Orders

## Overview
Classical Architecture originated in ancient Greece and Rome, codified into the five classical orders governed by strict geometric proportions and column capitals.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Three Greek Orders: 1. Doric: Sturdy, fluted columns resting directly on the stylobate without a base, crowned by a simple convex capital (e.g., the Parthenon in Athens); 2. Ionic: Slender, elegant fluted columns resting on a base, characterized by spiraling volute scrolls on the capital (e.g., the Erechtheion); 3. Corinthian: The most ornate order, featuring slender fluted shafts crowned by an inverted bell capital draped in sculpted acanthus leaves.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Roman Innovations: The Romans adopted the Greek orders, adding the Tuscan (simplified Doric) and Composite (fusing Ionic volutes and Corinthian acanthus) orders. Vitruvius established the threefold architectural triad: *Firmitas* (structural strength), *Utilitas* (functional utility), and *Venustas* (aesthetic beauty).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Foundation of Renaissance, Neoclassical, and Beaux-Arts architecture across Western capitols, banks, and monuments.

## Key Terminology
- **Classical Order**: Architectural system comprising an assemblage of parts (pedestal, column, entablature) proportioned to a modular standard.
- **Parthenon**: Former temple on the Athenian Acropolis dedicated to Athena, serving as the supreme exemplar of Doric architecture.
- **Vitruvian Triad**: Three fundamental tenets of successful architecture formulated by Vitruvius: firmness, commodity, and delight.
