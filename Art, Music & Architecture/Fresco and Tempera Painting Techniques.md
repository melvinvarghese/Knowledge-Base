# Fresco and Tempera Painting Techniques

## Overview
Before oil paint dominated European easel work in the 16th century, egg tempera and buon fresco were the premier painting media of antiquity, the Middle Ages, and the Renaissance. Both demanded extraordinary planning, speed, and technical discipline, producing luminous, matte surfaces capable of surviving for centuries.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Egg tempera mixes finely ground mineral or earth pigments with egg yolk (and a drop of water or vinegar) as a fast-drying binder. Applied in thin, semi-opaque brushstrokes onto wooden panels primed with smooth white gesso (chalk and animal glue), tempera dries almost instantaneously, preventing smooth wet-on-wet blending. Artists like Cimabue, Giotto, Fra Angelico, and Sandro Botticelli built modeled forms through delicate cross-hatching (tratteggio) and luminous layered glazes, often framed against burnished gold leaf (The Birth of Venus, c. 1485). Because egg yolk yellows far less than linseed oil, medieval and early Renaissance tempera panels retain remarkable chromatic clarity today.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Buon fresco ('true fresco') is the monumental mural technique of applying water-dispersed pigments directly onto freshly laid, wet lime plaster (intonaco). As the plaster dries and absorbs carbon dioxide from the air, a chemical reaction (carbonation) locks the pigment particles permanently inside a crystalline calcium carbonate surface, making the painting literally part of the wall. Because plaster remains workable for only 8 to 12 hours, artists worked in discrete daily patches called giornate ('day's work'), transferring full-scale preparatory drawings (cartoons) onto the damp wall by pouncing charcoal dust through pricked holes or incising outlines. Details that could not be finished before drying — especially expensive blue pigments like azurite or ultramarine, which reacted poorly with alkaline lime — were added afterwards on dry plaster (fresco a secco), though secco layers proved far more fragile over time.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
From Minoan Knossos and Roman Pompeii to Giotto's Scrovegni Chapel, Michelangelo's Sistine Chapel ceiling, and Raphael's Vatican Stanze, fresco carried the highest narrative ambitions of Western wall painting. In the 20th century, Mexican Muralists — Diego Rivera, José Clemente Orozco, and David Alfaro Siqueiros — revived buon fresco on a monumental public scale to narrate revolutionary history and indigenous heritage in civic buildings across Mexico and the United States.

## Key Terminology
- **Buon fresco**: True fresco technique painting water-based pigments into wet lime plaster so color chemically bonds with the wall.
- **Fresco a secco**: Painting on dry plaster using a binder; allows corrections and alkaline-sensitive pigments but flakes more easily.
- **Giornata**: Section of fresh plaster laid for a single day's fresco painting, often visible at close inspection as faint seam lines.
- **Egg tempera**: Fast-drying painting medium using egg yolk as a binder, applied in fine hatched strokes over gessoed wood panels.
- **Gesso**: Smooth white preparatory ground made of chalk or gypsum mixed with animal-skin glue.
- **Cartoon (art)**: Full-scale preparatory drawing transferred onto a fresco wall or tapestry loom via pouncing or tracing.
