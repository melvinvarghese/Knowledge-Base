# Abstract Expressionism and Action Painting

## Overview
Abstract Expressionism emerged in New York in the late 1940s as the first American avant-garde movement to achieve global dominance. Blending Surrealist automatism with monumental scale, artists split broadly between turbulent gestural 'Action Painting' (Jackson Pollock, Willem de Kooning) and meditative 'Color Field' abstraction (Mark Rothko, Barnett Newman).

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Catapulted by the arrival of European Surrealist émigrés during World War II and the trauma of the Holocaust and atomic age, New York artists rejected provincial American realism and geometric European Cubism. Jackson Pollock laid massive unprimed canvases on the floor and dripped industrial house paint with sticks and hardened brushes, turning painting into a rhythmic physical arena ('Action Painting,' coined by critic Harold Rosenberg). Willem de Kooning slashed vigorous figurative and abstract passages across his Woman series, while Franz Kline, Lee Krasner, and Joan Mitchell translated emotional urgency into sweeping black-and-white or chromatic gestures.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Parallel to gestural painting, Color Field artists sought quiet, transcendent encounters with the Sublime. Mark Rothko floated soft-edged, glowing rectangles of thin translucent paint across tall canvases designed to envelop the viewer at close range. Barnett Newman divided vast monochrome fields with vertical bands ('zips') to evoke existential presence, while Helen Frankenthaler invented the soak-stain technique (Mountains and Sea, 1952), pouring thinned acrylic directly onto raw canvas so pigment fused seamlessly with the weave. Critic Clement Greenberg championed the movement as the logical culmination of modernist painting's pursuit of pure flatness.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Abstract Expressionism shifted the center of the international art world from Paris to New York — a dominance later amplified by Cold War cultural diplomacy, as touring exhibitions presented American painterly freedom as a visual foil to Soviet Socialist Realism. By the early 1960s, younger Pop and Minimalist artists rebelled against its macho heroism and emotional solemnity, yet its monumental scale and direct material honesty remain foundational to contemporary painting.

## Key Terminology
- **Action painting**: Harold Rosenberg's term for gestural Abstract Expressionism where the canvas acts as an arena of physical movement.
- **Color Field painting**: Branch of Abstract Expressionism (Rothko, Newman) using large, flat expanses of luminous color to evoke contemplation.
- **Soak-stain technique**: Helen Frankenthaler's method of pouring diluted paint onto unprimed canvas so color dyes the fabric directly.
- **All-over composition**: Non-hierarchical distribution of marks across the entire canvas edge-to-edge without a central focal point.
- **Zip**: Barnett Newman's signature vertical band dividing a Color Field canvas.
- **New York School**: Informal collective of mid-century Abstract Expressionist painters, poets, and composers centered in downtown Manhattan.
