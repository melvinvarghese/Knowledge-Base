# Classical Music - The Sonata Form, Mozart, and Beethoven

## Overview
The Classical Era of Western music (c. 1750–1820), centered in Vienna, prioritized melodic clarity, structural symmetry, and the dynamic dramatic tension of the Sonata Form.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Sonata-Allegro Form: The architectural engine of symphonies, string quartets, and concertos, organized into three dynamic movements: 1. Exposition (introduces primary theme in home tonic key and contrasting secondary theme in dominant key); 2. Development (modulates themes through unstable, dramatic harmonic keys); 3. Recapitulation (resolves both themes harmoniously back in the home tonic key).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
From Mozart to Beethoven: Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart brought effortless melodic grace and operatic emotional psychology to the form (*Symphony No. 40*, *Don Giovanni*). Ludwig van Beethoven expanded the orchestra, infused symphonies with heroic struggle, and shattered classical restraint in his revolutionary *Eroica* (Symphony No. 3) and Ninth Symphony, bridging Classical architecture with Romantic expression.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Established the multi-movement symphony orchestra, string quartet, and piano concerto as primary Western musical forms.

## Key Terminology
- **Sonata Form**: Three-part musical structure comprising exposition, development, and recapitulation that governs classical symphonic movements.
- **Tonic and Dominant**: The foundational tonal harmonic relationship between the first scale degree (tonic, home key) and the fifth scale degree (dominant, tension).
- **Symphony**: Elaborate multi-movement instrumental composition for full orchestra, typically in four movements.
