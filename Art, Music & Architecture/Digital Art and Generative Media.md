# Digital Art and Generative Media

## Overview
Digital art encompasses artistic practices that use computational technology as an essential medium of creation, distribution, or exhibition. From 1960s mainframe plotter drawings and algorithmic systems to interactive installations, net art, and generative AI, digital art challenges traditional notions of authorship, originality, and physical permanence.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Early pioneers in the 1960s — Frieder Nake, Vera Molnár, Manfred Mohr, and Harold Cohen (creator of the AARON painting program) — wrote algorithms in Fortran and Lisp to drive pen plotters, treating mathematical rules and controlled randomness as compositional collaborators. In the 1980s and 1990s, personal computers, desktop graphics software, and the World Wide Web catalyzed Net.art (JODI, Olia Lialina), which treated browsers, hyperlinks, and HTML errors as sculptural material. Meanwhile, interactive software artists like Casey Reas and Ben Fry created Processing (2001), an open-source programming language that democratized code-based visual art and data visualization.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
In the 21st century, generative art expanded into real-time audiovisual environments, projection mapping, and immersive rooms pioneered by collectives like teamLab and artists such as Refik Anadol, who transforms massive datasets into fluid architectural sculptures. Blockchain technology briefly reshaped digital markets through NFTs (non-fungible tokens) by creating verifiable provenance for infinitely reproducible files. Most recently, diffusion models and generative adversarial networks (GANs) have enabled artists to synthesize imagery from latent spaces and natural-language prompts, sparking intense debates over copyright, training data ethics, and human agency.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Major institutions — from Ars Electronica in Linz and ZKM in Karlsruhe to MoMA and the Whitney Museum — now maintain dedicated media conservation departments to preserve software-based works against hardware obsolescence and 'bit rot.' Digital art has also dissolved boundaries between fine art, video game design, cinema VFX, and architectural projection.

## Key Terminology
- **Generative art**: Art created wholly or partly through an autonomous system, algorithm, or rule set defined by the artist.
- **Plotter drawing**: Early computer art output using a robotic pen plotter driven by mathematical coordinates.
- **Net.art**: 1990s artistic movement using the Internet, browsers, and network protocols as both medium and exhibition space.
- **Processing**: Open-source programming environment created by Casey Reas and Ben Fry in 2001 for visual artists and designers.
- **Bit rot / Emulation**: Media conservation challenge where obsolete hardware or software prevents digital artworks from running without virtual emulation.
- **Latent space**: Multidimensional mathematical representation learned by generative AI models from which novel images are sampled.
