# Roman Engineering - Arches, Domes, Vaults, and Roman Concrete

## Overview
Roman Architectural Engineering revolutionized monumental construction by pioneering the semicircular arch, barrel vault, and waterproof volcanic concrete.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Opus Caementicium (Roman Concrete): Made from a reactive mixture of volcanic ash (pozzolana from Pozzuoli), lime, and volcanic tuff rubble aggregate. The reaction formed crystalline calcium-silicate-hydrate structures that cured underwater and displayed self-healing properties via lime clasts over millennia.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Arch, The Barrel Vault, & The Pantheon: The true keystone arch redirects vertical downward gravitational weight into horizontal compressive lateral forces. Extending the arch creates the barrel vault and groin vault. Built under Hadrian (c. 126 CE), the Pantheon features the world's largest unreinforced concrete dome (43.3-meter diameter), tapering in thickness with an open central oculus.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Aqueducts (Pont du Gard), the Colosseum, monumental public baths (*thermae*), civil engineering infrastructure.

## Key Terminology
- **Pozzolana**: Volcanic ash used by ancient Romans to produce hydraulic self-healing waterproof concrete.
- **Keystone**: Wedge-shaped stone at the apex of a masonry arch that locks all other stones into compressive position.
- **Oculus**: Circular opening at the apex of a dome (as in the Pantheon) admitting natural light and ventilation.
