# Electronic Music and Synthesis

## Overview
Electronic music — music produced or transformed by electronic means — encompasses a vast range of practices from early 20th-century experiments with oscillators and tape through synthesizer-based pop and rock to contemporary dance music (techno, house, trance) and digital production. It represents the most significant transformation of musical timbre and production in the 20th century, fundamentally changing how music is made, distributed, and consumed.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Electronic music's origins lie in early 20th-century experiments: Thaddeus Cahill's Telharmonium (1897–1906), a 200-ton keyboard instrument generating pitches electromagnetically, was the first electric instrument; Leon Theremin's theremin (1920) introduced the haunting gesture-controlled oscillator; Lev Theremin and others developed rhythm machines. The tape recorder (Magnetophon, 1930s Germany) made it possible to record, splice, reverse, and layer sounds; Pierre Schaeffer in Paris developed musique concrète (1948) — music built from recorded real-world sounds (trains, spinning tops, boats) transformed by tape manipulation. Karlheinz Stockhausen at the WDR studio in Cologne developed elektronische Musik (1953) using purely electronically synthesized sounds (sine wave generators) rather than recorded reality. Robert Moog's modular synthesizer (1964) and Mini-Moog (1970) brought electronic sound generation into portable, affordable form; Wendy Carlos's Switched-On Bach (1968) demonstrated synthesizer's commercial viability by performing Bach on Moog. The Roland TR-808 drum machine (1980) and TB-303 bass synthesizer (1981), cheap commercial failures on release, became the foundational instruments of hip-hop (the 808's bass kick) and Chicago house (the 303's squelching acid bassline).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Synth-pop and electronic pop achieved commercial dominance in the 1980s: Kraftwerk (Autobahn, 1974; Trans-Europa Express, 1977; The Man-Machine, 1978) pioneered robotic electronic aesthetics that directly influenced hip-hop (Afrika Bambaataa's Planet Rock sampling Trans-Europa Express in 1982), synth-pop (Depeche Mode, Gary Numan), and techno. Brian Eno developed Ambient Music (Ambient 1: Music for Airports, 1978) as environmental music designed to be ignored or attended to with equal pleasure — a concept that influenced new age music, background music design, and later drone and post-rock. Chicago House (Frankie Knuckles, Larry Heard, Robert Owens, Ron Hardy at The Warehouse and Music Box clubs, 1983–86) fused synthesizers, drum machines, and soulful gospel-inflected vocal samples into a new 4/4 dance form. Detroit Techno (Juan Atkins, Derrick May, Kevin Saunderson) extended House's electronic austerity into faster, more mechanized forms explicitly influenced by Kraftwerk and science fiction. These genres spread to Europe (UK rave culture, 1988–92; German techno, Tresor club, Berlin) and diversified into hundreds of sub-genres.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Electronic music has transformed the production of virtually all commercial music: digital audio workstations (DAWs: Pro Tools, Ableton Live, Logic Pro) enable any musician to produce professional recordings independently. Sampling — using recorded audio as musical material — created hip-hop and transformed pop production. MIDI (Musical Instrument Digital Interface, 1983) standardized communication between electronic instruments, enabling complex automated production. Contemporary pop (Taylor Swift, Beyoncé, Drake) is entirely produced electronically. The global EDM (Electronic Dance Music) industry generates billions of dollars annually through recordings, streaming, and festival appearances. Modular synthesizers have experienced a 21st-century revival as instruments valued for their unpredictability and physicality in a digital age.

## Key Terminology
- **Musique concrète**: Pierre Schaeffer's post-WWII practice of building music from recorded real-world sounds transformed by tape manipulation.
- **Modular synthesizer**: Synthesizer system of separate sound-generating and processing modules connected by patch cables, allowing infinite custom signal routing.
- **808 (Roland TR-808)**: Drum machine (1980) whose bass drum became the foundation of hip-hop and whose open hi-hat is ubiquitous in R&B production.
- **Acid house**: Chicago House variant defined by the squelching, resonant basslines of the Roland TB-303 bass synthesizer.
- **Detroit Techno**: Electronic dance music genre created by Juan Atkins, Derrick May, and Kevin Saunderson in Detroit, drawing on Kraftwerk and science fiction.
- **DAW (Digital Audio Workstation)**: Software application (Pro Tools, Logic, Ableton Live) enabling recording, editing, mixing, and production of audio on a computer.
