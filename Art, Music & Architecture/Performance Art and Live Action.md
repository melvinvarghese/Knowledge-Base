# Performance Art and Live Action

## Overview
Performance art places the artist's own body, presence, and live action in front of an audience as the primary medium of visual art. Drawing roots from Futurist evenings, Dada cabarets, and Allan Kaprow's 1950s 'Happenings,' performance art challenged the art market's obsession with saleable objects by emphasizing ephemerality, vulnerability, and direct human encounter.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
In the late 1950s and 1960s, painters dissatisfied with the static canvas stepped into live action: Yves Klein staged Anthropometries (1960), directing paint-covered models to press their bodies onto paper before an orchestra, while Gutai artists in Japan leapt through paper screens and wrestled mud. Allan Kaprow coined 'Happenings' for unscripted, participatory events blending everyday gestures with chance, and Fluxus artists (Yoko Ono, George Brecht) wrote concise 'event scores' like Ono's Cut Piece (1964), in which audience members stepped onstage to snip away her clothing with scissors.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
During the 1970s, 'Body Art' turned inward toward physical endurance, pain, and psychological risk. Chris Burden had himself shot in the arm (Shoot, 1971); Vito Acconci tested physical boundaries; Ana Mendieta pressed her silhouette into earth, mud, and fire (Silueta Series) to unite body, land, and ritual. Marina Abramović became performance art's most prominent practitioner, first in grueling endurance pieces with Ulay and later in solo works like The Artist Is Present (MoMA, 2010), sitting silently for 736 hours across from museum visitors. Feminist artists such as Carolee Schneemann, Valie Export, and Rebecca Horn used performance to reclaim female bodily autonomy from the male gaze.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Because live actions vanish once completed, performance art initially resisted commercial gallery sales, relying on photographic and video documentation to enter art history. Today, however, major museums routinely commission live re-performances and durational works — notably Tino Sehgal's 'constructed situations' forbidding any photography — proving ephemeral human encounter has become a central pillar of contemporary art.

## Key Terminology
- **Happening**: Participatory, loosely scripted live art event coined by Allan Kaprow in the late 1950s.
- **Event score**: Brief written instruction used by Fluxus artists to trigger open-ended performances or everyday actions.
- **Durational performance**: Live artwork sustained over hours, days, or months to test physical endurance and alter viewer time perception.
- **Body art**: 1960s–70s branch of performance art using the artist's physical body as both subject and sculptural material.
- **Ephemerality**: Temporary, non-object nature of live performance, resisting easy commodification in the art market.
- **Constructed situation**: Tino Sehgal's term for live, unrecorded museum interactions between trained interpreters and visitors.
