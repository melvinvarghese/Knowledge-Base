# Islamic Architecture - Muqarnas, Geometric Arabesques, and the Alhambra

## Overview
Islamic Architecture developed sophisticated mathematical geometries, intricate non-figural tilework, and sweeping courtyards across Andalusia, Persia, and the Ottoman world.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Arabesque & Non-Figural Aniconism: Adhering to religious traditions discouraging anthropomorphic depictions of the divine, Islamic artists developed infinite interlacing geometric patterns (*girih*) and rhythmic floral foliage (*arabesque*), reflecting the infinite, transcendent unity of God (*Tawhid*).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Muqarnas Vaulting & The Alhambra: *Muqarnas*—corbelled stalactite honeycomb vaulting—dissolves transitions between square architectural bases and circular domes. The Nasrid palace of the Alhambra in Granada, Spain (14th century) epitomizes this synthesis, integrating delicate stucco filigree, marble fountains, reflecting pools, and poetic calligraphy carved into walls.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Influence on European Gothic arches, mathematical quasicrystalline Penrose tilings discovered in medieval Islamic tilework.

## Key Terminology
- **Muqarnas**: Decorative honeycomb corbeling or stalactite vaulting characteristic of traditional Islamic architecture.
- **Arabesque**: Intricate, flowing decorative design based on rhythmic interlaced linear patterns of foliage and geometry.
- **The Alhambra**: Palace and fortress complex constructed in Granada, Andalusia, renowned as the crowning jewel of Moorish architecture.
