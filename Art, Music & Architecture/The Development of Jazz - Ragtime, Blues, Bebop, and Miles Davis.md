# The Development of Jazz - Ragtime, Blues, Bebop, and Miles Davis

## Overview
Jazz is a uniquely American musical art form born from African musical traditions, blues, and European harmony, celebrated for improvisation, swing syncopation, and modal experimentation.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Origins & The Swing Era: Born in New Orleans from African American spirituals, field hollers, and brass marching bands (Louis Armstrong, Jelly Roll Morton). Duke Ellington and Count Basie popularized large big-band Swing in the 1930s, featuring complex arrangements and infectious dance rhythms.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Bebop & Modal Modernism: In the 1940s, Charlie Parker, Dizzy Gillespie, and Thelonious Monk created Bebop—ultra-fast, virtuoso art music utilizing altered extended chords (9ths, 11ths, 13ths) and chromatic substitution. In 1959, Miles Davis recorded *Kind of Blue*, pioneering Modal Jazz—improvising over static musical modes rather than rigid chord progressions, creating contemplative emotional spaciousness.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Profound cultural vehicle for African American civil rights expression, global influence across rock, hip-hop, and modern music theory.

## Key Terminology
- **Bebop**: Complex style of jazz characterized by fast tempos, instrumental virtuosity, and improvisation based on harmonic structure.
- **Syncopation**: Rhythmic displacement of regular accents onto unaccented off-beats, creating the swing groove.
- **Modal Jazz**: Jazz style pioneered by Miles Davis using musical modes as the harmonic framework for improvisation rather than rapid chord changes.
