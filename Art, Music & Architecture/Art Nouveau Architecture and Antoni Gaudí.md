# Art Nouveau Architecture and Antoni Gaudí

## Overview
Art Nouveau architecture (c. 1890–1910) broke decisively with historical revivals by integrating exposed iron, glass, stone, and ceramic tile into fluid, nature-inspired structures. Nowhere did this organic vision reach greater structural audacity than in Barcelona under Catalan architect Antoni Gaudí, whose parabolic arches and biomorphic facades anticipated contemporary parametric engineering.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
In Brussels, Victor Horta's Hôtel Tassel (1893) inaugurated Art Nouveau architecture by stripping away load-bearing interior walls to let slender cast-iron columns sprout like botanical vines across stained-glass stairwells. Hector Guimard brought sinuous cast-iron canopies to Paris Metro stations, while Charles Rennie Mackintosh in Glasgow (Glasgow School of Art, 1897–1909) and Otto Wagner in Vienna pursued crisper geometric syntheses balancing ornament with functional light.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Working within Barcelona's Catalan Modernisme movement, Antoni Gaudí (1852–1926) forged a singular architecture rooted in structural physics and natural observation. Rejecting straight lines because 'none exist in nature,' Gaudí modeled load-bearing forms using upside-down hanging chains and weighted strings: inverted, the catenary curves revealed pure compression lines, eliminating the need for Gothic flying buttresses. At Casa Batlló (1904–06) and Casa Milà / La Pedrera (1906–12), undulating limestone facades, bone-like pillars, and trencadís (broken ceramic mosaic) chimneys turned apartment buildings into living sculptures. His lifelong masterwork, the Basílica de la Sagrada Família (begun 1882), uses branching hyperbolic-paraboloid stone columns modeled on forest canopies to filter colored light across a towering nave.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Dismissed for decades by strict Functionalists as eccentric fantasy, Gaudí's structural innovations were vindicated by late-20th-century engineers and digital architects. Today, laser scanning and parametric CAD software guide the ongoing completion of the Sagrada Família, cementing Gaudí as a pioneer of biomimetic and computational architecture.

## Key Terminology
- **Catenary arch**: Natural curve formed by a hanging chain under gravity; inverted, it carries compressive loads without external buttresses.
- **Trencadís**: Catalan mosaic technique using shattered ceramic tiles and glass to clad curved architectural surfaces.
- **Modernisme**: Catalan cultural and architectural movement parallel to Art Nouveau, led by Gaudí, Domènech i Montaner, and Puig i Cadafalch.
- **Hyperbolic paraboloid**: Doubly curved saddle-shaped geometric surface used by Gaudí to build thin, strong stone vaults.
- **Biomimicry**: Design approach modeling architectural structures and ventilation directly on biological organisms and natural geometry.
- **Iron-and-glass synthesis**: Art Nouveau structural strategy exposing cast-iron framing as organic ornament rather than hiding it behind stone.
