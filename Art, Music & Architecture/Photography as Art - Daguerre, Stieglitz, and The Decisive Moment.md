# Photography as Art - Daguerre, Stieglitz, and The Decisive Moment

## Overview
Photography evolved from a 19th-century mechanical chemical curiosity into an autonomous fine art form through technological innovation and philosophical visual theory.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Chemical Dawn: Louis Daguerre introduced the daguerreotype in 1839 (silvered copper plates developed with mercury vapor). William Henry Fox Talbot patented the calotype (paper negatives enabling unlimited reproduction), decoupling visual representation from the slow hand of the painter.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Pictorialism to Straight Photography & The Decisive Moment: Alfred Stieglitz fought for photography's recognition as fine art via the Photo-Secession and Gallery 291, transitioning from soft-focus Pictorialism to razor-sharp 'Straight Photography' (Paul Strand, Ansel Adams). Henri Cartier-Bresson formulated 'The Decisive Moment'—the instantaneous visual recognition of geometric formal organization that reveals the essential meaning of a spontaneous event.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Democratization of visual memory, birth of photojournalism, transformed painting toward abstraction.

## Key Terminology
- **Daguerreotype**: Early photographic process using a silvered copper plate sensitized with iodine and developed with mercury fumes.
- **Straight Photography**: Photography that attempts to depict a scene realistically without darkroom manipulation or soft-focus filters.
- **The Decisive Moment**: Concept coined by Henri Cartier-Bresson describing the split second where visual elements align to express the essence of a situation.
