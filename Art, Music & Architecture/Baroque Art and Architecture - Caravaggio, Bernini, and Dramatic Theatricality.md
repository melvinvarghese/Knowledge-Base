# Baroque Art and Architecture - Caravaggio, Bernini, and Dramatic Theatricality

## Overview
Baroque Art (17th–18th centuries), catalyzed by the Catholic Counter-Reformation, utilized dramatic movement, intense emotion, dynamic diagonal compositions, and theatrical light.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Caravaggio & Tenebrism: Michelangelo Merisi da Caravaggio shocked contemporaries by painting raw, unidealized plebeian biblical figures modeled on common laborers. He pioneered *tenebrism*—an extreme, theatrical chiaroscuro where dramatic spotlighting illuminates subjects emerging from pitch-black darkness (*The Calling of Saint Matthew*).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Gian Lorenzo Bernini & Theatrical Sculpture: Bernini transformed marble into pliable flesh, turbulent drapery, and emotional ecstasy (e.g., *The Ecstasy of Saint Teresa*). As an architect, he designed the sweeping monumental colonnades enclosing St. Peter's Square in Rome, envisioned as the welcoming arms of the Church.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Dissemination to Dutch Golden Age masters (Rembrandt, Vermeer) and monumental palace architecture (Palace of Versailles).

## Key Terminology
- **Tenebrism**: Style of painting using violent contrasts of light and dark, with darkness becoming a dominating feature of the image.
- **Counter-Reformation**: Catholic revival and internal reform launched in response to the Protestant Reformation, leveraging dramatic art to inspire faith.
- **Bernini**: Preeminent Italian Baroque sculptor and architect who shaped Rome with works like St. Peter's colonnades.
