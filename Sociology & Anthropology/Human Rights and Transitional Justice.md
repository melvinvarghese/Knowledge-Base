# Human Rights and Transitional Justice

## Overview
Transitional justice refers to the array of mechanisms — criminal tribunals, truth commissions, reparations, lustration, and memorialization — through which societies emerging from mass atrocity, dictatorship, or conflict address legacies of human rights violations, pursue accountability, and attempt social repair.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The post-World War II Nuremberg and Tokyo tribunals established the principle of individual criminal accountability for war crimes and crimes against humanity, overriding sovereign immunity. The International Criminal Court (ICC, 2002) extended this to a permanent international criminal jurisdiction. Truth commissions — pioneered in Argentina, Chile, and South Africa's post-apartheid TRC — offer accountability through testimony and truth-telling rather than criminal prosecution, aiming at social healing and historical clarification rather than punishment.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
South Africa's Truth and Reconciliation Commission (1995–2002) became a global model: its hearings exposed apartheid crimes publicly, granted amnesty in exchange for full disclosure, and enabled victim testimony. Archbishop Tutu's 'ubuntu' philosophy (I am because we are) provided an African communitarian moral framework for reconciliation. Research shows mixed outcomes: truth commissions can break denial and document harm, but rarely produce full accountability or transform structural inequalities that generated atrocity.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Sociological analysis of transitional justice examines who is included and excluded from victim and perpetrator categories, how dominant groups evade accountability, the politics of historical memory and denial, and the differential capacity of international law to hold Global South versus Global North actors accountable. Decolonial critics note that Western powers that enabled or committed colonial atrocities (Belgium in Congo, US torture, UK in Kenya) have faced no comparable accountability mechanisms.

## Key Terminology
- **Truth Commission**: An official body examining past human rights abuses and producing a public historical record
- **Transitional Justice**: Mechanisms addressing human rights violations in societies emerging from conflict or dictatorship
- **Ubuntu**: African philosophical concept meaning 'I am because we are', used in South African reconciliation
- **Amnesty**: Formal forgiveness for political offenses, sometimes exchanged for full disclosure of crimes
- **Reparations**: Material or symbolic compensation offered to victims of historical injustice
- **Lustration**: The vetting or exclusion from public office of officials associated with a former oppressive regime
