# Cognitive Anthropology

## Overview
Cognitive anthropology examines the relationship between culture and human cognition — how cultural knowledge is organized in the mind, how categories, schemas, and mental models vary cross-culturally, and how cognitive universals and cultural variation interact.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Early cognitive anthropology (Goodenough, Lounsbury) used formal analysis to describe cultural knowledge as shared mental codes — 'what one must know to behave appropriately in a given society'. Componential analysis of kinship terms and folk botanical taxonomy mapped the logical structures of native classification systems. Berlin and Kay's cross-cultural study of color terminology (1969) found systematic patterns in how languages partition the color spectrum, suggesting cognitive universals constrain cultural variation.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Schema theory argues that cultural knowledge is organized in cognitive structures called schemas — general knowledge structures guiding interpretation of experience. Cultural models (D'Andrade) are shared schemas that organize domains of experience: illness, marriage, success, the economy. Research demonstrates how cultural models shape reasoning, decision-making, and emotional response — the American 'folk model' of the mind as rule-following machine versus Balinese models of personhood, for example.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Evolutionary cognitive anthropology (Boyer, Sperber) argues that universal cognitive capacities (agent detection, intuitive physics, folk biology) constrain which cultural representations spread and stabilize in human populations. Cross-cultural psychology tests whether findings from WEIRD (Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, Democratic) populations generalize universally. Embodied cognition approaches (Varela, Maturana) challenge representational models, arguing that cognition is enacted through body-environment interaction rather than symbol manipulation.

## Key Terminology
- **Folk Taxonomy**: Indigenous classification systems for natural or social categories studied in cognitive anthropology
- **Cultural Model**: A shared cognitive schema organizing knowledge and reasoning in a cultural domain
- **Componential Analysis**: A formal method for describing the logical structure of cultural categories
- **WEIRD Populations**: Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, Democratic populations over-represented in psychology research
- **Schema**: A cognitive framework or concept that helps organize and interpret information
- **Embodied Cognition**: The view that cognitive processes are shaped by the body and its interaction with the environment
