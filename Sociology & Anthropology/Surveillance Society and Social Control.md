# Surveillance Society and Social Control

## Overview
Surveillance society refers to the systematic, often automated monitoring of populations through data collection, cameras, biometrics, and digital tracking, raising questions about social control, privacy, power asymmetries, and the datafication of everyday life.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Foucault drew on Bentham's Panopticon — a prison design allowing continuous visibility of inmates who cannot see when they are being watched — as a metaphor for modern disciplinary power. The key insight is that surveillance operates through the internalization of the possibility of being watched: subjects regulate themselves even without actual observation. Modern institutions (school, factory, clinic, barracks) are panoptic in their organization of space, time, and body.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Contemporary surveillance has surpassed Foucault's model in its scope and dataveillance capacities. CCTV, facial recognition, metadata collection, credit scoring, predictive policing, and social media monitoring constitute an assemblage of overlapping surveillance regimes. David Lyon distinguishes 'surveillance capitalism' (Zuboff's concept of behavioral data harvesting for profit prediction) from state surveillance for security and social sorting.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Surveillance studies examines how surveillance is racialized (stop-and-frisk, predictive policing targeting Black neighborhoods), classed, and gendered. Resistance includes encryption, anonymization, surveillance art, and legal challenges. The expansion of algorithmic decision-making raises new questions about automated bias, accountability, and the black-boxing of consequential life decisions in hiring, lending, parole, and healthcare.

## Key Terminology
- **Panopticon**: Bentham's prison design used by Foucault as a metaphor for modern disciplinary surveillance
- **Surveillance Capitalism**: Zuboff's concept of data harvesting from human behavior for behavioral prediction and modification
- **Dataveillance**: The systematic monitoring and profiling of individuals through digital data collection
- **Social Sorting**: Surveillance use to classify populations into groups with differential access to services
- **Algorithmic Control**: The regulation of behavior through automated decision systems and predictive analytics
- **Chilling Effect**: The inhibition of behavior caused by awareness of surveillance or potential punishment
