# Medical Anthropology

## Overview
Medical anthropology examines health, illness, and healing in cultural, social, and political-economic context, analyzing how biomedical categories, illness experience, healing practices, and health inequalities are shaped by culture, power, and social structure.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Arthur Kleinman's explanatory models framework showed that patients and practitioners hold different causal explanations for illness — based on cultural beliefs, social experience, and institutional training — producing communication failures that undermine care. He distinguished 'disease' (biomedical pathology) from 'illness' (the patient's experienced suffering and its social meaning) and 'sickness' (the social role of being ill). Comparative cross-cultural research revealed profoundly diverse illness categories: 'culture-bound syndromes' (koro, susto, amok) challenge universalist biomedical nosology.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Political-economic approaches (Farmer, Scheper-Hughes) analyze 'structural violence' — how poverty, racism, displacement, and inequality kill people as surely as bacteria do, constraining lives and bodies in ways invisible to purely clinical medicine. AIDS, tuberculosis, malaria, and malnutrition are diseases of poverty and politics as much as biology. Medical anthropologists document how pharmaceutical markets, global health governance, and humanitarian medicine reproduce global inequalities even while saving lives.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
The 'body' in medical anthropology is not a stable biological object but a site of cultural inscription, social suffering, and political contestation. Research examines reproductive politics (abortion, surrogacy, birth medicalization), mental health in diverse cultural settings, end-of-life care, addiction, disability, and the social production of medical knowledge. Applied medical anthropology works within health systems to improve culturally appropriate care and community-based health responses.

## Key Terminology
- **Explanatory Model**: Kleinman's framework of patients' and providers' different causal accounts of illness
- **Illness**: The subjective experience of suffering and its social meaning, distinct from biological disease
- **Structural Violence**: Farmer's concept of harm caused by social inequalities, poverty, and political exclusion
- **Culture-Bound Syndrome**: An illness category specific to a cultural context, unrecognized in universal biomedical nosology
- **Biomedicine**: The dominant Western medical system based on biological science and clinical diagnosis
- **Medical Pluralism**: The coexistence of multiple healing systems — biomedical, traditional, religious — in a community
