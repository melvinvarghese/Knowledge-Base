# Work, Labor and Occupations

## Overview
The sociology of work examines how paid and unpaid labor is organized, controlled, compensated, and experienced, analyzing the labor process, labor markets, occupational identity, and the transformation of work under post-industrial capitalism and digitalization.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Braverman's 'Labor and Monopoly Capital' (1974) extended Marx's analysis of alienation to show how Taylorist scientific management deskilled work by separating conception (management) from execution (workers), giving capital control over the labor process. Workers' skill and knowledge were systematically expropriated and embodied in machines and management protocols, reducing labor to routine, controllable tasks. This 'degradation of work' thesis sparked labor process theory.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Post-Fordist and post-industrial transitions transformed work: the decline of manufacturing, rise of service sector employment, feminization of labor, erosion of standard employment (full-time, permanent, benefit-providing), and expansion of precarious work (part-time, temporary, gig). Standing's concept of 'the precariat' — a new class formation characterized by unstable employment, absent benefits, and weak labor rights — describes the experience of millions in platform economies and informal sectors.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Emotional labor (Hochschild) — the management of one's feelings as part of the job, particularly in service work — is disproportionately demanded of women and low-paid workers. Workers in airlines, hotels, healthcare, and call centers are required to feel and display appropriate emotions, subordinating authentic feeling to commercial imperatives. The gig economy (Uber, TaskRabbit, Deliveroo) intensifies precarity while misclassifying workers as independent contractors to avoid labor protections.

## Key Terminology
- **Alienation**: Marx's concept of workers' estrangement from their labor, its product, and their own potential
- **Labor Process**: Braverman's focus on how capital organizes and controls the actual work of production
- **Precariat**: Standing's concept of a class experiencing unstable, insecure employment without labor protections
- **Emotional Labor**: Hochschild's concept of workers managing feelings as part of paid work, especially in services
- **Taylorism**: Frederick Taylor's scientific management system decomposing work into measurable, controlled tasks
- **Deskilling**: The process by which skilled work is fragmented, routinized, and reduced to simpler tasks
