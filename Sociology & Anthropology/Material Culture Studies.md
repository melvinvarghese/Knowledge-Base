# Material Culture Studies

## Overview
Material culture studies examines the physical objects — tools, clothing, buildings, art, food — that humans make, use, and imbue with meaning, arguing that things are not mere instruments but active participants in social life, mediating relationships and encoding cultural values.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Arjun Appadurai's 'The Social Life of Things' (1986) introduced the concept of the 'commodity phase' in an object's biography: things move in and out of commodity status as their social contexts change. A religious artifact may be commodified in a tourist market, then re-sacralized by a collector or museum. This framework challenged static categories (art vs. commodity, gift vs. market object) by tracing objects' changing meanings across social contexts.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Daniel Miller's anthropology of consumption argues that people shape their identities, relationships, and values through the appropriation and use of mass-produced goods. Rather than passive cultural dupes, consumers actively 'objectify' themselves through objects — a home's decoration expresses social relationships, values, and aspirations. Cross-cultural studies reveal how global commodities (Coca-Cola, jeans, mobile phones) are locally appropriated and infused with new cultural meanings.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Science and technology studies (STS), particularly actor-network theory (Latour, Callon), argues that objects are 'actants' — they participate in social networks by enabling, constraining, and mediating human action. A speed bump enforces traffic rules more reliably than a police officer; a vaccine program enrolls doctors, patients, and vials into a network. ANT flattens the distinction between social and technical, human and nonhuman, treating all as participants in sociotechnical assemblages.

## Key Terminology
- **Material Culture**: The physical objects made and used by human groups that embody and transmit cultural meanings
- **Object Biography**: Kopytoff's concept of an object's changing meaning and status across different social contexts
- **Objectification**: Miller's concept of humans expressing and forming themselves through material objects
- **Commodity Phase**: Appadurai's concept of a stage in an object's biography when it is exchanged for money
- **Actor-Network Theory**: Latour's approach treating objects and humans as equally active participants in social networks
- **Actant**: Latour's term for any entity — human or nonhuman — that takes part in a social network
