# Ethnography and Fieldwork Methods

## Overview
Ethnography is the core method of cultural anthropology: the systematic study of a social group or cultural context through extended, immersive fieldwork — typically involving participant observation, interviews, and documentary analysis — aimed at producing rich, context-sensitive accounts of social life.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Bronislaw Malinowski established modern ethnographic norms in 'Argonauts of the Western Pacific' (1922): the anthropologist must live among the people, learn the language, participate in everyday life, and 'grasp the native's point of view'. Extended fieldwork (typically 12–24 months) is intended to move beyond surface impressions and tourist categories to understand social life from insider perspectives. Malinowski's functional approach analyzed the Kula ring of exchange as maintaining social relations across island communities.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Clifford Geertz's interpretive anthropology reformulated ethnography as 'thick description' — not merely cataloguing behaviors but interpreting their layered cultural meanings, as in his famous reading of a Balinese cockfight as a text encoding Balinese concepts of status, mortality, and masculinity. Post-Malinowski reflexive ethnography (Writing Culture, 1986) confronted the politics of representation: who speaks for whom? Whose knowledge is authoritative? How do power relations between anthropologist and informant shape ethnographic texts?

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Contemporary ethnography includes multi-sited ethnography (Marcus) tracing phenomena across locations, digital ethnography (online communities and virtual worlds), institutional ethnography (Smith), and collaborative ethnography involving research participants in writing. Visual ethnography uses photography, video, and film. Ethical challenges include informed consent, anonymization, vulnerability of research subjects, and the politics of knowledge extraction.

## Key Terminology
- **Participant Observation**: The ethnographic method of immersed engagement in community life while systematically observing
- **Thick Description**: Geertz's ethnographic approach interpreting layered cultural meanings beyond surface behavior
- **Multi-Sited Ethnography**: Marcus' approach tracing phenomena across multiple social locations and contexts
- **Fieldwork**: Extended immersion in a community or setting to conduct anthropological research
- **Informed Consent**: The ethical requirement that research subjects understand and agree to their participation
- **Reflexivity**: Critical examination of how the researcher's position and subjectivity affect knowledge production
