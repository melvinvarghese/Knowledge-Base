# Disability Studies and Sociology

## Overview
Disability studies is an interdisciplinary field that examines disability as a social and political category, contrasting the medical model (disability as individual deficit requiring cure) with the social model (disability as the product of social barriers excluding impaired people from full participation).

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The medical model locates disability in the individual body or mind as impairment that reduces functioning and requires medical management. The social model, developed by disabled activists in the UK (Union of Physically Impaired Against Segregation, 1976), argues that people are disabled not by their impairments but by social barriers — inaccessible buildings, discriminatory attitudes, exclusionary labor markets, lack of communication access — that could be removed by social change.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Critical disability studies has refined the social model to address criticisms that it too sharply separates impairment from disability and ignores the embodied experience of pain, fatigue, and illness. Intersectional disability studies examines how disability intersects with race (higher rates of disability in communities of color due to occupational hazard, poverty, and inadequate healthcare), gender (disabled women's multiply marginalized positions), class, and sexuality. Crip theory (McRuer) draws on queer theory to challenge compulsory able-bodiedness.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Neurodiversity advocates argue that neurological differences (autism, ADHD, dyslexia) are natural human variation, not disorders requiring normalization. The disability rights movement achieved landmark legislation (Americans with Disabilities Act, 1990; UK Disability Discrimination Act, 1995) mandating accessibility and non-discrimination. Contemporary debates address deinstitutionalization, community-based support, independent living, prenatal selection, and the politics of assisted dying.

## Key Terminology
- **Medical Model of Disability**: The view that disability is an individual bodily or cognitive deficit requiring medical treatment
- **Social Model of Disability**: The view that disability is produced by social barriers excluding impaired people from participation
- **Ableism**: Discrimination and prejudice against disabled people premised on assumptions of normative ability
- **Neurodiversity**: The view that neurological differences are natural human variation rather than disorders
- **Crip Theory**: A theoretical approach using queer frameworks to challenge compulsory able-bodiedness
- **Accessibility**: The design of environments and systems usable by people with diverse physical and cognitive abilities
