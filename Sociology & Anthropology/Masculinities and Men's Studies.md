# Masculinities and Men's Studies

## Overview
Masculinities studies, emerging from feminist scholarship and gay and queer theory, examines masculinity not as a natural male essence but as a socially constructed, institutionally reproduced, and internally diverse set of gender practices, organized hierarchically within gender order.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Raewyn Connell's hegemonic masculinity framework (1987, revised 2005) argues that multiple masculinities coexist in any society, organized in a hierarchy: hegemonic masculinity (the culturally dominant form in a given context — typically white, heterosexual, professional, physically able) subordinates femininities and marginalized masculinities (gay, working-class, Black, disabled men's gender practices). Hegemony is relational and contested, maintained not primarily by force but by cultural consent.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Research documents how hegemonic masculinity norms — strength, stoicism, dominance, breadwinner status, heterosexual prowess — produce significant costs for men themselves: poor health-seeking behavior, elevated occupational accidents, violence as conflict resolution, restricted emotional expression, and mental health stigma. Men's higher mortality from suicide, occupational disease, and violence is linked to masculinity norms that prohibit help-seeking. These insights have informed public health campaigns targeting men's health.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Intersectional masculinities research examines how race, class, age, and sexuality diversify masculinity experience: Black masculinity navigates stereotypes of hypersexuality and criminality (Duneier); working-class masculinity responds to deindustrialization with compensatory displays (Wacquant's boxers); young men in colonized settings negotiate between traditional and Western masculinity scripts. Contemporary debates address toxic masculinity, men's rights movements, the sociology of fatherhood, and masculinity in a feminist era.

## Key Terminology
- **Hegemonic Masculinity**: Connell's concept of the culturally dominant form of masculinity in a given social context
- **Masculinity**: Socially constructed, historically variable sets of practices associated with being a man
- **Subordinate Masculinity**: Connell's term for gender practices of marginalized men — gay, working-class, minority men
- **Toxic Masculinity**: Popular term for harmful masculinity norms including violence, emotional suppression, and domination
- **Fatherhood Sociology**: Research on how fatherhood is socially constructed, practiced, and distributed across class and race
- **Gender Order**: Connell's concept of the structured relations between masculinities and femininities in a society
