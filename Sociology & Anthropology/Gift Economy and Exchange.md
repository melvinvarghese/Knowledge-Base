# Gift Economy and Exchange

## Overview
Marcel Mauss's 'The Gift' (1925) is a foundational text in economic anthropology, arguing that gifts are never free — they obligate reciprocity — and that exchange systems in archaic societies create and maintain social bonds, moral obligations, and hierarchies rather than merely transferring goods.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Mauss analyzed 'total prestations' — complex exchange systems like the Northwest Coast potlatch and the Melanesian kula ring — in which gifts of objects, feasts, and rituals are exchanged between groups, creating relations of alliance, honor, and obligation. The gift has a 'spirit' (Maori hau) that demands return — to fail to reciprocate is to lose social standing, face, and honor. This insight challenged utilitarian economic models that treat exchange as driven purely by self-interest and profit-maximization.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The potlatch — ceremonial feasting involving massive gift-giving and sometimes destruction of property by Northwest Coast Indigenous peoples — was misread by early observers as irrational waste. Mauss showed it establishes social hierarchy, honors allies, and demonstrates wealth through generosity rather than accumulation. Levi-Strauss later linked gift exchange to kinship alliance, while Sahlin's 'Stone Age Economics' applied gift logic to the concept of 'original affluence'.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Contemporary applications of Maussian exchange theory analyze blood donation (Titmuss), open-source software, volunteering, care relationships, and 'sharing economy' platforms (Airbnb, Uber), questioning whether these escape market logic or simply commodify social relations. Derrida's 'Given Time' argued the gift is impossible: any gift that creates obligation ceases to be a gift, raising deeper questions about the limits of economic anthropology.

## Key Terminology
- **The Gift**: Mauss's analysis of exchange as creating social obligations, bonds, and hierarchies
- **Total Prestations**: Mauss's term for complex exchange systems involving gifts, feasts, and social relations
- **Potlatch**: Northwest Coast Indigenous ceremony of competitive gift-giving establishing rank and alliance
- **Reciprocity**: The norm of returning benefits received, analyzed by Mauss as the core of social exchange
- **Hau**: Maori concept analyzed by Mauss as the 'spirit' of the gift that demands return
- **Moral Economy**: Thompson's concept of the normative framework governing legitimate economic exchange in communities
