# Evolutionary Anthropology

## Overview
Evolutionary anthropology synthesizes biological anthropology, primatology, paleoanthropology, and genetics to understand human evolution — the morphological, cognitive, behavioral, and cultural transformations that produced Homo sapiens — and the evolutionary foundations of human social behavior.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Paleoanthropology uses fossil evidence, comparative anatomy, and genetic analysis to reconstruct the hominin lineage. Key species include Australopithecus africanus (3.2 million years ago), Homo habilis (first tool-users), Homo erectus (fire, long-distance migration), Homo neanderthalensis (complex cognition, symbolic behavior, interbreeding with modern humans), and Homo sapiens (behavioral modernity ~100,000–50,000 BCE). The 'Out of Africa' hypothesis is well-supported by both fossil and genomic evidence.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Primatology (Jane Goodall, Frans de Waal) revealed complex social behavior — tool use, coalition politics, empathy, deception, cultural transmission — in chimpanzees, bonobos, and gorillas, complicating simple human uniqueness claims. Bonobo social organization (female-dominated, sexually permissive, low aggression) versus chimpanzee organization (male-dominated, territorial, violent) illustrates the range of social possibilities among our closest relatives.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Evolutionary approaches to human behavior apply Darwinian selection to cognition, cooperation, language, morality, and cultural transmission. Evolutionary psychology identifies cross-cultural universals in mate preferences, kin investment, and social emotions as adaptations. Critics charge adaptationist accounts are unfalsifiable 'just-so stories' that naturalize social hierarchies. Cultural evolution (Richerson, Boyd) models how cultural traits — technologies, norms, beliefs — are differentially copied, modified, and transmitted in human populations.

## Key Terminology
- **Paleoanthropology**: The study of hominin evolution through fossil evidence and comparative anatomy
- **Hominin**: The taxonomic group including modern humans and their extinct bipedal relatives
- **Primatology**: The study of primate behavior, ecology, and evolution as a window on human origins
- **Out of Africa**: The hypothesis that Homo sapiens evolved in Africa and dispersed globally
- **Cultural Evolution**: The process by which cultural traits are selectively transmitted, modified, and spread
- **Behavioral Modernity**: The emergence of fully modern human cognition and symbolic behavior around 50,000-100,000 BCE
