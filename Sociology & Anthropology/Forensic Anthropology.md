# Forensic Anthropology

## Overview
Forensic anthropology applies biological and cultural anthropological methods to medicolegal contexts, primarily the analysis of human skeletal remains for identification, determination of cause and manner of death, and documentation of human rights abuses and mass atrocities.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Skeletal analysis allows forensic anthropologists to establish a biological profile of unknown individuals: estimated age at death (from growth plates, joint wear, and dental development), biological sex (from pelvis morphology, skull features), stature (from long bone length), and in some cases ancestry. Trauma analysis identifies perimortem injuries — blunt force, sharp force, projectile wounds — distinguishing ante-mortem (before death), perimortem (at or near death), and post-mortem bone modifications.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Forensic anthropologists play crucial roles in mass grave exhumation and analysis — from World War II era cases to contemporary mass atrocities (Argentina's dirty war, the Balkans, Rwanda, Guatemala). The Argentine Forensic Anthropology Team (EAAF), founded in the 1980s to identify 'disappeared' persons under the military dictatorship, pioneered the application of forensic methods to human rights documentation. Genetic analysis (DNA profiling) has dramatically improved identification of commingled and fragmented remains.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Beyond mass atrocities, forensic anthropologists contribute to disaster victim identification (plane crashes, tsunamis), criminal investigations, immigration enforcement disputes, and cold cases. Ethical challenges include respectful treatment of remains across cultural frameworks, communication with families, evidence standards in transitional justice contexts, and the politics of identification — whose remains are prioritized and why.

## Key Terminology
- **Osteology**: The scientific study of bones, foundational to skeletal analysis in forensic anthropology
- **Biological Profile**: The determination of age, sex, stature, and ancestry from skeletal remains
- **Perimortem Trauma**: Injuries to bone occurring at or near the time of death
- **Mass Grave**: A burial site containing multiple individuals, often associated with mass atrocity
- **DNA Profiling**: Genetic analysis used to identify individuals from biological samples including bone
- **Transitional Justice**: Mechanisms addressing past human rights abuses in post-conflict or post-authoritarian societies
