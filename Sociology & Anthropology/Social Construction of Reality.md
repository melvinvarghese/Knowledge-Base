# Social Construction of Reality

## Overview
Peter Berger and Thomas Luckmann's 'The Social Construction of Reality' (1966) argues that everyday knowledge, social institutions, and reality itself are human products created through ongoing social processes of externalization, objectivation, and internalization.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Human beings externalize their actions and ideas into a shared social world (society as human product). These products become objectivated — they take on a factual, thing-like character independent of any individual (society as objective reality). Individuals then internalize this objectivated social reality through socialization, taking the social world for granted as natural and inevitable (the individual as social product). This dialectical process is the ongoing creation of social reality.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Institutions emerge from habitualization — repeated actions become typed patterns that become codified and transmitted across generations. Legitimation provides second-order meanings explaining and justifying the institutional order: religion, ideology, and science all serve legitimating functions. The concept has been enormously influential in social epistemology, gender studies (gender as constructed), race theory, medical sociology (disease categories as social products), and science studies (Kuhn, Latour).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Critics argue social constructionism risks relativism — if reality is socially constructed, can any claims be objectively true? Scholars distinguish between ontological constructionism (reality is constructed) and epistemological constructionism (our knowledge of reality is shaped by social processes). The latter is more defensible and has become standard in science studies, feminist standpoint epistemology, and critical discourse analysis.

## Key Terminology
- **Social Construction**: The process by which social reality is created and maintained through human interaction
- **Externalization**: The process of human beings expressing themselves in the social world
- **Objectivation**: The process by which subjective human products gain an objective, factual character
- **Internalization**: The process by which individuals absorb objectivated social reality through socialization
- **Legitimation**: Processes that explain and justify the institutional order to social members
- **Taken-for-Granted Reality**: The assumption that social arrangements are natural, inevitable, and beyond question
