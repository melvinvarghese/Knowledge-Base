# Erving Goffman and Dramaturgy

## Overview
Erving Goffman's dramaturgical model analyzes social life as performance: individuals manage impressions in 'front stage' interactions while concealing dissonant aspects in 'back stage' regions, collaborating to maintain shared definitions of situations.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
In 'The Presentation of Self in Everyday Life' (1959), Goffman showed how people use props, costumes, scripts, and setting to project desired identities to audiences. Teams coordinate performances to prevent embarrassing disruptions; face-work repairs threats to social presentations. 'Asylums' (1961) extended this to 'total institutions' (prisons, hospitals), showing how they systematically strip individuals of their civilian identities through degradation ceremonies and enforce institutional selves.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Goffman's 'Stigma' (1963) analyzed how discrediting attributes — physical disability, mental illness, racial minority status, criminal record — are managed in interaction. The stigmatized person either 'passes' (concealing their attribute) or faces social rejection and identity contamination. 'Frame Analysis' (1974) examined how individuals organize experience into cognitive frames, understanding situations through interpretive schemata that can be transformed, misread, or contested.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Goffman's work is celebrated for its rich empirical observation of everyday life and its challenge to idealized conceptions of social order. Critics note he underestimated power structures and tended to bracket questions of race, class, and gender. Contemporary extensions apply Goffmanian concepts to digital self-presentation (social media profiles), online identity performance, and the sociology of organizations.

## Key Terminology
- **Dramaturgy**: Goffman's metaphor treating social life as theatrical performance
- **Front Stage**: The region of social performance where individuals manage impressions for an audience
- **Back Stage**: The region where performers relax, rehearse, and step out of their public roles
- **Total Institution**: Goffman's term for enclosed organizations that control all aspects of inmates' lives
- **Stigma**: A deeply discrediting attribute that reduces a person to a spoiled identity
- **Face-work**: Actions taken to manage and repair threats to one's social presentation
