# Poverty and Inequality

## Overview
The sociology of poverty and inequality examines the structural causes of economic deprivation, the measurement and comparison of inequalities, the social experiences of poverty, and the contested politics of redistribution and welfare state provision.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Absolute poverty measures deprivation below a threshold of basic necessities; relative poverty defines poverty in relation to median living standards — capturing the social exclusion of those falling significantly below the norm in their society. Amartya Sen's capabilities approach argues poverty is not merely material deprivation but the absence of real freedoms to live a dignified human life — including education, health, political participation, and social recognition. This framework underpins the UNDP's Human Development Index.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Research on the structural causes of poverty contradicts individualist 'culture of poverty' explanations (Lewis, later Murray): poverty is produced by labor market restructuring, housing market failures, educational inequality, racial discrimination, and welfare state retrenchment — not by deficient values or behavior. William Julius Wilson's analysis of concentrated poverty in Black urban communities showed how deindustrialization destroyed the economic base, triggering social disorganization, while Wacquant linked mass incarceration to management of surplus urban populations.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
The welfare state — systems of social insurance, income support, healthcare, housing, and education provided by the state — varies enormously across capitalist societies. Esping-Andersen's 'Three Worlds of Welfare Capitalism' (1990) classified welfare states as liberal (US, UK — means-tested, residual), conservative-corporatist (Germany — status-preserving, occupationally differentiated), and social-democratic (Scandinavia — universalist, decommodifying). Neoliberal restructuring since the 1980s has rolled back welfare provision across all three regimes, increasing precarity and inequality.

## Key Terminology
- **Relative Poverty**: Poverty defined as falling significantly below the median living standard of a society
- **Capabilities Approach**: Sen's framework defining poverty as the absence of real freedoms to live a dignified life
- **Culture of Poverty**: The discredited thesis that poverty is reproduced through deficient values and behaviors
- **Welfare State**: State institutions providing social protection through insurance, income support, and public services
- **Social Exclusion**: Multidimensional disadvantage blocking participation in normal social, economic, and civic life
- **Decommodification**: Esping-Andersen's concept of the welfare state's ability to insulate citizens from market dependence
