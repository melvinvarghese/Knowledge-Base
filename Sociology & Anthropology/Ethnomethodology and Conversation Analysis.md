# Ethnomethodology and Conversation Analysis

## Overview
Ethnomethodology, founded by Harold Garfinkel, investigates the ordinary methods and practical reasoning people use to make sense of, navigate, and produce recognizable social order in everyday situations.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Garfinkel rejected functionalist notions that social order is imposed by pre-existing normative consensus. Instead, order is an ongoing, practical accomplishment created in real-time by social actors through 'indexicality' (meanings tied to context) and 'reflexivity' (actions that simultaneously describe and constitute the situation). Garfinkel's famous 'breaching experiments' deliberately disrupted routine social interactions to reveal the invisible, taken-for-granted expectations sustaining everyday life.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Conversation analysis (CA), developed by Harvey Sacks, Emanuel Schegloff, and Gail Jefferson, grew out of ethnomethodology to rigorously study naturally occurring talk-in-interaction. CA demonstrates that speech is organized into systematic structural features such as turn-taking, adjacency pairs (e.g., question-answer, greeting-greeting), preference organization, and repair mechanisms that restore mutual understanding during interactional trouble.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Ethnomethodology and conversation analysis provide powerful empirical toolkits for analyzing institutional interaction in doctor-patient consultations, courtroom cross-examinations, air traffic control communication, and human-computer interfaces. While criticized for neglecting macro-level political and historical forces, their microsociological precision exposes how institutional realities are produced utterance by utterance.

## Key Terminology
- **Ethnomethodology**: The study of the everyday commonsense methods people use to construct and sustain social order
- **Indexicality**: The property of language and social action where meaning depends entirely on its specific situational context
- **Reflexivity**: The self-referential process whereby an account or action creates the very reality it claims to describe
- **Breaching Experiment**: A research method that deliberately breaks unspoken social rules to expose baseline normative expectations
- **Adjacency Pair**: A paired sequence of conversational turns where the first part invites a normative second part
- **Turn-Taking**: The universal, finely tuned interactional system regulating who speaks when in human communication
