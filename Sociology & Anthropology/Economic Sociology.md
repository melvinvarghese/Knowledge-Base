# Economic Sociology

## Overview
Economic sociology examines how economic activity — markets, money, exchange, production, consumption — is embedded in social relations, institutions, and cultural frameworks, challenging the decontextualized models of neoclassical economics with sociologically informed analysis.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Granovetter's 'Economic Action and Social Structure' (1985) revived the embeddedness concept: economic action is embedded in ongoing social networks of relationships — trust, reputation, information flow — that neither disappear in modern market economies (as sociologists of modernization implied) nor are purely individual rational choices (as economists assume). Network analysis reveals how market exchange depends on social infrastructure: who you know shapes the prices you pay, information you access, and partners you find.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The sociology of markets (White, Podolny) analyzes markets as social structures: firms take their cues from peers in production markets; status hierarchies organize exchange; institutional frameworks define what can be bought and sold. Viviana Zelizer's cultural economic sociology examines 'circuits of commerce': how earmarked money (pocket money, food stamps, insurance payouts) carries different social meanings; how intimate relationships are saturated with economic activity without being reducible to market exchange.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Financialization — the growing importance of financial markets, instruments, and logic in economic and social life — is a central focus of contemporary economic sociology. Mortgage markets, credit rating agencies, derivatives pricing, and financial crisis sociology examine how financial technologies shape who gets credit, what risks are distributed, and how financial instability becomes systemic. The 2008 financial crisis accelerated sociological attention to financial markets as social and moral constructions whose stability depends on shared models, trust, and institutional frameworks.

## Key Terminology
- **Economic Sociology**: The sociological analysis of economic action as embedded in social relations and institutions
- **Embeddedness**: Granovetter's concept that economic action is embedded in ongoing social relationships
- **Sociology of Markets**: Analysis of markets as social structures organized by status hierarchies and institutional frameworks
- **Financialization**: The expanding role of financial markets, motives, and institutions in social and economic life
- **Moral Economy**: Thompson and Polanyi's concept of normative expectations governing legitimate economic exchange
- **Circuits of Commerce**: Zelizer's concept of distinct social spheres with differentiated meanings and uses of money
