# Media and Society

## Overview
Media sociology examines how media institutions, technologies, and texts shape social reality, political opinion, cultural identity, and social power — from agenda-setting and framing to media ownership, algorithmic recommendation, and the politics of visibility.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Frankfurt School (Adorno, Horkheimer) coined the 'culture industry' to critique mass media as a system for manufacturing consent, standardizing culture, and creating passive consumers. Harold Lasswell's 'hypodermic needle' or 'magic bullet' model of media effects posited direct, powerful effects of mass media on audiences. Later research revealed more complex, mediated effects: 'limited effects' models (Klapper), agenda-setting (McCombs, Shaw — media may not tell us what to think but what to think about), framing (Entman), and cultivation theory (Gerbner).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Stuart Hall's 'Encoding/Decoding' model challenged media effects paradigms by arguing that media texts are polysemous — they carry preferred readings but audiences actively decode them in dominant, negotiated, or oppositional ways, depending on their social position and interpretive repertoire. Audiences are not passive dupes but active meaning-makers. Political economy approaches (Herman, Chomsky's 'Manufacturing Consent') focus on media ownership concentration, advertising dependence, and state-media relations as structural filters shaping news content.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Digital media have transformed media sociology: social media platforms enable user-generated content, networked publics, and direct political communication, while algorithmic curation creates filter bubbles, amplifies misinformation, and monetizes attention. Platform power (Gillespie) examines how platforms govern content through opaque algorithmic and policy decisions with profound social and political consequences. Media representations of race, gender, disability, and sexuality continue to reflect and reproduce social hierarchies.

## Key Terminology
- **Agenda-Setting**: McCombs and Shaw's finding that media influence public priorities by emphasizing certain issues
- **Framing**: Entman's concept of media selection and salience that shapes interpretation of events
- **Culture Industry**: Adorno and Horkheimer's critique of mass media as manufacturing standardized cultural consumption
- **Encoding/Decoding**: Hall's model of media meaning as produced through active audience interpretation
- **Filter Bubble**: Pariser's concept of algorithmically personalized information environments limiting exposure to diverse views
- **Platform Power**: The capacity of social media platforms to govern communication, visibility, and public discourse
