# Sociology of Everyday Life and Microsociology

## Overview
The sociology of everyday life examines the small-scale, routine, face-to-face interactions, spatial encounters, and micro-practices that constitute the lived reality of human social existence.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Georg Simmel was a pioneer of microsociology, analyzing the fleeting social forms of modern urban existence: sociability, flirtation, secrecy, the role of money, and the psychology of the metropolis. Simmel observed that urban sensory overload forces modern individuals to cultivate a 'blasé attitude' — an emotional detachment that protects personal autonomy while producing interpersonal indifference and social alienation.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Henri Lefebvre and Michel de Certeau developed critical theories of everyday life. Lefebvre examined the colonization of everyday existence by bureaucratic capitalism and consumerism, calling for radical everyday transformation. De Certeau ('The Practice of Everyday Life', 1980) emphasized tactical resistance: ordinary people are not passive consumers of dominant structures ('strategies'), but use clever improvisations ('tactics'), shortcuts, and poaching (such as walking in the city or taking detour paths) to reclaim agency.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Contemporary microsociology explores the micro-geographies of everyday spaces (elevators, public transit, coffee shops), micro-aggressions, smartphone interaction rituals, and the management of personal space in crowded environments. The field demonstrates that grand macro-structures of class, race, and gender are ultimately reproduced, negotiated, or subverted in the trivial moments of daily life.

## Key Terminology
- **Blasé Attitude**: Simmel's concept of the psychological detachment adopted by urban dwellers to cope with sensory overload
- **Tactics vs. Strategies**: De Certeau's distinction between the institutional power of rulers and the everyday micro-resistances of consumers
- **Sociability**: Simmel's term for the pure, playful form of social interaction pursued for its own intrinsic pleasure
- **Micro-Aggression**: Brief, commonplace verbal, behavioral, or environmental indignities communicating hostile or negative slights
- **Civil Inattention**: Goffman's concept of acknowledging another's presence in public without being intrusive or threatening
- **Everyday Resistance**: Scott's concept of subtle, uncoordinated ways ordinary individuals undermine oppressive power structures
