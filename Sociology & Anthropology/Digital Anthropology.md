# Digital Anthropology

## Overview
Digital anthropology examines how digital technologies shape, transform, and are shaped by human social life, applying ethnographic methods to online communities, social media platforms, digital economies, and the material conditions of digital infrastructure.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Early internet anthropology studied online communities as potentially utopian spaces of disembodied identity play (Turkle's 'Life on the Screen', 1995) where users could experiment with multiple selves. Ethnographic research in virtual worlds (Boellstorff's study of Second Life, 2008) demonstrated that virtual social life is deeply meaningful and culturally specific — not 'merely' virtual. Boellstorff argued that methods developed for face-to-face fieldwork translate productively to virtual contexts.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Daniel Miller and colleagues' global comparative study of social media ('How the World Changed Social Media', 2016) found that far from homogenizing culture, social media platforms are locally appropriated in strikingly different ways — Facebook functions differently in Trinidad, rural China, Brazil, and rural India. Social media platforms are vernacular digital cultures, not universal communication technologies. This challenged technological determinism by showing the primacy of local social contexts.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Contemporary digital anthropology examines platform algorithms and their role in shaping visibility, political polarization, and information access; the material infrastructure of the internet (submarine cables, data centers, rare minerals); digital labor (content moderation, gig work, micro-tasking); surveillance capitalism; digital kinship and intimacy; and the anthropology of artificial intelligence. Ethical challenges include platform gatekeeping of research data, the harm of data exposure, and the politics of digital colonialism.

## Key Terminology
- **Digital Ethnography**: Ethnographic methods applied to online communities and digital practices
- **Virtual World**: An online interactive environment with persistent social communities and economies
- **Platform Studies**: Analysis of how social media platforms shape communication, culture, and power
- **Digital Labor**: Work performed by users or platform workers that generates value for digital platforms
- **Technological Determinism**: The view that technology causes social change independently of social forces
- **Data Colonialism**: The extraction of data from Global South users by Western platform corporations
