# Structural Anthropology

## Overview
Claude Lévi-Strauss's structural anthropology applied Saussurean linguistics to culture, arguing that the underlying structures of myth, kinship, cuisine, and totemic classification reveal universal binary oppositions through which the human mind organizes social reality.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Drawing on Jakobson's phonology, Lévi-Strauss argued that meaning is relational and oppositional: cultural units acquire meaning through contrast, just as phonemes do. His four-volume 'Mythologiques' (1964–71) analyzed hundreds of Native American myths through a 'mytheme' approach, identifying binary codes (raw/cooked, fresh/rotten, nature/culture) that run through diverse mythic systems, expressing at their core the universal problem of the relationship between nature and culture.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Lévi-Strauss's totemism analysis argued that totems are good 'to think with' — they classify natural species in ways that model human social distinctions. The 'savage mind' operates by the same logical principles as modern scientific thought, contradicting evolutionary hierarchies of primitive versus civilized rationality. His kinship analysis (The Elementary Structures of Kinship, 1949) modeled prescriptive marriage systems as logical structures enabling cross-cousin exchange.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Structuralism was enormously influential but attracted major critiques: it neglects historical change, agency, and lived experience; treats culture as a closed system of signs detached from practice; and imposes Eurocentric binary logic on diverse cultures. Poststructuralism (Derrida, Foucault) challenged its stable binaries and logocentrism. Despite these critiques, structural analysis remains a productive method in mythology, ritual, and cognitive anthropology.

## Key Terminology
- **Binary Oppositions**: Lévi-Strauss's concept of paired contrasts (nature/culture, raw/cooked) structuring cultural thought
- **Mytheme**: Lévi-Strauss's term for the minimal constituent units of myth analogous to phonemes
- **Totemism**: The symbolic use of natural species to classify and distinguish human social groups
- **Elementary Structures**: Lévi-Strauss's model of prescriptive kinship systems based on logical exchange rules
- **Savage Mind**: Lévi-Strauss's argument that non-Western thought is logically equivalent to scientific rationality
- **Bricolage**: Lévi-Strauss's concept of constructing meaning from whatever materials are at hand
