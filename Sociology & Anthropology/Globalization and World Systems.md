# Globalization and World Systems

## Overview
Immanuel Wallerstein's world-systems theory analyzes the capitalist world economy as a single social system spanning core, semi-peripheral, and peripheral zones, in which surplus value is transferred from periphery to core through unequal exchange, maintaining global inequality since the 16th century.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The 'core' (Western Europe, North America, Japan) specializes in high-wage, capital-intensive production; the 'periphery' (Global South) provides raw materials and cheap labor under conditions of colonial and neocolonial extraction. The 'semi-periphery' (Brazil, India, China) performs intermediate roles, serving as a buffer zone that stabilizes the system. Dependent development theorists (Cardoso, Frank, Amin) argued that integration into world capitalism produced 'development of underdevelopment' — structural dependence inhibiting autonomous industrialization.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Contemporary globalization studies examine the acceleration of cross-border flows of capital, goods, information, people, and culture since the 1970s. Hyperglobalists argue a genuinely borderless world economy is emerging; skeptics note the continued centrality of nation-states and regional blocs; transformationalists see qualitative shifts in the relationship between states, markets, and civil society without converging on a single model.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Critics of world-systems theory argue it underestimates agency in peripheral zones, cannot adequately theorize cultural globalization, and relies on a functionalist logic of system reproduction. Nevertheless, it provides crucial tools for analyzing global commodity chains, labor arbitrage, offshore finance, and the structural dynamics of international development.

## Key Terminology
- **World-Systems Theory**: Wallerstein's model of the capitalist world economy as a single integrated system
- **Core**: Wallerstein's term for dominant zones specializing in high-profit capital-intensive production
- **Periphery**: Zones specializing in low-wage, labor-intensive production under conditions of dependency
- **Semi-Periphery**: Intermediate zones buffering conflict between core and periphery in world-system
- **Unequal Exchange**: Transfer of value from low-wage peripheral economies to high-wage core economies
- **Dependency Theory**: The argument that Global South underdevelopment is structurally produced by integration into world capitalism
