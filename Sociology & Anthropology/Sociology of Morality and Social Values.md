# Sociology of Morality and Social Values

## Overview
The sociology of morality investigates how moral orders, ethical norms, and social values are created, institutionalized, contested, and enforced in human societies, exploring morality as a collective social reality rather than individual philosophical conscience.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Émile Durkheim established that morality is a quintessential social fact: without shared moral rules that constrain individual egoism, society descends into anomie and chaos. For Durkheim, society itself is the source of all moral authority. Max Weber enriched this by analyzing 'value-spheres' (religion, science, politics, economics, art) in modern differentiated societies, showing that each sphere operates with its own distinct, often irreconcilable moral logic.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Stanley Cohen's concept of the 'moral panic' (1972) analyzed how societies periodically experience surges of collective anxiety regarding perceived threats to core social values. Media sensationalism and moral entrepreneurs target specific marginal groups ('folk devils' — e.g., mods and rockers, video gamers, migrant youth), amplifying fear, generating disproportionate legislative crackdowns, and reinforcing established social boundaries.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Contemporary scholars (Hitlin, Vaisey, Lamont) examine everyday moral boundary-drawing: how ordinary citizens evaluate themselves and others as worthy or unworthy based on criteria such as work ethic, kindness, religious devotion, or lifestyle purity. Cross-national value surveys trace global shifts along axes of survival versus self-expression values and traditional versus secular-rational authority, demonstrating how economic security reshapes the moral foundations of civilizations.

## Key Terminology
- **Social Fact**: Durkheim's concept of social forces, norms, and moral rules external to and coercive over the individual
- **Moral Panic**: Cohen's concept of a disproportionate, media-fueled societal outcry over a perceived cultural threat
- **Folk Devil**: A demonized group or subculture portrayed in moral panics as the embodiment of social evil
- **Value-Spheres**: Weber's concept of autonomous domains of modern life (politics, economy, art) guided by conflicting values
- **Moral Boundary-Drawing**: Lamont's concept of defining social inclusion and exclusion through moral character assessments
- **Survival vs. Self-Expression**: Inglehart's cultural dimension tracking the shift from economic security concerns to personal autonomy and rights
