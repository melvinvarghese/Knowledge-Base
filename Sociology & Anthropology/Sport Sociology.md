# Sport Sociology

## Overview
The sociology of sport examines sport as a social institution that reflects and reproduces inequalities of class, race, gender, and nation, analyzes the functions and meanings of athletic competition in modern societies, and traces how commercialization has transformed sport.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Functionalist approaches viewed sport as socializing participants into values of teamwork, discipline, achievement, and rule-following — an institution producing social integration and national identity. Conflict perspectives showed sport as a site of ideological reproduction: reproducing racial hierarchies (stacking of Black athletes in physically demanding but non-leadership positions), class-based access to 'gentleman' sports (golf, tennis, equestrian), and gender hierarchies (women's systematic exclusion from prestige sports and media coverage).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Sport and race: Jackie Robinson's MLB integration, Jesse Owens at the 1936 Berlin Olympics, Muhammad Ali's political resistance, and Colin Kaepernick's kneeling protest reveal sport as a contested political arena in which Black athletes both challenge and are contained by racial systems. The 'stacking' hypothesis documented how Black athletes were systematically placed in peripheral positions, while racial ideologies attributed Black athletic success to natural physical ability rather than intelligence or dedication.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Commercial sport has undergone profound transformation: global media rights, mega-events (World Cup, Olympics), athlete branding, fantasy leagues, and eSports have extended sport into entertainment, finance, and global culture industries. Title IX (1972) mandated gender equity in US school sports, transforming women's athletics. The sociology of sport examines doping as a reflection of achievement culture, sports fandom as identity and community, the Olympic movement as geopolitics, and the displacement costs of mega-event hosting.

## Key Terminology
- **Stacking**: The concentration of racial minority athletes in peripheral positions in team sports
- **Commercialization of Sport**: The transformation of athletic competition into entertainment commodities for media and sponsorship markets
- **Hegemony in Sport**: The reproduction of dominant ideologies through sport's everyday practices and representations
- **Title IX**: US legislation mandating gender equity in educational programs including athletics
- **Sports Fandom**: The social practices, identities, and communities formed around support for sports teams
- **Mega-Event**: Large-scale international sporting events (Olympics, World Cup) with significant political and economic dimensions
