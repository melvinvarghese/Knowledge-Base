# Environmental Anthropology

## Overview
Environmental anthropology examines relationships between human societies and their natural environments, analyzing how cultures construct, use, manage, and represent nature, and how environmental change and resource conflicts intersect with social inequality and cultural practice.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Julian Steward's cultural ecology argued that the natural environment shapes the 'cultural core' — subsistence technology and economic arrangements — through adaptive pressures. Marvin Harris's cultural materialism extended this to explain seemingly irrational practices (sacred cows in India, food taboos) as environmental and economic adaptations. Ethnoecology and ethnobiology document how indigenous and local communities classify, name, and manage biodiversity through traditional ecological knowledge, often with greater nuance than Western scientific taxonomy.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Political ecology emerged as a critique of apolitical ecological anthropology, arguing that environmental degradation is produced by political-economic power relations — not population growth or cultural backwardness — and that resource conflicts are fundamentally about inequality, land rights, and colonial extraction. Research examines deforestation, water privatization, climate displacement, and the dispossession of indigenous peoples from their territories by conservation projects and agribusiness.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Contemporary environmental anthropology addresses climate change as a cultural and political crisis: how diverse communities experience, interpret, and respond to changing climates; how indigenous climate knowledge is ignored by global governance frameworks; and how climate injustice distributes burdens disproportionately on the global poor and Indigenous peoples. Multispecies ethnography expands the field to analyze human relations with animals, plants, fungi, and microbes as social relationships.

## Key Terminology
- **Cultural Ecology**: Steward's approach analyzing how environments shape cultural practices and social organization
- **Political Ecology**: Analysis of environmental problems as produced by political-economic power relations
- **Traditional Ecological Knowledge**: Indigenous and local communities' accumulated knowledge of their environments and ecosystems
- **Multispecies Ethnography**: Ethnographic approaches examining relationships between humans and other species
- **Climate Justice**: The movement addressing disproportionate climate burdens on marginalized and Indigenous communities
- **Ethnoecology**: The study of how cultural groups perceive, classify, and manage their natural environments
