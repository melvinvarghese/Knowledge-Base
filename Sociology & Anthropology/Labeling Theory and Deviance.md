# Labeling Theory and Deviance

## Overview
Labeling theory, associated with Howard Becker, Edwin Lemert, and Frank Tannenbaum, argues that deviance is not an inherent quality of an act but is socially defined through reactions of audiences and official control agents, whose labels shape deviant careers and identities.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Becker's 'Outsiders' (1963) argued that deviance is 'behavior that people so label'. Moral entrepreneurs create rules; rule enforcement selectively targets some groups (poor, minority, young) over others. The person labeled deviant becomes an 'outsider', and the label reorganizes their social identity and opportunities. Lemert distinguished primary deviance (initial rule-breaking) from secondary deviance (rule-breaking in response to being labeled), showing how labels can entrench deviant careers.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Labeling theory revealed how the criminal justice system amplifies rather than simply responds to deviance — arrest, conviction, and incarceration damage employment prospects, family ties, and social networks, pushing individuals further into deviant pathways. This insight underpins debates over diversion programs, decriminalization, restorative justice, and stigma reduction in mental health policy.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Critics charge labeling theory neglects why primary deviance occurs, overestimates the power of labels (not everyone labeled deviant internalizes the identity), and romanticizes deviance while downplaying victims. Nevertheless, labeling remains essential to criminal justice reform, mental health advocacy, and analysis of how racial disparities in law enforcement are produced and reproduced.

## Key Terminology
- **Labeling Theory**: The perspective that deviance is created through social reactions and the application of labels
- **Primary Deviance**: Lemert's term for initial rule-breaking that has not yet triggered a deviant self-concept
- **Secondary Deviance**: Deviant behavior arising in response to being labeled and stigmatized by others
- **Moral Entrepreneur**: Becker's term for individuals or groups who crusade to create and enforce social rules
- **Deviant Career**: The process by which labeled individuals move progressively deeper into deviant identities
- **Social Control**: Mechanisms — formal and informal — that regulate and sanction rule-violating behavior
