# Conflict Theory

## Overview
Conflict theory, rooted in Marxist thought and developed by Weber, Coser, and Dahrendorf, holds that society is characterized by competition for scarce resources, and that social order is maintained through domination and coercion rather than consensus and integration.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Marx analyzed capitalist society as structured by the antagonism between bourgeoisie (owners of means of production) and proletariat (wage laborers who sell their labor). The superstructure — law, ideology, religion, education — functions to legitimate and reproduce class domination. Historical change is driven by class conflict: as contradictions within modes of production intensify, subordinate classes challenge ruling class power, potentially producing revolutionary transformation.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Weber added dimensions of status and power, while Dahrendorf reformulated conflict around authority relations rather than property ownership. C. Wright Mills identified a 'power elite' — interlocking corporate, military, and political directorates — as structurally shaping policy and suppressing democratic participation. Contemporary conflict theory encompasses feminist, postcolonial, and critical race perspectives, analyzing gender, race, and empire as dimensions of structured inequality and contestation.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Conflict theory has been criticized for overemphasizing antagonism and underestimating consensus, cooperation, and social solidarity. Critics also charge that early versions were economically reductive, ignoring cultural and symbolic dimensions of power. Despite these critiques, conflict theory remains essential for analyzing persistent inequalities, elite power, social movements, and systemic discrimination.

## Key Terminology
- **Bourgeoisie**: In Marxist theory, the class that owns the means of production and employs labor
- **Proletariat**: The working class that sells its labor power in exchange for wages
- **Superstructure**: Marx's term for ideology, law, and culture that reflect and reproduce economic base
- **Power Elite**: Mills' concept of interlocking institutional elites dominating political and economic life
- **Class Struggle**: The ongoing conflict of interest between capitalist and working classes
- **False Consciousness**: Marx's concept of workers adopting ruling-class ideology that obscures their exploitation
