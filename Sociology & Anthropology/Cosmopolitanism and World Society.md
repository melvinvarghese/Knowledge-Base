# Cosmopolitanism and World Society

## Overview
Cosmopolitanism refers to the political, moral, and cultural orientation toward a global rather than national or local community — the idea that all human beings are citizens of the world, bearing mutual obligations that transcend political borders, and that diversity is a resource for human flourishing.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Philosophical cosmopolitanism (Kant's 'Perpetual Peace') argued that universal hospitality and federation of republican states could overcome war and establish lasting peace. Contemporary normative cosmopolitanism (Pogge, Nussbaum) argues for global justice obligations: affluent individuals and states bear duties to address poverty, disease, and rights violations globally, not only within their own political communities. Nussbaum's capabilities approach grounds cosmopolitan justice in a universal list of central human capabilities all persons are owed.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Sociological cosmopolitanism (Beck, Delanty) examines whether genuine cosmopolitan identities, norms, and institutions are emerging empirically. Beck's 'cosmopolitan sociology' argued that global risks (climate change, terrorism, pandemics) make national methodologies obsolete — a 'cosmopolitan social science' must track transnational processes. Meyer's 'world society' theory documents the global spread of isomorphic institutions — human rights norms, educational credentials, environmental standards, gender equality law — through international organizations and civil society, suggesting a genuinely global cultural framework is emerging.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Postcolonial critics challenge Eurocentric cosmopolitanism: its universalist language often expresses particular Western liberal values; it ignores how global capitalism generates the inequalities that motivate cosmopolitan concern; and it reproduces colonial hierarchies between cosmopolitan global citizens (mobile, valued) and national subjects (contained, controlled). Subaltern cosmopolitanisms (Santos) emerge from below — from Global South social movements articulating alternative visions of global solidarity.

## Key Terminology
- **Cosmopolitanism**: The moral and political orientation toward global community and universal human obligations
- **World Society**: Meyer's concept of a global cultural framework diffusing universal institutional forms
- **Perpetual Peace**: Kant's philosophical program for international federation eliminating war between states
- **Capabilities Approach**: Sen and Nussbaum's framework grounding cosmopolitan justice in universal human capabilities
- **Global Justice**: The demand for equitable distribution of resources and opportunities across all human beings globally
- **Subaltern Cosmopolitanism**: Santos's concept of cosmopolitan visions emerging from Global South social movements and struggles
