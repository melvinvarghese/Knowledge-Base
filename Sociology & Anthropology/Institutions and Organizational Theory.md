# Institutions and Organizational Theory

## Overview
Social institutions are enduring complexes of values, norms, roles, and organizations that organize social life around fundamental human needs (family, economy, polity, religion, education), while organizational theory examines how formal organizations emerge, persist, and change within institutional environments.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Weber's analysis of bureaucracy identified it as the most technically efficient form of organization: written rules, hierarchical authority, fixed roles, merit-based recruitment, and separation of office from person. Bureaucracy was both necessary for modern administration and threatening — the 'iron cage' of formal rationality displacing human meaning. Contemporary organizational sociology distinguishes between formal structure (official rules) and informal structure (actual practices), showing persistent gaps.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Institutional theory (Meyer, Rowan, DiMaggio, Powell) argues organizations adopt structures not primarily for technical efficiency but for legitimacy in their institutional environment. Organizations mimic successful peers (mimetic isomorphism), comply with professional norms (normative isomorphism), and respond to regulatory requirements (coercive isomorphism), producing 'institutional isomorphism' — convergence of organizational forms across fields regardless of technical necessity.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
New institutionalism examines how 'institutional logics' — the organizing principles of fields (market logic, professional logic, religious logic) — shape organizational practices and identities. Organizations in multi-logic environments (hospital: professional, market, state logics) navigate tensions between competing rationalities. Organizational ecology analyzes population dynamics of organizational forms — founding, mortality, and density dependence — using evolutionary metaphors.

## Key Terminology
- **Bureaucracy**: Weber's model of rational organizational administration through fixed rules and hierarchical roles
- **Institutional Isomorphism**: DiMaggio and Powell's concept of convergence of organizational forms due to field pressures
- **Institutional Logic**: The organizing principles and practices of a social field guiding actors' decisions
- **Mimetic Isomorphism**: Organizational copying of successful peer structures under conditions of uncertainty
- **Organizational Field**: DiMaggio and Powell's term for the totality of organizations constituting a recognized area of life
- **Loose Coupling**: Weick's concept of weakly interconnected organizational subunits preserving flexibility
