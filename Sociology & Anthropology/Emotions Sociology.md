# Emotions Sociology

## Overview
The sociology of emotions examines how feelings are socially structured, culturally patterned, and interactionally managed, arguing that emotions are not purely biological states but are shaped by social norms, power relations, and institutional contexts.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Arlie Hochschild's 'The Managed Heart' (1983) introduced the concept of emotional labor — the management of one's emotions as part of paid work — through her study of flight attendants (required to maintain friendly warmth) and bill collectors (required to project intimidating sternness). Emotional labor is governed by 'feeling rules' that specify what emotions are appropriate in a given role. When emotional performance diverges from inner feeling, workers engage in surface acting (displaying required emotion without feeling it) or deep acting (working to actually feel it).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Randall Collins's interaction ritual chain theory argues that emotions are the emotional 'glue' of social life: face-to-face interactions generate emotional energy — feelings of confidence, enthusiasm, solidarity — or depleted emotional energy through failed rituals. Successful ritual interactions produce collective effervescence and solidarity symbols; individuals seek out interaction rituals that maximize their emotional energy. This micro-sociological framework connects to Durkheim's macro-sociology of collective rituals.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Cultural sociology of emotions examines how feeling rules vary across cultures and historical periods: Victorian emotional restraint, the 'happiness imperative' in contemporary consumer culture, honor cultures mandating anger in response to insult. The sociology of grief, moral emotions (shame, guilt, moral outrage), political emotions (fear, hope, anger in social movements), and digital emotions (online outrage, parasocial connection) are active research areas. Feminist sociology critiques how emotional labor burdens are disproportionately assigned to women.

## Key Terminology
- **Emotional Labor**: Hochschild's concept of managing emotions as part of paid work according to occupational feeling rules
- **Feeling Rules**: Cultural norms governing what emotions are appropriate to feel and display in social contexts
- **Surface Acting**: Displaying required emotions without genuinely feeling them
- **Emotional Energy**: Collins's concept of the heightened enthusiasm and solidarity generated by successful interaction rituals
- **Interaction Ritual Chain**: Collins's theory of emotional energy generated and depleted through sequences of social encounters
- **Affect**: Bodily, pre-conscious feeling states that shape social life and are analytically distinct from named emotions
