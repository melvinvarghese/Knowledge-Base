# Visual Anthropology

## Overview
Visual anthropology examines the role of visual media — photography, film, video, drawing, digital imagery — in anthropological research and representation, and analyzes the visual dimensions of cultural life: art, ceremony, material culture, and the gaze.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Robert Flaherty's 'Nanook of the North' (1922) is often cited as the first ethnographic film, though its staged authenticity raised immediate questions about representation and the ethics of filming 'others'. Jean Rouch developed 'cine-ethnography' in West Africa, collaborating with subjects and experimenting with 'shared anthropology'. The 'writing culture' debates of the 1980s extended to visual media: films and photographs are not transparent windows on reality but constructed representations shaped by the filmmaker's cultural position, technical choices, and narrative conventions.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Collier's photo-elicitation method uses photographs as interview stimuli, prompting community members to interpret and contextualize images in ways that generate culturally specific understandings. Participatory video and community filmmaking give research subjects cameras to document their own lives, challenging the anthropologist's monopoly on representation. The archive of colonial photography — the posed, labeled 'native' portrait — reveals the visual politics of power, knowledge, and objectification in anthropological history.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Contemporary visual anthropology examines social media photography, selfie cultures, digital surveillance, GIS mapping of indigenous territories, drone imagery, and satellite visualization of environmental change. It also analyzes how images circulate across global media, how indigenous communities reclaim visual representation, and the ethics of sharing and publishing images of vulnerable communities.

## Key Terminology
- **Ethnographic Film**: Film used to document and represent cultural practices and social life for anthropological purposes
- **Photo-Elicitation**: An interview technique using photographs to prompt culturally specific responses from subjects
- **Participatory Video**: A methodology giving cameras to community members to document their own lives and perspectives
- **Colonial Gaze**: The power-laden visual practices through which colonizers objectified and classified colonized peoples
- **Shared Anthropology**: Rouch's concept of collaborative filmmaking that shares the representational process with subjects
- **Visual Culture**: The study of visual practices, images, and their meanings within specific cultural contexts
