# Functionalism and Social Order

## Overview
Functionalism is a macro-sociological perspective that analyzes social institutions and practices in terms of their contributions to social stability, integration, and the maintenance of the system as a whole.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Emile Durkheim laid foundations by studying how collective rituals, education, and religion bind society together through shared consciousness. Talcott Parsons systematized functionalism into the AGIL schema: Adaptation (economic subsystem), Goal attainment (political), Integration (legal/community), Latency or pattern maintenance (family/cultural). Each social subsystem performs a necessary function; failures produce dysfunction. Robert Merton distinguished manifest functions (intended) from latent functions (unintended), and added the concept of dysfunction.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Functionalism provided tools to analyze education as social sorting, religion as cohesive force, and the family as the site of primary socialization. Its strength lies in illuminating social interdependence and the unexpected consequences of social arrangements. However, critics — particularly conflict theorists and feminists — argued functionalism is inherently conservative, naturalizing existing power arrangements and viewing social conflict as pathological rather than transformative.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Neo-functionalism (Alexander, Luhmann's systems theory) modernized the approach, while Luhmann's autopoietic systems theory argued each functional subsystem (law, economy, politics, science) operates on its own self-referential code, radically decentering the subject. Despite critiques, functionalist concepts remain influential in comparative sociology, organizational theory, and social systems analysis.

## Key Terminology
- **AGIL Schema**: Parsons' four-function framework: Adaptation, Goal attainment, Integration, Latency
- **Manifest Function**: Merton's term for intended and recognized consequences of a social practice
- **Latent Function**: Unintended or unrecognized consequences of a social practice
- **Dysfunction**: An element of social structure that disrupts stability or system maintenance
- **Social System**: Parsons' view of society as an integrated system of interrelated subsystems
- **Collective Consciousness**: Durkheim's concept of shared beliefs and sentiments that bind members of a society
