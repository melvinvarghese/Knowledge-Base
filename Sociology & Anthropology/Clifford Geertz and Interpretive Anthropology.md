# Clifford Geertz and Interpretive Anthropology

## Overview
Clifford Geertz's interpretive anthropology reconceived culture as a 'web of significance' that humans themselves have spun, and anthropology as the interpretive science of reading those webs — producing 'thick descriptions' that illuminate the meanings embedded in social action.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Geertz distinguished 'thinly' from 'thickly' described behavior: a wink and an eye twitch involve identical muscular movement but entirely different meanings, and to understand human action requires interpreting its layered cultural significance rather than observing its physical form. His analysis of the Balinese cockfight ('The Interpretation of Cultures', 1973) showed how the cockfight is a 'story Balinese tell themselves about themselves' — encoding local concepts of fate, status, masculinity, and the relationship between men and animals.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Geertz's semiotic approach drew on Gilbert Ryle's philosophy and Ricoeur's hermeneutics, treating culture as a text to be read. This enabled analysis of how religious rituals, national symbols, political spectacles, and everyday practices encode and communicate cultural meanings. His concept of 'local knowledge' insisted that anthropological understanding must be context-sensitive, resisting universal generalization in favor of particularistic insight.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Geertz's interpretivism was enormously influential but attracted critiques: it focuses on shared meaning while neglecting power, conflict, and inequality; it may project coherence onto contested cultural practices; and the hermeneutic circle (interpreting parts by the whole and whole by parts) raises methodological concerns. Nevertheless, interpretive approaches shaped much of medical anthropology, organizational culture studies, and qualitative sociology.

## Key Terminology
- **Interpretive Anthropology**: Geertz's approach treating culture as systems of meaning to be read and interpreted
- **Culture as Text**: Geertz's metaphor treating social action as a text to be interpreted by the analyst
- **Local Knowledge**: Geertz's insistence that anthropological understanding must be contextual and particular
- **Semiotic Approach**: The analysis of culture as a system of signs and symbols
- **Hermeneutics**: The theory and method of interpretation, especially of texts and social action
- **Cockfight Analysis**: Geertz's famous ethnographic essay reading Balinese cockfighting as cultural text
