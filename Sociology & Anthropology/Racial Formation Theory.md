# Racial Formation Theory

## Overview
Racial formation theory, developed by Michael Omi and Howard Winant, argues that race is a sociohistorical concept whose meanings are contested through ongoing political struggles, and that racial categories are produced and transformed through 'racial projects' linking representational practices to social structures.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Omi and Winant reject both biological determinism (race as natural fact) and racial essentialism (race as fixed cultural identity), as well as class reductionism (race as epiphenomenon of economic position). Race is real — it has material consequences for life chances, embodied experience, and social position — but it is not biological. Racial categories are produced historically, vary across societies and periods, and are continually contested in political, cultural, and institutional domains.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
A 'racial project' simultaneously interprets racial dynamics and proposes institutional arrangements. Racist projects naturalize white supremacy; anti-racist projects challenge these arrangements. The state is a key terrain of racial formation: immigration law, voting rights, census categories, and welfare policy are all racial projects. Racial hegemony is maintained not only by overt discrimination but by colorblind ideology — the denial of race's continuing relevance — which legitimates existing inequalities while precluding recognition of their racial dimensions.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Critical race scholars have extended racial formation to analyze the racial state (Goldberg), settler colonialism (Wolfe), racial capitalism (Robinson), and global dimensions of racialization. Contemporary debates examine anti-Black racism, settler colonialism, colorism, Islamophobia, and the racialization of immigration, revealing race as a continuously evolving system of categorization and hierarchy.

## Key Terminology
- **Racial Formation**: The sociohistorical process by which racial categories are created, contested, and transformed
- **Racial Project**: An effort to reorganize and redistribute resources along racial lines while interpreting racial meanings
- **Racialization**: The process by which previously unracialized categories become marked as racial
- **Colorblind Ideology**: The belief that racism is overcome by ignoring race, which legitimates existing racial inequalities
- **Racial Capitalism**: Robinson's concept of capitalism's historical dependence on racial hierarchy and exploitation
- **Whiteness**: A racial identity category examined as a socially constructed, unmarked, and privileged norm
