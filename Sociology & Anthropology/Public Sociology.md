# Public Sociology

## Overview
Public sociology, advocated by Michael Burawoy in his 2004 ASA presidential address, calls for sociologists to engage with broader publics — communities, social movements, media, policymakers — to make sociological knowledge relevant to public debates and social justice, beyond academic audiences.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Burawoy distinguished four types of sociology: professional (scientific research for academic audiences), critical (reflexive examination of professional sociology's assumptions), policy sociology (research commissioned by clients to solve defined problems), and public sociology (research engaging multiple publics in dialogue). Public sociology comes in organic (direct, sustained engagement with specific publics — communities, unions, movements) and traditional (writings and media interventions reaching general audiences) forms.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The call for public sociology reflects concerns that sociology has become too technical, insular, and irrelevant to pressing social problems — inequality, racism, climate change, democracy — while social scientists outside sociology (economists, psychologists) command more public and policy influence. Engaged scholarship, participatory action research, community-based participatory research, and 'scholar-activism' represent different models of integrating research with social engagement.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Critics argue public sociology risks compromising scientific rigor, that the distinction between sociological knowledge and political advocacy is blurred, and that the demand for 'relevance' may favor politically safe or fundable research over theoretically important work. Feminist and postcolonial public sociologists challenge Burawoy's framework for underweighting power dynamics in the relationship between sociologist and 'public', and for underemphasizing how race and gender shape whose sociology is published and whose public engagement is legitimated.

## Key Terminology
- **Public Sociology**: Burawoy's call for sociologists to engage non-academic publics in pursuit of social justice
- **Organic Public Sociology**: Direct, sustained engagement with specific communities or movements as research partners
- **Professional Sociology**: Scientific sociological research producing knowledge for academic disciplinary audiences
- **Participatory Action Research**: Research methodology in which communities are active partners in design, conduct, and application
- **Scholar-Activism**: The integration of academic research with political advocacy and social movement participation
- **Knowledge Translation**: The process of making research findings accessible and usable by non-specialist audiences
