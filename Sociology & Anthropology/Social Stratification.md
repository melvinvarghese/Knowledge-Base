# Social Stratification

## Overview
Social stratification refers to the hierarchical arrangement of individuals and groups in society based on dimensions such as economic class, social status, and political power, producing systematic inequalities in life outcomes.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Classical sociology distinguished three forms: economic class (relationship to means of production, per Marx), status groups (honor and prestige, per Weber), and party (organized political power). Modern stratification theory integrates race, gender, sexuality, and nationality as intersecting axes of hierarchy. Stratification systems range from relatively rigid castes (India's historical varna system) to more fluid class systems in capitalist societies, though mobility within them is often overstated.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Stratification produces differential access to education, healthcare, housing, nutrition, legal protection, and cultural capital. Research consistently shows that where you are born in a stratification hierarchy predicts health, longevity, educational attainment, and likelihood of incarceration more reliably than individual effort. Sociologists debate the relative weight of structural factors versus individual agency ('structure vs. agency') in reproducing inequality across generations.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Functionalist accounts (Davis and Moore) argued stratification rewards talent and effort, ensuring important roles are filled by qualified people. Critics counter that this naturalizes privilege and ignores how inherited advantages, discrimination, and unequal opportunity undermine any meritocratic ideal. Contemporary debates focus on wealth inequality (Piketty), intersecting hierarchies, and global dimensions of stratification.

## Key Terminology
- **Social Class**: A group sharing similar economic position, life chances, and relationship to productive resources
- **Status Group**: Weber's term for a community sharing social honor, lifestyle, and prestige
- **Caste System**: A rigid, hereditary stratification system where social position is ascribed at birth
- **Social Mobility**: Movement of individuals or groups between positions in the stratification hierarchy
- **Life Chances**: Weber's concept of the opportunities a person has to improve their quality of life
- **Meritocracy**: An ideology claiming that social position reflects individual talent and effort
