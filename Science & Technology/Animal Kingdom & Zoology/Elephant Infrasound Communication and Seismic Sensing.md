# Elephant Infrasound Communication and Seismic Sensing

## Overview
African Elephants (*Loxodonta africana*) coordinate herds across tens of kilometers using high-power, low-frequency **infrasonic rumbles** (14–24 Hz) that propagate through the air and as Rayleigh seismic ground waves.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Morphological Principles
Laryngeal Infrasound Physics: Giant vocal folds vibrate at fundamental frequencies below human hearing (<20 Hz); low-frequency sound experiences minimal atmospheric absorption, traveling up to 10 km.

---

## 2. Physiological Dynamics & Evolutionary Analysis
Seismic Bone Conduction: Ground vibrations generated by rumbles are detected by mechanoreceptors (Pacinian corpuscles) in elephant foot pads and trunk tips, conducted up leg bones directly to the middle ear.

---

## 3. Ecological Significance & Scientific Applications
Long-distance animal census acoustic monitoring, early tsunami and earthquake natural sensing studies.

---

## Key Terms
- **Infrasound**: Sound waves propagating at frequencies below the human lower audible limit of 20 Hz.
- **Pacinian Corpuscle**: Pressure-sensitive mechanoreceptor ending in skin detecting high-frequency vibrational cues.
- **Rayleigh Wave**: Acoustic surface wave traveling along the boundary of an elastic solid such as the ground.
