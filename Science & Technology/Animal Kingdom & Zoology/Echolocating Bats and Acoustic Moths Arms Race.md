# Echolocating Bats and Acoustic Moths Arms Race

## Overview
The coevolutionary acoustic arms race between echolocating insectivorous bats and nocturnal tiger moths (Erebidae) showcases mutual evolutionary adaptations in ultrasonic biosonar and acoustic jamming.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Morphological Principles
Bat Laryngeal Biosonar: Microchiropteran bats emit ultrasonic FM and CF sweeps (20–120 kHz), processing returning echoes to calculate insect distance, velocity (Doppler shift), and wing flutter frequency.

---

## 2. Physiological Dynamics & Evolutionary Analysis
Moth Tympanic Organs & Acoustic Jamming: Moths evolved thoracic tympanal ears that detect approaching bat pulses, executing evasive spiral dives, while tiger moths click timbal organs at 4,500 Hz to actively jam bat biosonar.

---

## 3. Ecological Significance & Scientific Applications
Military radar-jamming electronic countermeasures, bio-inspired ultrasonic spatial obstacle avoidance sensors.

---

## Key Terms
- **Tympanal Organ**: Hearing membrane located on the thorax or abdomen of insects sensitive to ultrasonic frequencies.
- **Acoustic Jamming**: Active emission of sound pulses to disrupt or confuse a predator's echolocation guidance.
- **Frequency Modulation (FM)**: Acoustic sweep varying frequency continuously across a sound pulse to resolve target distance.
