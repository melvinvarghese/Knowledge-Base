# Greenland Shark Longevity and Cold Water Metabolism

## Overview
The Greenland Shark (*Somniosus microcephalus*) is the longest-lived vertebrate on Earth, attaining lifespans between 250 and 500 years in freezing Arctic abyssal depths.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Morphological Principles
Eye Lens Radiocarbon Dating: Structural crystalline proteins in the core of the ocular lens form during embryonic development and never turn over, allowing radiocarbon bomb-pulse calibration that proved lifespans exceeding 390 years.

---

## 2. Physiological Dynamics & Evolutionary Analysis
Cryo-Protective Metabolites: Flesh is saturated with high concentrations of trimethylamine N-oxide (TMAO) and urea, which act as natural antifreeze, stabilizing cellular proteins against cryogenic denaturation and extreme hydrostatic pressure.

---

## 3. Ecological Significance & Scientific Applications
Comparative aging biology, genetic proteomic stability, and preservation of biological tissues in deep cold.

---

## Key Terms
- **Trimethylamine N-Oxide (TMAO)**: Osmolyte counteracting the destabilizing effects of urea and low temperatures on proteins.
- **Ocular Crystalline**: Transparent structural proteins forming the refractive core of vertebrate eye lenses.
- **Metabolic Depression**: Sustained reduction of physiological basal metabolic rate in response to extreme cold.
