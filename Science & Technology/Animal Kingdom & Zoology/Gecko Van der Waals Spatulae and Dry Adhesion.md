# Gecko Van der Waals Spatulae and Dry Adhesion

## Overview
Geckos (Gekkonidae) climb vertical glass and inverted ceilings using millions of microscopic foot hairs (setae) that generate powerful reversible dry adhesion via intermolecular **Van der Waals forces**.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Morphological Principles
Hierarchical Setae Architecture: Each foot pad contains ~500,000 keratin setae that split into hundreds of triangular nanoscale spatulae (200 nm wide), conformally contacting microscopic surface roughness.

---

## 2. Physiological Dynamics & Evolutionary Analysis
Directional Angle-Dependent Detachment: Adhesion engages when spatulae are dragged laterally at 30°; peeling the toe pad past 90° releases van der Waals contact instantaneously without sticky residues.

---

## 3. Ecological Significance & Scientific Applications
Synthetic dry adhesive tapes, robotic wall-climbing gecko grippers for space debris capture in vacuum.

---

## Key Terms
- **Seta**: Microscopic hair-like cuticular projection found on gecko toe pads.
- **Spatula**: Nanoscale terminal branch of a seta establishing direct intermolecular Van der Waals contact.
- **Dry Adhesion**: Adhesive binding achieved purely through physical contact forces without chemical glues or secretions.
