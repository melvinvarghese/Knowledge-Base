# Decorator Crab Masking and Epibiont Camouflage

## Overview
Decorator Crabs (Majoidea) practice morphological active camouflage by cutting living algae, sponges, and anemones and fastening them onto specialized hooking hairs (hooked setae) on their carapaces.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Morphological Principles
Velcro-Like Setae Mechanics: Setae covering the shell act as biological Velcro hooks, gripping cellular fibers of attached organisms; attached sponges and sea anemones continue growing, providing living camouflage.

---

## 2. Physiological Dynamics & Evolutionary Analysis
Chemical & Visual Crypsis: Attached toxic sponges and stinging anemones deter predators chemically and tactically, rendering the crab visually and olfactorily indistinguishable from surrounding reef substrates.

---

## 3. Ecological Significance & Scientific Applications
Bio-inspired velcro mechanical fasteners, chemical concealment strategies in marine ecology.

---

## Key Terms
- **Epibiont**: An organism that lives attached to the external surface of another living host organism.
- **Hooked Setae**: Curved cuticular hairs engineered to mechanically clasp foreign camouflage items.
- **Crypsis**: Ability of an animal to avoid observation or detection by other animals via visual or chemical blending.
