# Cheetah Biomechanics - Flexible Spine and Non-Retractile Claws

## Overview
The Cheetah (*Acinonyx jubatus*) is the fastest terrestrial mammal, capable of accelerating from 0 to 100 km/h in 3 seconds through a specialized flexible vertebral column, semi-retractile traction claws, and muscular tail stabilization.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Morphological Principles
Double-Suspension Gallop: Cheetahs achieve stride lengths exceeding 7 meters by alternating between extended flight (body stretched) and gathered flight (legs overlapping), powered by deep flexion of an elastic spine.

---

## 2. Physiological Dynamics & Evolutionary Analysis
Traction and Rudder Aerodynamics: Non-retractile blunt claws act like athletic running spikes; a flattened, muscular tail executes high-speed counter-torque rotations to prevent spinouts during 4g turns.

---

## 3. Ecological Significance & Scientific Applications
High-speed quadrupedal robotics (MIT Cheetah), athletic track footwear sprint-traction engineering.

---

## Key Terms
- **Double-Suspension Gallop**: High-speed quadrupedal gait characterized by two distinct phases of aerial flight per stride.
- **Non-Retractile Claws**: Claws permanently exposed to function as high-traction running spikes.
- **Counter-Torque**: Rotational force exerted in the opposite direction to stabilize vehicle or body orientation.
