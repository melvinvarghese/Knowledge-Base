# Coelacanth Living Fossil and Lobe-Finned Morphology

## Overview
The Coelacanth (*Latimeria*) is a lobe-finned fish (Sarcopterygii) once believed extinct for 66 million years until its dramatic rediscovery in 1938, providing critical anatomical insight into tetrapod terrestrial evolution.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Morphological Principles
Lobed Fin Skeletal Architecture: Pectoral and pelvic fins are supported by internal bony axes matching tetrapod limb bones (humerus, radius, ulna), moving in an alternating diagonal gait mirroring walking terrestrial animals.

---

## 2. Physiological Dynamics & Evolutionary Analysis
Intracranial Joint & Rostral Organ: Retains a functional intracranial cartilage hinge allowing the upper jaw to flex upward during feeding, and an electroreceptive gel-filled rostral organ in its snout.

---

## 3. Ecological Significance & Scientific Applications
Evolutionary transition from aquatic vertebrates to land tetrapods, deep-sea low-metabolism survival mechanisms.

---

## Key Terms
- **Sarcopterygii**: Class of lobe-finned bony fishes directly ancestral to all terrestrial tetrapods.
- **Living Fossil**: Extant species displaying anatomical features largely unchanged from deep geological fossil records.
- **Rostral Organ**: Specialized electroreceptive cavity located in the snout of coelacanths.
