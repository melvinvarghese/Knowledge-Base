# Eusociality in Hymenoptera - Kin Selection, Haplodiploidy, and Pheromone Trails

## Overview
Eusociality is the highest level of animal social organization, characterized by cooperative brood care, overlapping generations, and reproductive division of labor into fertile and sterile castes.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Hamilton's Rule & Kin Selection: W.D. Hamilton (1964) formulated the genetic basis of altruism: $r B > C$, where an altruistic gene spreads if the relatedness ($r$) multiplied by the recipient's reproductive benefit ($B$) exceeds the altruist's personal reproductive cost ($C$).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Haplodiploidy in Ants, Bees, and Wasps: In Hymenoptera, females develop from fertilized diploid eggs ($2n$), while males develop from unfertilized haploid eggs ($n$). Consequently, sisters share 75% of their genes with each other ($r = 0.75$), but only 50% with their own potential offspring ($r = 0.5$). This genetic asymmetry predisposes sterile female workers to raise their sisters rather than reproduce individually.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Superorganism stigmergy, chemical communication via volatile cuticular hydrocarbons and trail pheromones, collective hive intelligence.

## Key Terminology
- **Eusociality**: Complex social system featuring cooperative brood care, overlapping adult generations, and division of labor into castes.
- **Haplodiploidy**: Sex-determination system where males are haploid (unfertilized eggs) and females are diploid (fertilized eggs).
- **Hamilton's Rule**: Mathematical formula stating altruistic behavior is favored when relatedness times benefit exceeds personal cost ($rB > C$).
