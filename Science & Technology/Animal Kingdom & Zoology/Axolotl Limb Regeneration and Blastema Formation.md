# Axolotl Limb Regeneration and Blastema Formation

## Overview
The Axolotl (*Ambystoma mexicanum*) is a paedomorphic salamander exhibiting perpetual neoteny and the remarkable capacity to scarlessly regenerate complete limbs, heart tissue, spinal cord, and parts of its brain.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Morphological Principles
Blastema Formation & Epimorphic Regeneration: Following amputation, specialized wound epidermis covers the stump and signals dedifferentiation of mature cells into a proliferative mesenchymal limb bud (blastema).

---

## 2. Physiological Dynamics & Evolutionary Analysis
Wnt and FGF Signaling Cascades: Blastema cells retain lineage memory while reactivating embryonic developmental gene networks, faithfully reconstructing skeletal bones, muscle fibers, nerve bundles, and blood vessels.

---

## 3. Ecological Significance & Scientific Applications
Regenerative human medicine targets, scar-free wound healing therapeutics, and developmental genetics.

---

## Key Terms
- **Blastema**: Mass of undifferentiated, highly proliferative cells capable of regenerating an organ or appendicular structure.
- **Neoteny**: Retention of juvenile or larval traits into mature adult stages.
- **Epimorphosis**: Regeneration of a missing anatomical structure via cellular proliferation and blastema differentiation.
