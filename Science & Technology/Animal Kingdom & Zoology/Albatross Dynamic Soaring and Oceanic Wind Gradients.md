# Albatross Dynamic Soaring and Oceanic Wind Gradients

## Overview
Albatrosses (Diomedeidae) travel thousands of ocean kilometers without flapping their wings by practicing **dynamic soaring**, extracting mechanical energy from the vertical wind shear gradient above oceanic waves.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Morphological Principles
The Four-Phase Dynamic Soaring Cycle: 1. Windward climb converting horizontal airspeed into altitude; 2. Upper altitude turn in fast wind; 3. Leeward dive converting altitude into kinetic speed; 4. Sea-surface turn in slow boundary wind.

---

## 2. Physiological Dynamics & Evolutionary Analysis
Shoulder Tendon Lock (Shoulder Humeri): An anatomical skeletal tendon latch locks wings rigidly outstretched at high aspect ratios (wingspan up to 3.5 m) with zero muscular expenditure.

---

## 3. Ecological Significance & Scientific Applications
Autonomous uncrewed aerial vehicle (UAV) oceanic surveillance drones utilizing dynamic soaring algorithms.

---

## Key Terms
- **Dynamic Soaring**: Flight technique extracting energy from horizontal wind gradients to fly indefinitely without flapping.
- **Wind Gradient**: Vertical rate of change of horizontal wind speed above ocean wave surfaces.
- **Aspect Ratio**: Ratio of a wing's span to its mean aerodynamic chord line.
