# Echolocation in Cetaceans and Chiropterans - Acoustic Biosonar

## Overview
Biosonar (echolocation) is an acoustic sensory modality evolved independently in toothed whales (Odontoceti) and bats (Microchiroptera) through convergent evolutionary refinement.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Cetacean Ultrasound & The Melon: Toothed whales (dolphins, sperm whales) produce high-frequency ultrasonic clicks by forcing pressurized air through phonic lips (*monkey lips*) in the nasal passages. The acoustic sound beam is focused and impedance-matched to seawater by the lipid-rich acoustic lens known as the **melon**. Returning echo reflections are received by oil-filled cavities in the lower jawbone, transmitting acoustic vibrations to the middle ear.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Chiropteran Frequency Modulation & Doppler Shift Compensation: Bats emit ultrasonic vocal pulses through the larynx or nostrils, utilizing Frequency Modulated (FM) sweeps for precise target distance ranging and Constant Frequency (CF) pulses to detect insect wing-flutter velocities via Doppler acoustic shifts.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Convergent genetic evolution in prestin motor proteins, high-resolution 3D environmental mapping in zero visibility.

## Key Terminology
- **Melon**: Mass of adipose tissue found in the forehead of toothed whales that focuses acoustic echolocation sound clicks.
- **Phonic Lips**: Anatomical structures in cetacean nasal passages that vibrate to generate echolocating ultrasound pulses.
- **Doppler Shift Compensation**: Behavioral adjustment of emitted echolocation frequencies by bats to compensate for flight movement velocity shifts.
