# Dinosaur Growth and Histology - LAGs, Medullary Bone, and Endothermy

## Overview
Dinosaur Paleohistology slices fossilized limb bones into microscopic thin sections to decipher growth rates, physiological metabolism, and reproductive maturity.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Lines of Arrested Growth (LAGs) & Growth Curves: Bone histology reveals **Lines of Arrested Growth (LAGs)**, cyclical annual rings formed during seasonal winter metabolic slowdowns, akin to tree rings. Counting LAGs in *Tyrannosaurus rex* shows they reached full adult size (~9 metric tons) at age 20, undergoing a massive teenage growth spurt gaining 2 kg per day.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Fibrolamellar Bone & Medullary Bone: Dinosaurs deposited dense, highly vascularized **fibrolamellar bone tissue**, proving sustained fast bone growth rates matching modern birds and mammals rather than slow ectothermic reptiles (supporting dinosaur mesothermy/endothermy). Female theropods deposited calcium-rich **medullary bone** inside limb cavities as a labile calcium reservoir for eggshell production.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Resolving dinosaur ontogenetic lumping debates (e.g., *Nanotyrannus* proven to be juvenile *T. rex*), dinosaur sexual maturity determination.

## Key Terminology
- **Lines of Arrested Growth (LAGs)**: Annual growth rings in bone tissue used in paleontology to determine the chronological age and growth rate of extinct animals.
- **Fibrolamellar Bone**: Rapidly formed, highly vascularized bone tissue characteristic of fast-growing endothermic animals.
- **Medullary Bone**: Specialized temporary calcium-storage bone tissue found in female birds and dinosaurs during egg-laying cycles.
