# Homo floresiensis - Insular Dwarfism on Flores Island

## Overview
*Homo floresiensis* (nicknamed the 'Hobbit') was an endemic dwarf hominin discovered in Liang Bua cave on Flores, Indonesia, standing only 1.06 meters (3.5 feet) tall and surviving until ~50,000 years ago.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Morphological Principles
Insular Dwarfism (Foster's Rule): Isolated on Flores with limited resources and absent large terrestrial predators, large hominins underwent evolutionary reduction in body mass and brain volume (~417 cc) to lower metabolic caloric requirements.

---

## 2. Physiological Dynamics & Evolutionary Analysis
Primitive Mosaic Anatomy: Combined primitive *Homo erectus* or *Australopithecus*-like traits (long feet, chimplike wrists, lack of chin) with sophisticated toolmaking and coordinated hunting of dwarf stegodons.

---

## 3. Ecological Significance & Scientific Applications
Island biogeographic evolution in primates, hominin morphological plasticity and late Quaternary survival.

---

## Key Terms
- **Insular Dwarfism**: Reduction in body size of large animals over generations when isolated on resource-limited islands.
- **Foster's Rule**: Ecological rule stating island species evolve toward intermediate body sizes based on resource limits.
- **Endocranial Volume**: Measurement of the interior volume of the braincase reflecting brain size.
