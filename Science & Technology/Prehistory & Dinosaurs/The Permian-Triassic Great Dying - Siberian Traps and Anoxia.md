# The Permian-Triassic Great Dying - Siberian Traps and Anoxia

## Overview
The Permian-Triassic Extinction (~252 Ma), dubbed 'The Great Dying', was Earth's most severe extinction event, eradicating over **96% of marine species and 70% of terrestrial vertebrate families**.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Morphological Principles
The Siberian Traps Volcanism: Massive flood basalt eruptions engulfed over 2 million square kilometers of Siberia, venting trillions of tons of carbon dioxide and sulfur dioxide, vaporizing vast coal basins.

---

## 2. Physiological Dynamics & Evolutionary Analysis
Lethal Kill Mechanisms: Catastrophic runaway greenhouse warming (+10°C), global ocean acidification, marine anoxia (deep ocean deoxygenation), and toxic hydrogen sulfide ($H_2S$) oceanic burps that depleted the protective stratospheric ozone layer.

---

## 3. Ecological Significance & Scientific Applications
Paleoclimatic tipping point modeling, understanding modern ocean deoxygenation and climate warming risks.

---

## Key Terms
- **Siberian Traps**: Massive flood basalt geological formation resulting from explosive Permian volcanism.
- **Ocean Anoxia**: Severe depletion of dissolved oxygen in seawater, suffocating marine aerobic life.
- **The Great Dying**: Informal name for the catastrophic Permian-Triassic mass extinction event.
