# Pleistocene Megafauna - Mammoths, Saber-Toothed Cats, and Overkill

## Overview
The Pleistocene epoch (2.6 Ma – 11,700 years ago) was characterized by repeated glacial-interglacial cycles and the dominance of colossal terrestrial mammalian megafauna.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Megafaunal Giants: Cold-adapted giants dominated the mammoth steppe: Woolly Mammoths (*Mammuthus primigenius*) with thick fur and curved ivory tusks, Woolly Rhinoceroses, and the iconic saber-toothed cat (*Smilodon fatalis*) possessing 28-centimeter serrated maxillary canines adapted for severing jugular veins of mega-herbivores.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Late Quaternary Extinction (Quaternary Megafaunal Die-Off): Roughly 50,000 to 10,000 years ago, nearly all terrestrial mammals exceeding 44 kg vanished from the Americas, Australia, and northern Eurasia. Scientists debate the primary driver between rapid post-glacial climatic warming versus the **Overkill Hypothesis** (Paul Martin): human overkill by expanding Paleolithic hunters armed with projectile technology.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
La Brea Tar Pits fossil asphalt preservation, ancient DNA extraction from permafrost-preserved specimens.

## Key Terminology
- **Smilodon**: Extinct genus of machairodont saber-toothed cat that lived in the Americas during the Pleistocene epoch.
- **Overkill Hypothesis**: Hypothesis proposing that human hunting was the primary cause of the extinction of Pleistocene megafauna.
- **Mammoth Steppe**: Extensive, highly productive cold and dry biome that covered northern Eurasia and North America during the Last Glacial Maximum.
