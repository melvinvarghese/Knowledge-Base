# Tiktaalik roseae - The Fish-Tetrapod Transition

## Overview
Discovered in 2004 in the Canadian Arctic, *Tiktaalik roseae* (~375 Ma, Late Devonian) is a famous 'fishapod' transitional fossil demonstrating the anatomical bridge between lobe-finned fish and four-limbed tetrapods.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Morphological Principles
Transitional Mosaic Morphology: Retains fish-like scales, gills, and fin rays, but possesses tetrapod features: a flattened crocodile-like skull with eyes on top, a flexible neck decoupled from the shoulder girdle, and a robust ribcage supporting air-breathing lungs.

---

## 2. Physiological Dynamics & Evolutionary Analysis
Proto-Limb Internal Homology: Pectoral fins contain homologous internal bones to tetrapod forelimbs: humerus, radius, ulna, and precursor wrist bones with functional joint mobility capable of propping the body out of water.

---

## 3. Ecological Significance & Scientific Applications
Vertebrate terrestrialization evolutionary models, developmental genetics of limb bud patterning.

---

## Key Terms
- **Tiktaalik**: Late Devonian transitional fossil displaying intermediate traits between fish and land tetrapods.
- **Fin-to-Limb Transition**: Macroevolutionary transformation of paired fish fins into weight-bearing walking limbs.
- **Spiracle**: Modified cranial gill opening that evolved into the vertebrate middle ear stapes cavity.
