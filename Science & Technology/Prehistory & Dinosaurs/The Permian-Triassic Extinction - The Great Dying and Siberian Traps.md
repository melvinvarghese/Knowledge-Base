# The Permian-Triassic Extinction - The Great Dying and Siberian Traps

## Overview
The Permian-Triassic Extinction (c. 252 Ma), dubbed 'The Great Dying', was the most severe mass extinction event in Phanerozoic history, wiping out roughly 96% of all marine species and 70% of terrestrial vertebrate families.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Siberian Traps Eruption: The primary trigger was colossal flood basalt volcanism in Siberia, erupting millions of cubic kilometers of basaltic lava through subterranean carbonaceous coal beds. This released massive volumes of carbon dioxide ($CO_2$), sulfur dioxide ($SO_2$), and methane.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Cascading Ecological Collapse: Triggered runaway global greenhouse warming (ocean temperatures exceeding $40^\circ\text{C}$), severe ocean acidification, widespread marine anoxia, and toxic oceanic emissions of hydrogen sulfide produced by anaerobic bacteria. Trilobites, sea scorpions (eurypterids), and major synapsid clades vanished permanently.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Cleared ecological space that permitted the evolutionary radiation of archosaurs, leading directly to the dawn of the dinosaurs.

## Key Terminology
- **The Great Dying**: Informal name for the catastrophic Permian-Triassic mass extinction event occurring 252 million years ago.
- **Ocean Anoxia**: Severe depletion of dissolved oxygen in marine waters, fatal to aerobic marine organisms.
- **Synapsid**: Group of animals that includes mammals and every animal more closely related to mammals than to reptiles, decimated in the extinction.
