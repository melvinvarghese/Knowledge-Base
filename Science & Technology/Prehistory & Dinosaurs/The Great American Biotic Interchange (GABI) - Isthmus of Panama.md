# The Great American Biotic Interchange (GABI) - Isthmus of Panama

## Overview
The Great American Biotic Interchange (c. 2.7 Ma) was a major paleobiogeographic event triggered by the volcanic formation of the Isthmus of Panama, connecting North and South America.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Long Isolation of South America: For 65 million years following the K-Pg extinction, South America was an isolated island continent, evolving a unique endemic mammalian fauna: giant ground sloths (*Megatherium*), armored glyptodonts, native ungulates (notoungulates, litoptherns), and predatory terror birds (Phorusrhacidae).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Land Bridge & Asymmetric Interchange: The closure of the Central American Seaway allowed faunal interchange. North American taxa (felids, canids, bears, camelids, horses) migrated south, while South American taxa (opossums, armadillos, porcupines, ground sloths) migrated north. The interchange was markedly asymmetric: northern placental carnivores outcompeted native southern marsupial carnivores and ungulates, restructuring South American ecology.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Oceanic disruption diverting Atlantic currents into the Gulf Stream, driving Northern Hemisphere glaciation cycles.

## Key Terminology
- **GABI**: Great American Biotic Interchange; mass exchange of terrestrial fauna between North and South America following Panama isthmus closure.
- **Glyptodont**: Extinct, heavily armored relative of armadillos that lived during the Pleistocene epoch in the Americas.
- **Terror Bird (Phorusrhacid)**: Extinct clade of large, apex carnivorous flightless birds that dominated Cenozoic South America.
