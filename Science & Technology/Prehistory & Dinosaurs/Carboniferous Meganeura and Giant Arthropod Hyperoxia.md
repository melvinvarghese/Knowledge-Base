# Carboniferous Meganeura and Giant Arthropod Hyperoxia

## Overview
During the Carboniferous period (~300 Ma), atmospheric oxygen levels reached an unprecedented **35%** (compared to 21% today), driving arthropod gigantism including the giant dragonfly *Meganeura monyi* with a 75 cm wingspan.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Morphological Principles
Tracheal Oxygen Diffusion Constraints: Insects breathe via passive tracheal tubes where gas diffusion rate scales inversely with body length; high atmospheric $pO_2$ allowed oxygen to diffuse deeper into tissues, overcoming physiological body-size limits.

---

## 2. Physiological Dynamics & Evolutionary Analysis
Carboniferous Gigantism Examples: *Meganeura* (giant predatory griffin-fly), *Arthropleura* (2.5-meter giant millipede), and *Pulmonoscorpius* (70 cm scorpion) dominated swamp forests.

---

## 3. Ecological Significance & Scientific Applications
Physiological respiratory scaling models, atmospheric biomechanics of high-density air flight.

---

## Key Terms
- **Meganeura**: Extinct genus of giant dragonfly-like griffin-flies from the Carboniferous period.
- **Hyperoxia**: State of elevated oxygen concentration exceeding standard atmospheric levels.
- **Tracheal System**: Network of air-filled tubes delivering oxygen directly to insect body tissues.
