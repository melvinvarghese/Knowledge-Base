# Synapsid Evolution - Pelycosaurs, Therapsids, and Cynodont Ancestry

## Overview
Synapsids ('mammal-like reptiles') are amniotes possessing a single temporal fenestra opening behind the eye socket, tracking the 100-million-year anatomical transition from ectothermic basal forms to true mammals.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Pelycosaurs & The Thermal Sail: Early Permian pelycosaurs (*Dimetrodon*, *Edaphosaurus*) possessed neural spine sails vascularized with blood vessels, used for thermoregulatory heat absorption and intraspecific display.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Cynodont Transition to Mammalia: In the Triassic, cynodonts (*Thrinaxodon*, *Probainognathus*) evolved key mammalian hallmarks: secondary bony palates (allowing breathing while chewing), differentiated heterodont dentition (incisors, canines, multi-cusped postcanines), whisker hair pits, double occipital condyles, and the migration of reptilian jaw bones (articular and quadrate) into the mammalian middle ear (malleus and incus).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Evolution of endothermy, ancestral lineage leading to modern monotremes, marsupials, and placentals.

## Key Terminology
- **Temporal Fenestra**: Opening in the skull behind the eye socket that allows large jaw muscle attachment, single in synapsids.
- **Dimetrodon**: Apex predatory pelycosaur of the Early Permian featuring a prominent neural spine dorsal sail.
- **Cynodont**: Therapsid clade that arose in the Late Permian giving rise directly to true mammals.
