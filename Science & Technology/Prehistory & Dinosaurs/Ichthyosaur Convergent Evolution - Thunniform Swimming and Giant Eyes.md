# Ichthyosaur Convergent Evolution - Thunniform Swimming and Giant Eyes

## Overview
Ichthyosaurs ('fish-lizards') represent one of the most striking examples of convergent evolution in vertebrate history, independently evolving the streamlined body shape of dolphins and tuna.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Thunniform Hydrodynamics: Early Triassic ichthyosaurs were eel-like anguilliform swimmers, but by the Jurassic, advanced parvipelvians (*Stenopterygius*, *Ophthalmosaurus*) evolved rigid fusiform bodies, dorsal stabilising fins, and crescent-shaped vertical tail flukes, executing high-speed, energy-efficient thunniform locomotion.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Extreme Sensory Adaptations - Sclerotic Rings: *Ophthalmosaurus* possessed the largest eyes relative to body size of any vertebrate (up to 25 cm diameter), supported by bony **sclerotic rings** that resisted high hydrostatic water pressure, allowing vision in mesopelagic deep-sea twilight zones to hunt bioluminescent belemnites and squids.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Soft-tissue fossil preservation in the German Posidonia Shale confirming blubber layers, countershading, and viviparous live birth.

## Key Terminology
- **Ichthyosaur**: Extinct marine reptile of the Mesozoic Era exhibiting extreme convergent morphological similarity to modern dolphins.
- **Thunniform**: Type of swimming locomotion where propulsion is generated exclusively by the stiff oscillation of the caudal tail fin.
- **Sclerotic Ring**: Ring of bone found in the eye sockets of many vertebrates protecting the eye against deformation.
