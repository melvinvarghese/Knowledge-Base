# Ceratopsians and Hadrosaurs - Dental Batteries and Cranial Crests

## Overview
Ceratopsians (horned dinosaurs) and Hadrosaurs (duck-billed dinosaurs) were the dominant, hyper-successful herbivorous dinosaur clades of the Late Cretaceous.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Self-Sharpening Dental Batteries: Hadrosaurs (*Edmontosaurus*) and Ceratopsians (*Triceratops*) evolved the most sophisticated chewing dentition in vertebrate history. Instead of single rows of teeth, they possessed tightly packed 'dental batteries' containing over 1,000 interlocking teeth of differential tissue hardness (enamel, orthodentine, secondary dentine) that self-sharpened during chewing, allowing them to grind tough fibrous conifers and woody angiosperms.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Cranial Crests & Visual Display: Ceratopsian parietosquamosal neck frills and horns functioned primarily for intra-species visual display, species recognition, and sexual selection rather than pure defense. Hadrosaur hollow cranial crests (*Parasaurolophus*) served as acoustic acoustic resonance chambers, blowing low-frequency trumpeting herd calls.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Herding behavior evidenced by mass bonebeds, dental micro-wear evolutionary biomechanics.

## Key Terminology
- **Dental Battery**: Complex array of hundreds of densely packed, constantly replacing teeth specialized for grinding fibrous vegetation.
- **Hadrosaur**: Family of herbivorous ornithischian 'duck-billed' dinosaurs prevalent in the Late Cretaceous.
- **Parasaurolophus**: Hadrosaur known for its large, cranial acoustic hollow crest extending backward from the skull.
