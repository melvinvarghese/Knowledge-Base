# Mosasaur Predation and K-Pg Marine Extinction

## Overview
Mosasaurs (e.g., *Mosasaurus hoffmannii*) were apex marine squamates (closely related to modern monitor lizards and snakes) dominating Late Cretaceous oceans up to 17 meters long with double-hinged jaws.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Morphological Principles
Pterygoid Teeth & Kinetic Jaws: Equipped with an intramandibular joint in the lower jaw and a second row of curved pterygoid teeth on the roof of the mouth, ratcheting struggling ammonites and marine turtles down the gullet like modern snakes.

---

## 2. Physiological Dynamics & Evolutionary Analysis
Abrupt K-Pg Extinction Collapse: As top marine predators, mosasaurs collapsed instantaneously 66 million years ago when the Chicxulub asteroid impact eradicated oceanic phytoplankton base productivity.

---

## 3. Ecological Significance & Scientific Applications
Apex marine predator macroevolution, squamate skull kinesis, and marine collapse mechanisms.

---

## Key Terms
- **Mosasaur**: Large, carnivorous marine lizard that dominated the oceans during the Late Cretaceous.
- **Pterygoid Teeth**: Secondary row of teeth situated on the palate bones helping restrain prey.
- **Intramandibular Joint**: Hinge joint within the lower jawbone providing flexible expansion for swallowing large prey.
