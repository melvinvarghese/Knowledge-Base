# The Devonian Nekton Revolution - Placoderms and Dunkleosteus

## Overview
The Devonian Period (419–359 Ma), the 'Age of Fishes', witnessed the Nekton Revolution: the evolution of jaws and paired fins that transformed swimming vertebrates into active pelagic macropredators.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Placoderm Armor & Self-Sharpening Jaw Blades: Placoderms were armored prehistoric jawed fishes featuring a head and thorax covered in heavy interlocking dermal bony plates. The apex predator *Dunkleosteus terrelli* (up to 8 meters long) lacked true teeth, instead possessing self-sharpening gnathal dermal bone shearing plates.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Extreme Biomechanical Bite Force: Biomechanical kinematic linkage modeling demonstrates *Dunkleosteus* could open its jaws in 20 milliseconds (generating massive suction to draw in prey) and snap shut with a catastrophic bite force exceeding 5,000 Newtons at the blade tips, capable of crushing armored ammonites and primitive sharks.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Evolution of the gnathostome jaw from modified pharyngeal gill arches, internal fertilization evidenced by fossil placoderm embryos (*Materpiscis*).

## Key Terminology
- **Placoderm**: Class of extinct armored prehistoric jawed fish that flourished during the Devonian Period.
- **Dunkleosteus**: Apex predatory placoderm of the Late Devonian armed with self-sharpening dermal bone jaw blades.
- **Gnathostome**: Jawed vertebrate, a group comprising 99% of all living vertebrates originating in the Silurian.
