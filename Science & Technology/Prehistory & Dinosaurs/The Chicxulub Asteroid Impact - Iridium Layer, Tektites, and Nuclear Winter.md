# The Chicxulub Asteroid Impact - Iridium Layer, Tektites, and Nuclear Winter

## Overview
The Cretaceous-Paleogene (K-Pg) extinction (66 Ma) was triggered by the hypervelocity impact of a 10-kilometer asteroid at Chicxulub, Mexico, terminating 75% of terrestrial species including non-avian dinosaurs.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Alvarez Hypothesis & The Global Iridium Anomaly: Luis and Walter Alvarez (1980) discovered a worldwide millimetric clay layer at the K-Pg boundary enriched in **iridium** (an element rare in Earth's crust but abundant in chondritic asteroids) by up to 160 times background levels, paired with shocked quartz and microtektite impact spherules.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Catastrophic Impact Geophysics & Impact Winter: Impacting sulfur-rich anhydrite marine target rocks with the kinetic energy of 100 million megatons of TNT, the collision ignited global wildfires and vaporized 500 billion tons of sulfur and soot into the stratosphere. Solar irradiance was blocked for years, halting photosynthesis and collapsing terrestrial and marine food chains.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Discovery of the 180-kilometer subsurface Chicxulub crater (1991), rise of mammalian adaptive radiation in the Paleocene.

## Key Terminology
- **Chicxulub Crater**: Massive 180-kilometer diameter impact crater buried beneath the Yucatán Peninsula in Mexico.
- **Iridium Anomaly**: Unusually high concentration of the platinum-group element iridium found in rock strata at the Cretaceous-Paleogene boundary.
- **Microtektite**: Minute, aerodynamically shaped glassy spherules formed from molten target rock ejected during hypervelocity meteorite impacts.
