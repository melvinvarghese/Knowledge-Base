# Carboniferous Giant Arthropods - Arthropleura, Meganeura, and Hyperoxia

## Overview
During the Carboniferous Period (c. 359–299 Ma), atmospheric oxygen levels reached an unprecedented 35% (compared to 21% today), driving gigantism in terrestrial arthropods.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Lignin Evolution & Coal Swamp Forests: The evolutionary emergence of lignin allowed lycopsid trees (scale trees *Lepidodendron*) to reach 40 meters in height. Because wood-decaying fungi and bacteria had not yet evolved enzymes to degrade lignin, fallen trees did not rot, sequestering colossal amounts of organic carbon into coal deposits and releasing massive quantities of free oxygen into the atmosphere.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Arthropod Gigantism & Respiratory Limits: Insects breathe passively through branching tracheal tubes, where oxygen diffusion limits maximum body size. Under Carboniferous hyperoxia, oxygen diffused deeper into tissues, permitting giant forms: *Meganeura* (a predatory griffinflies with a 70-centimeter wingspan) and *Arthropleura* (a giant millipede-like myriapod reaching 2.6 meters in length and 50 kg in mass).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
World's major economic coal reserves, evolutionary constraint of respiratory gas diffusion on invertebrate physiology.

## Key Terminology
- **Arthropleura**: Extinct genus of giant myriapod arthropod that inhabited the coal swamp forests of the Carboniferous Period.
- **Meganeura**: Genus of extinct predatory insects from the Carboniferous Period resembling and related to modern dragonflies.
- **Hyperoxia**: State in which an environment has a higher than normal concentration of oxygen, reaching 35% in the Carboniferous.
