# Trilobite Anatomy - Calcite Lenses, Glabella, and enrollment

## Overview
Trilobites are an iconic extinct class of marine arthropods that flourished across the Paleozoic oceans for 270 million years, possessing hard calcified exoskeletons.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Tripartite Body Plan & Optical Calcite Lenses: The body is divided longitudinally into three lobes (axial lobe and two pleural lobes) and transversely into three regions: cephalon (head), thorax, and pygidium (tail). Trilobite eyes were revolutionary compound optical systems composed of optically transparent crystals of pure **inorganic calcite ($CaCO_3$)**, functioning as doublet lenses correcting spherical aberration.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Defensive Enrollment: When threatened by predatory nautiloids or sea scorpions, trilobites flexed their articulated thoracic segments, locking their pygidium against the cephalon in an impenetrable spherical or capsule **enrollment**, sealing all vulnerable soft ventral legs inside.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Premier Paleozoic index fossils, evolutionary radiation across 20,000 cataloged species, totally extinguished at the Permian-Triassic boundary.

## Key Terminology
- **Trilobite**: Extinct marine arthropod of the Paleozoic Era characterized by a three-lobed, chitinous-calcitic segmented exoskeleton.
- **Schizochroal Eye**: Unique compound eye found in phacopid trilobites composed of large individual biconvex calcite lenses with separate corneas.
- **Enrollment**: Defensive reflex where a trilobite rolled into a tight ball to protect its vulnerable ventral soft tissues.
