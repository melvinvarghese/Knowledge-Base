# Spinosaurus and Semiaquatic Dinosaurs - Sail Biomechanics and Piscivory

## Overview
*Spinosaurus aegyptianus* (100–95 Ma, Cretaceous of North Africa) was the longest predatory dinosaur known (15 meters), exhibiting revolutionary adaptations for a semiaquatic riverine lifestyle.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Aquatic Anatomical Specializations: Discovered in Morocco's Kem Kem beds, *Spinosaurus* possessed a crocodile-like elongated snout with conical, unserrated teeth designed for capturing slippery fish, neurovascular pressure receptors in the snout tip for detecting swimming aquatic prey, dense pachyostotic bones for buoyancy control, short hind limbs, and a flexible paddle-like tail propelled by deep caudal vertebral spines.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Dorsal Sail & River Monster Ecology: The colossal 1.8-meter neural spine dorsal sail functioned as a hydrodynamic stabilizer and visual display organ in Cretaceous river systems populated by giant coelacanths, sawfish (*Onchopristis*), and crocodylomorphs.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Shattered the paradigm that dinosaurs were strictly terrestrial animals, computational fluid dynamic swimming simulations.

## Key Terminology
- **Spinosaurus**: Colossal theropod dinosaur of the Cretaceous known for its elongated snout, dense bones, and neural spine dorsal sail.
- **Pachyostosis**: Non-pathological thickening of bones in aquatic vertebrates that acts as natural ballast for diving.
- **Piscivore**: Carnivorous animal that primarily eats fish.
