# Velociraptor mongoliensis - Sickle Claws and Quill Knobs

## Overview
*Velociraptor mongoliensis* was a small, agile dromaeosaurid theropod (turkey-sized, ~15 kg) discovered in Mongolia's Gobi Desert, possessing a hyperextensible sickle claw and fossilized forearm quill knobs proving full feather coverage.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Morphological Principles
The Sickle Claw & RPR Hunting Hypothesis: The hypertrophied second digit claw was held off the ground during running. Biomechanical modeling indicates claws were used for pinning struggling prey to the ground (Raptor Prey Restraint, RPR) rather than disemboweling.

---

## 2. Physiological Dynamics & Evolutionary Analysis
Ulnar Quill Knobs: Distinct bony papillae (quill knobs) along the ulna bone confirm the attachment of large pennaceous primary flight feathers, used for flap-running stability and egg brooding.

---

## 3. Ecological Significance & Scientific Applications
Dromaeosaurid biomechanical reconstruction, evolutionary origin of avian wing-assisted incline running (WAIR).

---

## Key Terms
- **Sickle Claw**: Enlarged, strongly recurved predatory claw on the second toe of dromaeosaurid dinosaurs.
- **Quill Knob**: Bony tubercle along the forearm bone anchoring primary feather calami via ligaments.
- **Raptor Prey Restraint (RPR)**: Biomechanical hunting model where predatory birds and dromaeosaurs pin prey using foot talons.
