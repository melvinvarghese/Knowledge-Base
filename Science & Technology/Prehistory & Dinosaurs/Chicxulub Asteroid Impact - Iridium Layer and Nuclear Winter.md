# Chicxulub Asteroid Impact - Iridium Layer and Nuclear Winter

## Overview
The Cretaceous-Paleogene (K-Pg) extinction event 66 million years ago was triggered by the impact of a **10 km carbonaceous asteroid** at Chicxulub, Mexico, wiping out 75% of Earth's species, including all non-avian dinosaurs.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Morphological Principles
Geological Impact Markers: Worldwide boundary clay layers exhibit an unmistakable global spike in **Iridium** (rare in Earth's crust but abundant in asteroids), shock-metamorphosed quartz, and microscopic tektite micro-spherules.

---

## 2. Physiological Dynamics & Evolutionary Analysis
Impact Winter and Atmospheric Darkness: The impact excavated sulfur-rich evaporite and hydrocarbon carbonate rocks, lofting billions of tons of sulfate aerosols and soot into the stratosphere, blocking sunlight for years and collapsing global photosynthesis.

## Key Terms
- **Chicxulub Crater**: 180 km wide buried impact crater under the Yucatán Peninsula formed by the K-Pg impactor.
- **Iridium Anomaly**: Unusually high concentration of iridium in K-Pg boundary clays confirming extraterrestrial impact.
- **Impact Winter**: Prolonged period of global darkness and sub-zero temperatures caused by atmospheric impact dust.
