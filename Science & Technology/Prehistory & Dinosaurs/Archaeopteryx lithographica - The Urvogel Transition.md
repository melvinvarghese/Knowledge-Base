# Archaeopteryx lithographica - The Urvogel Transition

## Overview
Discovered in 1861 in the Solnhofen limestone of Germany, *Archaeopteryx lithographica* (~150 Ma) is the iconic transitional fossil capturing the evolutionary bridge between non-avian theropod dinosaurs and modern avians.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Morphological Principles
Mosaic Avian and Reptilian Traits: Possesses fully developed asymmetric flight feathers and a fused furcula (wishbone), yet retains reptilian teeth, clawed wing fingers, a flat non-keeled sternum, and a long bony tail.

---

## 2. Physiological Dynamics & Evolutionary Analysis
Aerodynamic Flight Capabilities: Synchrotron X-ray analysis confirms asymmetric flight feathers and robust humeri capable of burst flapping flight, transitioning from arboreal gliding to active avian locomotion.

---

## 3. Ecological Significance & Scientific Applications
Direct fossil evidence validating Darwin's theory of natural selection and transitional forms.

---

## Key Terms
- **Archaeopteryx**: Iconic Late Jurassic transitional fossil linking feathered theropod dinosaurs to modern birds.
- **Furcula**: The wishbone formed by the fusion of the clavicles, storing elastic energy during avian flight.
- **Asymmetric Feather**: Feather where the vane on one side of the central shaft is narrower than the other, essential for generating lift.
