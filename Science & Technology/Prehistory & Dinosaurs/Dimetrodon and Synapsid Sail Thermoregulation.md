# Dimetrodon and Synapsid Sail Thermoregulation

## Overview
Often mistaken for a dinosaur, *Dimetrodon* was a predatory Permian **synapsid** (stem-mammal) possessing a giant dorsal sail formed by elongated vertebral neural spines, functioning primarily for thermoregulation.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Morphological Principles
Thermoregulatory Thermal Exchange: Highly vascularized skin webbed across spines acted as an external solar radiator/collector; orienting the sail perpendicular to morning sunbeams accelerated warming, raising body temperature to hunt lethargic ectothermic prey.

---

## 2. Physiological Dynamics & Evolutionary Analysis
Synapsid Skull Architecture: Characterized by a single temporal fenestra behind each eye socket, differentiating the synapsid mammal lineage from diapsid reptiles and dinosaurs.

---

## 3. Ecological Significance & Scientific Applications
Evolution of mammalian endothermy, early terrestrial vertebrate predator-prey dynamics.

---

## Key Terms
- **Synapsid**: Amniote clade characterized by a single temporal opening in the skull, including mammals and their ancestors.
- **Neural Spine**: Dorsal bony projection extending upward from the vertebral body.
- **Thermoregulatory Sail**: Large, vascularized skin flap supported by spines facilitating rapid heat exchange.
