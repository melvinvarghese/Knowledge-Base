# Neanderthal Cranial Morphology and Mousterian Culture

## Overview
Neanderthals (*Homo neanderthalensis*) were archaic hominins inhabiting Eurasia until ~40,000 years ago, displaying cold-adapted cranial features, complex Mousterian flint tool technology, and genetic interbreeding with modern humans.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Morphological Principles
Cranial & Post-Cranial Morphology: Cranial capacity (~1,500 cc) exceeded modern *Homo sapiens*; characterized by an elongated dolichocephalic skull, prominent supraorbital brow ridge, occipital bun, large nasal aperture, and robust stocky limbs.

---

## 2. Physiological Dynamics & Evolutionary Analysis
Mousterian Technology & Culture: Manufactured specialized stone flake tools using the sophisticated **Levallois reduction technique**; buried dead with grave goods, created personal shell ornamentation, and interbred with humans (1–2% Neanderthal DNA persists in non-African humans).

---

## 3. Ecological Significance & Scientific Applications
Human paleogenomics, evolutionary origin of symbolic thought, hominin hybridization dynamics.

---

## Key Terms
- **Levallois Technique**: Stone-knapping method where a flint core is prepared to strike off flakes of predetermined size and shape.
- **Occipital Bun**: Prominent bulge of the occipital bone at the posterior base of Neanderthal skulls.
- **Mousterian**: Middle Paleolithic archaeological stone tool industry associated predominantly with Neanderthals.
