# Wi-Fi (IEEE 802.11) - Radio Frequencies and Orthogonal Frequency Modulation

## Overview
Wi-Fi is a family of wireless networking protocols based on IEEE 802.11 standards, delivering high-speed local area wireless connectivity across 2.4 GHz, 5 GHz, and 6 GHz ISM radio bands.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
OFDM and Orthogonal Subcarriers: Orthogonal Frequency-Division Multiplexing splits high-speed data streams across hundreds of overlapping orthogonal narrowband subcarrier frequencies, preventing multi-path interference.

---

## 2. Structural Mechanics & Scientific Analysis
MIMO Spatial Multiplexing: Multiple-Input Multiple-Output uses multi-antenna arrays at transmitter and receiver to beamform signals and transmit multiple parallel data streams simultaneously over identical frequencies.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Technological Impact
Residential home networking, corporate enterprise wireless LANs, public wireless hot spots, and IoT device connectivity.

---

## Key Terms
- **OFDM**: Modulation scheme transmitting data over multiple closely spaced orthogonal subcarrier frequencies.
- **MIMO**: Antenna technology using multiple antennas at transmitter and receiver to multiply data capacity.
- **Beamforming**: Signal processing technique focusing wireless transmission directly toward a specific receiver.
