# Flash Memory - NAND and NOR Floating-Gate MOSFETs

## Overview
Flash Memory is an electronic non-volatile solid-state computer storage medium that stores data in arrays of floating-gate transistors, retaining bit states without continuous electrical power.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
Quantum Fowler-Nordheim Tunneling: Electrons are injected through a thin silicon oxide dielectric barrier into an isolated conductive polysilicon floating gate, shifting transistor threshold voltage ($V_{\text{th}}$).

---

## 2. Structural Mechanics & Scientific Analysis
NAND vs NOR Architectures: NOR provides fast random-access byte reading for firmware execution; NAND strings cells in series, minimizing die silicon area for ultra-dense bulk storage.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Technological Impact
Solid-State Drives (SSDs), USB flash drives, smartphone internal storage, and camera SD cards.

---

## Key Terms
- **Floating Gate**: Conductive gate electrode electrically isolated within a MOSFET to trap electrical charges.
- **NAND Flash**: Dense flash memory architecture connecting memory cells in series strings.
- **Fowler-Nordheim Tunneling**: Wave-mechanical quantum tunneling of electrons through a thin triangular potential barrier.
