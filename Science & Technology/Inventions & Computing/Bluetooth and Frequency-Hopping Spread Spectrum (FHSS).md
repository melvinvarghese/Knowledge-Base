# Bluetooth and Frequency-Hopping Spread Spectrum (FHSS)

## Overview
Bluetooth is a short-range wireless technology standard operating at 2.4 GHz ISM frequencies, designed for creating secure Personal Area Networks (PANs) with low power consumption.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
Frequency-Hopping Spread Spectrum (FHSS): Bluetooth avoids radio interference by hopping pseudorandomly across 79 discrete 1-MHz channels at a rate of 1,600 frequency hops per second.

---

## 2. Structural Mechanics & Scientific Analysis
Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE): Optimized for periodic micro-transmissions from low-power sensors, operating for years on single coin-cell batteries using sleeping states.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Technological Impact
Wireless audio headphones, computer peripheral mice and keyboards, medical continuous glucose monitors, and asset tracking beacons.

---

## Key Terms
- **Frequency-Hopping Spread Spectrum (FHSS)**: Transmission method repeatedly switching carrier frequencies to avoid radio jamming and interference.
- **Piconet**: Ad-hoc computer network formed by a primary Bluetooth device coordinating up to seven secondary devices.
- **Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE)**: Power-conserving variant of Bluetooth tailored for intermittent sensor telemetry.
