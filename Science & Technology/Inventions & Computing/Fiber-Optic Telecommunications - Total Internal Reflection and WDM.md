# Fiber-Optic Telecommunications - Total Internal Reflection and WDM

## Overview
Fiber-Optic Telecommunications transmits information as pulses of infrared light guided through glass optical fibers, forming the high-bandwidth backbone of the global internet.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
Total Internal Reflection in Silica Fibers: Light entering an ultra-pure silica glass core ($n=1.48$) reflects losslessly off lower-index cladding ($n=1.46$) at attenuation levels below $0.2\text{ dB/km}$ at 1550 nm.

---

## 2. Structural Mechanics & Scientific Analysis
Wavelength-Division Multiplexing (WDM): Transmitting dozens of independent data channels simultaneously down a single fiber strand by modulating separate laser wavelengths, boosting fiber capacity to tens of terabits per second.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Technological Impact
Transoceanic submarine internet cables, terrestrial long-haul telecommunication backbones, and Fiber-To-The-Home (FTTH) gigabit broadband.

---

## Key Terms
- **Wavelength-Division Multiplexing (WDM)**: Technique combining multiple optical carrier signals onto a single optical fiber.
- **Attenuation**: The gradual loss of signal intensity as light propagates through a transmission medium.
- **Erbium-Doped Fiber Amplifier (EDFA)**: Optical amplifier boosting light signals directly without electrical conversion.
