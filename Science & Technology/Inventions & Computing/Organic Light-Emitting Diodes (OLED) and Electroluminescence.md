# Organic Light-Emitting Diodes (OLED) and Electroluminescence

## Overview
OLEDs are solid-state emissive displays where organic conjugated thin-film semiconductor layers emit brilliant light in response to an electric current via radiative exciton recombination.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
Direct Electroluminescence: Holes injected from the anode and electrons from the cathode recombine within an organic emissive layer (e.g., Alq3), generating excitons that decay radiatively emitting photons.

---

## 2. Structural Mechanics & Scientific Analysis
True Black Contrast & Flexibility: Because every pixel is an independent light emitter (self-emissive), individual pixels turn off completely to produce true zero-nit black, enabling infinite dynamic contrast ratios.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Technological Impact
High-end smartphone displays, curved and foldable television screens, automotive lighting, and virtual reality (VR) headsets.

---

## Key Terms
- **OLED**: Light-emitting diode whose emissive electroluminescent layer is an organic compound film.
- **Exciton**: Bound state of an electron and an electron hole attracted via electrostatic Coulomb force.
- **Self-Emissive**: Display technology where each individual subpixel generates its own light without a backlight.
