# The Global Positioning System (GPS) - Trilateration and Relativistic Correction

## Overview
GPS is a satellite-based radionavigation constellation providing precise three-dimensional position, velocity, and timing synchronization worldwide via time-of-flight trilateration.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
Trilateration Mathematics: Receiver calculates pseudo-ranges from at least four satellites by timing radio signal arrivals ($d = c \cdot \Delta t$), solving four simultaneous spherical distance equations to eliminate receiver clock bias.

---

## 2. Structural Mechanics & Scientific Analysis
Relativistic Corrections: Satellite atomic clocks run faster due to general relativity (weaker gravity: +45.9 µs/day) and slower due to special relativity (high orbital speed: -7.1 µs/day), requiring a net correction of +38.6 µs/day.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Technological Impact
Civilian smartphone navigation, commercial aviation auto-landing, precision agricultural guidance, and global cellular network timestamp synchronization.

---

## Key Terms
- **Trilateration**: Geometrical positioning method determining coordinates using distances from known reference points.
- **Pseudorange**: Calculated distance from a satellite to a receiver including internal clock error bias.
- **Clock Drift**: Time divergence between satellite atomic clocks and receiver clocks requiring relativistic tuning.
