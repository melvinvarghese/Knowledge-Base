# Epiphytes - Air Plants and Bromeliad Phytotelmata

## Overview
Epiphytes are non-parasitic plants that grow harmlessly upon other trees for physical support, absorbing moisture and nutrients directly from rain and mist using aerial roots and specialized foliar trichomes.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Morphological Principles
Trichome Absorptive Physics (Tillandsia): Air plants possess microscopic dead peltate trichomes (scales) that lay flat when dry to reflect radiation and expand when wet, capillary-siphoning water into underlying mesophyll cells.

---

## 2. Physiological Dynamics & Evolutionary Analysis
Bromeliad Phytotelmata: Tightly overlapping leaf rosettes form aquatic water-storage tanks (phytotelmata) holding liters of rainwater, supporting micro-ecosystems of treefrogs, aquatic insects, and nutrient-cycling microbes.

---

## 3. Ecological Significance & Scientific Applications
Canopy biodiversity conservation, vertical green wall architectural building facades.

---

## Key Terms
- **Epiphyte**: Plant growing harmlessly on another plant deriving moisture and nutrients from air and rain.
- **Phytotelma**: Water body held by terrestrial plants forming micro-aquatic ecological habitats.
- **Peltate Trichome**: Umbrella-shaped microscopic scale specialized for absorbing atmospheric humidity.
