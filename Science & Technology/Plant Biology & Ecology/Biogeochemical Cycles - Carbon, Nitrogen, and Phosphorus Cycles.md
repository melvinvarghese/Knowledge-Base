# Biogeochemical Cycles - Carbon, Nitrogen, and Phosphorus Cycles

## Overview
Biogeochemical cycles describe the global pathways through which essential chemical elements circulate between the biosphere, atmosphere, hydrosphere, and lithosphere.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Global Carbon Cycle: Carbon flows between atmospheric carbon dioxide ($CO_2$), oceanic carbonate pools, terrestrial biomass via photosynthesis ($6 CO_2 + 6 H_2O 	o C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6 O_2$), and deep lithospheric fossil fuels, with anthropocentrically released fossil carbon driving anthropogenic global warming.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Nitrogen & Phosphorus Dynamics: Atmospheric inert nitrogen gas ($N_2$) must be 'fixed' into reactive bioavailable ammonia ($NH_3$) by symbiotic diazotrophic bacteria (*Rhizobium*) or the industrial Haber-Bosch process. Phosphorus lacks a gaseous phase, cycling slowly via rock weathering, soil absorption, and aquatic sedimentary burial, acting as the primary limiting nutrient in freshwater ecosystems.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Anthropogenic eutrophication of waterways, planetary boundaries framework, agricultural fertilizer management.

## Key Terminology
- **Biogeochemical Cycle**: Path by which a chemical element or molecule moves through both biotic and abiotic compartments of Earth.
- **Nitrogen Fixation**: Biological or chemical conversion of atmospheric $N_2$ into reactive ammonia or nitrate compounds.
- **Haber-Bosch Process**: Industrial chemical process synthesizing ammonia from atmospheric nitrogen and hydrogen gas under high heat and pressure.
