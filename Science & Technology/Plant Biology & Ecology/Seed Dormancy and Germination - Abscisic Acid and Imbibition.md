# Seed Dormancy and Germination - Abscisic Acid and Imbibition

## Overview
Seed Dormancy is an evolutionary adaptation that suspends embryonic development to prevent germination during unfavorable seasonal conditions, ensuring survival.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Hormonal Antagonism (ABA vs GA): Seed dormancy is maintained by high concentrations of **abscisic acid (ABA)**, which inhibits cellular elongation and protects seed embryos from desiccation. Environmental triggers (prolonged winter cold stratification, water availability, or fire smoke chemicals like karrikins) trigger the enzymatic breakdown of ABA and the surge of **gibberellins (GA)**.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Imbibition & Alpha-Amylase Activation: Germination initiates with **imbibition**—rapid physical uptake of water by the dry seed coat, rehydrating cellular enzymes. Gibberellins released by the embryo diffuse to the surrounding aleurone layer, activating genes encoding $\alpha$-amylase, which hydrolyzes insoluble endosperm starch into glucose to fuel embryonic radicle emergence.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Crop seed viability banking (Svalbard Global Seed Vault), agricultural synchronization of crop seedling emergence.

## Key Terminology
- **Abscisic Acid (ABA)**: Plant hormone that maintains seed and bud dormancy and coordinates stomatal closure during water stress.
- **Imbibition**: Physical absorption of water by dry colloids causing substantial swelling, the initial step in seed germination.
- **Stratification**: Process of treating stored seeds with cold and moisture to simulate winter conditions and break dormancy.
