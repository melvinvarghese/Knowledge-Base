# Calvin Cycle and Carbon Fixation by RuBisCO

## Overview
The Calvin Cycle (light-independent reactions) occurs in the chloroplast stroma, utilizing ATP and NADPH generated by light reactions to fix atmospheric carbon dioxide into triose phosphate sugars.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Morphological Principles
The RuBisCO Catalytic Fixation: Ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate carboxylase/oxygenase (RuBisCO) catalyzes the carboxylation of ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate ($RuBP$) with $CO_2$, forming an unstable 6-carbon intermediate that cleaves into two 3-PGA molecules.

---

## 2. Physiological Dynamics & Evolutionary Analysis
The Three Phases: 1. Carbon Fixation (catalyzed by RuBisCO); 2. Reduction (ATP/NADPH phosphorylate and reduce 3-PGA to G3P); 3. Regeneration (5 G3P molecules rearranged into 3 RuBP molecules).

---

## 3. Ecological Significance & Scientific Applications
Genetic engineering to increase RuBisCO catalytic efficiency, synthetic biology crop yield enhancement.

---

## Key Terms
- **RuBisCO**: The primary enzyme responsible for fixing atmospheric carbon dioxide into organic biomass.
- **3-PGA**: 3-Phosphoglycerate, the first stable 3-carbon intermediate in C3 photosynthesis.
- **Stroma**: Fluid-filled space surrounding thylakoids in chloroplasts where carbon fixation occurs.
