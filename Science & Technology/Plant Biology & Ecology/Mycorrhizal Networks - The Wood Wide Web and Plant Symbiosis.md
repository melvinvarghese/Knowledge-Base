# Mycorrhizal Networks - The Wood Wide Web and Plant Symbiosis

## Overview
Mycorrhizal Networks are underground mutualistic fungal networks connecting plant root systems, facilitating bidirectional nutrient transfer and inter-plant signaling.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Endo- vs Ectomycorrhizae: Roughly 90% of all land plant species participate in mycorrhizal symbioses: Arbuscular mycorrhizae (endomycorrhizae) penetrate cortical root cell walls, forming branch-like arbuscules for nutrient exchange; Ectomycorrhizae form an external mantle and Hartig net surrounding root tip cells without penetrating cell walls.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Resource Sharing & Chemical Signaling: Fungi deliver poorly mobile soil nutrients (phosphorus, zinc, nitrogen) and water; in exchange, host plants allocate up to 20–30% of photosynthetically fixed carbon sugars to the fungus. These hyphal networks connect disparate trees into a common mycorrhizal network ('Wood Wide Web'), transferring carbon from mature canopy trees to shaded saplings and relaying chemical defense warning signals against insect pests.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Crucial for terrestrial forest carbon sequestration, sustainable agriculture reducing synthetic fertilizer dependencies.

## Key Terminology
- **Mycorrhiza**: Symbiotic association between a fungus and the roots of a vascular host plant.
- **Arbuscule**: Finely branched microscopic fungal structure formed inside plant root cells where nutrient transfer occurs.
- **Common Mycorrhizal Network**: Continuous underground fungal mycelial network linking the root systems of multiple individual plants.
