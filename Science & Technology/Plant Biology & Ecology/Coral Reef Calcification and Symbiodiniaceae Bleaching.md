# Coral Reef Calcification and Symbiodiniaceae Bleaching

## Overview
Coral Reefs are biogenic limestone structures formed by scleractinian corals hosting endosymbiotic dinoflagellates (*Symbiodiniaceae*), vulnerable to thermal coral bleaching under warming sea surface temperatures.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Morphological Principles
Aragonite Calcification & Mutualism: Coral polyps precipitate calcium carbonate ($Ca^{2+} + 2HCO_3^- \rightarrow CaCO_3 + CO_2 + H_2O$); symbiotic zooxanthellae provide up to 90% of coral energy requirements via photosynthetic glycerol and glucose.

---

## 2. Physiological Dynamics & Evolutionary Analysis
Thermal Stress & Bleaching: When ocean temperatures exceed normal summer maxima by 1–2°C for consecutive weeks, zooxanthellae photosynthetic systems generate destructive reactive oxygen species (ROS); corals expel the symbionts, exposing white calcium skeletons.

---

## 3. Ecological Significance & Scientific Applications
Coral reef restoration, breeding thermally resilient 'super-corals', and marine protected area monitoring.

---

## Key Terms
- **Zooxanthellae**: Endosymbiotic dinoflagellates living within tissues of reef-building stony corals.
- **Coral Bleaching**: Loss of intracellular endosymbionts triggered by thermal stress and light irradiance.
- **Aragonite Saturation ($\Omega_{\text{arag}}$)**: Chemical index measuring oceanic calcium carbonate availability for reef calcification.
