# Island Biogeography Theory - MacArthur and Wilson's Equilibrium Model

## Overview
The Equilibrium Theory of Island Biogeography, developed by Robert MacArthur and E.O. Wilson (1967), predicts species richness on ecological islands based on island area and isolation.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Equilibrium Model: Species richness ($S^*$) represents a dynamic equilibrium between two opposing rates: Immigration of new species (decreases as an island becomes more distant from the mainland source pool) and Extinction of resident species (decreases as an island becomes larger due to higher population sizes and microhabitat diversity).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Conservation Applications (SLOSS Debate): In terrestrial conservation, habitat fragmentation creates 'islands' of natural reserves amidst urban and agricultural landscapes. The theory informed the 'SLOSS debate' (Single Large Or Several Small reserves), establishing the critical value of wildlife corridors connecting fragmented patches.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Design of national parks and ecological reserves, forecasting extinction rates from deforestation.

## Key Terminology
- **Island Biogeography**: Field of biogeography examining the factors that affect the species richness of isolated natural communities.
- **SLOSS Debate**: Debate in conservation biology over whether a Single Large Or Several Small habitat reserves are superior for preserving species.
- **Wildlife Corridor**: Strip of natural habitat connecting wildlife populations separated by human activities or structures.
