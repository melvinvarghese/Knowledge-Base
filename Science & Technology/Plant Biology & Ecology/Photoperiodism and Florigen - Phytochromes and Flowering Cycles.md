# Photoperiodism and Florigen - Phytochromes and Flowering Cycles

## Overview
Photoperiodism is the biological capacity of plants to measure the relative length of day and night to coordinate seasonal flowering and dormant phenology.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Phytochrome Photoreceptors ($P_r$ and $P_{fr}$): Plants monitor light using **phytochrome** pigment proteins that alternate between two conformational states: red-absorbing $P_r$ (absorbs 660 nm, converting to active $P_{fr}$) and far-red-absorbing $P_{fr}$ (absorbs 730 nm, reverting to inactive $P_r$). Sunlight daytime exposure accumulates active $P_{fr}$; in the dark, $P_{fr}$ slowly reverts to $P_r$, acting as a molecular astronomical night-clock.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Florigen & The CONSTANS Pathway: Rather than day length, plants actually measure the uninterrupted duration of darkness. In long-day plants, photoreceptor activation stabilizes the CONSTANS transcription factor in leaves, triggering expression of the elusive flowering hormone **Florigen (FT protein)**, which travels through phloem sieve tubes to the shoot apical meristem to initiate floral organ morphogenesis.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Agricultural greenhouse light-supplementation schedules, latitude-specific crop breeding.

## Key Terminology
- **Photoperiodism**: Physiological reaction of organisms to the length of day or night.
- **Phytochrome**: Photoreceptor pigment protein in plants that senses red and far-red light to regulate germination and flowering.
- **Florigen**: Systemic mobile protein hormone (FT) synthesized in leaves that travels to meristems to trigger flowering.
