# Plant Hormones - Auxin and Polar Tropisms

## Overview
Auxin (indole-3-acetic acid, IAA) is the master plant morphogenetic hormone orchestrating cell elongation, apical dominance, and directional tropic growth responses toward light and gravity.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Morphological Principles
The Acid Growth Hypothesis & PIN Transporters: Auxin activates plasma membrane proton pumps, acidifying the cell wall ($pH < 5.0$) and activating expansin enzymes that loosen cellulose microfibrils, enabling turgor-driven cell elongation.

---

## 2. Physiological Dynamics & Evolutionary Analysis
Phototropism & Gravitropism: Asymmetric PIN efflux carrier localization transports auxin to shaded stem sides (inducing phototropic bending toward light) or to the lower side of roots (inhibiting root elongation for downward geotropism).

---

## 3. Ecological Significance & Scientific Applications
Synthetic agricultural rooting hormones, weed control selective herbicides (2,4-D), tissue culture plant micropropagation.

---

## Key Terms
- **Auxin (IAA)**: Principal plant phytohormone controlling directional growth and cell enlargement.
- **Phototropism**: Directional growth of a plant organ in response to directional light cues.
- **PIN Protein**: Transmembrane auxin efflux carrier directing polar auxin transport throughout plant tissues.
