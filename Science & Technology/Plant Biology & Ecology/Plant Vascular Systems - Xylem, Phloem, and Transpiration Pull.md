# Plant Vascular Systems - Xylem, Phloem, and Transpiration Pull

## Overview
Vascular plants transport water, inorganic minerals, and organic photosynthate across vertical distances exceeding 100 meters using specialized xylem and phloem tissues.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Xylem & Cohesion-Tension Theory: Water movement is passive, driven by solar-powered **transpiration pull**. Evaporation of water molecules through stomata creates massive negative hydrostatic tension ($\Psi \sim -2\text{ MPa}$ in canopy foliage). Due to water's strong hydrogen bonding (cohesion) and attraction to cellulose xylem walls (adhesion), continuous unbroken water columns are pulled upward through dead, lignified tracheids and vessel elements.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Phloem & The Pressure-Flow Hypothesis: Photosynthetic sugars (sucrose) are actively loaded into living phloem sieve tube elements at source leaves. The resulting osmotic pressure draws in water, creating high turgor pressure that drives bulk flow toward metabolic sinks (roots, fruits) where sugar is unloaded.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Cavitation and embolism risks during severe droughts, hydraulic limit governing maximum tree height.

## Key Terminology
- **Cohesion-Tension Theory**: Theory explaining how water is pulled upward through xylem against gravity by transpirational pull and water cohesion.
- **Transpiration**: Evaporation of water from plant surfaces, primarily occurring through microscopic leaf stomata.
- **Phloem Sieve Tubes**: Living conductive cells in vascular plants responsible for transporting organic nutrients throughout the plant body.
