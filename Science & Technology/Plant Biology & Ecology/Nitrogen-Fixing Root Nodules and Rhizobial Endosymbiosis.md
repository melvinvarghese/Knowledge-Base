# Nitrogen-Fixing Root Nodules and Rhizobial Endosymbiosis

## Overview
Rhizobial endosymbiosis is a mutualistic association between leguminous plants (Fabaceae: beans, clover, soybeans) and soil bacteria (*Rhizobium*) that biologically fixes atmospheric nitrogen.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Molecular Signaling & Infection Thread: Legume roots secrete flavonoid chemical signals into the soil. Compatible rhizobia respond by producing lipochitooligosaccharide **Nod factors**, which induce root hair curling. Bacteria enter the root via an infection thread, triggering cortical cell divisions that assemble specialized root nodules.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Nitrogenase & The Leghemoglobin Oxygen Buffer: Inside the nodule, bacteria differentiate into bacteroids that utilize the iron-molybdenum enzyme **nitrogenase** to reduce atmospheric $N_2$ into bioavailable ammonia ($NH_3$): $N_2 + 8 H^+ + 8 e^- + 16 \text{ ATP} \to 2 NH_3 + H_2 + 16 \text{ ADP} + 16 P_i$. Because nitrogenase is irreversibly poisoned by oxygen, host plants synthesize **leghemoglobin** (an oxygen-binding heme protein that turns root nodule interiors pink) to deliver oxygen to bacteroid respiration while keeping free oxygen levels low.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Foundation of crop rotation in agriculture, reducing reliance on fossil-fuel-intensive synthetic Haber-Bosch fertilizers.

## Key Terminology
- **Rhizobium**: Genus of soil bacteria that fix nitrogen after becoming established inside the root nodules of legumes.
- **Nitrogenase**: Enzyme complex that catalyzes the energy-intensive reduction of atmospheric nitrogen gas to ammonia.
- **Leghemoglobin**: Oxygen-binding hemoprotein found in the nitrogen-fixing root nodules of leguminous plants that buffers oxygen levels.
