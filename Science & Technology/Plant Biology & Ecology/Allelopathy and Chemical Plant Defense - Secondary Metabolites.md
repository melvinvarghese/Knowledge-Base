# Allelopathy and Chemical Plant Defense - Secondary Metabolites

## Overview
Allelopathy is an ecological phenomenon where plants produce biochemical secondary metabolites that inhibit the germination, growth, or survival of competing neighboring plants.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Chemistry of Allelochemicals: Unlike primary metabolites essential for cellular respiration, secondary metabolites serve ecological warfare functions: Black walnut trees (*Juglans nigra*) exude **juglone** from roots, which inhibits respiratory electron transport in sensitive neighboring solanaceous crops (tomatoes, peppers); Eucalyptus trees secrete volatile terpenoid oils that inhibit ground vegetation and fuel canopy wildfires.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Constitutive vs Induced Anti-Herbivore Defenses: Plants defend against herbivores using constitutive chemical toxins (alkaloids like nicotine and morphine, cyanogenic glycosides releasing hydrogen cyanide upon tissue crushing) and induced defenses mediated by the jasmonic acid pathway upon insect saliva detection.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Natural bio-herbicides for organic weed management, companion planting strategies in regenerative agriculture.

## Key Terminology
- **Allelopathy**: Biological phenomenon by which an organism produces biochemicals that influence the growth and survival of other organisms.
- **Juglone**: Allelopathic allelochemical produced by black walnut trees that inhibits cell respiration in neighboring plants.
- **Jasmonic Acid**: Plant hormone that regulates systemic defense responses against herbivore attack and pathogen infection.
