# Nutrient Cycling - The Global Phosphorus Cycle

## Overview
The Global Phosphorus Cycle is a sedimentary biogeochemical cycle circulating essential phosphorus—crucial for nucleic acids (DNA/RNA) and ATP—without a significant atmospheric gaseous phase.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Morphological Principles
Geological Weathering & Biotic Cycling: Phosphate rocks ($Ca_5(PO_4)_3OH$) weather slowly, releasing inorganic orthophosphate ($PO_4^{3-}$) into soils; plants assimilate phosphate into organic biomass, transferring it through food chains.

---

## 2. Physiological Dynamics & Evolutionary Analysis
Sedimentary Deposition & Eutrophication: Excess runoff carries phosphorus into lakes and oceans, depositing into marine sediments for millions of years until tectonic uplift; agricultural fertilizer runoff causes massive algal blooms and anoxic dead zones.

---

## 3. Ecological Significance & Scientific Applications
Agricultural sustainable fertilizer management, addressing global peak phosphorus mineral depletion, and lake restoration.

---

## Key Terms
- **Phosphorus Cycle**: Biogeochemical cycle of phosphorus through lithosphere, hydrosphere, and biosphere.
- **Eutrophication**: Water body nutrient enrichment causing explosive algal blooms, oxygen depletion, and fish kills.
- **Orthophosphate ($PO_4^{3-}$)**: Biologically available inorganic form of phosphorus absorbed by plant roots.
