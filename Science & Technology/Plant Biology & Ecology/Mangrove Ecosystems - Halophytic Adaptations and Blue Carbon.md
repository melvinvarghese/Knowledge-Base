# Mangrove Ecosystems - Halophytic Adaptations and Blue Carbon

## Overview
Mangrove forests are coastal intertidal wetland ecosystems dominated by halophytic trees that flourish in salt-saturated, oxygen-depleted marine environments.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Halophytic Morphological Adaptations: Mangroves survive coastal extremes through specialized traits: 1. Aerial breathing roots (pneumatophores and stilt roots) equipped with lenticels that absorb atmospheric oxygen during low tides; 2. Salt exclusion roots that filter out up to 90% of seawater salt ions via selective ultrafiltration; 3. Salt excretion glands on leaves that exude excess salt crystals.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Blue Carbon & Coastal Protection: Mangrove roots trap fine sediment in anaerobic waterlogged conditions, preventing organic matter decomposition and sequestering up to ten times more carbon per hectare than terrestrial rainforests (**Blue Carbon**). Their dense root complexes dissipate storm surge wave energy, protecting coastlines from hurricanes and tsunamis.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Nursery habitats for 75% of commercial tropical fish species, coastal carbon credit offset projects.

## Key Terminology
- **Halophyte**: Salt-tolerant plant adapted to growing in soil or waters of high salinity.
- **Pneumatophore**: Specialized aerial root structure emerging above water or mud enabling gas exchange in waterlogged soils.
- **Blue Carbon**: Carbon captured and stored by coastal and oceanic ecosystems, especially mangroves, tidal marshes, and seagrasses.
