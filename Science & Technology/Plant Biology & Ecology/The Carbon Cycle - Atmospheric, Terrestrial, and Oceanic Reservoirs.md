# The Carbon Cycle - Atmospheric, Terrestrial, and Oceanic Reservoirs

## Overview
The Global Carbon Cycle regulates Earth's climate and sustains life, circulating carbon across the atmosphere, terrestrial biosphere, oceans, and geological lithosphere.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Morphological Principles
Flux Kinetics & Atmospheric Equilibrium: Terrestrial photosynthesis ($120\text{ Gt C/yr}$) and autotrophic/heterotrophic respiration ($120\text{ Gt C/yr}$) maintain fast balance; oceanic dissolution ($CO_2 + H_2O \rightleftharpoons H_2CO_3 \rightleftharpoons HCO_3^- + H^+$) buffers planetary carbon.

---

## 2. Physiological Dynamics & Evolutionary Analysis
Anthropogenic Imbalance & Ocean Acidification: Fossil fuel combustion emits ~10 Gt C/yr, overwhelming natural sinks, raising atmospheric $CO_2$ past 420 ppm, and dropping oceanic surface pH by 0.1 units (a 30% increase in acidity).

---

## 3. Ecological Significance & Scientific Applications
Carbon capture and storage (CCS) engineering, global climate change policy and modeling, soil carbon sequestration practices.

---

## Key Terms
- **Carbon Flux**: Rate of carbon exchange between two global geochemical reservoirs over time.
- **Ocean Acidification**: Reduction in ocean pH caused by continuous uptake of anthropogenic $CO_2$ from the atmosphere.
- **Lithosphere**: Rigid outer rock crust of Earth containing the vast majority of global carbon as carbonate rocks.
