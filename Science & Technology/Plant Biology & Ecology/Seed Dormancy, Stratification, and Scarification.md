# Seed Dormancy, Stratification, and Scarification

## Overview
Seed Dormancy is an evolutionary adaptation that prevents viable seeds from germinating during unfavorable ecological conditions, broken by physiological stratification or physical scarification.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Morphological Principles
The ABA-Gibberellin Antagonism: Dormancy is maintained by elevated seed coat concentrations of abscisic acid (ABA); cold winter temperatures (cold stratification) degrade ABA and trigger gibberellin synthesis, promoting germination in spring.

---

## 2. Physiological Dynamics & Evolutionary Analysis
Physical Seed Coat Scarification: Hard impermeable seed coats (e.g., legumes) require mechanical abrasion, wildfire heat shock, or passage through animal digestive acids to permit water and oxygen imbibition.

---

## 3. Ecological Significance & Scientific Applications
Seed bank preservation protocols (Svalbard Global Seed Vault), agricultural crop uniform germination management.

---

## Key Terms
- **Dormancy**: State of temporary metabolic suspension in a viable seed preventing premature germination.
- **Stratification**: Process of subjecting seeds to cold moist conditions to overcome physiological dormancy.
- **Scarification**: Mechanical, thermal, or chemical treatment to breach an impermeable seed coat.
