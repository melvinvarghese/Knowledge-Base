# Gymnosperms vs Angiosperms - Cones, Flowers, and Seeds

## Overview
Vascular seed plants are divided into Gymnosperms ('naked seeds' borne on cone scales) and Angiosperms (flowering plants whose seeds develop enclosed within an ovary/fruit), representing a major evolutionary transition.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Morphological Principles
The Angiosperm Flower & Double Fertilization: Angiosperms evolved flowers for targeted animal pollination and unique **double fertilization**: one sperm fertilizes the egg ($2n$ embryo), while a second sperm fertilizes the central cell forming triploid ($3n$) nutrient endosperm.

---

## 2. Physiological Dynamics & Evolutionary Analysis
Gymnosperm Ovuliferous Cones: Conifers bear exposed ovules on open cone scales, relying predominantly on passive wind pollination (anemophily) with slow reproductive cycles extending over two years.

---

## 3. Ecological Significance & Scientific Applications
Commercial forestry timber production (softwoods vs hardwoods), global agricultural cereal grain cultivation.

---

## Key Terms
- **Angiosperm**: Flowering vascular plant whose seeds are enclosed within an ovary or mature fruit.
- **Gymnosperm**: Vascular plant bearing naked seeds exposed on scales, such as conifers and cycads.
- **Double Fertilization**: Fertilization mechanism in angiosperms producing a diploid zygote and a triploid nutritive endosperm.
