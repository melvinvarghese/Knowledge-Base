# Photosynthesis - Light Reactions and Z-Scheme

## Overview
The Light Reactions of photosynthesis occur within the thylakoid membranes of chloroplasts, converting solar photon energy into chemical potential energy in the form of ATP and NADPH.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Morphological Principles
The Z-Scheme and Water Photolysis: Photosystem II ($P_{680}$) absorbs a 680 nm photon, exciting an electron and oxidizing water: $2H_2O \rightarrow O_2 + 4H^+ + 4e^-$. Electrons cascade through plastoquinone and cytochrome $b_6f$ to Photosystem I ($P_{700}$).

---

## 2. Physiological Dynamics & Evolutionary Analysis
Chemiosmotic Photophosphorylation: Proton accumulation in the thylakoid lumen creates an electrochemical proton gradient ($\Delta\text{pH}$) driving ATP synthase to produce ATP from ADP and inorganic phosphate.

---

## 3. Ecological Significance & Scientific Applications
Artificial photosynthetic water splitting for green hydrogen production, photovoltaic dye-sensitized solar cells.

---

## Key Terms
- **Thylakoid**: Membrane-bound compartment inside chloroplasts where light reactions take place.
- **Photolysis**: Chemical decomposition of water molecules induced by light energy in Photosystem II.
- **Plastoquinone**: Lipophilic electron carrier shuttling electrons between PSII and cytochrome $b_6f$.
