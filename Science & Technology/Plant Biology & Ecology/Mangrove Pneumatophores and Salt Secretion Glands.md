# Mangrove Pneumatophores and Salt Secretion Glands

## Overview
Mangroves (Rhizophoraceae, Avicenniaceae) dominate coastal intertidal zones, thriving in anoxic, hypersaline sediments through aerial breathing roots (pneumatophores) and specialized root salt-ultrafiltration.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Morphological Principles
Pneumatophores and Lenticels: Black mangroves (*Avicennia*) send thousands of vertical pencil-like roots (pneumatophores) up through tidal mud, utilizing hydrophobic lenticel pores and internal aerenchyma sponge tissue to conduct oxygen to submerged roots.

---

## 2. Physiological Dynamics & Evolutionary Analysis
Salt Ultrafiltration and Foliar Secretion: Red mangroves (*Rhizophora*) filter out 90–97% of salt at the root surface via high hydrostatic transpirational pull; black mangroves exude excess salt crystals through specialized foliar salt glands.

---

## 3. Ecological Significance & Scientific Applications
Coastal shoreline tsunami and storm surge protection, blue carbon coastal sequestration ecosystems.

---

## Key Terms
- **Pneumatophore**: Specialized aerial root structure growing vertically upward out of water or mud for gas exchange.
- **Lenticel**: Porous tissue on woody stems and roots providing direct pathway for gas exchange.
- **Halophyte**: Plant adapted to growing in saline soils or waters with high salt concentrations.
