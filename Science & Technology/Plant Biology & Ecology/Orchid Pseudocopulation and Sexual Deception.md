# Orchid Pseudocopulation and Sexual Deception

## Overview
Sexually deceptive orchids (*Ophrys*) achieve highly specific pollination by physically, visually, and chemically mimicking female wasps or bees, inducing male insects into fruitless pseudocopulation.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Morphological Principles
Chemical Pheromone Mimicry: The floral lip (labellum) synthesizes complex hydrocarbons (alkenes and alkanes) that match the exact female sex pheromone blend of specific solitary wasp species (*Andrena*, *Campsoscolia*).

---

## 2. Physiological Dynamics & Evolutionary Analysis
Morphological & Tactile Realism: The labellum mimics female insect body contours, hair texture, and UV coloration; as the male attempts to mate, pollen packets (**pollinia**) glue themselves to his head or abdomen for transport to the next flower.

---

## 3. Ecological Significance & Scientific Applications
Coevolutionary evolutionary arms race studies, semiochemical insect pest pheromone lure development.

---

## Key Terms
- **Pseudocopulation**: Attempted copulation by a male insect with a flower mimicking a female conspecific.
- **Labellum**: Modified central petal of an orchid flower specialized for attracting pollinators.
- **Pollinium**: Cohesive mass of thousands of pollen grains transported as a single unit by pollinators.
