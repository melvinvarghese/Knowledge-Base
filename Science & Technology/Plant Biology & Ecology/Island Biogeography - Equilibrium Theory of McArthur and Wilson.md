# Island Biogeography - Equilibrium Theory of McArthur and Wilson

## Overview
The Equilibrium Theory of Island Biogeography, proposed by Robert MacArthur and E.O. Wilson (1967), models species richness on islands as a dynamic equilibrium between immigration and extinction rates.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Morphological Principles
The Distance and Area Effects: Immigration rates drop as distance from the mainland increases; extinction rates drop as island surface area increases (larger islands sustain larger populations with lower stochastic extinction risk).

---

## 2. Physiological Dynamics & Evolutionary Analysis
Equilibrium Species Richness ($\hat{S}$): Equilibrium point occurs where immigration curve intersects extinction curve. Species identity continues turning over dynamically while total species count remains constant.

---

## 3. Ecological Significance & Scientific Applications
Conservation biological reserve design (SLOSS debate: Single Large or Several Small reserves), wildlife habitat corridor planning.

---

## Key Terms
- **Island Biogeography**: Field of ecology examining factors affecting species richness of isolated natural communities.
- **Species Turnover**: Rate at which existing species go extinct and are replaced by newly colonizing species.
- **Habitat Corridor**: Strip of natural habitat connecting otherwise fragmented wildlife populations.
