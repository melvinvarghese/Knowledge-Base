# Plant Vascular Systems - Xylem Cohesion-Tension Theory

## Overview
The Cohesion-Tension Theory explains how terrestrial plants transport water and dissolved inorganic minerals upward through the xylem against gravity from roots to canopy leaves without mechanical pumps.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Morphological Principles
Transpirational Pull & Water Cohesion: Water evaporating from leaf mesophyll pores creates a negative hydrostatic capillary tension (suction up to -2 MPa); hydrogen bonding cohesion maintains continuous unbroken water columns in xylem vessels.

---

## 2. Physiological Dynamics & Evolutionary Analysis
Tracheids and Vessel Elements: Dead hollow tracheary elements reinforced with lignified secondary cell walls resist inward collapse under high negative xylem pressure tensions.

---

## 3. Ecological Significance & Scientific Applications
Biomimetic microfluidic capillary pump design, agricultural drought vulnerability acoustic cavitation monitoring.

---

## Key Terms
- **Xylem**: Plant vascular tissue responsible for upward water and mineral transport.
- **Transpirational Pull**: Negative hydrostatic tension generated by leaf evaporation drawing water upward.
- **Cavitation**: Breakdown of xylem water columns caused by air bubble entry (embolism) under drought stress.
