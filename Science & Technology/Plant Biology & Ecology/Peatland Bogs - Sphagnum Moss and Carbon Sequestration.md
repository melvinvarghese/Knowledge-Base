# Peatland Bogs - Sphagnum Moss and Carbon Sequestration

## Overview
Peatland Bogs, dominated by *Sphagnum* mosses, cover only 3% of Earth's land surface yet store over 30% of global soil carbon—more carbon than all terrestrial forests combined.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Morphological Principles
Cation Exchange & Acidification: *Sphagnum* cell walls possess abundant polyuronic acids that actively exchange hydrogen ions ($H^+$) for calcium and magnesium cations, lowering water table pH down to 3.5–4.5.

---

## 2. Physiological Dynamics & Evolutionary Analysis
Anoxic Phenolic Inhibition of Decay: Waterlogged anoxia paired with extreme acidity and release of **sphagnan** pectin-like polymers completely inhibits microbial decomposition, accumulating undecomposed organic peat over millennia.

---

## 3. Ecological Significance & Scientific Applications
Global climate mitigation peatland restoration, archival preservation of archaeological bog bodies (Tollund Man).

---

## Key Terms
- **Sphagnum**: Genus of peat mosses creating cold, acidic, waterlogged wetland bog environments.
- **Peat**: Accumulated deposit of dead, partially decayed plant matter preserved in waterlogged anoxia.
- **Sphagnan**: Pectin-like polysaccharide released by Sphagnum moss that immobilizes microbial decomposers.
