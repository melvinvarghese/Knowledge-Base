# Biological Invasions - The Enemy Release Hypothesis and Biodiversity Loss

## Overview
Invasive alien species are non-native organisms introduced beyond their natural historical range that proliferate rapidly, displacing native taxa and altering ecosystem functions.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Enemy Release Hypothesis (ERH): Posits that introduced species proliferate because they escape the co-evolved specialized herbivores, parasites, predators, and pathogens that kept their population numbers checked in their native habitat, allowing them to reallocate energy from defense to competitive growth and reproduction.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Ecological & Economic Devastation: Invasive species (e.g., zebra mussels in North American lakes, brown tree snakes in Guam, kudzu vines in the American South) drive native extinctions through predation, competition, novel disease transmission, and habitat modification.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Global biosecurity phytosanitary protocols, classical biological control using co-evolved natural enemies.

## Key Terminology
- **Invasive Species**: Non-native species whose introduction causes or is likely to cause economic or environmental harm or harm to human health.
- **Enemy Release Hypothesis**: Hypothesis stating that introduced species thrive because they are freed from the natural regulators of their home range.
- **Biosecurity**: Procedures and measures designed to protect the population and environment against harmful biological agents.
