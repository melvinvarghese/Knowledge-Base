# Phloem Translocation and Pressure-Flow Hypothesis

## Overview
The Pressure-Flow Hypothesis, formulated by Ernst Münch, describes the active translocation of photosynthetic sugars (chiefly sucrose) through the phloem from source tissues to metabolic sinks.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Morphological Principles
Osmotically Generated Hydrostatic Pressure: Proton-sucrose symporters actively load sucrose into sieve tube elements at source leaves, drawing in water by osmosis from adjacent xylem and generating high turgor pressure (~1.5 MPa).

---

## 2. Physiological Dynamics & Evolutionary Analysis
Sink Unloading & Flow: At sink tissues (roots, fruits), sucrose is actively unloaded and converted to starch; water exits by osmosis back to xylem, establishing a continuous bulk pressure gradient driving mass flow.

---

## 3. Ecological Significance & Scientific Applications
Optimizing agricultural crop fruit filling, modeling plant carbon allocation under changing climatic conditions.

---

## Key Terms
- **Phloem**: Living vascular tissue transporting soluble organic nutrients throughout the plant body.
- **Sieve Tube Element**: Specialized conductive cell in phloem lacking a nucleus, connected via perforated sieve plates.
- **Companion Cell**: Metabolically active parenchymal cell supporting sieve tube nutrient transport.
