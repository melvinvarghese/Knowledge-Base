# Sundew Mucilage Glands and Rapid Thigmonasty

## Overview
Sundews (*Drosera*) capture insect prey using glistening glandular tentacles tipped with sticky, viscoelastic mucilage droplets, exhibiting rapid thigmonastic leaf curling to maximize digestive contact.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Morphological Principles
Viscoelastic Mucilage Secretion: Tentacle glands secrete polysaccharide-rich acidic mucilage exhibiting shear-thinning and high extensional viscosity, trapping insects in sticky elastic threads.

---

## 2. Physiological Dynamics & Evolutionary Analysis
Thigmonastic Bending: Mechanical stimulation of gland cells triggers electrical membrane depolarization and localized asymmetric auxin transport, prompting the entire leaf blade to curl around the trapped prey within minutes.

---

## 3. Ecological Significance & Scientific Applications
Bio-adhesives for surgical tissue repair, dynamic mechanical soft hydrogel actuators.

---

## Key Terms
- **Mucilage**: Gelatinous substance consisting of protein and polysaccharides produced by plant glands.
- **Thigmonasty**: Non-directional plant movement in response to touch or mechanical contact.
- **Tentacle**: Specialized glandular hair on carnivorous sundew leaves secreting digestive fluids.
