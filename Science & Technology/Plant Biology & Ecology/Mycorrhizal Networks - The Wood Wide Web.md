# Mycorrhizal Networks - The Wood Wide Web

## Overview
Mycorrhizal Networks are underground mutualistic networks connecting plant root systems via fungal mycelia, facilitating nutrient exchange and biochemical signaling across entire forest communities.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Morphological Principles
Mutualistic Exchange Thermodynamics: Fungi supply host plants with otherwise inaccessible soil phosphorus and nitrogen harvested by expansive hyphal surface area; in return, plants translocate 10–30% of photosynthetic carbohydrates and lipids to fungi.

---

## 2. Physiological Dynamics & Evolutionary Analysis
Arbuscular vs Ectomycorrhizae: Arbuscular mycorrhizae (AMF, Glomeromycota) penetrate cortical root cell walls forming branched arbuscules; ectomycorrhizae (EMF) form an outer fungal sheath (Hartig net) between forest tree root cells.

---

## 3. Ecological Significance & Scientific Applications
Forest ecosystem resilience, carbon sequestration in subterranean glomalin soil proteins, organic sustainable agriculture.

---

## Key Terms
- **Mycorrhiza**: Symbiotic association between a fungus and the root system of a vascular host plant.
- **Arbuscule**: Branched fungal structure formed inside a plant root cell specialized for nutrient exchange.
- **Glomalin**: Stable, sticky glycoprotein produced by mycorrhizal fungi that stores soil organic carbon.
