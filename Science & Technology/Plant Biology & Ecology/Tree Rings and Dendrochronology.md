# Tree Rings and Dendrochronology

> **The Big Idea:** Trees record environmental history inside their trunks. Counting and analyzing annual growth rings reveals ancient droughts, volcanic eruptions, solar storms, and wildfires.

---

## 🪵 Reading the Trunk
* **Earlywood vs. Latewood:** Wide, light rings form during rapid spring growth; narrow, dark rings form during late summer drought.
* **Cross-Dating:** Matching overlapping ring patterns across living and dead ancient wood creates continuous climate records stretching back over 10,000 years!
* **Bristlecone Pines:** The oldest individual living non-clonal trees on Earth, surviving over 4,800 years in harsh California mountain ranges.
