# Ocean Acidification and Marine Coral Reef Bleaching

## Overview
Ocean Acidification, dubbed 'the other $CO_2$ problem', occurs as the global oceans absorb roughly 30% of anthropogenic atmospheric carbon dioxide emissions.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Chemical Carbonate Equilibrium: Dissolved $CO_2$ reacts with seawater to form carbonic acid: $CO_2 + H_2O \rightleftharpoons H_2CO_3 \rightleftharpoons H^+ + HCO_3^-$. The surplus hydrogen ions ($H^+$) lower ocean pH (having dropped by 0.1 units since industrialization, representing a 30% increase in acidity) and strip carbonate ions ($CO_3^{2-}$): $H^+ + CO_3^{2-} \rightleftharpoons HCO_3^-$, reducing calcium carbonate saturation state ($\Omega$).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Coral Bleaching & Calcification Crisis: Marine calcifiers (corals, pteropods, shellfish) cannot build aragonite skeletons. Elevated sea surface temperatures cause thermal stress, prompting corals to expel their symbiotic photosynthetic zooxanthellae dinoflagellates, causing coral bleaching and reef mortality.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Threat to marine biodiversity (reefs support 25% of all marine species), loss of coastal storm surge protection barriers.

## Key Terminology
- **Ocean Acidification**: Ongoing decrease in the pH of the Earth's oceans caused by the uptake of anthropogenic carbon dioxide from the atmosphere.
- **Aragonite Saturation**: Measure of the thermodynamic availability of aragonite calcium carbonate needed by marine organisms to build shells.
- **Coral Bleaching**: Process where stressed corals expel their symbiotic colorful algae (*zooxanthellae*), turning white and risking starvation.
