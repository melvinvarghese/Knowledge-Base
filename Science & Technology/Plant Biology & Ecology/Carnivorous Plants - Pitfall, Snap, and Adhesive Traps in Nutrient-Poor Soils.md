# Carnivorous Plants - Pitfall, Snap, and Adhesive Traps in Nutrient-Poor Soils

## Overview
Carnivorous plants are photosynthetic vascular plants that have evolved predatory adaptations to capture and digest animals (primarily insects) to acquire limiting nitrogen and phosphorus in acidic, nutrient-deficient bogs.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Trap Biomechanics: 1. Pitfall traps (*Nepenthes*, *Sarracenia* pitcher plants): Wax-lined slippery conduits filled with digestive enzymes and rainwater; 2. Snap traps (*Dionaea muscipula* Venus flytrap): Rapid bistable leaf closure triggered by action potentials when insect prey touches two sensitive trigger hairs within 20 seconds, sealing the leaf into an external digestive stomach; 3. Adhesive traps (*Drosera* sundews): Sticky glandular trichomes secreting mucilage.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Cost-Benefit Energetics of Carnivory: Carnivorous leaves are structurally expensive and photosynthetically less efficient than standard leaves. Therefore, carnivory is an evolutionary advantage strictly in sun-drenched, waterlogged habitats where soil nitrogen is scarce.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Biomechanics of rapid non-muscular plant movements, evolutionary convergence across divergent angiosperm orders.

## Key Terminology
- **Pitfall Trap**: Carnivorous plant leaf modified into a slippery pitcher-like structure containing digestive fluids to drown prey.
- **Snap Trap**: Active trapping mechanism utilizing rapid bistable mechanical snap-buckling triggered by electrical action potentials.
- **Trichome**: Hair-like glandular outgrowth on plant epidermal surfaces secreting sticky digestive enzymes in sundews.
