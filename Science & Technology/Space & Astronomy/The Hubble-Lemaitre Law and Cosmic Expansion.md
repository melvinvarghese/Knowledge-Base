# The Hubble-Lemaitre Law and Cosmic Expansion

## Overview
The Hubble-Lemaître Law describes the observation that galaxies are receding from Earth at speeds directly proportional to their distance, providing direct evidence for the metric expansion of the universe.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
The Linear Velocity-Distance Formula: $v = H_0 \cdot d$, where $v$ is recession velocity ($km/s$), $d$ is proper distance ($Mpc$), and $H_0$ is the Hubble Constant (~70 km/s/Mpc).

---

## 2. Structural Analysis & Mathematical Modeling
The Hubble Tension: Discrepancy between early-universe Cosmic Microwave Background measurements (Planck: $67.4 \pm 0.5\text{ km/s/Mpc}$) and late-universe Cepheid-Type Ia supernova measurements (SH0ES: $73.0 \pm 1.0\text{ km/s/Mpc}$) at $>5\sigma$ statistical significance.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Scientific Impact
Determining the age of the universe ($t_0 \approx 1/H_0 \approx 13.8\text{ billion years}$), cosmic scale factor evolution ($a(t)$).

---

## Key Terms
- **Hubble Constant ($H_0$)**: Current expansion rate of the universe measuring velocity increase per megaparsec.
- **Metric Expansion**: Increase in physical distance between parts of the universe over time governed by the FLRW metric.
- **Hubble Tension**: Statistically significant divergence between local and early-universe measurements of $H_0$.
