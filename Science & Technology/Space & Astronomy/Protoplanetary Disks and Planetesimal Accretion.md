# Protoplanetary Disks and Planetesimal Accretion

## Overview
Protoplanetary Disks are rotating circumstellar disks of dense gas and sub-micron dust grains surrounding newborn stars, within which planetary systems form through **planetesimal accretion**.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
The Core Accretion Model: Sub-micron dust grains settle to the midplane, sticking via electrostatic Van der Waals forces to form centimeter pebbles; streaming instabilities concentrate pebbles into kilometer-sized planetesimals that merge gravitationally into planetary cores.

---

## 2. Structural Analysis & Mathematical Modeling
The Ice/Frost Line: Volatile compounds ($H_2O, CO_2, CH_4$) can condense into solid ice grains only beyond the snow line (~3 AU), increasing solid surface density fourfold and enabling rapid core accretion to form giant gas planets (Jupiter) before the gas disk disperses.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Scientific Impact
ALMA high-resolution imaging of concentric gaps and rings carved by forming protoplanets (e.g., HL Tauri), planetary system architecture modeling.

---

## Key Terms
- **Protoplanetary Disk**: Rotating circumstellar disk of gas and dust from which planets accrete around newborn stars.
- **Frost Line**: Specific distance from a protostar beyond which water and volatiles condense into solid ice grains.
- **Streaming Instability**: Aerodynamic drag phenomenon concentrating dust pebbles into dense filaments triggering gravitational collapse.
