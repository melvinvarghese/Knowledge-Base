# Chandrasekhar Mass Limit and Type Ia Supernovae

## Overview
The Chandrasekhar Mass Limit (~$1.44 M_\odot$) is the maximum theoretical mass supportable by electron degeneracy pressure in a non-rotating white dwarf before gravitational collapse.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
Relativistic Electron Degeneracy Pressure: Quantum Fermi-Dirac statistics dictate non-relativistic degeneracy pressure ($P \propto \rho^{5/3}$); when electron speeds approach $c$, relativistic degeneracy softens to $P \propto \rho^{4/3}$, which cannot resist gravitational contraction ($P_{\text{grav}} \propto \rho^{4/3}$): $M_{\text{Ch}} \approx \frac{\omega_3^0}{4\pi} \left(\frac{hc}{G}\right)^{3/2} \left(\frac{1}{\mu_e m_u}\right)^2 \approx 1.44 M_\odot$.

---

## 2. Structural Analysis & Mathematical Modeling
Type Ia Supernova Thermonuclear Runaway: A carbon-oxygen white dwarf accreting mass from a binary companion toward $M_{\text{Ch}}$ undergoes unconstrained carbon fusion detonation, releasing $10^{44}\text{ Joules}$ with no remnant core.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Scientific Impact
Standard candle cosmological distance measurement, discovery of the accelerating universe (Dark Energy).

---

## Key Terms
- **Chandrasekhar Limit**: Upper mass limit (~1.44 solar masses) for a stable electron-degenerate white dwarf.
- **Degeneracy Pressure**: Quantum mechanical pressure exerted by fermions obeying the Pauli Exclusion Principle.
- **Type Ia Supernova**: Thermonuclear explosion of a carbon-oxygen white dwarf reaching critical mass.
