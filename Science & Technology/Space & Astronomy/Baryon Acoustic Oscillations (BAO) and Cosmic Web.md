# Baryon Acoustic Oscillations (BAO) and Cosmic Web

## Overview
Baryon Acoustic Oscillations (BAO) are regular, periodic density fluctuations in the distribution of visible galaxies, functioning as a primordial 'standard ruler' imprinted by acoustic sound waves in the early plasma universe.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
Sound Horizon Freeze-Out: Prior to recombination (380,000 years), photon radiation pressure generated acoustic sound waves traveling at $c_s = c/\sqrt{3(1 + 3\rho_b/4\rho_\gamma)}$; at decoupling, sound waves froze at a characteristic sound horizon radius of **147 megaparsecs (~480 million light-years)**.

---

## 2. Structural Analysis & Mathematical Modeling
The Cosmic Web Large-Scale Architecture: Galaxies preferentially cluster in spherical shells separated by 147 Mpc, organizing into giant filaments, dense nodes, and expansive cosmic voids.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Scientific Impact
High-precision geometric measurement of cosmic expansion history, independent constraint on dark energy equation of state.

---

## Key Terms
- **Sound Horizon**: Maximum distance acoustic sound waves could travel through primordial plasma before decoupling.
- **Cosmic Web**: Large-scale network of interconnected galaxy filaments separated by vast cosmic voids.
- **Recombination**: Epoch when free electrons and protons bound into neutral hydrogen atoms, decoupling the CMB.
