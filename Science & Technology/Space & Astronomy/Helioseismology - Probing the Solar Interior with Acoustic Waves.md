# Helioseismology - Probing the Solar Interior with Acoustic Waves

## Overview
Helioseismology probes the opaque interior structure of the Sun by analyzing resonant acoustic sound waves ($p$-modes) propagating through the solar plasma, detected as Doppler velocity oscillations on the photosphere.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
Acoustic Wave Trapping & Refraction: Sound speed increases with depth ($c_s \propto \sqrt{T}$); downward-propagating acoustic waves refract back toward the surface, while the steep density drop at the photosphere reflects waves downward, forming resonant 3D standing wave cavities.

---

## 2. Structural Analysis & Mathematical Modeling
Inferring Internal Solar Rotation: Frequency splitting of acoustic modes by rotation and magnetic fields revealed that the convective zone rotates differentially while the radiative zone rotates as a solid rigid body, locating the tachocline dynamo.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Scientific Impact
Solar standard model validation, resolving the solar neutrino problem (confirming neutrino oscillations), far-side active region imaging.

---

## Key Terms
- **Helioseismology**: Study of solar interior structure and dynamics using acoustic surface oscillations.
- **p-Mode**: Acoustic pressure wave in the solar interior where pressure acts as the restoring force.
- **Doppler Velocity Imaging**: Measurement of surface plasma motion toward and away from the observer using spectral line shifts.
