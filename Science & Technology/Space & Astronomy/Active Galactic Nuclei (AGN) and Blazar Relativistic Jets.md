# Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN) and Blazar Relativistic Jets

## Overview
Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN) are compact, luminous regions at the centers of galaxies powered by the accretion of matter onto **supermassive black holes ($10^6–10^{10} M_\odot$)**, outshining the host galaxy.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
Unified Model of AGN: An accretion disk surrounded by an obscuring toroidal dusty molecular torus; broad-line regions (BLR) and narrow-line regions (NLR). Observed appearance (Quasar, Seyfert, Radio Galaxy, Blazar) depends on orientation viewing angle relative to the line of sight.

---

## 2. Structural Analysis & Mathematical Modeling
Blazar Relativistic Beaming: When a collimated relativistic plasma jet is pointed directly toward Earth, special relativistic Doppler beaming amplifies apparent luminosity ($I \propto \delta^4$) and compresses variability timescales down to minutes, producing apparent superluminal motion.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Scientific Impact
Galactic feedback quenching star formation, origin of ultra-high-energy cosmic rays, gravitational wave event host galaxies.

---

## Key Terms
- **Active Galactic Nucleus (AGN)**: Luminous central core of a galaxy powered by supermassive black hole accretion.
- **Relativistic Beaming**: Concentration of emitted electromagnetic radiation in the forward direction of relativistic motion.
- **Blazar**: Active galactic nucleus with its relativistic jet pointed directly at Earth.
