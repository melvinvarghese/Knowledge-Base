# Hawking Radiation and Black Hole Thermodynamics

## Overview
Hawking Radiation is thermal blackbody radiation predicted by Stephen Hawking (1974) to be emitted by black holes due to quantum field fluctuations near the event horizon, leading to black hole evaporation.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
The Hawking Temperature Formula: Combining quantum field theory in curved spacetime with the event horizon surface gravity ($\kappa$):

$$T_H = \frac{\hbar c^3}{8\pi G M k_B}$$

Notice that Hawking temperature is inversely proportional to black hole mass ($T_H \propto 1/M$), meaning smaller black holes radiate more violently.

---

## 2. Structural Analysis & Mathematical Modeling
The Black Hole Information Paradox: The four laws of black hole mechanics mirror thermodynamics (Bekenstein-Hawking entropy: $S_{\text{BH}} = \frac{k_B c^3 A}{4 G \hbar}$); pure quantum states evaporating into mixed thermal states challenges quantum unitarity.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Scientific Impact
Quantum gravity unification theories, holographic principle, string theory black hole microstates.

---

## Key Terms
- **Hawking Radiation**: Blackbody radiation released by black holes due to quantum vacuum effects near the horizon.
- **Bekenstein-Hawking Entropy**: Entropy proportional to the surface area of a black hole's event horizon.
- **Information Paradox**: Apparent contradiction between quantum mechanics unitarity and black hole thermal evaporation.
