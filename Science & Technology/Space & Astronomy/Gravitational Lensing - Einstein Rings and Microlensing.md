# Gravitational Lensing - Einstein Rings and Microlensing

## Overview
Gravitational Lensing occurs when the intense gravitational field of a foreground massive body (galaxy, cluster, star) bends light rays from a distant background source, producing magnified images, arcs, and **Einstein rings**.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
Lens Equation & Angular Deflection: The deflection angle $\hat{\alpha} = \frac{4GM}{c^2 \xi}$ maps source position ($\beta$) to observed image positions ($\theta$). Perfect collinear alignment generates a circular Einstein ring with radius $\theta_E = \sqrt{\frac{4GM}{c^2}\frac{D_{ls}}{D_l D_s}}$.

---

## 2. Structural Analysis & Mathematical Modeling
Strong, Weak, and Microlensing Regimes: Strong lensing produces multiple resolved distorted arcs; weak lensing measures minute statistical shear distortions across thousands of galaxies to map invisible dark matter; microlensing detects temporary amplification of stellar brightness.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Scientific Impact
Detecting rogue exoplanets via gravitational microlensing, mapping dark matter distributions in galaxy clusters (Bullet Cluster), Hubble constant time-delay cosmography.

---

## Key Terms
- **Einstein Ring**: Deformation of light from a distant source into a complete ring around a gravitational lens.
- **Gravitational Shear**: Coherent distortion of background galaxy shapes induced by intervening weak gravitational lensing.
- **Microlensing**: Transient photometric magnification of background starlight caused by an intervening stellar-mass object.
