# Near-Earth Asteroids (NEAs) and Planetary Defense Strategies

## Overview
Near-Earth Asteroids (NEAs) are rocky bodies with perihelion distances $q \le 1.3	ext{ AU}$, monitored as Potential Impact Hazards and targeted by **Planetary Defense** kinetic deflection architectures.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
Classification by Orbital Geometry: 1. Atiras (interior to Earth's orbit); 2. Atens (semi-major axis $<1	ext{ AU}$, crossing Earth); 3. Apollos (semi-major axis $>1	ext{ AU}$, Earth-crossing); 4. Amors (outer approaches).

---

## 2. Structural Analysis & Mathematical Modeling
Kinetic Impact Deflection & The NASA DART Mission: In 2022, NASA's DART spacecraft slammed into Dimorphos at 6.6 km/s, transferring momentum ($\Delta p = eta m v$); momentum enhancement factor $eta pprox 3.6$ from high-velocity crater ejecta shortened the asteroid's orbital period by 33 minutes.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Scientific Impact
Civil defense impact mitigation strategies, optical automated sky surveys (Pan-STARRS, Vera C. Rubin Observatory).

---

## Key Terms
- **Kinetic Impactor**: Spacecraft engineered to deflect an asteroid's orbital trajectory through high-velocity collision.
- **Momentum Enhancement Factor ($\beta$)**: Ratio of total momentum transferred to the momentum of the impactor itself.
- **Potentially Hazardous Asteroid (PHA)**: Asteroid with a minimum orbital intersection distance $<0.05	ext{ AU}$ and diameter $>140	ext{ m}$.
