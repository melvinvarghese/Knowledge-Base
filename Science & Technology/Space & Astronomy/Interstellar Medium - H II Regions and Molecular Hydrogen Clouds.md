# Interstellar Medium - H II Regions and Molecular Hydrogen Clouds

## Overview
The Interstellar Medium (ISM) is the gas and microscopic dust permeating space between stars within a galaxy, serving as the material reservoir for new star formation.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
The Multi-Phase ISM: 1. Hot Ionized Medium (supernova remnants, $10^6	ext{ K}$); 2. Warm Ionized & Neutral Medium; 3. Giant Molecular Clouds (GMCs: cold dense $H_2$ gas at 10–20 K, shielded from UV radiation by silicate/carbon dust grains).

## 3. Real-World Applications & Scientific Impact

## Key Terms
- **H II Region**: Interstellar cloud of glowing gas and plasma in which hydrogen is ionized.
- **Giant Molecular Cloud (GMC)**: Massive, dense, cold interstellar cloud where molecular hydrogen forms and stars are born.
- **Strömgren Radius**: Theoretical boundary of an ionization sphere surrounding a hot luminous star in a hydrogen cloud.
