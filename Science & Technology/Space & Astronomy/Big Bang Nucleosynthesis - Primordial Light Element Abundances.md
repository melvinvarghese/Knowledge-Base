# Big Bang Nucleosynthesis - Primordial Light Element Abundances

## Overview
Big Bang Nucleosynthesis (BBN) describes the production of light atomic nuclei during the first three minutes following the Big Bang ($T \sim 10^9\text{ K}$), establishing the primordial chemical composition of the universe.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
The Neutron-to-Proton Freeze-Out: At $t \sim 1\text{ s}$, weak interactions freeze out at $T \approx 0.8\text{ MeV}$ with $n/p \approx 1/6$; free neutron decay lowers the ratio to $n/p \approx 1/7$ before the 'deuterium bottleneck' breaks, fusing virtually all free neutrons into Helium-4.

---

## 2. Structural Analysis & Mathematical Modeling
Primordial Abundance Fractions: BBN predicts a primordial universe composed of **75% Hydrogen ($^1H$), 25% Helium ($^4He$)**, with trace quantities of Deuterium ($^2H \sim 10^{-5}$), Helium-3 ($^3He$), and Lithium-7 ($^7Li$).

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Scientific Impact
Determining the cosmic baryon density parameter ($\Omega_b \approx 0.048$), proving non-baryonic dark matter must exist.

---

## Key Terms
- **Deuterium Bottleneck**: Delay in nucleosynthesis until cosmic temperature cooled below deuterium photodisintegration threshold.
- **Freeze-Out**: Point where the expansion rate of the universe exceeds a nuclear or particle interaction rate.
- **Primordial Abundance**: Relative elemental ratio produced directly in the early universe prior to stellar nucleosynthesis.
