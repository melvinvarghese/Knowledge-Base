# Exoplanet Atmospheres - Transmission Spectroscopy and Biosignatures

## Overview
Transmission Spectroscopy analyzes starlight filtering through the thin atmospheric annulus of an exoplanet during transit, identifying chemical atmospheric constituents and potential **biosignatures**.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
Transit Depth & Atmospheric Scale Height: Apparent transit depth varies with wavelength: $\Delta D(\lambda) \propto 2 R_p H / R_*^2$, where $H = k_B T / (\mu g)$ is the atmospheric scale height. Absorbing molecules (water, methane, $CO_2$) make the planet appear larger at specific wavelengths.

---

## 2. Structural Analysis & Mathematical Modeling
Biosignature Gas Disequilibrium: The simultaneous detection of atmospheric chemical species in thermodynamic disequilibrium (e.g., abundant Oxygen/Ozone paired with Methane) serves as primary evidence of biological metabolic activity, as they rapidly react and destroy each other without continuous biological replenishment.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Scientific Impact
James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) characterization of TRAPPIST-1 terrestrial exoplanet atmospheres, habitability assessment.

---

## Key Terms
- **Transmission Spectroscopy**: Spectroscopic method measuring wavelength-dependent starlight absorption through an exoplanet atmosphere.
- **Scale Height ($H$)**: Vertical distance over which atmospheric pressure and density decrease by a factor of $e$.
- **Chemical Disequilibrium**: Coexistence of reacting gases in an atmosphere indicating continuous non-equilibrium replenishment.
