# Tidal Disruption Events (TDEs) by Supermassive Black Holes

## Overview
A Tidal Disruption Event (TDE) occurs when a star wanders too close to a supermassive black hole, where tidal gravitational forces exceed the star's self-gravity, ripping the star apart into an elongated stream of stellar debris.

---

## 2. Structural Analysis & Mathematical Modeling
Debris Stream Circularization & Accretion Flare: Approximately half of the stellar mass is ejected into parabolic trajectories; the remaining bound debris streams wrap around the black hole, colliding via relativistic precession to form a temporary accretion disk emitting optical, UV, and soft X-ray flares peaking at Eddington luminosity.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Scientific Impact
Probing dormant supermassive black holes in distant inactive galaxies, measuring black hole spin through relativistic precession.

---

## Key Terms
- **Tidal Disruption Event (TDE)**: Transient astronomical flare produced when a star is gravitationally shredded by a supermassive black hole.
- **Tidal Radius ($R_t$)**: Critical distance within which tidal gravitational shear exceeds a star's self-gravitational cohesion.
- **Hills Limit**: Maximum black hole mass beyond which a main-sequence star is swallowed without tidal disruption.
