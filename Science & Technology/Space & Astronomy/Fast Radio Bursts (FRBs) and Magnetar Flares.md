# Fast Radio Bursts (FRBs) and Magnetar Flares

## Overview
Fast Radio Bursts (FRBs) are millisecond-duration bursts of intense, coherent radio emission originating predominantly from extragalactic distances, releasing as much energy in a few milliseconds as the Sun emits in days.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
Plasma Dispersion Measure (DM): Interstellar and intergalactic free electrons delay lower-frequency radio waves; measuring the dispersion delay ($\Delta t \propto 	ext{DM} / 
u^2$) reveals the total integrated electron column density along the cosmic line of sight.

---

## 2. Structural Analysis & Mathematical Modeling
The Magnetar Flare Engine: The 2020 discovery of an FRB from galactic magnetar SGR 1935+2154 confirmed that crustal magnetic reconnection events and starquakes in extreme magnetic fields ($B > 10^{14}	ext{ Gauss}$) drive relativistic shocks and coherent synchrotron maser emission.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Scientific Impact
Solving the 'missing baryon problem' by measuring intergalactic gas density using FRB dispersion, probing extragalactic magnetic fields.

---

## Key Terms
- **Fast Radio Burst (FRB)**: Transient millisecond-duration high-energy cosmic radio pulse of extragalactic origin.
- **Dispersion Measure (DM)**: Column density of free electrons between an astronomical source and the observer.
- **Magnetar**: Type of neutron star possessing an extreme magnetic field up to $10^{15}$ Gauss.
