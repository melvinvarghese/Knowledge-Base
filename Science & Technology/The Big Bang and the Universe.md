# The Big Bang and the Universe

> **The Big Idea:** 13.8 billion years ago, everything in existence—all matter, space, and time itself—erupted from an unimaginably hot, dense point (a Singularity). The universe didn't explode *into* space; space itself has been expanding outward ever since.

## 🔍 The 3 Irrefutable Proofs of the Big Bang

How do scientists know this actually happened billions of years ago?

### 1. Redshift & Hubble's Law (Galaxies Flying Apart)
In 1929, Edwin Hubble pointed his telescope at distant galaxies and noticed their light waves were stretched toward the red end of the spectrum (**Redshift**). Just like a siren drops in pitch as an ambulance races away (Doppler Effect), light stretches as galaxies move away. The farther a galaxy is, the faster it is flying away from us.

### 2. The Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB)
In 1965, scientists accidentally detected a faint, uniform radio hiss coming from every single direction in the night sky. It was the cooled-down afterglow of the Big Bang itself—light released 380,000 years after the start of time.

### 3. Elemental Abundance
Physics calculations predicted that a Big Bang fireball should create roughly **75% Hydrogen and 25% Helium**. When astronomers inspect ancient clouds of gas across the universe, the ratio matches the math perfectly.

## 🌌 The Dark Mysteries: Dark Matter & Dark Energy

What is the universe actually made of? Ordinary matter (stars, planets, trees, humans) is only a tiny fraction:

```
┌───────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  DARK ENERGY (68%): Mysteriously pushing the  │
│                     universe to expand faster │
├───────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  DARK MATTER (27%): Invisible gravitational   │
│                     glue holding galaxies     │
├───────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  NORMAL MATTER (5%): Stars, gas, and humans   │
└───────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## 🛠️ Practical Takeaway

* Space has no "center" or "edge." Every point in space is expanding away from every other point, like raisins moving apart inside a baking loaf of bread.
