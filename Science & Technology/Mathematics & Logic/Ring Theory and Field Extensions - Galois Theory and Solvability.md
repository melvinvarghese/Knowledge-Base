# Ring Theory and Field Extensions - Galois Theory and Solvability

## Overview
Galois Theory establishes a profound bridge between Field Extensions and Group Theory, proving why general polynomial equations of degree five or higher (quintics) cannot be solved by radicals.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
Rings, Fields, and Polynomial Roots: A Field is an algebraic structure with addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division. Adjoining roots of polynomial $P(x)$ to base field $\mathbb{Q}$ constructs a splitting field extension $K/\mathbb{Q}$.

---

## 2. Structural Analysis & Mathematical Modeling
Évariste Galois & Solvability by Radicals: Galois associated a symmetry permutation group $\text{Gal}(K/F)$ with each polynomial. A polynomial is solvable by algebraic radicals if and only if its Galois group is a **solvable group**; the alternating group $A_5$ of degree 5 is non-solvable, proving quintics cannot be solved by radicals.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Scientific Impact
Proving impossibility of classical compass-and-straightedge constructions (squaring the circle, doubling the cube), cryptography.

---

## Key Terms
- **Field**: Algebraic set supporting commutative addition and multiplication with full inverses.
- **Galois Group**: Group of automorphisms of a field extension that fix the underlying base field.
- **Solvability by Radicals**: Ability to express roots of a polynomial using algebraic operations and nth roots.
