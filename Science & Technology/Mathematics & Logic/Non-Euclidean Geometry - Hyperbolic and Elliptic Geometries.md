# Non-Euclidean Geometry - Hyperbolic and Elliptic Geometries

## Overview
Non-Euclidean Geometries arose in the 19th century by modifying Euclid's Fifth (Parallel) Postulate, demonstrating that consistent geometries exist on curved surfaces where triangles do not sum to 180°.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
Hyperbolic vs Elliptic Geometry: 1. **Hyperbolic Geometry** (Gauss, Lobachevsky, Bolyai: negative curvature $K < 0$, infinite parallel lines through a point, triangle angles sum to $<180°$); 2. **Elliptic Geometry** (Riemann: positive curvature $K > 0$, zero parallel lines, triangle angles sum to $>180°$).

---

## 2. Structural Analysis & Mathematical Modeling
Riemannian Manifolds & Spacetime: Bernhard Riemann generalized differential geometry to arbitrary n-dimensional curved spaces, providing the essential mathematical language for Einstein's General Relativity.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Scientific Impact
Gravitational spacetime curvature calculations, computer graphics Poincaré disk rendering, GPS relativistic coordinate mapping.

---

## Key Terms
- **Parallel Postulate**: Euclid's fifth postulate stating exactly one parallel line passes through a point not on a given line.
- **Hyperbolic Geometry**: Non-Euclidean geometry on surfaces of constant negative curvature.
- **Gaussian Curvature ($K$)**: Intrinsic measure of surface curvature equal to the product of principal curvatures: $K = \kappa_1 \kappa_2$.
