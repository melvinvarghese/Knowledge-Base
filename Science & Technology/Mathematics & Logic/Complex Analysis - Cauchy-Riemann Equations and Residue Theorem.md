# Complex Analysis - Cauchy-Riemann Equations and Residue Theorem

## Overview
Complex Analysis explores functions of complex numbers, where differentiability (holomorphicity) imposes rigid geometric rigidity and guarantees infinite differentiability.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Cauchy-Riemann Equations & Holomorphic Rigidity: A complex function $f(z) = u(x,y) + i v(x,y)$ (where $z = x + iy$) is complex-differentiable if and only if $u$ and $v$ satisfy the Cauchy-Riemann equations: $\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} = \frac{\partial v}{\partial y}$ and $\frac{\partial u}{\partial y} = -\frac{\partial v}{\partial x}$.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Cauchy Integral Formula & Residue Theorem: If $f$ is holomorphic inside and on a closed contour $\gamma$, Cauchy's Theorem states $\oint_\gamma f(z)\,dz = 0$. In the presence of isolated singularities (poles), the Residue Theorem calculates contour integrals purely by summing local residue values: $\oint_\gamma f(z)\,dz = 2\pi i \sum_{k=1}^m \text{Res}(f, z_k)$.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Evaluation of intractable real improper integrals, 2D fluid aerodynamics (Joukowsky aerofoils), electrical impedance calculations.

## Key Terminology
- **Holomorphic Function**: Complex function that is complex-differentiable at every point in an open subset of $\mathbb{C}$.
- **Residue Theorem**: Tool in complex analysis computing closed line integrals by summing residues at enclosed singularities.
- **Conformal Mapping**: Angle-preserving coordinate transformation generated by holomorphic functions with non-zero derivative.
