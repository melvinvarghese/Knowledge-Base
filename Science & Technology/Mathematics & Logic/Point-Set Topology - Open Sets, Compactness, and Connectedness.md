# Point-Set Topology - Open Sets, Compactness, and Connectedness

## Overview
Point-Set Topology (general topology) investigates geometric and spatial properties preserved under continuous deformations without relying on a metric or distance function.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Topological Space Axiomatization: A topological space $(X, \tau)$ consists of a set $X$ and a collection of subsets $\tau$ (open sets) satisfying: 1. $\emptyset, X \in \tau$; 2. Arbitrary unions of open sets are open; 3. Finite intersections of open sets are open.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Compactness & Connectedness: A space is compact if every open cover has a finite subcover (Heine-Borel theorem equates compactness to closed and bounded subsets in $\mathbb{R}^n$). By the Extreme Value Theorem, continuous functions on compact sets attain global extrema. A space is connected if it cannot be partitioned into two disjoint non-empty open sets.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Foundational framework for functional analysis, manifold definitions, theoretical economics equilibria proofs.

## Key Terminology
- **Topological Space**: Set equipped with a topology defining neighborhood structure without requiring distance metrics.
- **Compactness**: Property where every open cover admits a finite subcover, generalizing closed and bounded sets.
- **Open Cover**: Collection of open sets whose union contains the entire topological space.
