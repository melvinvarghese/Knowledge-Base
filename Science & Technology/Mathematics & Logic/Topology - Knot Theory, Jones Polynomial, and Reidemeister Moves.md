# Topology - Knot Theory, Jones Polynomial, and Reidemeister Moves

## Overview
Knot Theory is the branch of low-dimensional topology studying the spatial embeddings of closed loops ($S^1$) in three-dimensional Euclidean space ($\mathbb{R}^3$) up to ambient isotopy.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Reidemeister Moves & Knot Equivalence: Two 2D knot diagram projections represent the same 3D topological knot if and only if one can be transformed into the other via a finite sequence of three local diagrammatic transformations: Type I (twist/untwist a loop), Type II (slide one strand over another), and Type III (slide a strand across a crossing).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Knot Invariants & The Jones Polynomial: A knot invariant assigns an invariant mathematical object (number, polynomial, group) to a knot regardless of projection. Vaughan Jones (Fields Medal 1990) discovered the **Jones Polynomial** $V(t)$, which detects knot chirality (distinguishing a knot from its mirror image) and revealed profound connections to quantum field theory and statistical mechanics.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
DNA topoisomerase enzyme strand cleavage, polymer chain entanglement physics, topological quantum computing (braiding anyons).

## Key Terminology
- **Knot**: Closed, non-self-intersecting curve embedded in three-dimensional space.
- **Reidemeister Moves**: Three local diagrammatic manipulations that do not alter the ambient topological knot type.
- **Jones Polynomial**: Knot polynomial invariant discovered by Vaughan Jones that distinguishes many knots and links.
