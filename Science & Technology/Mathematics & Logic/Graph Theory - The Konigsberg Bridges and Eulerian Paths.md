# Graph Theory - The Konigsberg Bridges and Eulerian Paths

## Overview
Graph Theory, founded by Leonhard Euler in 1736 to resolve the **Seven Bridges of Königsberg**, models pairwise relations between objects using abstract nodes (**vertices**) and links (**edges**).

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
Euler's Königsberg Proof & Eulerian Paths: Euler proved that walking across every bridge exactly once and returning to start (Eulerian circuit) is possible if and only if **every vertex has an even degree**. Königsberg had four landmasses with odd degrees (3, 3, 3, 5), proving the problem mathematically impossible.

---

## 2. Structural Analysis & Mathematical Modeling
Formal Graph Definition: $G = (V, E)$, with adjacency matrices $A$ encoding topology. Spanning trees, Dijkstra's shortest-path algorithm ($O(|E| + |V|\log|V|)$), and planar graph coloring (Four Color Theorem).

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Scientific Impact
GPS road navigation routing, internet packet switching topology, metabolic chemical reaction networks.

---

## Key Terms
- **Vertex**: A fundamental point or node within a graph network.
- **Edge**: A connection or line linking two vertices in a graph.
- **Eulerian Path**: A trail in a graph that visits every edge exactly once.
