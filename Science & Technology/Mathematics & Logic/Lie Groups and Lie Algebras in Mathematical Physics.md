# Lie Groups and Lie Algebras in Mathematical Physics

## Overview
Lie Groups are continuous mathematical groups that are simultaneously smooth differentiable manifolds, encoding continuous symmetries of physical and geometric spaces.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Lie Group to Lie Algebra Correspondence: The Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ is the tangent space to the Lie group $G$ at the identity element $e$ ($\mathfrak{g} = T_e G$), equipped with an alternating bilinear Lie bracket $[X, Y] = XY - YX$ satisfying the Jacobi identity. The exponential map $\exp: \mathfrak{g} \to G$ maps algebra vectors back to group manifold elements.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Matrix Lie Groups in Physics: Fundamental continuous symmetry groups include $SO(3)$ (3D spatial rotations), $SU(2)$ (quantum spin-1/2 state transformations), and the Lorentz group $SO^+(1,3)$ and Poincaré group governing spacetime transformations in special relativity.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Standard Model gauge symmetries ($U(1) \times SU(2) \times SU(3)$), robotics rigid body kinematics (SE(3)), computer vision rotation representation (quaternions).

## Key Terminology
- **Lie Group**: Smooth manifold that also possesses a compatible group structure where group operations are smooth.
- **Lie Algebra**: Vector space equipped with an alternating bilinear Lie bracket satisfying the Jacobi identity.
- **Exponential Map**: Mapping from the Lie algebra tangent space to the Lie group manifold.
