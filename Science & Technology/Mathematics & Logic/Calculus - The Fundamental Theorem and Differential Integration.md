# Calculus - The Fundamental Theorem and Differential Integration

## Overview
Calculus, invented independently by Isaac Newton and Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz, provides the mathematical framework for modeling continuous change, united by the **Fundamental Theorem of Calculus**.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus (FTC): Part 1 proves differentiation and integration are inverse operations: $\frac{d}{dx} \int_a^x f(t) dt = f(x)$; Part 2 provides the definite evaluation formula: $\int_a^b f(x) dx = F(b) - F(a)$.

---

## 2. Structural Analysis & Mathematical Modeling
Limits and Infinitesimals: Rigorously grounded by Augustin-Louis Cauchy and Karl Weierstrass using formal $(\epsilon, \delta)$ limit definitions, replacing ambiguous historical infinitesimals.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Scientific Impact
Classical mechanics, macroeconomic optimization, orbital flight trajectory engineering.

---

## Key Terms
- **Fundamental Theorem of Calculus**: Theorem establishing that differentiation and integration are exact inverse mathematical operations.
- **Derivative**: Instantaneous rate of change of a mathematical function with respect to a variable.
- **Definite Integral**: Accumulated sum of infinitesimal products representing the net area beneath a curve.
