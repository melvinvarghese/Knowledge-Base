# Boolean Algebra - Logic Gates and Digital Circuit Minimization

## Overview
Boolean Algebra, developed by George Boole (1847), is the branch of algebra where variable values are truth values (`TRUE` and `FALSE`, or `1` and `0`), forming the mathematical foundation of digital circuits.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
The Three Fundamental Operations: AND (conjunction $\wedge$), OR (disjunction $\vee$), and NOT (negation $\neg$). De Morgan's Laws govern algebraic negation transformations:

$$\neg(A \wedge B) = \neg A \vee \neg B, \quad \neg(A \vee B) = \neg A \wedge \neg B$$

---

## 2. Structural Analysis & Mathematical Modeling
Karnaugh Maps and Logic Gate Synthesis: Claude Shannon (1937) proved electrical switching relay networks evaluate Boolean algebra; logic gate minimization using Karnaugh maps and Quine-McCluskey algorithms optimizes hardware silicon area.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Scientific Impact
Microprocessor ALU logic circuit synthesis, digital FPGA hardware design, computer programming conditional expressions.

---

## Key Terms
- **Boolean Algebra**: Algebraic system manipulating binary variables using logical conjunction, disjunction, and negation.
- **Logic Gate**: Physical electronic device implementing a Boolean logical operation on electrical signals.
- **De Morgan's Laws**: Pair of transformation rules expressing duality between conjunction and disjunction under negation.
