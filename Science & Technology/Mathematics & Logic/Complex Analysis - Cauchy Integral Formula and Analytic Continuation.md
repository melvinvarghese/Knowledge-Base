# Complex Analysis - Cauchy Integral Formula and Analytic Continuation

## Overview
Analytic function theory explores functions of complex variables, characterized by infinite differentiability and rigid global uniqueness.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Cauchy's Integral Formula: For a function $f$ holomorphic inside and on a closed contour $\gamma$, the value of $f$ at any interior point $z_0$ is completely determined by its boundary contour values: $f(z_0) = \frac{1}{2\pi i} \oint_\gamma \frac{f(z)}{z - z_0} \, dz$. Repeated differentiation proves that holomorphicity guarantees infinite differentiability ($C^\infty$) and analyticity (representation by convergent Taylor series).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Analytic Continuation & The Riemann Zeta Function: By the Identity Theorem, an analytic function is uniquely determined globally by its values on any open subset. Analytic continuation extends the domain of definition of an analytic function into regions where its original power series diverges, e.g., extending the Riemann Zeta function $\zeta(s) = \sum n^{-s}$ across the entire complex plane with a simple pole at $s=1$.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Aero-hydrodynamic conformal mapping (Joukowsky transformation), quantum scattering theory, Dirichlet series in analytic number theory.

## Key Terminology
- **Cauchy Integral Formula**: Fundamental formula expressing the value of a holomorphic function inside a disk in terms of its contour integral on the boundary.
- **Analytic Continuation**: Technique to extend the domain of definition of a given analytic function beyond its initial boundary of convergence.
- **Holomorphic**: Function of a complex variable that is complex-differentiable at every point of an open domain.
