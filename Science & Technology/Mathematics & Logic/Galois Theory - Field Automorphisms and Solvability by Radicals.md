# Galois Theory - Field Automorphisms and Solvability by Radicals

## Overview
Galois Theory, developed by Évariste Galois (1832), connects field theory with group theory, famously proving that polynomial equations of degree five or higher are unsolvable by radicals.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Galois Correspondence: For a Galois field extension $K / F$, the Galois group $\text{Gal}(K/F)$ consists of field automorphisms of $K$ that fix every element of $F$. The Fundamental Theorem of Galois Theory establishes a bijective, order-reversing correspondence between subfields $E$ ($F \subseteq E \subseteq K$) and subgroups $H \le \text{Gal}(K/F)$, where $E = K^H$.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Abel-Ruffini Theorem & Quintics: A polynomial $f(x)$ is solvable by radicals (using $+,-,\times,\div,\sqrt[n]{}$) if and only if its Galois group is a **solvable group**. The alternating group $A_5$ is simple and non-abelian, making the symmetric group $S_5$ non-solvable, proving that no general algebraic quadratic-like formula can exist for general quintic equations ($a x^5 + b x^4 + c x^3 + d x^2 + e x + f = 0$).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Proving the impossibility of classical Greek geometric constructions: doubling the cube, trisecting arbitrary angles, and squaring the circle.

## Key Terminology
- **Galois Group**: Group of field automorphisms of an algebraic extension that leave the base field elements fixed.
- **Solvable Group**: Group that can be constructed from abelian groups using a series of normal sub-group quotients.
- **Abel-Ruffini Theorem**: Theorem stating that there is no general algebraic solution in radicals to polynomial equations of degree five or higher.
