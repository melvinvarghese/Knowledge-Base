# Prime Number Distribution - The Riemann Hypothesis and Prime Number Theorem

## Overview
The distribution of prime numbers is governed by the **Prime Number Theorem** and the legendary **Riemann Hypothesis** concerning the non-trivial zeros of the Riemann Zeta Function.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
The Prime Number Theorem (PNT): The prime-counting function $\pi(x)$ asymptotes to logarithmic integral: $\pi(x) \sim \frac{x}{\ln x}$.

---

## 2. Structural Analysis & Mathematical Modeling
The Riemann Hypothesis Formulation: The analytic continuation of the zeta function $\zeta(s) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty n^{-s}$ has all non-trivial zeros lying on the critical line with real part $\text{Re}(s) = 1/2$. The zeros govern error bounds on prime distribution.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Scientific Impact
Millennium Prize problem, cryptographic prime generation, quantum chaotic energy level spacing correlations.

---

## Key Terms
- **Riemann Zeta Function**: Complex analytic function linking complex zeros to the distribution of prime numbers.
- **Critical Line**: The vertical line in the complex plane $\text{Re}(s) = 1/2$ where all non-trivial zeta zeros are conjectured to lie.
- **Prime-Counting Function ($\pi(x)$)**: Function counting the exact number of prime numbers less than or equal to $x$.
