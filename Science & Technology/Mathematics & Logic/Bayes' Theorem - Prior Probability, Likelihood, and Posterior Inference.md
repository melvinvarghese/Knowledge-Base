# Bayes' Theorem - Prior Probability, Likelihood, and Posterior Inference

## Overview
Bayes' Theorem formalizes rational belief revision in probability theory, calculating how new empirical evidence alters the **posterior probability** of a hypothesis.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
Mathematical Formulation: $P(H|E) = \frac{P(E|H) P(H)}{P(E)}$. Prior probability $P(H)$ is multiplied by likelihood $P(E|H)$ and normalized by marginal evidence $P(E)$ to yield posterior $P(H|E)$.

---

## 2. Structural Analysis & Mathematical Modeling
Bayesian vs Frequentist Epistemology: Frequentists define probability strictly as long-run relative frequency of repeatable events; Bayesians treat probability as a measure of epistemic confidence in a hypothesis given available information.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Scientific Impact
Machine learning Bayesian networks, clinical medical diagnostic test interpretation (positive predictive value), spam email filters.

---

## Key Terms
- **Prior Probability**: Initial probability assessment of a hypothesis prior to observing new evidence.
- **Posterior Probability**: Updated conditional probability of a hypothesis calculated after incorporating new evidence.
- **Likelihood**: Probability of observing the empirical evidence given that a specific hypothesis is true.
