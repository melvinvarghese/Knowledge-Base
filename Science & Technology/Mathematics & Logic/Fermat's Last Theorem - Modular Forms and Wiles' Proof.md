# Fermat's Last Theorem - Modular Forms and Wiles' Proof

## Overview
Fermat's Last Theorem, conjectured by Pierre de Fermat in 1637, states that no three positive integers $a, b, c$ can satisfy $a^n + b^n = c^n$ for any integer value $n > 2$.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
The Frey Curve & Taniyama-Shimura Conjecture: Gerhard Frey showed that a non-trivial solution would generate an impossible, non-modular elliptic curve; Kenneth Ribet proved that the Modularity Theorem implied Fermat's Last Theorem.

---

## 2. Structural Analysis & Mathematical Modeling
Andrew Wiles' Proof (1994): Using horizontal deformation rings of Galois representations and Hecke algebras, Wiles and Richard Taylor proved the semi-stable Modularity Theorem, solving the 358-year-old mathematical puzzle.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Scientific Impact
Triumph of modern algebraic geometry, Langlands program advancement, elliptic curve cryptography foundations.

---

## Key Terms
- **Fermat's Last Theorem**: Assertion that $a^n + b^n = c^n$ has no positive integer solutions for $n > 2$.
- **Elliptic Curve**: Smooth projective algebraic curve of genus one with a specified base point.
- **Modularity Theorem**: Theorem asserting every elliptic curve over $\mathbb{Q}$ is parameterized by modular forms.
