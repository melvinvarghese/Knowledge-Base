# Number Theory - Diophantine Equations and Fermat's Last Theorem

## Overview
Diophantine Equations are polynomial equations seeking solutions strictly restricted to integers, representing some of the most historically elusive problems in mathematics.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Fermat's Last Theorem: Pierre de Fermat conjectured in 1637 that no positive integers $x, y, z$ satisfy the equation $x^n + y^n = z^n$ for any integer exponent $n > 2$. For $n = 2$, infinitely many integer Pythagorean triples exist (e.g., $3^2 + 4^2 = 5^2$).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Wiles Modular Proof: Andrew Wiles (with Richard Taylor) proved Fermat's Last Theorem in 1994 by establishing the Modularity Theorem for semistable elliptic curves, proving that every such elliptic curve over $\mathbb{Q}$ is modular (associated with a weight-2 modular form), which ruled out the Frey curve constructed from hypothetical Fermat counterexamples.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Elliptic curve cryptography parameter safety, computational algebraic geometry, integer programming.

## Key Terminology
- **Diophantine Equation**: Polynomial equation whose sought solutions are restricted entirely to the integers.
- **Fermat's Last Theorem**: Theorem stating no non-trivial integer solutions exist for $x^n + y^n = z^n$ when $n > 2$.
- **Elliptic Curve**: Smooth, projective algebraic curve of genus one having a specified base point, defined by the Weierstrass equation.
