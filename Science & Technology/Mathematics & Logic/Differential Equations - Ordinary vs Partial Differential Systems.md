# Differential Equations - Ordinary vs Partial Differential Systems

## Overview
Differential Equations relate unknown functions to their spatial or temporal derivatives, providing the universal mathematical language for physical laws across all engineering and physics.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
ODEs vs PDEs: Ordinary Differential Equations (ODEs) involve functions of a single independent variable (e.g., $m \ddot{x} + kx = 0$); Partial Differential Equations (PDEs) involve multivariable spatial and temporal gradients (e.g., Heat Equation, Wave Equation, Schrödinger Equation).

---

## 2. Structural Analysis & Mathematical Modeling
Boundary Value Problems & Separation of Variables: Linear second-order PDEs classified into Elliptic (Laplace $\nabla^2 u = 0$), Parabolic (diffusion $\partial_t u = \alpha \nabla^2 u$), and Hyperbolic (waves $\partial_{tt} u = c^2 \nabla^2 u$) solved via Fourier series expansions.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Scientific Impact
Aerospace aerodynamic modeling, financial Black-Scholes option pricing, epidemiological infectious spread simulation.

---

## Key Terms
- **Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE)**: Equation containing an unknown function of one independent variable and its derivatives.
- **Partial Differential Equation (PDE)**: Equation involving unknown multivariable functions and their partial derivatives.
- **Boundary Condition**: Set of constraints specified on the boundaries of a differential equation domain (Dirichlet, Neumann).
