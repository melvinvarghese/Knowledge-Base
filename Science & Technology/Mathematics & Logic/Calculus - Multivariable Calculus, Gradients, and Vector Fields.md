# Calculus - Multivariable Calculus, Gradients, and Vector Fields

## Overview
Multivariable Calculus extends single-variable calculus to functions of multiple independent variables, modeling multi-dimensional continuous physical systems.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Gradients, Divergence, and Curl: 1. Gradient: $\nabla f = \left( \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}, \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}, \frac{\partial f}{\partial z} \right)$ points in the direction of steepest rate of increase of a scalar field; 2. Divergence: $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{F} = \frac{\partial F_x}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial F_y}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial F_z}{\partial z}$ measures the net outward flux density of a vector field from an infinitesimal volume (sources and sinks); 3. Curl: $\nabla \times \mathbf{F}$ measures the infinitesimal circulation rotation around an axis.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Divergence Theorem & Stokes' Theorem: Stokes' Theorem unifies the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus in higher dimensions: $\oint_{\partial S} \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = \iint_S (\nabla \times \mathbf{F}) \cdot d\mathbf{S}$. The Divergence (Gauss's) Theorem equates boundary surface flux to volume divergence: $\iint_{\partial V} \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{S} = \iiint_V (\nabla \cdot \mathbf{F}) \, dV$.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Mathematical formulation of Maxwell's electromagnetic equations, Navier-Stokes fluid mechanics, machine learning gradient descent backpropagation.
