# Category Theory - Objects, Morphisms, and Functors

## Overview
Category Theory, often dubbed 'the mathematics of mathematics', formalizes structural relationships and patterns across diverse mathematical fields using **Objects**, **Morphisms**, and **Functors**.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
Foundational Categorical Axioms: A Category $\mathcal{C}$ consists of objects and directed arrows (**morphisms**) $f: A \rightarrow B$ satisfying associativity $h \circ (g \circ f) = (h \circ g) \circ f$ and identity morphisms $1_A$.

---

## 2. Structural Analysis & Mathematical Modeling
Functors and Natural Transformations: A **Functor** maps objects and morphisms from category $\mathcal{C}$ to category $\mathcal{D}$ preserving structure; **Natural Transformations** map between functors, providing the universal language for modern topology and computer science.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Scientific Impact
Functional programming type systems (Haskell monads), categorical quantum mechanics, algebraic geometry schemes (Grothendieck).

---

## Key Terms
- **Category**: Mathematical structure comprised of objects and directed composable morphisms between them.
- **Functor**: Structure-preserving mapping from one category to another.
- **Natural Transformation**: Way of transforming one functor into another while respecting internal categorical structure.
