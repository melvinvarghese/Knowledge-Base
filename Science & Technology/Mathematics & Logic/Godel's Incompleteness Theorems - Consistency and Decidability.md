# Godel's Incompleteness Theorems - Consistency and Decidability

## Overview
Kurt Gödel's Incompleteness Theorems (1931) revolutionized mathematical logic, shattering David Hilbert's dream of proving mathematics complete and consistent.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
The First Incompleteness Theorem: Any consistent formal axiomatic system capable of basic arithmetic is incomplete—there exist true mathematical statements that cannot be proven within the system.

---

## 2. Structural Analysis & Mathematical Modeling
The Second Incompleteness Theorem & Gödel Numbering: A consistent formal system cannot prove its own consistency. Gödel encoded logical syntax into unique prime factorization numbers (**Gödel numbering**), constructing a self-referential statement equivalent to: 'This statement is unprovable.'

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Scientific Impact
Limits of formal verification, theoretical computer science, mathematical philosophy.

---

## Key Terms
- **Incompleteness Theorem**: Theorem proving any consistent axiomatic system contains unprovable true statements.
- **Gödel Numbering**: Arithmetical encoding assigning unique integer values to mathematical symbols and proofs.
- **Consistency**: Property of an axiomatic system that it contains no contradictions ($P \wedge \neg P$).
