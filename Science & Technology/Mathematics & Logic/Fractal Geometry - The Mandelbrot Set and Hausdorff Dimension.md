# Fractal Geometry - The Mandelbrot Set and Hausdorff Dimension

## Overview
Fractal Geometry, pioneered by Benoit Mandelbrot (1975), investigates infinitely complex mathematical shapes exhibiting **self-similarity** across scales and non-integer **Hausdorff dimensions**.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
The Mandelbrot Set Formula: The set of complex numbers $c \in \mathbb{C}$ for which the recursive quadratic polynomial iteration remains bounded:

$$z_{n+1} = z_n^2 + c, \quad z_0 = 0$$

Plotting escape velocity yields infinitely intricate self-similar geometric boundaries containing copies of the whole.

---

## 2. Structural Analysis & Mathematical Modeling
Fractional Hausdorff Dimension: Traditional lines have dimension $D=1$, planes $D=2$. Fractals possess fractional dimensions (e.g., Koch snowflake $D = \log 4 / \log 3 \approx 1.2619$), quantifying space-filling roughness.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Scientific Impact
Computer graphics procedural landscape generation, compact cellular antenna design, biological vascular branching modeling.

---

## Key Terms
- **Fractal**: Geometric shape exhibiting self-similarity and a non-integer fractional Hausdorff dimension.
- **Mandelbrot Set**: The set of complex numbers $c$ for which $z_{n+1} = z_n^2 + c$ does not diverge to infinity.
- **Self-Similarity**: Property where a structure looks identical or qualitatively similar across all scales of magnification.
