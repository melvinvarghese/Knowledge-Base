# Game Theory - Nash Equilibrium and Prisoner's Dilemma

## Overview
Game Theory is the mathematical study of strategic interaction among rational decision-makers, formalized by John von Neumann and John Nash's concept of **Nash Equilibrium**.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
The Prisoner's Dilemma & Dominant Strategy: Two prisoners can Cooperate ($C$) or Defect ($D$). Defection strictly dominates cooperation for each individual ($D$ yields higher personal payoff regardless of the other's choice), leading to mutual defection $(D, D)$ despite mutual cooperation $(C, C)$ yielding a globally superior outcome.

---

## 2. Structural Analysis & Mathematical Modeling
Nash Equilibrium Theorem: John Nash proved that every finite non-cooperative game possesses at least one equilibrium in mixed strategies where no player can unilaterally increase their expected payoff by changing strategy.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Scientific Impact
Evolutionary biology ESS strategies, corporate telecommunication spectrum auctions, international nuclear deterrence.

---

## Key Terms
- **Nash Equilibrium**: State in game theory where no player benefits by changing strategy while others keep theirs unchanged.
- **Prisoner's Dilemma**: Classic game demonstrating why two rational individuals might not cooperate even when it is in their best interest.
- **Dominant Strategy**: Strategy delivering a superior payoff regardless of the strategies selected by competitors.
