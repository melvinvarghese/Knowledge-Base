# Non-Euclidean Geometries - Hyperbolic and Elliptic Spaces

## Overview
Non-Euclidean Geometries arise by replacing Euclid's Fifth Postulate (the Parallel Postulate), revealing consistent spatial geometries with non-zero curvature.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Hyperbolic Geometry (Bolyai, Lobachevsky): Replaces the parallel postulate with the axiom that through a point not on a line, there exist infinitely many parallel lines. Hyperbolic space has constant negative Gaussian curvature ($K < 0$). Triangles have interior angle sums strictly less than $180^\circ$ ($\sum \alpha_i < \pi$), with area proportional to angular defect: $\text{Area} = -R^2 (\pi - \alpha - \beta - \gamma)$. Models include the Poincaré disk and half-plane.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Elliptic Geometry (Riemann): Assumes no parallel lines exist; every pair of straight lines intersects. Elliptic space has constant positive Gaussian curvature ($K > 0$), modeled on the surface of a sphere where lines are great circles. Triangle angle sums strictly exceed $180^\circ$ with positive angular excess.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Einstein's curved spacetime in General Relativity, geometric deep learning embedding tree-like hierarchical networks into hyperbolic latent space.

## Key Terminology
- **Hyperbolic Geometry**: Non-Euclidean geometry with constant negative curvature where parallel lines diverge and triangle angles sum to $< 180^\circ$.
- **Elliptic Geometry**: Non-Euclidean geometry with constant positive curvature where all lines intersect and triangle angles sum to $> 180^\circ$.
- **Poincaré Disk Model**: Conformal model of 2D hyperbolic geometry confined within the unit disk where lines are circular arcs perpendicular to the boundary.
