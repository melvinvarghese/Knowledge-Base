# Differential Equations - Ordinary vs Partial and Boundary Value Problems

## Overview
Differential equations relate an unknown multivariable function to its rates of change (derivatives), serving as the universal language of physical laws.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Classification: Ordinary Differential Equations (ODEs) involve functions of a single independent variable (e.g., $\frac{dy}{dx} = f(x, y)$). Partial Differential Equations (PDEs) involve functions of multiple variables with partial derivatives, categorized into elliptic (Laplace equation $\nabla^2 u = 0$), parabolic (Heat equation $\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = \alpha \nabla^2 u$), and hyperbolic (Wave equation $\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial t^2} = c^2 \nabla^2 u$).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Boundary & Initial Value Problems: Existence and uniqueness of solutions (Picard-Lindelöf Theorem for ODEs) require boundary conditions, classified into Dirichlet (specified function values on boundary $\partial \Omega$), Neumann (specified normal derivative flux $\nabla u \cdot \hat{n}$), and Robin (linear combinations).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Acoustic wave propagation, heat transfer engineering, mechanical structural stress analysis, orbital dynamics.

## Key Terminology
- **ODE**: Differential equation containing one independent variable and its ordinary derivatives.
- **PDE**: Differential equation containing multiple independent variables and partial derivatives.
- **Dirichlet Boundary Condition**: Constraint specifying the exact values that a solution must take along the domain boundary.
