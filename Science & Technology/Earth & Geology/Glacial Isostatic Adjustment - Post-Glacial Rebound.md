# Glacial Isostatic Adjustment - Post-Glacial Rebound

## Overview
Glacial Isostatic Adjustment (GIA) is the continuing viscoelastic rebound of Earth's crust and mantle following the deglaciation and melting of massive kilometers-thick ice sheets that covered continents during the Last Glacial Maximum.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
Viscoelastic Mantle Relaxation: Massive ice sheets depressed Earth's crust by hundreds of meters; melting removed the overburden load, triggering slow upward rebound governed by lower mantle rock viscosity ($\sim 10^{21}\text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$).

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Contemporary Crustal Uplift & Sea Level Shifts: Scandinavia and Canada continue uplifting at rates up to 10 mm/year, while peripheral bulge regions (the North Sea, US mid-Atlantic) slowly subside, modulating local relative sea-level rise.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Calibrating global tide gauge satellite altimetry measurements, long-term nuclear waste deep repository geological stability.

---

## Key Terms
- **Glacial Isostatic Adjustment (GIA)**: Ongoing vertical deformation of the crust and mantle responding to deglaciation.
- **Viscoelasticity**: Property of materials exhibiting both viscous fluid and elastic solid characteristics under deformation.
- **Forebulge**: Elevated bulge of crust pushed up around the margins of a heavy continental ice sheet.
