# Transform Faults - Strike-Slip Mechanics and the San Andreas Fault

## Overview
Transform Faults are conservative plate boundaries where tectonic plates slide horizontally past one another along vertical strike-slip fault planes, neither creating nor destroying lithosphere.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
Elastic Rebound Theory & Stick-Slip Seismology: Tectonic forces continuously apply shear strain across locked fault asperities; the fault sticks due to friction until accumulated shear stress exceeds frictional strength, releasing centuries of strain in seconds (earthquake).

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
The San Andreas Fault System: Continental transform boundary spanning 1,200 km through California accommodating horizontal relative motion (~35 mm/yr) between the Pacific Plate and North American Plate.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Probabilistic seismic hazard assessment, building code structural earthquake engineering, GPS crustal deformation tracking.

---

## Key Terms
- **Transform Fault**: Conservative strike-slip fault plate boundary accommodating horizontal lateral plate motion.
- **Elastic Rebound**: Sudden release of progressively stored elastic strain energy along a fault surface generating earthquakes.
- **Strike-Slip**: Fault motion where opposing fault blocks slide past each other horizontally.
