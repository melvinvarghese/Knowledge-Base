# Sedimentary Basin Formation - Subsidence and Stratigraphy

## Overview
Sedimentary Basins are large-scale geological depressions in Earth's crust where long-term tectonic subsidence accommodates the multi-kilometer accumulation of mineral sediments and organic matter.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
The Three Subsidence Driving Mechanisms: 1. Thermal Subsidence (lithospheric cooling and densification following rifting); 2. Tectonic Flexural Loading (isostatic bending under the weight of mountain belts); 3. Fault-Controlled Mechanical Stretching.

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Principles of Stratigraphy: Steno's laws of Superposition (younger strata overlie older), Original Horizontality, and Lateral Continuity, integrated with modern Sequence Stratigraphy linking depositional cycles to relative eustatic sea-level fluctuations.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Petroleum reservoir and natural gas exploration, groundwater aquifer mapping, geological carbon sequestration reservoirs.

---

## Key Terms
- **Sedimentary Basin**: Low-lying area of the Earth's crust where sediments accumulate over geological time.
- **Subsidence**: Downward settling or sinking of the Earth's surface relative to surrounding geological areas.
- **Sequence Stratigraphy**: Branch of geology classifying sedimentary strata into chronostratigraphic frameworks based on sea-level shifts.
