# Paleocene-Eocene Thermal Maximum (PETM) - Rapid Greenhouse Influx

## Overview
The Paleocene-Eocene Thermal Maximum (PETM, ~56 Ma) was a rapid, extreme global warming event where thousands of gigatons of carbon surged into the atmosphere, spiking temperatures by 5–8°C.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
Carbon Isotope Excursion (CIE): Marine and terrestrial sediments exhibit an abrupt negative excursion in $\delta^{13}\text{C}$, confirming the injection of thousands of gigatons of $^{13}C$-depleted carbon from subsea methane clathrates, volcanic intrusions, or peat burns.

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Planetary Consequences: Deep-sea ocean acidification dissolved carbonate sediments worldwide, creating an extinction event in deep benthic foraminifera; polar regions warmed into subtropical forests (crocodilians in the Arctic).

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
The prime deep-time geological analogue for modern anthropogenic fossil fuel carbon emission rates and ecosystem tipping points.

---

## Key Terms
- **PETM**: Rapid global warming event 56 million years ago caused by a massive greenhouse gas release.
- **Carbon Isotope Excursion**: Abrupt shift in stable carbon isotope ratios reflecting massive influx of biogenic carbon.
- **Thermal Acidification**: Drop in oceanic pH accompanied by severe shoaling of the calcite compensation depth (CCD).
