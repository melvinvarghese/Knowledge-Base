# The Milankovitch Cycles - Orbital Eccentricity, Obliquity, and Precession

## Overview
The Milankovitch Cycles describe periodic variations in Earth's orbital geometry that modulate the seasonal and latitudinal distribution of incoming solar radiation, driving quaternary ice age cycles.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
The Three Orbital Pacemakers: 1. **Eccentricity** (~100,000 and 400,000 yr cycles: orbit shape shifts from nearly circular to mildly elliptical); 2. **Obliquity** (~41,000 yr cycle: axial tilt oscillates between 22.1° and 24.5°); 3. **Axial Precession** (~23,000 yr cycle: gyroscopic wobble of Earth's rotational axis).

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Northern Hemisphere Insolation Driver: Milankovitch demonstrated that ice ages are triggered when cool summers at 65°N allow winter snowfall to survive through summer without melting, accumulating continental ice sheets via positive albedo feedback.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Paleoclimate synchronization of deep-sea sediment oxygen isotope records (LR04 benthic stack), climate sensitivity forecasting.

---

## Key Terms
- **Milankovitch Cycles**: Cyclic astronomical variations in Earth's orbital parameters driving long-term climate changes.
- **Orbital Eccentricity**: Measurement of how much an astronomical orbit deviates from a perfect circle.
- **Axial Obliquity**: The angle between a planet's rotational axis and its orbital plane perpendicular.
