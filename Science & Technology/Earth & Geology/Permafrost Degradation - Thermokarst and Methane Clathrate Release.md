# Permafrost Degradation - Thermokarst and Methane Clathrate Release

## Overview
Permafrost Degradation is the widespread thawing of permanently frozen ground in Arctic and boreal regions, triggering ground subsidence (**thermokarst**) and releasing vast stores of sequestered carbon and methane.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
Thermokarst Subsidence Dynamics: As subsurface ground ice wedges melt, soil loses structural volume and collapses irregularly, creating drunken forests, sinkhole pits, slumps, and thermokarst thaw lakes.

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
The Permafrost Carbon Feedback: Thawing activates previously dormant anaerobic microbial decomposers, decomposing ancient organic matter into carbon dioxide and **methane** ($CH_4$), amplifying global climate warming.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Arctic civil infrastructure collapse (ruptured pipelines, collapsing roads and foundations), destabilization of subsea methane clathrates.

---

## Key Terms
- **Permafrost**: Soil, rock, or sediment remaining at or below 0°C continuously for two or more consecutive years.
- **Thermokarst**: Uneven hummocky topography created by the thawing and collapse of ground ice.
- **Methane Clathrate**: Crystalline water ice cage physically trapping high-pressure methane gas molecules in permafrost and deep seabed sediments.
