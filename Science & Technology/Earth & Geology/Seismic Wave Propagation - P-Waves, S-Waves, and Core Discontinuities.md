# Seismic Wave Propagation - P-Waves, S-Waves, and Core Discontinuities

## Overview
Seismic Waves generated by earthquakes propagate through Earth's interior, functioning as a planetary ultrasound revealing internal core and mantle discontinuities via reflection and refraction.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
Body Wave Mechanics: 1. Primary ($P$) waves are longitudinal compressional waves traveling fastest through solids and liquids ($v_p = \sqrt{(K + \frac{4}{3}\mu)/\rho}$); 2. Secondary ($S$) waves are transverse shear waves that travel only through solids ($v_s = \sqrt{\mu/\rho}$).

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Shadow Zones and Core Discovery: The disappearance of S-waves past 104° proven by Richard Oldham in 1906 demonstrates the outer core is liquid; the P-wave shadow zone (104°–140°) and central reflections identified by Inge Lehmann (1936) confirmed a solid inner core.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Earthquake epicenter triangulation, global nuclear test monitoring, seismic tomography 3D Earth imaging.

---

## Key Terms
- **P-Wave**: Compressional seismic wave traveling through both solid and liquid planetary layers.
- **S-Wave**: Transverse shear seismic wave incapable of propagating through liquid media.
- **Mohorovičić Discontinuity (Moho)**: Seismic boundary separating Earth's crust from the underlying denser mantle.
