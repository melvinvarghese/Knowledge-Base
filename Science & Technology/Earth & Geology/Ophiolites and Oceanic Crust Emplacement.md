# Ophiolites and Oceanic Crust Emplacement

## Overview
An ophiolite is a section of Earth's oceanic crust and underlying upper mantle that has been tectonically uplifted and emplaced (obducted) onto continental crustal margins.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Stratigraphic Sequence of the Penrose Ophiolite: Displays a classic stratified profile: 1. Deep marine pelagic sediments (chert, radiolarite); 2. Basaltic pillow lavas formed by rapid submarine quenching; 3. Sheeted dolerite dykes representing fossil seafloor spreading conduits; 4. Layered gabbros crystallized in magma chambers; 5. Ultramafic mantle peridotites (harzburgite and lherzolite) displaying serpentinization.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Obduction & Tectonic Suture Zones: Ophiolites (such as the Semail Ophiolite in Oman and Troodos Ophiolite in Cyprus) mark fossil convergent margins and suture zones where ancient ocean basins (such as the Tethys Ocean) closed during continental collisions.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Direct sampling of ancient oceanic mantle lithosphere, exploration of deep-sea hydrothermal vent sulfide mineral deposits.

## Key Terminology
- **Ophiolite**: Section of Earth's oceanic crust and upper mantle uplifted and exposed on land through tectonic obduction.
- **Obduction**: Tectonic process whereby oceanic crust is overthrust onto continental crust at a convergent boundary.
- **Sheeted Dykes**: Subvertical intrusive igneous rock bodies formed as conduits feeding submarine seafloor lava flows.
