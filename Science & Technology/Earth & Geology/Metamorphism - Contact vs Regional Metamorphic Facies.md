# Metamorphism - Contact vs Regional Metamorphic Facies

## Overview
Metamorphism is the solid-state recrystallization of existing protolith rocks driven by extreme temperatures, confining lithostatic pressures, and chemically active tectonic fluids.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
Contact vs Regional Metamorphism: Contact metamorphism is thermal baking around hot igneous magma intrusions (low pressure, high temperature, forming non-foliated hornfels); Regional metamorphism occurs over vast mountain-building collision zones (high pressure, high temperature, forming foliated slate, schist, and gneiss).

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Metamorphic Facies & Index Minerals: Characteristic mineral assemblages define distinct pressure-temperature regimes (e.g., Blueschist facies indicating high pressure/low temperature in subduction zones; Granulite facies in deep continental roots).

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Structural bedrock engineering, geological geothermometry and geobarometry, commercial decorative marble and slate quarrying.

---

## Key Terms
- **Metamorphism**: Solid-state alteration of rock composition or structure by heat, pressure, and fluids.
- **Foliation**: Repetitive planar layering in metamorphic rocks caused by directional tectonic shear stresses.
- **Metamorphic Facies**: Set of metamorphic mineral assemblages reflecting a specific range of pressure and temperature conditions.
