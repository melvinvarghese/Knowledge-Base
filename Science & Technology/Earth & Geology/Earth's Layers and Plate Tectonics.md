# Earth's Layers and Plate Tectonics

> **The Big Idea:** The Earth is not a dead lump of rock; it is a dynamic heat engine. A churning liquid iron core powers a protective magnetic shield, while slow-moving tectonic plates in the crust glide across the semi-molten mantle, creating mountains, volcanoes, and oceans.

---

## 🌍 The 4 Layers of Earth

```
                      CROSS-SECTION OF EARTH
                                │
             [ 1. CRUST: 5 to 70 km thin brittle rock shell ]
                                │
             [ 2. MANTLE: 2,900 km thick semi-solid hot rock ]
                                │
             [ 3. OUTER CORE: 2,200 km swirling liquid iron/nickel ]
                                │
             [ 4. INNER CORE: 1,220 km solid iron ball (5,400°C) ]
```

1. **The Crust:** The paper-thin skin where we live. Continental crust (granite, thick and light) and Oceanic crust (basalt, thin and dense).
2. **The Mantle:** 84% of Earth's volume. Heat from deep below causes slow, sluggish convection currents of plastic rock (the Asthenosphere).
3. **The Outer Core:** Liquid churning metal. The swirling of molten iron generates Earth’s **Geomagnetic Dynamo**—the invisible magnetic shield that deflects deadly solar radiation.
4. **The Inner Core:** Although hotter than the surface of the Sun, the immense pressure of the entire planet crushes the iron and nickel into a solid metallic sphere.

---

## 🗺️ Plate Tectonics: A Jigsaw Puzzle in Motion

Earth's brittle crust is broken into roughly **15 major tectonic plates** that float on the churning mantle below, drifting at about 2 to 10 cm per year (the speed fingernails grow).

```
┌─────────────────────────────────┐       ┌─────────────────────────────────┐
│     CONVERGENT BOUNDARY         │       │      DIVERGENT BOUNDARY         │
│ (Plates crash together)         │       │ (Plates pull apart)             │
├─────────────────────────────────┤       ├─────────────────────────────────┤
│ One plate slides under another  │  vs.  │ Magma rises to fill the gap,    │
│ (Subduction), building giant    │       │ creating new ocean floor        │
│ mountain ranges (Himalayas)     │       │ (Mid-Atlantic Ridge) or rift    │
│ and deep ocean trenches.        │       │ valleys in continents.          │
└─────────────────────────────────┘       └─────────────────────────────────┘
```

* **Transform Boundaries (Sliding Past):** Plates grind sideways against each other (e.g., the San Andreas Fault in California). Friction locks the plates until they suddenly snap, releasing violent shockwaves: **Earthquakes**.
* **The Ring of Fire:** The horseshoe-shaped belt around the Pacific Ocean where 75% of the world's volcanoes and 90% of all earthquakes occur due to widespread subduction zones.

---

## 🛠️ Key Takeaway

* Continents were once united in a single supercontinent called **Pangaea** 250 million years ago. Millions of years from now, tectonic drift will re-assemble them into another supercontinent.
