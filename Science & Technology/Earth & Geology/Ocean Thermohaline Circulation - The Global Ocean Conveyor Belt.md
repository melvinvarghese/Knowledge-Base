# Ocean Thermohaline Circulation - The Global Ocean Conveyor Belt

## Overview
Thermohaline Circulation (the Global Ocean Conveyor Belt) is a density-driven, deep-ocean circulation pattern driven by global gradients of temperature (*thermo*) and salinity (*haline*), distributing heat across the planet.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
Deep Water Formation: In the North Atlantic (Greenland/Norwegian Seas), warm surface water cools and sea ice formation expels brine, creating ultra-dense cold, saline water that sinks to form **North Atlantic Deep Water (NADW)**.

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
The 1,000-Year Conveyor: Deep cold waters flow southward through the Atlantic, wrap around Antarctica, and resurface in the Indian and Pacific Oceans via upwelling, taking ~1,000 years to complete a single planetary loop.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation (AMOC) collapse tipping point research, heat distribution warming Western Europe.

---

## Key Terms
- **Thermohaline Circulation**: Global deep-ocean circulation driven by temperature and salinity density differences.
- **North Atlantic Deep Water (NADW)**: Dense, cold, high-salinity water mass sinking in the North Atlantic driving the AMOC.
- **Brine Rejection**: Process during sea ice formation where salts are excluded from growing ice crystals, increasing surrounding water salinity.
