# Continental Rifting - The East African Rift System

## Overview
Continental Rifting is the process of extensional crustal thinning that splits a single continental landmass, representing the embryonic initial stage in the creation of a new ocean basin.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
Rift Architecture - Grabens and Normal Faults: Horizontal extensional stress creates central down-dropped valley blocks (**grabens**) bounded by uplifting shoulder blocks (horsts) along steep normal fault systems.

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
The East African Rift Valley Case Study: Spans 6,000 km from the Red Sea to Mozambique; active crustal extension thinning continental crust accompanied by alkaline volcanism (Kilimanjaro, Ol Doinyo Lengai), ultimately destined to separate the Somali Plate from Africa.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Geothermal clean energy extraction in rift valleys, early hominin fossil site depositional environments (Olduvai Gorge).

---

## Key Terms
- **Continental Rift**: Linear zone where continental lithosphere pulls apart under extensional tectonic stress.
- **Graben**: Depressed tectonic crustal block flanked by parallel fault walls.
- **Normal Fault**: Fault where the hanging wall moves downward relative to the footwall under extensional tension.
