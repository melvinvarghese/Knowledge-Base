# Mineral Cleavage and Crystal Symmetry Systems

## Overview
Crystallography classifies solid mineral crystals according to internal atomic lattice symmetry and directional planes of weakness (**Cleavage**).

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
The Seven Crystal Symmetry Systems: Minerals organize into seven foundational symmetry systems based on unit cell axis lengths and interaxial angles: Cubic (isometric), Tetragonal, Orthorhombic, Hexagonal, Trigonal, Monoclinic, and Triclinic.

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Cleavage Mechanics: Cleavage occurs along specific crystallographic planes where chemical bonds are weakest (e.g., basal cleavage in micas splitting along weak Van der Waals planes; perfect cubic cleavage in halite along ionic planes).

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Geological gemstone cutting and faceting, X-ray diffraction mineral identification, semiconductor wafer slicing (silicon cleavage planes).

---

## Key Terms
- **Cleavage**: Tendency of a crystalline mineral to split cleanly along definite crystallographic structural planes.
- **Unit Cell**: Smallest repeating structural unit of a crystalline lattice displaying full symmetry.
- **Miller Indices**: Notation system in crystallography describing the orientation of lattice planes in a crystal.
