# Subduction Zones - Trench Rollback and Wadati-Benioff Zones

## Overview
Subduction Zones are convergent plate boundaries where cold, dense oceanic lithosphere sinks beneath lighter continental or island arc lithosphere into the mantle, recycling oceanic crust.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
The Wadati-Benioff Seismic Zone: Sinking slabs generate dipping zones of deep-focus earthquakes (down to 700 km depth) tracking mechanical fracture and dehydration phase transitions of the subducting oceanic plate.

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Flux Melting & Volcanic Arcs: Hydrated minerals in the sinking slab release water into the overlying mantle wedge, depressing the peridotite melting point and generating volatile-rich magma fueling explosive volcanic arcs (Ring of Fire).

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Megathrust earthquake hazard assessment (e.g., 2004 Sumatra, 2011 Tohoku), tsunami generation forecasting, continental crust formation.

---

## Key Terms
- **Subduction Zone**: Convergent boundary where one tectonic plate is forced downward beneath another into the mantle.
- **Wadati-Benioff Zone**: Planar zone of seismicity tracing the trajectory of a subducting tectonic slab.
- **Flux Melting**: Melting of mantle rocks triggered by the addition of volatile fluids (water) that lower rock melting temperatures.
