# Karst Topography - Caves, Sinkholes, and Stalactites

## Overview
Karst Topography is a distinctive landscape sculpted by the chemical dissolution of soluble carbonate bedrock (limestone, dolomite, gypsum) by acidic groundwater.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
Carbonic Acid Dissolution Chemistry: Rainwater absorbs atmospheric and soil $CO_2$, forming weak carbonic acid that dissolves calcium carbonate:

$$CaCO_3(s) + H_2O(l) + CO_2(g) \rightleftharpoons Ca^{2+}(aq) + 2HCO_3^-(aq)$$

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Subterranean Speleothems & Collapse Sinkholes: Cavities expand into vast cave networks; degassing of $CO_2$ inside aerated caves reprecipitates calcite into ceiling **stalactites** and floor **stalagmites**; catastrophic roof failure creates **sinkholes**.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Karst aquifer drinking water management, structural engineering ground subsidence hazard assessments.

---

## Key Terms
- **Karst**: Landscape formed by the dissolution of soluble rocks such as limestone, dolomite, and gypsum.
- **Sinkhole**: Depression in the ground surface formed by the dissolution or structural collapse of underlying carbonate rock.
- **Speleothem**: Mineral deposit formed in a cave by the evaporation or degassing of mineral-rich water drops.
