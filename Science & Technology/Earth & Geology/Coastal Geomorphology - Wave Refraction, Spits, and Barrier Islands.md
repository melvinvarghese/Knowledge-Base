# Coastal Geomorphology - Wave Refraction, Spits, and Barrier Islands

## Overview
Coastal Geomorphology investigates how oceanic wave energy, tidal currents, and longshore drift continuously reshape dynamic shorelines into erosional headlands, sandy **spits**, and offshore **barrier islands**.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
Wave Refraction & Headland Focus: Waves approaching an irregular shoreline feel bottom friction first in shallow water near headlands, slowing and bending (refracting) wave crests to concentrate erosional energy on rocky headlands while dissipating energy in sheltered bays.

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Longshore Drift & Barrier Island Systems: Oblique wave break pushes sand up the beach at an angle; backwash retreats perpendicular to gravity, creating a net lateral zig-zag transport of sand (longshore drift) that constructs spits, baymouth bars, and protective barrier islands.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Coastal erosion mitigation (groins, seawalls, beach nourishment), storm surge resilience in coastal urban centers.

---

## Key Terms
- **Wave Refraction**: The bending of wave crests as they enter shallower water at an angle to the shoreline.
- **Longshore Drift**: Movement of sediment along a coastline parallel to shore driven by oblique wave action.
- **Barrier Island**: Long, narrow offshore sandy island running parallel to the mainland protecting inland lagoons.
