# Mass Wasting - Landslides, Slumps, and Debris Flows

## Overview
Mass Wasting is the down-slope movement of rock, regolith, and soil under the direct influence of gravitational shear stress, occurring when gravitational driving forces exceed internal shear strength.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
Factor of Safety ($FS$) Force Balance: $FS = \frac{\text{Resisting Shear Strength}}{\text{Driving Shear Stress}}$. Water infiltration triggers slope failure by adding mechanical weight and dramatically increasing pore water pressure ($u$), which reduces effective normal stress ($\sigma' = \sigma - u$).

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Mass Wasting Classification: 1. Rockfalls (free fall); 2. Rotational Slumps (failure along concave curved surfaces); 3. Translational Slides (sliding along planar bedding planes); 4. Debris Flows (rapid, fluid saturated slurry flows).

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Slope stabilization engineering (soil nailing, gabions, horizontal drainage boreholes), landslide early warning radars.

---

## Key Terms
- **Mass Wasting**: Geomorphological process by which soil, sand, and rock move downslope under gravitational force.
- **Pore Water Pressure**: Pressure of groundwater held within soil or rock voids counteracting normal frictional strength.
- **Rotational Slump**: Mass movement where a cohesive block of rock or soil slips along a concave-upward curved failure surface.
