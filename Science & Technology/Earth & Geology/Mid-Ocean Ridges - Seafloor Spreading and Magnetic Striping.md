# Mid-Ocean Ridges - Seafloor Spreading and Magnetic Striping

## Overview
Mid-Ocean Ridges form an interconnected 65,000 km volcanic underwater mountain chain where divergent tectonic plates pull apart, creating new oceanic crust through seafloor spreading.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
Decompression Melting & Crustal Accretion: Tectonic rifting pulls lithospheric plates apart; mantle peridotite wells up passively, undergoing **decompression melting** without added heat, crystallizing into sheeted dikes, pillow basalts, and gabbros.

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Paleomagnetic Striping (Vine-Matthews-Morley Hypothesis): As newly erupted basalt cools below the Curie point (580°C), magnetic minerals align with Earth's contemporary magnetic field. Symmetrical alternating magnetic stripes parallel to ridges provided definitive proof of plate tectonics.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Marine hydrothermal mineral deposit exploration, seafloor geological age mapping.

---

## Key Terms
- **Seafloor Spreading**: Process occurring at mid-ocean ridges where new oceanic crust is formed through volcanic activity.
- **Decompression Melting**: Melting induced by a drop in confining lithostatic pressure without an increase in temperature.
- **Curie Temperature**: Critical temperature threshold below which magnetic minerals lock in permanent remanent magnetization.
