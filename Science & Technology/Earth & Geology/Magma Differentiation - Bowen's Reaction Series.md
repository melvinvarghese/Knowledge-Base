# Magma Differentiation - Bowen's Reaction Series

## Overview
Magma Differentiation describes the geochemical processes by which a single parent basaltic magma diversifies into a wide spectrum of igneous rocks through fractional crystallization, modeled by **Bowen's Reaction Series**.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
The Continuous vs Discontinuous Reaction Branches: As basaltic magma cools: 1. Discontinuous branch crystallizes distinct silicate structures as temperature drops: Olivine $\rightarrow$ Pyroxene $\rightarrow$ Amphibole $\rightarrow$ Biotite; 2. Continuous branch progressively shifts plagioclase feldspar from calcium-rich anorthite to sodium-rich albite.

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Fractional Crystallization & Silica Enrichment: Early-crystallizing minerals are dense and silica-poor (mafic), settling out gravitationally; the residual melt becomes progressively enriched in silica, potassium, and water, evolving toward viscous felsic granitic/rhyolitic compositions.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Locating precious magmatic ore deposits (platinum, chromite in layered mafic intrusions), igneous petrogenesis.

---

## Key Terms
- **Bowen's Reaction Series**: Empirical sequence of mineral crystallization from cooling basaltic magma.
- **Fractional Crystallization**: Removal and segregation of mineral precipitates from a melt, altering remaining liquid chemistry.
- **Mafic vs Felsic**: Classification of igneous rocks: mafic is rich in magnesium/iron; felsic is rich in feldspar/silica.
