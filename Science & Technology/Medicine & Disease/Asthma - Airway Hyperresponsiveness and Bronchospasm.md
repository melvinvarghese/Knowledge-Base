# Asthma - Airway Hyperresponsiveness and Bronchospasm

## Overview
Asthma is a chronic inflammatory disorder of the conducting airways characterized by bronchial hyperresponsiveness, reversible airflow obstruction, and acute episodic **bronchospasm**.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
Type 2 (Th2) Allergic Cascade: Inhaled allergens cross-link IgE antibodies on airway mast cells, releasing preformed histamine, leukotrienes ($LTC_4, LTD_4$), and cytokines (IL-4, IL-5, IL-13) that trigger eosinophilic infiltration, goblet cell mucus hypersecretion, and smooth muscle contraction.

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Airway Remodeling: Chronic recurring inflammation induces subepithelial collagen basement membrane thickening, bronchial smooth muscle hypertrophy, and irreversible fixed airflow limitation.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Stepwise pharmacotherapy: Short-acting beta-2 agonists (SABA: albuterol) for acute rescue, inhaled corticosteroids (ICS) for maintenance, and anti-IL-5 biologics.

---

## Key Terms
- **Bronchospasm**: Sudden constriction and narrowing of airway smooth muscle surrounding the bronchioles.
- **Airway Hyperresponsiveness**: Exaggerated bronchoconstrictor response to environmental physical or chemical triggers.
- **Inhaled Corticosteroid (ICS)**: Anti-inflammatory medication suppressing mucosal immune cytokine transcription in asthma.
