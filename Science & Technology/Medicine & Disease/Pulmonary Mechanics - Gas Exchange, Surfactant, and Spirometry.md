# Pulmonary Mechanics - Gas Exchange, Surfactant, and Spirometry

## Overview
Pulmonary mechanics describes the physical forces, lung compliance, surface tensions, and gas diffusion kinetics governing mammalian external respiration.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Boyle's Law & Surfactant Surface Tension: Diaphragm contraction expands thoracic cavity volume, lowering alveolar pressure below atmospheric pressure ($P_1 V_1 = P_2 V_2$) to draw air in. Type II alveolar cells secrete **pulmonary surfactant** (dipalmitoylphosphatidylcholine), which reduces surface tension at the air-liquid alveolar interface according to Laplace's Law ($P = 2\gamma / r$), preventing small alveoli from collapsing during exhalation.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Alveolar-Capillary Gas Diffusion: Oxygen and carbon dioxide diffuse across the razor-thin ($0.5\text{ }\mu\text{m}$) respiratory membrane driven by partial pressure gradients according to Fick's Law of Diffusion: $V_{\text{gas}} \propto \frac{A \cdot \Delta P \cdot D}{T}$. Hemoglobin saturation follows a sigmoidal allosteric binding curve shifted by pH and $CO_2$ (the Bohr Effect).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Neonatal respiratory distress syndrome (synthetic surfactant therapy), spirometry diagnostics for obstructive (COPD, asthma) vs restrictive lung diseases.

## Key Terminology
- **Pulmonary Surfactant**: Lipoprotein complex secreted by alveolar cells that lowers surface tension to prevent lung atelectasis.
- **Bohr Effect**: Physiological phenomenon where an increase in blood $CO_2$ and decrease in pH decreases hemoglobin's affinity for oxygen.
- **Spirometry**: Diagnostic pulmonary function test measuring the volume and speed of air an individual can inhale and exhale.
