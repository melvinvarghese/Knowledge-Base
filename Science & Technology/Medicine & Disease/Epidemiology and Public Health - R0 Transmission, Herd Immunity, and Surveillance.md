# Epidemiology and Public Health - R0 Transmission, Herd Immunity, and Surveillance

## Overview
Epidemiology is the foundational science of public health, studying the distribution, determinants, and mathematical transmission modeling of disease in populations.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Basic Reproduction Number ($R_0$): Quantifies the average number of secondary infectious cases generated by a single infected individual in a completely susceptible population: $R_0 = \beta \cdot c \cdot d$ (transmission probability per contact $\times$ contact rate $\times$ duration of infectiousness). If $R_0 > 1$, an epidemic spreads exponentially; if effective reproduction number $R_t < 1$, the outbreak declines.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Herd Immunity Threshold ($HIT$): The proportion of a population that must acquire immunity (via vaccination or recovery) to halt endemic community transmission: $HIT = 1 - \frac{1}{R_0}$. Highly transmissible pathogens like measles ($R_0 \approx 12\text{–}18$) require $92\text{–}95\%$ population immunity coverage to prevent outbreaks.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Contact tracing epidemiology, wastewater genomic surveillance, global vaccination schedule design.

## Key Terminology
- **Basic Reproduction Number ($R_0$)**: Mathematical metric indicating the contagiousness of an infectious agent in a fully susceptible population.
- **Herd Immunity**: Indirect protection from infectious disease that occurs when a sufficient percentage of a population becomes immune.
- **Syndromic Surveillance**: Public health monitoring of clinical signs and symptoms in real time before formal laboratory diagnostic confirmation.
