# Hemophilia A and B - Factor VIII and IX Deficiencies

## Overview
Hemophilia A and B are X-linked recessive bleeding disorders caused by inherited genetic deficiencies in coagulation **Factor VIII** (Hemophilia A) or **Factor IX** (Hemophilia B), crippling intrinsic pathway thrombin generation.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
Disrupted Clotting Kinetics: Factor VIIIa and IXa assemble on platelet membrane phospholipids to form the **tenase complex**, which exponentially activates Factor X. Their deficiency halts the propagation burst of thrombin, leaving initial platelet plugs unstable.

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Clinical Hemarthrosis Manifestations: Spontaneous or minor trauma-induced deep bleeding into weight-bearing joints (hemarthrosis, particularly knees and ankles) leads to chronic crippling joint arthropathy, deep muscle hematomas, and intracranial hemorrhage.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Recombinant clotting factor concentrate prophylaxis, non-factor bispecific antibodies (emicizumab bridging FIXa and FX), and liver-directed adeno-associated virus (AAV) gene therapy.

---

## Key Terms
- **Hemarthrosis**: Extravasation and accumulation of blood within a synovial joint cavity.
- **Tenase Complex**: Enzymatic complex of Factor IXa, Factor VIIIa, and calcium activating Factor X.
- **X-Linked Recessive**: Genetic inheritance pattern affecting predominantly males while females are asymptomatic carriers.
