# Cardiovascular Disease - Atherosclerosis, Myocardial Infarction, and Stents

## Overview
Cardiovascular disease remains the leading cause of mortality globally, driven by chronic arterial inflammation and ischemic myocardial infarction.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Pathogenesis of Atherosclerosis: Endothelial injury allows apolipoprotein B-containing low-density lipoproteins (LDL) to infiltrate the arterial subendothelial intima. Entrapped LDL undergoes oxidation, triggering monocyte recruitment. Monocytes differentiate into macrophages, engulfing oxidized LDL to become lipid-laden **foam cells**, which aggregate into fatty streaks and form fibrous atherosclerotic plaques with necrotic lipid cores.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Plaque Rupture & Acute Myocardial Infarction (Heart Attack): Inflammatory enzymes (matrix metalloproteinases) thin the plaque's fibrous cap. Hemodynamic stress causes plaque rupture, exposing thrombogenic subendothelial collagen and tissue factor, triggering immediate platelet aggregation and occlusive thrombus formation that cuts off coronary arterial blood flow, causing ischemic cardiomyocyte necrosis.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Percutaneous coronary intervention (drug-eluting stents), statin lipid-lowering therapies, bypass grafting (CABG).

## Key Terminology
- **Atherosclerosis**: Chronic vascular disease characterized by the deposition of fatty plaques on the inner walls of arteries.
- **Foam Cell**: Lipid-loaded macrophage that ingests oxidized low-density lipoproteins, forming the hallmark of atherosclerotic plaques.
- **Myocardial Infarction**: Necrosis of heart muscle tissue resulting from acute obstruction of coronary blood supply.
