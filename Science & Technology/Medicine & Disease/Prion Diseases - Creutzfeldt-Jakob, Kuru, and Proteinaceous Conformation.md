# Prion Diseases - Creutzfeldt-Jakob, Kuru, and Proteinaceous Conformation

## Overview
Prion diseases (Transmissible Spongiform Encephalopathies / TSEs) are fatal neurodegenerative conditions caused by infectious proteinaceous particles devoid of nucleic acids.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Prion Hypothesis (Stanley Prusiner, Nobel Prize 1997): The normal cellular prion protein ($\text{PrP}^C$), rich in $\alpha$-helices, is anchored to neuronal membranes. In prion disease, an aberrant, insoluble conformation ($\text{PrP}^{\text{Sc}}$), rich in $\beta$-sheets, acts as a conformational template, binding normal $\text{PrP}^C$ and catalyzing its refolding into the infectious $\text{PrP}^{\text{Sc}}$ state in an autocatalytic chain reaction.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Spongiform Encephalopathy & Transmission: Aggregated $\text{PrP}^{\text{Sc}}$ is exceptionally resistant to proteases, heat, radiation, and formalin. Aggregation causes rapid neuronal vacuolation, creating microscopic sponge-like voids in the cortex. Manifests as sporadic Creutzfeldt-Jakob Disease (sCJD), familial fatal insomnia (FFI), or acquired forms: kuru (ritualistic cannibalism in Papua New Guinea) and variant CJD (transmitted via bovine spongiform encephalopathy / 'Mad Cow Disease').

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Invariable 100% case-fatality rate, extreme hospital surgical sterilization decontamination protocols.

## Key Terminology
- **Prion**: Infectious misfolded protein particle that induces abnormal folding of specific normal cellular proteins in the brain.
- **Spongiform Encephalopathy**: Brain pathology characterized by microscopic vacuolization ('spongy' appearance) and severe neuronal loss.
- **PrPSc**: Protease-resistant, beta-sheet-rich abnormal conformational isoform of the prion protein.
