# Neurodegenerative Diseases - Alzheimer's, Parkinson's, and Protein Misfolding

## Overview
Neurodegenerative diseases are progressive, incurable conditions characterized by the slow selective death of vulnerable neuronal populations driven by pathological protein misfolding.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Alzheimer's Disease (AD) Pathology: Characterized by two pathognomonic proteinaceous lesions: 1. Extracellular **amyloid-beta ($A\beta_{42}$)** plaques formed from aberrant cleavage of Amyloid Precursor Protein (APP) by $\beta$- and $\gamma$-secretases; 2. Intracellular **neurofibrillary tangles (NFTs)** composed of hyperphosphorylated microtubule-associated tau protein, causing widespread cortical synaptic loss and hippocampal atrophy.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Parkinson's Disease (PD) & Lewy Bodies: Characterized by the progressive degeneration of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra pars compacta, causing resting tremors, rigidity, and bradykinesia. Histopathology reveals intracellular Lewy bodies composed of misfolded **$\alpha$-synuclein** aggregates propagating through neural circuits in a prion-like cascade.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Monoclonal anti-amyloid antibody therapies (lecanemab), deep brain stimulation (DBS) for Parkinson's motor symptom alleviation.

## Key Terminology
- **Amyloid-Beta ($Aeta$)**: Peptide fragment that aggregates into neurotoxic extracellular plaques in the brains of Alzheimer's patients.
- **Tau Protein**: Microtubule-stabilizing protein that forms insoluble intracellular paired helical filaments when hyperphosphorylated.
- **Lewy Body**: Abnormal intracellular protein aggregates predominantly composed of $lpha$-synuclein characteristic of Parkinson's disease.
