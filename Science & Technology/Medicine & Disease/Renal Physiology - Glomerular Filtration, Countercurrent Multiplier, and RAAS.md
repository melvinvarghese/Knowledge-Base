# Renal Physiology - Glomerular Filtration, Countercurrent Multiplier, and RAAS

## Overview
Renal physiology governs blood filtration, metabolic waste clearance (urea, creatinine), electrolyte balance, and systemic arterial blood pressure regulation.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Glomerular Filtration Rate (GFR): High hydrostatic pressure in glomerular capillaries forces fluid across the three-layer filtration barrier (fenestrated endothelium, basement membrane, and podocyte slit diaphragms) into Bowman's capsule, filtering roughly 180 liters of blood plasma daily while retaining albumin and cells.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Countercurrent Multiplier & The RAAS Axis: The hairpin loop of Henle establishes a high-osmolarity medullary interstitial gradient ($1,200\text{ mOsm/L}$) through active $NaCl$ pumping in the thick ascending limb, enabling antidiuretic hormone (ADH / vasopressin) to concentrate urine in collecting ducts. When blood pressure drops, the juxtaglomerular apparatus secretes **renin**, activating the Renin-Angiotensin-Aldosterone System (RAAS) to promote vasoconstriction and renal sodium retention.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Dialysis therapy for end-stage renal disease, ACE inhibitors and ARBs for hypertension management.

## Key Terminology
- **GFR**: Glomerular Filtration Rate; volume of fluid filtered from the renal glomerular capillaries into Bowman's capsule per unit time.
- **Loop of Henle**: U-shaped portion of the nephron conducting urine within each nephron of the kidney of reptiles, birds, and mammals.
- **RAAS**: Renin-Angiotensin-Aldosterone System; hormone system essential for the regulation of blood pressure and fluid balance.
