# Immunotherapy - Checkpoint Inhibitors, CAR-T Cells, and Monoclonal Antibodies

## Overview
Cancer Immunotherapy harnesses the patient's own adaptive immune system to identify, target, and destroy malignant cancer cells.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Immune Checkpoint Blockade: Tumors evade T-cell attack by engaging inhibitory checkpoint receptors (CTLA-4 and PD-1). Monoclonal antibody checkpoint inhibitors (ipilimumab, pembrolizumab, James Allison and Tasuku Honjo, Nobel Prize 2018) block these inhibitory interactions, releasing T-cell brakes to attack tumors.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
CAR-T Cell Therapy: Chimeric Antigen Receptor (CAR) T-cell therapy genetically engineers a patient's autologous T-cells with a synthetic receptor fusing an extracellular antibody single-chain variable fragment (scFv, e.g., targeting CD19 on B-cell lymphomas) with intracellular T-cell activation and co-stimulatory signaling domains ($CD3\zeta$ and $4\text{-}1BB$), returning re-infused 'living drugs' to eradicate leukemias.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Dramatically improved survival rates in advanced metastatic melanoma, non-small cell lung cancer, and hematologic malignancies.

## Key Terminology
- **Immune Checkpoint**: Regulatory pathway on immune cells that prevents autoimmune destruction, hijacked by tumors to suppress immune surveillance.
- **CAR-T Cell**: T-cell genetically engineered to express a synthetic receptor targeting a specific tumor-associated antigen.
- **PD-1 / PD-L1**: Programmed cell death protein 1 and its ligand, an immune checkpoint pathway targeted by immunotherapeutic antibodies.
