# Type 1 vs Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus - Pathophysiology and Complications

## Overview
Diabetes Mellitus encompasses chronic metabolic disorders of carbohydrate metabolism characterized by hyperglycemia, split into autoimmune Type 1 and lifestyle/insulin-resistant Type 2.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
Type 1 Autoimmune Beta-Cell Destruction: T-cell mediated autoimmune destruction of insulin-producing pancreatic $\beta$-cells (autoantibodies to GAD65, insulin), resulting in absolute insulin deficiency.

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Type 2 Insulin Resistance and Progressive Failure: Peripheral tissue resistance to insulin action (impaired GLUT4 translocation in skeletal muscle and adipose tissue) combined with progressive $\beta$-cell secretory burnout and amyloid polypeptide deposition.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Long-term vascular microvascular and macrovascular complications: diabetic retinopathy, nephropathy, peripheral neuropathy, and accelerated coronary artery disease.

---

## Key Terms
- **Insulin Resistance**: Impaired physiological cellular response to circulating concentrations of insulin hormone.
- **Hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c)**: Glycated hemoglobin reflecting mean blood glucose concentrations over the preceding 3 months.
- **GLUT4**: Insulin-regulated glucose transporter protein mediating glucose uptake into muscle and fat.
