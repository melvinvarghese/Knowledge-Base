# Hemostasis and Thrombosis - Coagulation Cascades and Platelet Aggregation

## Overview
Hemostasis is the tightly regulated physiological defense process that arrests bleeding at sites of vascular injury through platelet plug formation and fibrin mesh stabilization.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Primary vs Secondary Hemostasis: Primary hemostasis begins when subendothelial von Willebrand factor (vWF) binds platelet GPIb receptors, activating platelets to change shape, degranulate (releasing ADP and thromboxane $A_2$), and cross-link via fibrinogen and GPIIb/IIIa receptors into a temporary platelet plug.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Coagulation Cascade & Fibrin Clot Formation: Secondary hemostasis triggers sequential enzymatic cleavage of proenzymes. Tissue Factor (extrinsic pathway) and exposed collagen (intrinsic pathway) converge on Factor X activation. Factor Xa (with Factor Va) forms the **prothrombinase complex**, converting prothrombin into active **thrombin**. Thrombin cleaves soluble fibrinogen into insoluble **fibrin monomers**, which polymerize and are covalently cross-linked by Factor XIIIa into a stable clot.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Target of anticoagulants (heparin, warfarin, direct factor Xa inhibitors like rivaroxaban), antiplatelet therapy (aspirin).

## Key Terminology
- **Thrombin**: Key serine protease enzyme that converts soluble fibrinogen into insoluble fibrin during blood clot formation.
- **von Willebrand Factor (vWF)**: Blood glycoprotein involved in hemostasis that mediates platelet adhesion to damaged vascular endothelium.
- **Fibrin**: Fibrous, non-globular protein involved in the clotting of blood, forming a mesh over the platelet plug.
