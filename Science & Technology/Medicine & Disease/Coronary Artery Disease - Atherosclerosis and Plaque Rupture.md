# Coronary Artery Disease - Atherosclerosis and Plaque Rupture

## Overview
Coronary Artery Disease (CAD) is the progressive narrowing of coronary arteries caused by **atherosclerosis**—a chronic lipid-driven fibro-inflammatory disease of the arterial intima.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
Endothelial Dysfunction & Foam Cell Formation: Circulating LDL particles penetrate damaged vascular endothelium, undergoing oxidation; resident macrophages phagocytose oxidized LDL via scavenger receptors, transforming into lipid-laden **foam cells** forming early fatty streaks.

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Fibrous Cap Thinning & Acute Plaque Rupture: Pro-inflammatory cytokines stimulate matrix metalloproteinases that degrade the protective collagen fibrous cap; cap rupture exposes thrombogenic subendothelial Tissue Factor to blood, triggering rapid occlusive platelet thrombus formation.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Statin lipid-lowering therapy (HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors), percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) drug-eluting stents, coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG).

---

## Key Terms
- **Atherosclerosis**: Chronic vascular disease characterized by lipid deposition and fibrous plaque formation in arteries.
- **Foam Cell**: Lipid-engorged macrophage characteristic of early atherosclerotic plaque development.
- **Fibrous Cap**: Layer of collagen and smooth muscle cells overlying the necrotic lipid core of an atheroma.
