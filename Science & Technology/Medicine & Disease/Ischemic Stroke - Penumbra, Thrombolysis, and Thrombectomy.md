# Ischemic Stroke - Penumbra, Thrombolysis, and Thrombectomy

## Overview
Ischemic Stroke (87% of all strokes) occurs when acute occlusion of a cerebral artery halts blood flow to focal brain parenchyma, triggering rapid neuronal excitotoxic cell death.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
The Ischemic Core vs The Penumbra: The core suffers rapid irreversible necrosis (<10 mL/100g/min); the surrounding **ischemic penumbra** remains hypoperfused but viable for hours, kept alive by collateral flow, representing the critical target for emergency reperfusion.

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Reperfusion Therapies & The Time Window: Intravenous tissue plasminogen activator (IV-tPA / alteplase) within 4.5 hours dissolves fibrin clots; mechanical endovascular thrombectomy physically extracts large-vessel occlusions (up to 24 hours).

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
CT/MRI perfusion neuroimaging, secondary stroke prevention with antiplatelet and statin regimens, carotid endarterectomy.

---

## Key Terms
- **Ischemic Penumbra**: Zone of hypoperfused, electrically silent but structurally viable brain tissue surrounding a stroke core.
- **Tissue Plasminogen Activator (tPA)**: Serine protease thrombolytic drug converting plasminogen into plasmin to dissolve clots.
- **Endovascular Thrombectomy**: Minimally invasive catheter procedure mechanically retrieving blood clots from cerebral arteries.
