# Tuberculosis - Mycobacterium Pathogenesis, Caseous Granulomas, and Latency

## Overview
Tuberculosis (TB), caused by the acid-fast bacillus *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*, is a chronic infectious disease characterized by pulmonary necrotizing **caseous granulomas** and prolonged clinical latency.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
Intracellular Survival & Granuloma Formation: Bacteria are inhaled into alveoli; cell wall mycolic acids inhibit phagolysosomal maturation, allowing intracellular replication in macrophages. Cell-mediated immunity walls off infection in a **Ghon focus** (caseous necrosis core surrounded by epithelioid histiocytes and multinucleated Langhans giant cells).

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Latent vs Active Reactivation: 90% of immunocompetent individuals contain infection in a clinically dormant latent state; immunosuppression (e.g., HIV, anti-TNF therapy) compromises the granuloma, causing cavitary reactivation, hemoptysis, and contagious aerosol shedding.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Acid-fast Ziehl-Neelsen staining, GeneXpert molecular testing, six-month RIPE antibiotic protocol (Rifampin, Isoniazid, Pyrazinamide, Ethambutol).

---

## Key Terms
- **Caseous Necrosis**: Form of tissue necrosis presenting as a soft, cheese-like cellular debris core.
- **Mycolic Acid**: Unique long-chain waxy lipid in the cell wall of mycobacteria resisting chemical destruction.
- **Ghon Focus**: Primary circumscribed tuberculous parenchymal lung lesion that may calcify into a Ranke complex.
