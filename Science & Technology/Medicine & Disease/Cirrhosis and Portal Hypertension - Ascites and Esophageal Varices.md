# Cirrhosis and Portal Hypertension - Ascites and Esophageal Varices

## Overview
Cirrhosis is the end-stage consequence of chronic liver disease characterized by diffuse architectural distortion, regenerative parenchymal nodules, and dense fibrous bridging scar tissue.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
Hepatic Stellate Cell Activation & Fibrogenesis: Chronic hepatocellular injury (viral hepatitis, alcohol, NASH/MASH) activates quiescent vitamin-A storing hepatic stellate cells into contractile myofibroblasts, depositing excessive type I and III fibrillar collagen.

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Hemodynamics of Portal Hypertension: Vascular resistance increases across fibrotic sinusoids; elevated portal pressure ($>10\text{ mmHg}$) drives splanchnic arterial vasodilation, formation of lethal bleeding **esophageal varices**, and fluid extravasation into the peritoneal cavity (**ascites**).

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Endoscopic band ligation of variceal bleeds, non-selective beta-blockers, transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt (TIPS), and liver transplantation.

---

## Key Terms
- **Cirrhosis**: Late-stage fibrosis of the liver accompanied by loss of normal lobular architecture.
- **Portal Hypertension**: Abnormally elevated blood pressure in the portal venous system supplying the liver.
- **Ascites**: Abnormal accumulation of serous fluid within the peritoneal abdominal cavity.
