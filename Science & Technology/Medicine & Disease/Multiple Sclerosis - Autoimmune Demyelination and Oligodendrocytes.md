# Multiple Sclerosis - Autoimmune Demyelination and Oligodendrocytes

## Overview
Multiple Sclerosis (MS) is a chronic inflammatory, autoimmune demyelinating disease of the central nervous system where autoreactive lymphocytes attack and strip the protective **myelin sheath** surrounding axons.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
Autoimmune Neuro-Inflammation: Autoreactive CD4+ myelin-specific T cells cross the Blood-Brain Barrier, orchestrating macrophage and microglial destruction of myelin basic protein and oligodendrocytes, producing multifocal demyelinating sclerotic plaques.

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Axonal Conduction Block & Relapse Remission: Loss of myelin insulation impairs saltatory action potential conduction; initial remyelination occurs via oligodendrocyte precursor cells, but chronic recurrent attacks cause permanent axonal transaction and progressive neurological disability.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Disease-modifying therapies: B-cell-depleting monoclonal antibodies (ocrelizumab targeting CD20), sphingosine-1-phosphate receptor modulators, and beta-interferons.

---

## Key Terms
- **Demyelination**: Pathological destruction or loss of the myelin nerve sheath surrounding central or peripheral axons.
- **Oligodendrocyte**: Glial cell in the central nervous system responsible for producing insulating myelin sheaths.
- **Oligoclonal Bands**: Diagnostic bands of immunoglobulin G detected in CSF electrophoresis indicating CNS inflammation.
