# Diabetes Mellitus - Insulin Signaling, GLUT4 Translocation, and Complications

## Overview
Diabetes Mellitus is a chronic metabolic disorder of carbohydrate, fat, and protein metabolism characterized by persistent hyperglycemia due to insulin deficiency or insulin resistance.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Insulin Receptor Kinase & GLUT4 Translocation: Postprandial glucose surges stimulate pancreatic $\beta$-cells to secrete insulin. Insulin binds cell-surface tyrosine kinase receptors, activating the IRS-1 / PI3K / Akt signaling cascade. Phosphorylated Akt mobilizes storage vesicles containing **GLUT4 glucose transporters**, fusing them with the plasma membrane of skeletal muscle and adipocytes to absorb blood glucose.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Type 1 vs Type 2 & Chronic Microvascular Complications: Type 1 diabetes is autoimmune $\beta$-cell destruction. Type 2 diabetes involves peripheral insulin resistance paired with progressive secretory exhaustion. Chronic hyperglycemia causes advanced glycation end-products (AGEs) and microvascular damage: diabetic retinopathy (blindness), nephropathy (kidney failure), and peripheral neuropathy with poor wound healing.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Continuous glucose monitors (CGM), closed-loop artificial pancreas pumps, GLP-1 receptor agonists (semaglutide).

## Key Terminology
- **GLUT4**: Insulin-regulated glucose transporter protein found primarily in skeletal muscle and adipose tissue.
- **Insulin Resistance**: Pathological condition in which cells fail to respond normally to the hormone insulin.
- **Advanced Glycation End-Products (AGEs)**: Proteins or lipids that become non-enzymatically glycated after exposure to sugars, contributing to vascular aging.
