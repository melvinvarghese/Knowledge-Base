# Cancer Hallmarks - Oncogenes, Tumor Suppressors, and Metastasis

## Overview
The Hallmarks of Cancer, defined by Hanahan and Weinberg, describe the core biological capabilities acquired by human cells during the multistep development of malignant tumors.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
Proto-Oncogenes vs Tumor Suppressors: Gain-of-function mutations transform proto-oncogenes into constitutively active **oncogenes** (e.g., $KRAS, MYC$) driving uncontrolled proliferation; loss-of-function mutations disable **tumor suppressor genes** (e.g., $TP53, RB1$), abolishing cell-cycle checkpoints and apoptotic arrest.

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
The Metastatic Cascade: Primary tumor cells undergo Epithelial-Mesenchymal Transition (EMT), intravasate into blood/lymphatic vessels, survive circulatory mechanical shear stress, extravasate into distant organs, and colonize pre-metastatic niches.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Immune checkpoint blockade inhibitors (anti-PD-1/PD-L1, pembrolizumab), targeted small-molecule kinase inhibitors (imatinib, osimertinib).

---

## Key Terms
- **Proto-Oncogene**: Normal cellular gene that can transform into an oncogene through mutation or overexpression.
- **TP53**: Master tumor suppressor gene ('guardian of the genome') encoding p53 protein mediating DNA repair and apoptosis.
- **Epithelial-Mesenchymal Transition (EMT)**: Cellular biological process where polarized epithelial cells acquire invasive migratory mesenchymal phenotypes.
