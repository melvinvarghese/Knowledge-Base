# CRISPR-Cas9 Gene Editing and Targeted Genetic Therapies

## Overview
CRISPR-Cas9 is an adaptive bacterial RNA-guided immune system engineered into a precise tool for targeted genomic editing in living organisms.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Molecular Mechanism (Doudna and Charpentier, Nobel Prize 2020): Single guide RNA (sgRNA) matches a 20-nucleotide genomic target sequence immediately adjacent to a Protospacer Adjacent Motif (PAM: $5'\text{-NGG-3'}$). The Cas9 endonuclease unwinds the target DNA and introduces a blunt double-strand break (DSB) three nucleotides upstream of the PAM.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
DNA Repair Pathways & Therapeutics: Host cells repair the DSB via Non-Homologous End Joining (NHEJ, introducing gene-knocking indels) or Homology-Directed Repair (HDR, utilizing a donor template to introduce precise sequence corrections).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
FDA-approved therapies for sickle cell disease and $\beta$-thalassemia (Casgevy), CAR-T cell immunotherapy optimization, gene drives for malaria vector eradication.

## Key Terminology
- **CRISPR-Cas9**: Targeted gene-editing system adapted from bacterial immune systems utilizing a guide RNA and Cas9 endonuclease.
- **PAM Sequence**: Protospacer Adjacent Motif; short DNA sequence required for Cas9 protein binding and cleavage.
- **Homology-Directed Repair (HDR)**: High-fidelity DNA repair mechanism that uses a homologous template sequence to repair double-strand breaks.
