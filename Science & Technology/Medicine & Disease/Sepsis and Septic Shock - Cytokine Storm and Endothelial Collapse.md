# Sepsis and Septic Shock - Cytokine Storm and Endothelial Collapse

## Overview
Sepsis is a life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated, uncontrolled host immune response to infection, progressing in severe cases to **Septic Shock**.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
The Cytokine Storm & Endothelial Collapse: Massive PRR activation releases surges of pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-$\alpha$, IL-1$\beta$, IL-6); systemic endothelial activation triggers profound nitric oxide ($NO$) synthesis, widespread systemic vasodilation, and vascular barrier breakdown causing third-space plasma leakage.

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Microvascular Hypoperfusion & Lactic Acidosis: Disseminated microvascular microthrombosis (coagulation activation) and systemic capillary leak impair tissue oxygen delivery; cellular metabolism shifts to anaerobic glycolysis, elevating serum lactate ($>2\text{ mmol/L}$).

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Hour-1 Sepsis Bundle: Early broad-spectrum intravenous antibiotics, aggressive crystalloid fluid boluses, and norepinephrine vasopressor infusion to maintain mean arterial pressure (MAP $\ge 65\text{ mmHg}$).

---

## Key Terms
- **Septic Shock**: Subtype of sepsis characterized by profound circulatory, cellular, and metabolic abnormalities.
- **Cytokine Storm**: Severe systemic immune response marked by rapid hyper-release of pro-inflammatory cytokines.
- **Mean Arterial Pressure (MAP)**: Average arterial pressure throughout one cardiac cycle: $\text{MAP} = DBP + \frac{1}{3}(SBP - DBP)$.
