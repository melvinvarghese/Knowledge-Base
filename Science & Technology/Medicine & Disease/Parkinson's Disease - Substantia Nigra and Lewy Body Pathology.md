# Parkinson's Disease - Substantia Nigra and Lewy Body Pathology

## Overview
Parkinson's Disease is a progressive neurodegenerative movement disorder characterized by the preferential loss of dopaminergic neurons in the **substantia nigra pars compacta** and intracellular $\alpha$-synuclein **Lewy bodies**.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
Basal Ganglia Circuit Imbalance: Degeneration of nigrostriatal dopaminergic projections disinhibits the indirect motor pathway and reduces direct pathway activation, resulting in net excessive thalamic inhibition that produces resting tremor, rigidity, bradykinesia, and postural instability.

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Alpha-Synuclein Protein Misfolding: Misfolded $\alpha$-synuclein aggregates into toxic oligomers and insoluble fibrils, forming round eosinophilic intracytoplasmic inclusions (Lewy bodies) that disrupt vesicular transport and mitochondrial function.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Dopaminergic replacement therapy with Levodopa/Carbidopa, dopamine receptor agonists, Deep Brain Stimulation (DBS) targeting the subthalamic nucleus.

---

## Key Terms
- **Substantia Nigra**: Basal ganglia brain structure producing dopamine essential for motor planning and movement control.
- **Lewy Body**: Pathological abnormal intracellular aggregate of misfolded $\alpha$-synuclein protein.
- **Bradykinesia**: Slowness of physical movement and impaired progressive amplitude, a cardinal motor sign of Parkinson's.
