# Stem Cell Biology - Pluripotency, Yamanaka Factors, and Regenerative Medicine

## Overview
Stem Cell Biology explores undifferentiated biological cells capable of self-renewal and multilineage differentiation into specialized cell types.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Potency Hierarchy & Embryonic Stem Cells: Zygotes are totipotent (can form all embryonic and extra-embryonic placental tissues). Embryonic stem cells (ESCs), isolated from the blastocyst inner cell mass, are pluripotent—capable of differentiating into any cell type from the three primary germ layers (ectoderm, mesoderm, endoderm).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells (iPSCs) & Yamanaka Factors: Shinya Yamanaka (Nobel Prize 2012) disproved the dogma that cellular differentiation is irreversible. By introducing four defined transcription factors—**Oct3/4, Sox2, Klf4, and c-Myc (OSKM)**—into mature differentiated somatic adult fibroblasts, he reprogrammed adult cells back into pluripotent stem cells with indefinite self-renewal and developmental plasticity.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Patient-specific organoid disease modeling, regenerative cell replacement therapies for macular degeneration, Parkinson's, and spinal cord injuries.

## Key Terminology
- **Pluripotency**: Ability of a stem cell to differentiate into all three primary embryonic germ layers and all adult cell types.
- **iPSC**: Induced Pluripotent Stem Cell; adult differentiated cell genetically reprogrammed back into an embryonic-like pluripotent state.
- **Yamanaka Factors**: Set of four transcription factors (Oct4, Sox2, Klf4, c-Myc) capable of reverting mature cells to pluripotency.
