# Thyroid Disorders - Graves' Disease vs Hashimoto's Thyroiditis

## Overview
Autoimmune Thyroid Disorders represent polar manifestations of immune dysregulation: **Graves' Disease** produces hyperthyroidism via stimulatory antibodies, while **Hashimoto's Thyroiditis** produces hypothyroidism via destructive cell-mediated autoimmunity.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
Graves' Disease Agonistic Hyperthyroidism: Autoreactive B cells produce Thyroid-Stimulating Immunoglobulins (TSI / TRAb) that bind and continuously activate the TSH receptor, driving autonomous thyroxine overproduction, goiter, and Graves' orbitopathy (exophthalmos).

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Hashimoto's Destructive Hypothyroidism: Anti-TPO and anti-thyroglobulin antibodies recruit cytotoxic T cells and natural killer cells, progressively destroying thyroid follicular architecture via apoptosis and replacing tissue with dense lymphocytic infiltrates and Hürthle cells.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Anti-thyroid medications (methimazole), radioactive iodine ablation ($^{131}I$), and synthetic levothyroxine hormone replacement.

---

## Key Terms
- **Graves' Disease**: Autoimmune condition where stimulating autoantibodies trigger autonomous thyroid hormone overproduction.
- **Hashimoto's Thyroiditis**: Autoimmune inflammatory condition causing gradual destruction and functional failure of the thyroid gland.
- **Thyrotropin Receptor Antibody (TRAb)**: Stimulatory autoantibody characteristic of Graves' disease binding the TSH receptor.
