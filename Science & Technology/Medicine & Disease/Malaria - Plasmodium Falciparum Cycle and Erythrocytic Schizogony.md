# Malaria - Plasmodium Falciparum Cycle and Erythrocytic Schizogony

## Overview
Malaria is a mosquito-borne protozoan parasite infection (*Plasmodium falciparum*) characterized by cyclical paroxysmal fevers, hemolytic anemia, and microvascular cerebral sequestration.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
The Two-Host Life Cycle: *Anopheles* mosquitoes inject sporozoites; sporozoites invade hepatocytes, undergoing asymptomatic liver schizogony to release thousands of merozoites; merozoites invade red blood cells, initiating synchronous cycles of intraerythrocytic schizogony.

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Cytoadherence and Cerebral Malaria: *P. falciparum* expresses *PfEMP1* adhesive proteins on erythrocyte surfaces (knobs), binding host endothelial receptors (CD36, ICAM-1) to sequester infected RBCs in deep microvessels, avoiding splenic clearance and causing microvascular cerebral ischemia.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Artemisinin-based combination therapies (ACTs), RTS,S and R21 pre-erythrocytic circumsporozoite malaria vaccines.

---

## Key Terms
- **Merozoite**: Motile protozoan life cycle stage emerging from schizonts to invade red blood cells.
- **Cytoadherence**: Adhesion of infected erythrocytes to capillary endothelial walls mediated by PfEMP1.
- **Schizogony**: Asexual multiple fission reproduction occurring in liver and red blood cells during malaria infection.
