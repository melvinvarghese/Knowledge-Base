# Inflammatory Bowel Disease - Crohn's Disease vs Ulcerative Colitis

## Overview
Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD) encompasses chronic autoimmune relapsing inflammatory conditions of the gastrointestinal tract, divided into **Crohn's Disease** and **Ulcerative Colitis**.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
Pathological Divergence: Crohn's Disease features transmural, discontinuous 'skip lesions' from mouth to anus with non-caseating granulomas, strictures, and fistulae; Ulcerative Colitis features continuous, superficial mucosal inflammation restricted to the colon and rectum with crypt abscesses.

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Immunological Dysregulation: Defective mucosal epithelial barrier integrity and altered gut microbiome trigger uncontrolled Th1/Th17 immune responses (Crohn's) or Th2-like responses (Ulcerative Colitis), driving mucosal ulceration.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Targeted biologic therapies: anti-TNF-$\alpha$ monoclonal antibodies (infliximab), anti-integrin agents (vedolizumab), and surgical bowel resection.

---

## Key Terms
- **Crohn's Disease**: Transmural, granulomatous inflammatory bowel disease capable of affecting any segment of the GI tract.
- **Ulcerative Colitis**: Superficial inflammatory condition confined to the mucosal lining of the colon and rectum.
- **Transmural Inflammation**: Pathological inflammation extending across the full thickness of the intestinal wall.
