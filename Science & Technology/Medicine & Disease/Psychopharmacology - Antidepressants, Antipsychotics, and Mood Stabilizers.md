# Psychopharmacology - Antidepressants, Antipsychotics, and Mood Stabilizers

## Overview
Psychopharmacology studies the chemical modulation of mood, cognition, and behavior through psychoactive pharmaceutical agents.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Antidepressants & The Monoamine Hypothesis: Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitors (SSRIs: fluoxetine, sertraline) selectively block the serotonin transporter (SERT), acutely increasing synaptic 5-HT concentrations. Therapeutic clinical efficacy requires 2 to 4 weeks, driven downstream by neuroplastic adaptations, including upregulated brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) and hippocampal neurogenesis.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Antipsychotics & Dopamine $D_2$ Antagonism: First-generation typical antipsychotics (haloperidol) non-selectively block dopamine $D_2$ receptors in the mesolimbic pathway to suppress psychotic hallucinations, but risk extrapyramidal motor side effects. Second-generation atypical antipsychotics (olanzapine, quetiapine) combine $D_2$ antagonism with $5\text{-HT}_{2A}$ receptor antagonism, reducing motor risks.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Bipolar disorder management with lithium carbonate (modulating inositol monophosphatase and GSK-3 pathways), treatment-resistant depression therapies (ketamine NMDA antagonism).

## Key Terminology
- **SSRI**: Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitor; class of drugs typically used as antidepressants in major depressive disorder.
- **Dopamine $D_2$ Receptor**: G-protein coupled dopamine receptor subtype targeted by clinical antipsychotic medications.
- **BDNF**: Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor; protein that promotes the survival, growth, and synaptic plasticity of neurons.
