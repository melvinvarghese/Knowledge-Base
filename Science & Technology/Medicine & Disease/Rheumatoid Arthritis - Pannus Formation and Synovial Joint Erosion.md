# Rheumatoid Arthritis - Pannus Formation and Synovial Joint Erosion

## Overview
Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA) is a chronic, systemic inflammatory autoimmune disorder targeting the synovial lining of diarthrodial joints, causing progressive bone erosion, cartilage destruction, and joint deformities.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
Synovial Pannus Formation: Citrullinated self-proteins (detected clinically via Anti-CCP antibodies and Rheumatoid Factor) trigger autoimmune infiltration of CD4+ Th1/Th17 cells; synoviocytes hyper-proliferate, forming an invasive, vascularized inflammatory tissue mass (**pannus**).

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Osteoclast Activation & Cartilage Dissolution: The inflammatory pannus secretes matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) and high levels of RANKL, driving osteoclasts to dissolve subchondral bone and degrade articular cartilage.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Disease-Modifying Antirheumatic Drugs (DMARDs: methotrexate) and biologic targeted inhibitors of TNF-$\alpha$ (adalimumab), IL-6, and JAK kinase inhibitors.

---

## Key Terms
- **Pannus**: Abnormal invasive granulation tissue mass in synovial joints mediating bone and cartilage erosion.
- **Rheumatoid Factor (RF)**: Autoantibody (typically IgM) directed against the Fc region of host IgG antibodies.
- **Anti-CCP**: Highly specific autoantibody targeting cyclic citrullinated peptides used in early RA diagnosis.
