# Autoimmune Diseases - Loss of Self-Tolerance, Lupus, and Type 1 Diabetes

## Overview
Autoimmune diseases arise when the adaptive immune system fails to maintain self-tolerance, mounting destructive humoral and cellular attacks against healthy host tissues.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Central & Peripheral Tolerance Failure: During thymic T-cell education, autoimmune regulator (AIRE) proteins present self-antigens; self-reactive clones undergo negative selection (apoptosis). In autoimmune disease, autoreactive T- and B-cell clones escape into the periphery and fail to be suppressed by regulatory T cells ($T_{\text{reg}}$), triggered by genetic susceptibility (HLA alleles) and environmental insults (molecular mimicry after viral infections).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Systemic vs Organ-Specific Manifestations: 1. Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE): Characterized by anti-nuclear autoantibodies (ANA, anti-dsDNA) forming immune complexes that deposit in renal glomeruli, skin, and joints; 2. Type 1 Diabetes: Autoimmune CD8+ T-cell destruction of insulin-secreting $\beta$-cells in the pancreatic islets of Langerhans, causing absolute insulin deficiency.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Targeted biologic therapies (anti-CD20 rituximab depleting B-cells, anti-TNF monoclonal antibodies in rheumatoid arthritis).

## Key Terminology
- **Self-Tolerance**: Inability of an immune system to mount an immune response against the host's own cellular antigens.
- **Molecular Mimicry**: Theoretical mechanism where foreign viral or bacterial antigens share sequence similarity with self-antigens, triggering cross-reactive autoimmunity.
- **Regulatory T Cell ($T_{	ext{reg}}$)**: Subpopulation of T cells that modulate the immune system, maintain tolerance to self-antigens, and abrogate autoimmune disease.
