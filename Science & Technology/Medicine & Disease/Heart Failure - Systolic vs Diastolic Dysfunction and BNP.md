# Heart Failure - Systolic vs Diastolic Dysfunction and BNP

## Overview
Heart Failure is a complex clinical syndrome where the heart is unable to pump sufficient blood to meet the metabolic demands of peripheral tissues, or can do so only at elevated filling pressures.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
HFrEF (Systolic) vs HFpEF (Diastolic) Pathophysiology: Heart Failure with Reduced Ejection Fraction (HFrEF, EF <40%) features eccentric remodeling and impaired contractile ejection; Heart Failure with Preserved Ejection Fraction (HFpEF, EF $\ge 50%$) features concentric hypertrophy and impaired ventricular diastolic relaxation.

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
B-Type Natriuretic Peptide (BNP): Ventricular myocardial stretch stimulates secretion of BNP; BNP induces systemic vasodilation and renal natriuresis, serving as a sensitive clinical biomarker differentiating cardiogenic from pulmonary dyspnea.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Four-pillar medical therapy for HFrEF: SGLT2 inhibitors, beta-blockers, ARNI (sacubitril/valsartan), and mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (spironolactone).

---

## Key Terms
- **Ejection Fraction (EF)**: Volumetric percentage of blood ejected from the left ventricle with each heartbeat.
- **B-Type Natriuretic Peptide (BNP)**: Cardiac hormone secreted by ventricles in response to elevated wall stretch and pressure.
- **Ventricular Remodeling**: Structural change in ventricular size, shape, and function following myocardial injury.
