# Cancer Hallmarks and Oncogenesis - The Hanahan-Weinberg Paradigm

## Overview
The Hallmarks of Cancer (Douglas Hanahan and Robert Weinberg) organize the biological capabilities acquired during the multistep development of human tumors.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Core Biological Hallmarks: Cancer cells acquire distinct autonomous capabilities: 1. Sustaining proliferative signaling (mutations in oncogenes like $RAS$ or $MYC$); 2. Evading growth suppressors (inactivation of tumor suppressor genes like $TP53$ or $RB$); 3. Resisting cell death (apoptosis avoidance via $BCL2$ overexpression); 4. Enabling replicative immortality (telomerase reactivation); 5. Inducing angiogenesis (VEGF-driven blood vessel formation); 6. Activating invasion and metastasis.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Emerging Hallmarks & The Tumor Microenvironment: Recent updates incorporate metabolic reprogramming (the Warburg Effect—aerobic glycolysis) and evading immune destruction (checkpoint upregulation like PD-L1), alongside pro-tumor inflammatory microenvironments.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Foundation of modern targeted molecular oncology (tyrosine kinase inhibitors) and checkpoint immunotherapy (anti-PD-1 / anti-CTLA-4).

## Key Terminology
- **Oncogene**: Gene that has the potential to cause cancer when mutated or expressed at abnormally high levels.
- **Tumor Suppressor Gene**: Gene that protects a cell from one step on the path to cancer, such as $TP53$ ('guardian of the genome').
- **Angiogenesis**: Physiological process through which new blood vessels form from pre-existing vessels, exploited by tumors to feed growth.
