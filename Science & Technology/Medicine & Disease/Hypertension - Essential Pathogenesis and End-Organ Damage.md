# Hypertension - Essential Pathogenesis and End-Organ Damage

## Overview
Hypertension (sustained blood pressure $\ge 130/80\text{ mmHg}$) is a leading global risk factor for cardiovascular disease, categorized into primary (essential) hypertension and secondary hypertension.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
Pathogenetic Mechanisms: Complex interplay of inappropriate renal sodium retention, heightened sympathetic nervous tone, vascular endothelial stiffening, and dysregulated RAAS activity, driving sustained high systemic vascular resistance ($BP = CO \times SVR$).

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
End-Organ Target Damage: Chronic barotrauma accelerates atherosclerosis, induces left ventricular concentric hypertrophy, causes hypertensive nephrosclerosis leading to renal failure, and damages cerebral microvessels causing lacunar strokes.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Antihypertensive drug combinations (calcium channel blockers, thiazide diuretics, ACE inhibitors), dietary sodium restriction (DASH diet).

---

## Key Terms
- **Systemic Vascular Resistance (SVR)**: Resistance to blood flow offered by all systemic peripheral vascular beds.
- **Arteriosclerosis**: Thickening, hardening, and loss of elasticity of the walls of arterial blood vessels.
- **End-Organ Damage**: Pathological damage to heart, brain, kidneys, and retina resulting from chronic uncontrolled hypertension.
