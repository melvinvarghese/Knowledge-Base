# Monoclonal Antibodies - Hybridoma Technology and Biologic Therapeutics

## Overview
Monoclonal Antibodies (mAbs) are monospecific antibodies produced by identical immune clones, revolutionized by Georges Köhler and César Milstein's 1975 **hybridoma technology**.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
Hybridoma Fusion Technology: Spleen B lymphocytes from an immunized animal are fused with immortal myeloma cancer cells using polyethylene glycol (PEG); surviving hybridomas grown in HAT selection medium produce indefinite, pure monoclonal antibodies targeting a single specific epitope.

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Humanization & Molecular Engineering: Murine antibodies are chimerized or fully humanized via transgenic human-Ig mice (reducing HAMA immune clearance), engineered into antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) and bispecific T-cell engagers (BiTEs).

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Targeted cancer biologics (trastuzumab, rituximab), autoimmune blockade (adalimumab), and emerging neurodegenerative anti-amyloid therapies.

---

## Key Terms
- **Hybridoma**: Immortalized cell hybrid created by fusing an antibody-producing B cell with a myeloma cell.
- **Humanized Antibody**: Antibody engineered to replace animal non-binding constant regions with human protein sequences.
- **Antibody-Drug Conjugate (ADC)**: Monoclonal antibody chemically linked to a potent cytotoxic chemotherapy payload.
