# Osteoporosis - Trabecular Microarchitecture Degradation and T-Scores

## Overview
Osteoporosis is a systemic skeletal disorder characterized by low bone mineral density and microarchitectural deterioration of trabecular bone tissue, predisposing to fragility fractures.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
Imbalanced Remodeling Kinetics: Estrogen deficiency (post-menopause) removes tonic inhibition on osteoclasts, causing bone resorption to outpace osteoblast bone formation. Trabecular plates perforate and thin, reducing structural compressive load strength.

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Diagnostic DEXA & T-Score Criteria: Dual-Energy X-Ray Absorptiometry (DEXA) scans measure bone mineral density ($g/cm^2$); a T-score $\le -2.5$ standard deviations below young healthy reference population defines clinical osteoporosis.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Oral bisphosphonates (alendronate inducing osteoclast apoptosis), teriparatide (recombinant PTH pulsing for anabolic bone formation), fall prevention programs.

---

## Key Terms
- **Bone Mineral Density (BMD)**: Quantitative measurement of mineral mass per area of bone tissue.
- **T-Score**: Number of standard deviations above or below the mean bone density of young healthy adults.
- **Trabecular Bone**: Spongy, highly porous internal bone meshwork highly sensitive to metabolic remodeling.
