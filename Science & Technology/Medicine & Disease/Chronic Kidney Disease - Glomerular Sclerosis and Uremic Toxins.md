# Chronic Kidney Disease - Glomerular Sclerosis and Uremic Toxins

## Overview
Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) is the progressive, irreversible loss of renal function spanning months to years, culminating in end-stage renal disease (ESRD) and the retention of nitrogenous **uremic toxins**.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
Hyperfiltration Injury & Glomerulosclerosis: Loss of nephrons forces remaining surviving nephrons to hyperfilter under high capillary pressure; chronic glomerular hypertension induces podocyte detachment, mesangial expansion, and irreversible focal segmental glomerulosclerosis.

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Systemic Uremic Complications: Uremic toxin accumulation (urea, indoxyl sulfate, p-cresyl sulfate) causes uremic encephalopathy, pericarditis, platelet dysfunction, anemia of chronic disease (EPO deficiency), and secondary hyperparathyroidism.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Renal replacement therapies: hemodialysis, peritoneal dialysis, and kidney transplantation; early treatment with SGLT2 inhibitors and ACE inhibitors.

---

## Key Terms
- **Glomerulosclerosis**: Scarring and fibrosis of the renal glomeruli destroying blood-filtering capabilities.
- **Uremia**: Clinical toxic syndrome resulting from the accumulation of waste products normally cleared by kidneys.
- **Hemodialysis**: Extracorporeal medical procedure filtering metabolic wastes and excess water from blood.
