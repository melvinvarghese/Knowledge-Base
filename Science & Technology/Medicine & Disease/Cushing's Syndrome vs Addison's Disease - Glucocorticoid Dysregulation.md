# Cushing's Syndrome vs Addison's Disease - Glucocorticoid Dysregulation

## Overview
Adrenocortical endocrine pathology encompasses the extremes of cortisol regulation: **Cushing's Syndrome** (pathological hypercortisolemia) versus **Addison's Disease** (primary adrenocortical adrenal insufficiency).

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
Cushing's Syndrome Phenotype: Excess cortisol stimulates gluconeogenesis and lipogenesis, producing central obesity, buffalo hump, moon facies, abdominal purple striae, osteoporosis, muscle wasting, and hypertension.

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Addison's Disease Adrenal Failure: Autoimmune destruction of all three adrenal cortical zones results in glucocorticoid and mineralocorticoid deficiency, producing refractory hypotension, severe fatigue, hypoglycemia, and cutaneous hyperpigmentation (elevated POMC/ACTH stimulating melanocytes).

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Dexamethasone suppression testing and ACTH stimulation testing, hydrocortisone and fludrocortisone replacement therapy, transsphenoidal pituitary adenomectomy.

---

## Key Terms
- **Hypercortisolemia**: State of chronically elevated circulating cortisol levels producing Cushing's syndrome.
- **Adrenal Crisis**: Acute, life-threatening cardiovascular collapse and shock in patients with severe adrenal insufficiency.
- **Pro-opiomelanocortin (POMC)**: Precursor polypeptide cleaved into ACTH and melanocyte-stimulating hormone (MSH).
