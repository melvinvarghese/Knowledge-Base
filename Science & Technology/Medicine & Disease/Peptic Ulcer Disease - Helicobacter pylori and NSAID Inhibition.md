# Peptic Ulcer Disease - Helicobacter pylori and NSAID Inhibition

## Overview
Peptic Ulcer Disease (PUD) is a mucosal break in the stomach or duodenum extending through the muscularis mucosae, predominantly caused by *Helicobacter pylori* infection or nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) use.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
H. pylori Pathogenesis: Flagellated microaerophilic bacteria survive gastric acid using urease to generate an alkaline ammonia cloud ($pH$ shield); bacterial CagA and VacA virulence toxins trigger chronic active mucosal inflammation and localized mucosal barrier breakdown.

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
NSAID Prostaglandin Suppression: Non-selective NSAIDs inhibit cyclooxygenase-1 (COX-1), blocking mucosal synthesis of protective prostaglandins ($PGE_2$), halting gastric mucus and bicarbonate secretion and exposing mucosa to acid digestion.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Triple/Quadruple antibiotic eradication therapy for *H. pylori* (PPI + clarithromycin + amoxicillin), endoscopic thermal cautery and hemoclip placement.

---

## Key Terms
- **Helicobacter pylori**: Gram-negative, microaerophilic spiral bacterium colonizing the gastric mucosa.
- **Urease**: Enzyme hydrolyzing urea into ammonia and carbon dioxide, buffering local gastric acidity.
- **Cyclooxygenase-1 (COX-1)**: Constitutive enzyme synthesizing cytoprotective prostaglandins in the gastric mucosa.
