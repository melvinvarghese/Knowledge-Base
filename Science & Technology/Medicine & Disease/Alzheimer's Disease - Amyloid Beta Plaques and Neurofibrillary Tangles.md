# Alzheimer's Disease - Amyloid Beta Plaques and Neurofibrillary Tangles

## Overview
Alzheimer's Disease is the most common cause of progressive dementia, histopathologically defined by extracellular **Amyloid-Beta ($A\beta$) plaques** and intracellular **hyperphosphorylated tau neurofibrillary tangles**.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
The Amyloid Cascade Hypothesis: Dysfunctional sequential cleavage of Amyloid Precursor Protein (APP) by $\beta$-secretase (BACE1) and $\gamma$-secretase generates neurotoxic, aggregate-prone $A\beta_{42}$ peptides that aggregate into insoluble oligomers and senile plaques.

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Tau Hyperphosphorylation & Synaptic Loss: Microtubule-stabilizing protein tau becomes hyperphosphorylated, dissociating from microtubules and aggregating into paired helical filaments (neurofibrillary tangles), causing axonal transport collapse and hippocampal synaptic loss.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Amyloid-clearing monoclonal antibody therapeutics (lecanemab, donanemab), acetylcholinesterase inhibitors (donepezil), and NMDA antagonists (memantine).

---

## Key Terms
- **Amyloid-Beta ($A\beta$)**: Peptide fragment derived from APP that misfolds into extracellular neurotoxic plaques.
- **Neurofibrillary Tangle**: Intracellular aggregate of hyperphosphorylated tau protein disrupting neuronal cytoskeleton.
- **Hippocampus**: Medial temporal lobe brain structure essential for converting short-term to long-term memory.
