# Pneumonia - Alveolar Consolidation, Sepsis, and Pathogens

## Overview
Pneumonia is an acute inflammatory infection of the pulmonary parenchyma where microbial proliferation fills alveolar spaces with inflammatory exudate, causing physical **consolidation**.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
Pathological Stages of Lobar Pneumonia: 1. Congestion (vascular engorgement); 2. Red Hepatization (massive erythrocyte, neutrophil, and fibrin exudation resembling liver tissue); 3. Grey Hepatization (fibrinopurulent exudate); 4. Resolution.

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Common Pathogens: Community-acquired pneumonia is predominantly caused by *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, atypical bacteria (*Mycoplasma*), and respiratory viruses; hospital-acquired pneumonia involves multi-drug resistant *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* and MRSA.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
CURB-65 prognostic severity scoring, empiric antibiotic regimens (beta-lactams + macrolides), chest radiography consolidation diagnostics.

---

## Key Terms
- **Consolidation**: Solidification of lung tissue resulting from the accumulation of cellular inflammatory exudate.
- **Streptococcus pneumoniae**: Gram-positive lancet-shaped diplococcus causing typical lobar pneumonia.
- **CURB-65**: Clinical risk score predicting mortality and guiding hospitalization in community-acquired pneumonia.
