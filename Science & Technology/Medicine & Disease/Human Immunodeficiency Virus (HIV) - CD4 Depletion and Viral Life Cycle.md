# Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV) - CD4 Depletion and Viral Life Cycle

## Overview
Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV) is an enveloped retrovirus that targets and progressively depletes helper **CD4+ T lymphocytes**, leading to profound immunodeficiency and Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome (AIDS).

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
The Retroviral Replication Cycle: 1. Viral envelope gp120 binds CD4 and co-receptors (CCR5 or CXCR4); 2. Viral entry; 3. Reverse Transcriptase converts viral single-stranded RNA into double-stranded cDNA; 4. Integrase inserts viral DNA into the host genome; 5. Protease cleaves polyprotein precursors during virion maturation.

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
CD4+ T Cell Depletion Mechanisms: Direct viral cytopathic lysis, persistent pyroptosis of abortively infected bystander cells, and continuous chronic immune hyperactivation driving premature exhaustion of the T cell pool ($CD4 < 200\text{ cells/}\mu\text{L}$ defining AIDS).

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Combination Antiretroviral Therapy (cART), Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis (PrEP), long-acting injectable integrase inhibitors.

---

## Key Terms
- **Reverse Transcriptase**: RNA-dependent DNA polymerase enzyme transcribing retroviral RNA into complementary DNA.
- **Integrase**: Retroviral enzyme responsible for inserting viral cDNA into host chromosomal DNA.
- **Opportunistic Infection**: Infection caused by pathogens that take advantage of a compromised host immune system (e.g., *Pneumocystis*).
