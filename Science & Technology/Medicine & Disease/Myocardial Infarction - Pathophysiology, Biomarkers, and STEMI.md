# Myocardial Infarction - Pathophysiology, Biomarkers, and STEMI

## Overview
Myocardial Infarction (heart attack) occurs when an acute complete or partial occlusion of a coronary artery deprives downstream myocardium of blood and oxygen, causing irreversible ischemic necrosis within 20–40 minutes.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
Ischemic Necrosis & The Wavefront Phenomenon: Ischemia progresses from subendocardium outward toward the epicardium; cellular ATP depletion arrests $Na^+/K^+$ pumps, causing cell swelling, membrane rupture, and release of cardiac enzymes.

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Diagnostic Biomarkers and ECG Categorization: ST-Elevation Myocardial Infarction (STEMI) indicates transmural occlusion requiring emergency catheterization; **High-Sensitivity Cardiac Troponin I and T** ($cTnI, cTnT$) provide gold-standard diagnostic detection of microscopic myocardial necrosis.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Emergency primary angioplasty (door-to-balloon target <90 min), dual antiplatelet therapy (aspirin + P2Y12 inhibitor), thrombolytic therapy.

---

## Key Terms
- **Myocardial Infarction**: Irreversible necrosis of heart muscle resulting from prolonged acute ischemia.
- **Troponin (cTnI/cTnT)**: Regulatory cardiac muscle protein released into circulation during myocardial cellular necrosis.
- **STEMI**: ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction indicating acute transmural coronary occlusion.
