# Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation (CPR) - Hemodynamics and Defibrillation

## Overview
Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation (CPR) is an emergency lifesaving intervention providing artificial coronary and cerebral perfusion during sudden cardiac arrest, paired with electrical **defibrillation** for shockable rhythms.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
The Thoracic Pump vs Cardiac Pump Mechanisms: External chest compressions (100–120/min at $\ge 5\text{ cm}$ depth) compress the heart between sternum and spine, generating a forward pressure gradient that yields ~25–30% of normal cardiac output, sustaining critical myocardial coronary perfusion pressure ($CPP \ge 15\text{ mmHg}$).

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Electrical Defibrillation Physics: In shockable ventricular fibrillation (VF) or pulseless ventricular tachycardia (pVT), a biphasic electrical shock (150–200 Joules) passes through the myocardium, simultaneously depolarizing the critical mass of myocytes and allowing the natural SA node pacemaker to reset rhythm.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Public Automated External Defibrillator (AED) networks, high-performance mechanical compression devices (LUCAS), targeted post-resuscitation care bundles.

---

## Key Terms
- **Ventricular Fibrillation (VF)**: Lethal chaotic, unsynchronized quivering of ventricular heart muscle producing zero cardiac output.
- **Defibrillation**: Therapeutic administration of electrical shock terminating cardiac arrhythmias by depolarizing myocardial tissue.
- **Coronary Perfusion Pressure (CPP)**: Aortic diastolic pressure minus right atrial diastolic pressure, the primary determinant of cardiac arrest survival.
