# Vaccine Technologies - Attenuated, mRNA, and Viral Vector Platforms

## Overview
Vaccine technology trains the adaptive immune system to recognize pathogen antigens, establishing protective immunological memory without causing natural disease.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Traditional Platforms (Attenuated, Inactivated, Subunit): Live attenuated vaccines (MMR) utilize weakened pathogens capable of transient replication, generating robust CD4+ and CD8+ memory. Inactivated vaccines (polio Salk) and recombinant protein subunits (hepatitis B) present non-replicating antigens, requiring chemical **adjuvants** (aluminum salts) to stimulate innate toll-like receptors.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Next-Generation mRNA & Viral Vector Platforms: Katalin Karikó and Drew Weissman (Nobel Prize 2023) discovered that incorporating modified nucleosides ($N^1$-methylpseudouridine) prevents synthetic mRNA from triggering destructive inflammatory reactions. Encapsulated in **lipid nanoparticles (LNPs)**, mRNA enters host cells, instructing ribosomes to synthesize target antigens (e.g., SARS-CoV-2 spike protein) directly within the patient's cells.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Eradication of smallpox, near-eradication of wild poliovirus, rapid global pandemic response capability.

## Key Terminology
- **Lipid Nanoparticle (LNP)**: Microscopic lipid envelope protecting and delivering fragile mRNA molecules into human cells.
- **Adjuvant**: Pharmacological agent added to a vaccine that boosts the recipient's immune response to the antigen.
- **Pseudouridine**: Modified synthetic RNA nucleoside that avoids destructive innate toll-like receptor immune detection.
