# The Complement System - Classical, Lectin, and Alternative Cascades

## Overview
The Complement System is an enzymatic cascade of over 30 soluble plasma proteins that enhances (complements) the ability of antibodies and phagocytes to clear microbial pathogens.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
The Three Activation Pathways: 1. Classical Pathway (antigen-antibody immune complexes binding C1q); 2. Lectin Pathway (mannose-binding lectin binding microbial carbohydrates); 3. Alternative Pathway (spontaneous low-rate C3 hydrolysis tick-over).

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
The Membrane Attack Complex (MAC): All three pathways converge on C3 convertase, cleaving C3 into anaphylatoxin C3a and opsonin C3b, culminating in C5b assembling C6, C7, C8, and multiple C9 proteins into the pore-forming **Membrane Attack Complex ($C5b-9$)** that lyses target cells.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria (treated with eculizumab anti-C5 antibody), autoimmune immune-complex glomerulonephritis.

---

## Key Terms
- **Membrane Attack Complex (MAC)**: Pore-forming multiprotein complex punching structural holes through pathogen lipid bilayers.
- **Opsonization**: Immune marking process coating pathogens with C3b or antibodies to promote phagocytosis.
- **Anaphylatoxin**: Pro-inflammatory peptide fragments (C3a, C5a) inducing histamine release and vascular permeability.
