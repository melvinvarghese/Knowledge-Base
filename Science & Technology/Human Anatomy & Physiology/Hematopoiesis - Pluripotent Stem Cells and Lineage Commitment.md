# Hematopoiesis - Pluripotent Stem Cells and Lineage Commitment

## Overview
Hematopoiesis is the biological process of blood cell generation within bone marrow, where rare self-renewing **Hematopoietic Stem Cells (HSCs)** differentiate into all mature erythrocytes, leukocytes, and platelets.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
Myeloid vs Lymphoid Lineage Bifurcation: Multipotent HSCs split into Common Myeloid Progenitors (producing red blood cells, megakaryocytes, monocytes, and granulocytes) and Common Lymphoid Progenitors (giving rise to B, T, and NK lymphocytes).

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Cytokine Signaling Regulators: Lineage commitment is directed by specific growth factors: Erythropoietin (EPO) stimulates erythrocyte production; Thrombopoietin (TPO) drives platelet budding; G-CSF stimulates neutrophil production.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Bone marrow stem cell transplantation for leukemia, synthetic recombinant EPO doping in athletics, regenerative cord blood banking.

---

## Key Terms
- **Hematopoietic Stem Cell (HSC)**: Multipotent, self-renewing stem cell giving rise to all functional blood cell lineages.
- **Erythropoietin (EPO)**: Renal glycoprotein hormone stimulating erythropoiesis in response to cellular hypoxia.
- **Megakaryocyte**: Massive bone marrow cell whose cytoplasmic fragmentation generates circulating platelets.
