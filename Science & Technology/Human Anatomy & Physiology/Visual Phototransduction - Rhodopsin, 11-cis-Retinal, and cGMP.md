# Visual Phototransduction - Rhodopsin, 11-cis-Retinal, and cGMP

## Overview
Visual Phototransduction is the biochemical G-protein coupled cascade in retinal photoreceptors (rods and cones) converting incident photons into electrical hyperpolarizing neural signals.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
11-cis-Retinal Photoisomerization: Absorption of a photon by rhodopsin isomerizes chromophore **11-cis-retinal** into all-trans-retinal within picoseconds, triggering a conformational shift into active Metarhodopsin II.

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
The cGMP Phosphodiesterase Cascade: Metarhodopsin II activates the G-protein transducin; transducin stimulates phosphodiesterase (PDE6) to hydrolyze cyclic GMP (cGMP). Declining cGMP closes cyclic nucleotide-gated $Na^+/Ca^{2+}$ channels, **hyperpolarizing** the photoreceptor and reducing glutamate release.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Retinitis pigmentosa genetic mutations, night blindness vitamin A deficiency, optogenetic biological actuators.

---

## Key Terms
- **Rhodopsin**: Biological light-sensitive pigment protein found in the rod cells of the vertebrate retina.
- **Photoreceptor Hyperpolarization**: Unique biological response where light stimulation makes the cell membrane more negative.
- **Transducin**: Heterotrimeric G-protein coupling light-activated rhodopsin to downstream phosphodiesterase.
