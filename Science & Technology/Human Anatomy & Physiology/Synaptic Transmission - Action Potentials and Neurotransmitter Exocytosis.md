# Synaptic Transmission - Action Potentials and Neurotransmitter Exocytosis

## Overview
Synaptic transmission is the electrochemical process by which neurons communicate signals across microscopic synaptic clefts via neurotransmitters.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Action Potential Generation & The Hodgkin-Huxley Model: Depolarization exceeding threshold ($\sim -55\text{ mV}$) triggers voltage-gated $Na^+$ channels to rush in, spiking membrane potential to $+30\text{ mV}$. Voltage-gated $K^+$ channels then open, repolarizing and hyperpolarizing the cell, governed by the Nernst-Goldman electrochemical gradient.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Vesicle Fusion & Chemical Exocytosis: When the action potential invades the axon terminal, voltage-gated $Ca^{2+}$ channels open. Intracellular calcium binds to synaptotagmin, triggering SNARE protein complex zipper-zipping that fuses neurotransmitter vesicles with the presynaptic membrane, releasing neurotransmitters into the 20-nm synaptic cleft to bind postsynaptic receptors.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Target of psychotropic drugs (SSRIs, benzodiazepines), neurotoxin mechanisms (botulinum, tetanus blocking SNAREs).

## Key Terminology
- **Action Potential**: Rapid, temporary electrical voltage spike along a neuronal membrane triggered by ion channel openings.
- **Synaptic Cleft**: Microscopic 20-nanometer physical gap separating presynaptic and postsynaptic neuronal membranes.
- **SNARE Complex**: Group of cellular proteins that mediate vesicle docking and membrane fusion during neurotransmitter exocytosis.
