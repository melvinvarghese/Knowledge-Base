# Hypothalamic-Pituitary-Adrenal (HPA) Axis and Cortisol

## Overview
The Hypothalamic-Pituitary-Adrenal (HPA) Axis coordinates the mammalian neuroendocrine response to physiological and psychological stress, culminating in the secretion of systemic **Cortisol**.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
The Neuroendocrine Cascade: Hypothalamic paraventricular neurons release Corticotropin-Releasing Hormone (CRH); CRH stimulates anterior pituitary corticotrophs to secrete Adrenocorticotropic Hormone (ACTH); ACTH prompts the adrenal cortex to synthesize cortisol.

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Metabolic and Immunosuppressive Actions: Cortisol stimulates gluconeogenesis, enhances lipolysis, mobilizes amino acids, and suppresses immune inflammation by inhibiting NF-$\kappa$B transcription, while exerting negative feedback on the hypothalamus and pituitary.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Addison's disease adrenal insufficiency, Cushing's syndrome hypercortisolemia, systemic glucocorticoid pharmacotherapy (dexamethasone).

---

## Key Terms
- **HPA Axis**: Major neuroendocrine system controlling physiological reactions to stress and homeostatic processes.
- **Cortisol**: Primary glucocorticoid steroid hormone regulating glucose metabolism and immune response.
- **Negative Feedback**: Regulatory biological mechanism where the end product inhibits upstream initiating pathways.
