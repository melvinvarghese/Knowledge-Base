# Auditory Transduction - Cochlear Hair Cells and Tip Links

## Overview
Auditory Transduction transforms acoustic sound pressure waves into neural action potentials within the organ of Corti in the cochlea via mechanically-gated ion channels on stereocilia.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
Tonotopic Basilar Membrane Resonance: High-frequency sounds resonate the narrow, stiff base of the basilar membrane; low-frequency sounds resonate the wide, flexible apex, establishing a spatial tonotopic frequency map.

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Tip Link Mechanotransduction: Shearing displacement of stereocilia pulls delicate extracellular protein filaments (**tip links**, composed of cadherin-23 and protocadherin-15), mechanically springing open TMC1/TMC2 ion channels, allowing potassium ($K^+$) from endolymph to flood in and depolarize the hair cell.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Cochlear implant neuro-prosthetic design, sensorineural hearing loss diagnostics, aminoglycoside ototoxicity.

---

## Key Terms
- **Stereocilia**: Actin-rich microvillar projections on cochlear hair cells deflecting under acoustic vibrations.
- **Tip Link**: Nanoscale protein filament connecting adjacent stereocilia that mechanically gates ion channels.
- **Tonotopy**: Spatial structural organization of acoustic frequency detection along the cochlea.
