# Synaptic Plasticity - Long-Term Potentiation (LTP) and Memory Consolidation

## Overview
Synaptic Plasticity is the biological capacity of neuronal connections to strengthen or weaken over time in response to activity, forming the cellular substrate of learning and memory.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Hebb's Postulate & Long-Term Potentiation (LTP): Donald Hebb (1949) hypothesized: 'Neurons that fire together, wire together.' In the hippocampus, high-frequency stimulation triggers LTP—persistent strengthening of synapses mediated by glutamate NMDA and AMPA receptors.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Molecular Mechanism of NMDA Receptor Activation: At resting potential, NMDA receptor channels are physically plugged by a magnesium ion ($Mg^{2+}$). Strong postsynaptic depolarization expels the magnesium block, allowing $Ca^{2+}$ influx. Calcium activates CaMKII enzymes, inserting additional AMPA receptors into the postsynaptic density, increasing synaptic sensitivity.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Hippocampal episodic memory encoding, Alzheimer's disease synaptic degeneration, artificial neural network synaptic weight updates.

## Key Terminology
- **Long-Term Potentiation (LTP)**: Persistent strengthening of synapses based on recent patterns of activity, producing long-lasting increases in signal transmission.
- **NMDA Receptor**: Glutamate receptor with a voltage-dependent magnesium block acting as a molecular coincidence detector.
- **Hebb's Rule**: Neuroscientific learning rule stating that simultaneous activation of cells leads to increased synaptic connection strength.
