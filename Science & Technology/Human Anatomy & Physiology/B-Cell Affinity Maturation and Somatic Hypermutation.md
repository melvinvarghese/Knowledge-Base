# B-Cell Affinity Maturation and Somatic Hypermutation

## Overview
B-Cell Affinity Maturation is the evolutionary process occurring within germinal centers of secondary lymphoid organs whereby activated B lymphocytes iteratively mutate and select antibodies for higher target antigen affinity.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
Somatic Hypermutation (SHM) via AID: The enzyme Activation-Induced Cytidine Deaminase (AID) induces high-rate targeted point mutations in the immunoglobulin variable gene regions ($V_H, V_L$) at a million times the baseline genomic mutation rate.

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Follicular Selection & Class-Switch Recombination: B cell variants compete for limited antigen presented on follicular dendritic cells; high-affinity clones receive survival signals, while class-switch recombination swaps constant regions from IgM to IgG, IgA, or IgE.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Vaccine design inducing long-lived neutralising antibodies, therapeutic monoclonal antibody engineering, lymphoma pathogenesis.

---

## Key Terms
- **Affinity Maturation**: Process increasing average antibody binding affinity for an antigen during an immune response.
- **Somatic Hypermutation**: Targeted cellular mutation mechanism diversifying antibody variable binding regions.
- **Germinal Center**: Dynamic microarchitecture within lymph nodes where mature B cells proliferate and mutate.
