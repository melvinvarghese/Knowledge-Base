# Thermoregulation - Hypothalamic Set Point and Shivering Thermogenesis

## Overview
Human Core Body Temperature is tightly defended at approximately **37.0°C (98.6°F)** by thermal control centers in the preoptic anterior hypothalamus through autonomous physical effectors.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
Hypothalamic Thermal Set-Point Defense: Central and peripheral thermoreceptors signal hypothalamic integrators. When core temperature drops below set point, the hypothalamus triggers cuticular vasoconstriction, shivering skeletal muscle thermogenesis, and brown adipose tissue uncoupling (UCP1 thermogenin).

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Heat Dissipation & Sweating: When temperature rises, cutaneous vasodilation shunts blood to skin surfaces; cholinergic sympathetic stimulation activates eccrine sweat glands, shedding heat through the high latent heat of water evaporation.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Clinical management of malignant hyperthermia, targeted therapeutic hypothermia post-cardiac arrest, fever pyrogen mechanisms.

---

## Key Terms
- **Hypothalamic Set Point**: Target temperature threshold maintained by hypothalamic thermoregulatory centers.
- **Thermogenin (UCP1)**: Uncoupling protein in brown adipose tissue mitochondria producing heat by uncoupling ATP synthesis.
- **Eccrine Gland**: Major sweat gland producing dilute hypoosmotic fluid for evaporative cooling.
