# Vestibular Equilibrium - Semicircular Canals and Otolith Organs

## Overview
The Vestibular System of the inner ear detects rotational angular acceleration and linear gravitational acceleration, stabilizing visual gaze (vestibulo-ocular reflex) and upright posture.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
Semicircular Canals (Rotational Velocity): Three orthogonal fluid-filled canals detect rotation in roll, pitch, and yaw; fluid endolymph inertia deflects the gelatinous **cupula** inside the ampulla, shearing hair cell bundles.

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Otolith Organs (Linear & Gravitational Acceleration): The utricle and saccule contain dense calcium carbonate crystals (**otoconia**); linear acceleration or head tilting exerts gravitational shear across the macula, deflecting embedded hair cells.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Benign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo (BPPV, treated via the Epley maneuver), astronaut space motion sickness, Ménière's disease.

---

## Key Terms
- **Otoconia**: Dense calcium carbonate crystalline stones resting on the gelatinous otolithic membrane.
- **Cupula**: Gelatinous structure spanning the ampulla of semicircular canals deflected by fluid inertia.
- **Vestibulo-Ocular Reflex (VOR)**: Involuntary reflex stabilizing retinal images on the fovea during head motion.
