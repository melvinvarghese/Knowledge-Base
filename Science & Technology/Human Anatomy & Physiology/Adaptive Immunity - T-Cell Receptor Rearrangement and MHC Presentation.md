# Adaptive Immunity - T-Cell Receptor Rearrangement and MHC Presentation

## Overview
Adaptive Cell-Mediated Immunity provides targeted, antigen-specific defense and long-lasting immunological memory, governed by somatic **T-Cell Receptor (TCR)** gene rearrangement and MHC antigen presentation.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
V(D)J Genetic Recombination: During thymic development, RAG-1/RAG-2 recombinases randomly combine Variable ($V$), Diversity ($D$), and Joining ($J$) gene segments with junctional nucleotide diversity, generating $>10^{15}$ unique TCR specificities.

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
MHC Class I vs Class II Presentation: CD8+ cytotoxic T cells recognize intracellular endogenous peptide fragments presented on **MHC Class I** (present on all nucleated cells); CD4+ helper T cells recognize exogenous engulfed peptides on **MHC Class II** (dendritic cells, macrophages, B cells).

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Cancer CAR-T cell immunotherapy, organ transplant rejection cross-matching, autoimmune disease pathogenesis.

---

## Key Terms
- **T-Cell Receptor (TCR)**: Antigen-specific heterodimeric protein complex on T lymphocytes recognizing peptide-MHC complexes.
- **Major Histocompatibility Complex (MHC)**: Cell surface protein complex displaying peptide antigens for immune surveillance.
- **Thymic Selection**: Educational process eliminating self-reactive T cells (negative selection) to enforce self-tolerance.
