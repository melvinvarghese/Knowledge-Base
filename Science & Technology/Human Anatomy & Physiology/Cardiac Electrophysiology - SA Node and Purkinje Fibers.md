# Cardiac Electrophysiology - SA Node and Purkinje Fibers

## Overview
Cardiac Electrophysiology governs the rhythmic, coordinated contraction of the human heart through specialized pacemaker tissue (Sinoatrial Node) and high-speed conduction pathways.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
SA Node Pacemaker Prepotentials: Unstable resting membrane potentials driven by hyperpolarization-activated cyclic nucleotide-gated 'funny' channels ($I_f$), steadily leaking sodium and calcium into cells to reach automatic firing threshold (60–100 BPM).

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Ventricular Conduction Hierarchy: Electrical impulses propagate from the SA node through atria, delay at the Atrioventricular (AV) node to allow complete atrial emptying, then accelerate down the Bundle of His and Purkinje fibers at 4 m/s for synchronized ventricular systole.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Electrocardiogram (ECG) clinical rhythm interpretation, artificial electronic cardiac pacemakers, antiarrhythmic pharmacological classification (Vaughan-Williams).

---

## Key Terms
- **Sinoatrial (SA) Node**: The primary physiological pacemaker of the mammalian heart located in the right atrium.
- **Purkinje Fibers**: Specialized conductive myocardial fibers conducting electrical impulses rapidly to ventricles.
- **Funny Current ($I_f$)**: Inward mixed sodium-potassium ionic current driving spontaneous cardiac pacemaker depolarization.
