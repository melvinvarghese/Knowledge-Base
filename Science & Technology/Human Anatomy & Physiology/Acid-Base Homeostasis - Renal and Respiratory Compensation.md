# Acid-Base Homeostasis - Renal and Respiratory Compensation

## Overview
Human physiological arterial blood pH is maintained within the narrow homeostatic range of **7.35 to 7.45** through the synchronized action of chemical buffers, respiratory ventilation, and renal tubular compensation.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
Bicarbonate Buffer Equilibrium: $CO_2 + H_2O \rightleftharpoons H_2CO_3 \rightleftharpoons H^+ + HCO_3^-$. Pulmonary ventilation controls volatile $PCO_2$ within minutes; renal tubules excrete $H^+$ and reabsorb/generate $HCO_3^-$ over hours to days.

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
The Four Acid-Base Disturbances: 1. Respiratory Acidosis (hypoventilation, high $CO_2$); 2. Respiratory Alkalosis (hyperventilation); 3. Metabolic Acidosis (loss of $HCO_3^-$ or acid accumulation); 4. Metabolic Alkalosis.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Arterial Blood Gas (ABG) clinical interpretation in intensive care, diabetic ketoacidosis stabilization, mechanical ventilation tuning.

---

## Key Terms
- **Acid-Base Balance**: Homeostatic maintenance of extracellular fluid hydrogen ion concentration within survivable limits.
- **Anion Gap**: Diagnostic calculation identifying unmeasured organic acid anions in metabolic acidosis.
- **Respiratory Compensation**: Rapid alteration of alveolar ventilation rate to normalize blood pH during metabolic disturbances.
