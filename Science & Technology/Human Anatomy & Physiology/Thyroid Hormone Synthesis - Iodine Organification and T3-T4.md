# Thyroid Hormone Synthesis - Iodine Organification and T3-T4

## Overview
The Thyroid Gland synthesizes iodinated thyronines (**Thyroxine, T4** and **Triiodothyronine, T3**) within follicular lumens, regulating basal metabolic rate, oxygen consumption, and protein synthesis throughout the body.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
Iodine Trapping and Organification: Sodium-Iodide Symporters ($NIS$) actively concentrate dietary iodide ($I^-$); Thyroid Peroxidase (TPO) oxidizes iodide and iodinates tyrosine residues on thyroglobulin, forming monoiodotyrosine (MIT) and diiodotyrosine (DIT).

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Coupling and Peripheral Deiodination: TPO couples MIT and DIT into T4 (90%) and T3 (10%). Peripheral selenodeiodinase enzymes convert prohormone T4 into active T3, which binds nuclear thyroid hormone receptors to upregulate $Na^+/K^+$ ATPase expression.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Clinical management of hypothyroidism (levothyroxine substitution), hyperthyroidism, and dietary iodine supplementation preventing endemic goiters.

---

## Key Terms
- **Thyroid Peroxidase (TPO)**: Enzyme catalyzing iodide oxidation, thyroglobulin iodination, and thyronine coupling.
- **Thyroxine (T4)**: Major circulating prohormone secreted by the thyroid gland containing four iodine atoms.
- **Triiodothyronine (T3)**: Biologically active thyroid hormone binding nuclear receptors to stimulate metabolic rate.
