# Neurotransmitters - Dopamine, Serotonin, GABA, and Glutamate

## Overview
Neurotransmitters are endogenous biochemical messengers that transmit signals across chemical synapses, modulating neural circuit excitability, emotion, and behavior.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Excitatory vs Inhibitory Balance: Glutamate is the primary excitatory neurotransmitter of the central nervous system, binding AMPA/NMDA receptors to drive depolarization; excessive glutamate causes excitotoxicity. Gamma-Aminobutyric Acid (GABA) is the primary inhibitory neurotransmitter, opening $Cl^-$ channels to hyperpolarize neurons, targeted by sedatives (benzodiazepines, alcohol).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Monoamine Neuromodulators: Dopamine (mesolimbic and nigrostriatal pathways) governs reward prediction error, motivation, and motor planning (depleted in Parkinson's disease). Serotonin (5-HT from raphe nuclei) regulates mood, appetite, and sleep architecture, modulated by selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Addiction neurobiology (dopaminergic hijacking), major depressive disorder treatments, epilepsy antiepileptic drug targets.

## Key Terminology
- **Glutamate**: Principal excitatory neurotransmitter in the brain responsible for synaptic transmission and cognitive functions.
- **GABA**: Principal inhibitory neurotransmitter in the mammalian central nervous system, reducing neuronal excitability.
- **Dopamine**: Monoamine neurotransmitter playing a critical role in motor control, motivation, reward reinforcement, and executive function.
