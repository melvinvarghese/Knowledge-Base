# Synaptic Transmission - Neurotransmitters and Vesicle Exocytosis

## Overview
Synaptic Transmission is the biological process by which a presynaptic neuron communicates chemically with a postsynaptic target cell across a narrow synaptic cleft.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
SNARE Complex & Vesicle Exocytosis: Arriving action potentials open voltage-gated $Ca^{2+}$ channels; calcium influx triggers synaptotagmin to bind SNARE core complexes (syntaxin, SNAP-25, synaptobrevin), fusing synaptic vesicles with the presynaptic membrane in <1 ms.

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Postsynaptic Ionotropic vs Metabotropic Receptors: Fast ligand-gated ion channels (AMPA, GABA-A) generate excitatory/inhibitory postsynaptic potentials (EPSPs/IPSPs); G-protein coupled receptors (GPCRs) activate second-messenger cascades (cAMP, IP3).

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Psychiatric psychopharmacology (SSRIs, dopamine agonists), botulinum toxin paralysis (cleaving SNARE proteins), neurodegenerative diseases.

---

## Key Terms
- **SNARE Complex**: Protein complex mediating the fusion of synaptic vesicles with target presynaptic membranes.
- **Synaptic Cleft**: Microscopic extracellular gap (~20 nm) separating presynaptic and postsynaptic membranes.
- **Exocytosis**: Cellular process releasing vesicle contents into extracellular space via membrane fusion.
