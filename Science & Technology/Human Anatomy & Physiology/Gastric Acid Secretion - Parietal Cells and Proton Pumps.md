# Gastric Acid Secretion - Parietal Cells and Proton Pumps

## Overview
Gastric Acid (hydrochloric acid, HCl, pH 1.5–2.0) is secreted by gastric parietal cells in the stomach lining, essential for denaturing dietary proteins, activating pepsinogen, and sterilizing ingested food.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
The $H^+/K^+$ ATPase Proton Pump: Driven by ATP hydrolysis, the gastric proton pump actively transports $H^+$ into the gastric lumen against a million-fold concentration gradient in exchange for $K^+$, while $Cl^-$ exits through conductive apical channels.

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Neurohormonal Secretory Regulation: Parietal cells are stimulated by three synergistic pathways: Histamine ($H_2$ receptors), Acetylcholine ($M_3$ muscarinic receptors), and Gastrin (CCK-B receptors); inhibited by Somatostatin and Prostaglandin $E_2$.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Proton Pump Inhibitors (PPIs like omeprazole) treating peptic ulcers, $H_2$ receptor antagonists, Zollinger-Ellison syndrome.

---

## Key Terms
- **Parietal Cell**: Specialized epithelial stomach cell responsible for secreting hydrochloric acid and intrinsic factor.
- **$H^+/K^+$ ATPase**: Primary active transport proton pump acidifying the gastric lumen.
- **Intrinsic Factor**: Glycoprotein secreted by parietal cells essential for subsequent vitamin B12 absorption in the ileum.
