# Calcium Homeostasis - Parathyroid Hormone and Calcitonin

## Overview
Extracellular ionized calcium ($Ca^{2+}$) is tightly maintained between 1.1 and 1.3 mmol/L through the reciprocal endocrine actions of **Parathyroid Hormone (PTH)** and Calcitonin.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
PTH Calcium Elevation Cascades: Calcium-Sensing Receptors ($CaSR$) on parathyroid chief cells detect hypocalcemia, triggering rapid PTH release. PTH stimulates osteoclast bone resorption, promotes renal calcium reabsorption, and activates 1$\alpha$-hydroxylase to synthesize active Vitamin D ($1,25(OH)_2D$).

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Calcitonin Antagonism: Thyroid C-cells secrete calcitonin in response to hypercalcemia, acting on osteoclasts to temporarily inhibit bone resorption and promote urinary calcium excretion.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Clinical treatment of hyperparathyroidism, hypocalcemic tetany diagnostics (Chvostek's sign), osteoporosis bone mineral density preservation.

---

## Key Terms
- **Parathyroid Hormone (PTH)**: Polypeptide hormone increasing extracellular calcium concentrations.
- **Calcium-Sensing Receptor (CaSR)**: G-protein coupled receptor in parathyroid cells detecting extracellular calcium ions.
- **1,25-Dihydroxyvitamin D**: Active hormone calcitriol enhancing intestinal calcium absorption.
