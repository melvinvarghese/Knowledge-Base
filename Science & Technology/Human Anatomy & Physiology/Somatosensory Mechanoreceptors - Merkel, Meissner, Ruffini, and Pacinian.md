# Somatosensory Mechanoreceptors - Merkel, Meissner, Ruffini, and Pacinian

## Overview
Cutaneous Mechanoreceptors transduce mechanical touch, vibration, and skin stretch into neural signals, categorized by receptive field size and adaptation rate into four specialized tactile sensors.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
The Four Mechanoreceptor Classes: 1. **Meissner Corpuscles** (rapidly adapting, small field: low-frequency slip and flutter); 2. **Merkel Discs** (slowly adapting, small field: fine form, edges, and Braille reading); 3. **Pacinian Corpuscles** (rapidly adapting, large field: high-frequency 250 Hz vibration); 4. **Ruffini Endings** (slowly adapting, large field: skin stretch and joint angle).

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Piezo2 Ion Channel Activation: Mechanical compression physically opens transmembrane **Piezo2** mechanically-activated cation channels, causing rapid inward depolarizing ionic currents.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Haptic feedback tactile interface design, prosthetic sensory restoration, tactile neuropathy diagnostics.

---

## Key Terms
- **Mechanoreceptor**: Sensory receptor responding to physical mechanical pressure, touch, or distortion.
- **Piezo2**: Mechanically-activated ion channel protein essential for human touch and proprioception.
- **Receptive Field**: Specific geographic area of skin whose stimulation alters a neuron's firing rate.
