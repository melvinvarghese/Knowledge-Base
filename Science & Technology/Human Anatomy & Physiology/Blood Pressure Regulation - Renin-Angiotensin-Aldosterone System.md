# Blood Pressure Regulation - Renin-Angiotensin-Aldosterone System

## Overview
The Renin-Angiotensin-Aldosterone System (RAAS) is a hormonal cascade regulating systemic vascular resistance, fluid balance, and arterial blood pressure.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
The Enzymatic Hormone Cascade: Renal juxtaglomerular cells detect low perfusion pressure, releasing **Renin**; Renin cleaves liver-derived angiotensinogen into Angiotensin I; pulmonary Angiotensin-Converting Enzyme (ACE) converts it into **Angiotensin II**.

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Systemic Hemodynamic Actions: Angiotensin II acts as a potent systemic vasoconstrictor, stimulates thirst in the hypothalamus, and triggers the adrenal cortex to secrete **Aldosterone**, promoting renal $Na^+$ reabsorption and water retention.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
First-line pharmaceutical antihypertensive therapies: ACE inhibitors (lisinopril) and Angiotensin Receptor Blockers (ARBs like losartan).

---

## Key Terms
- **Renin**: Enzyme secreted by renal juxtaglomerular apparatus initiating the RAAS hormone cascade.
- **Angiotensin II**: Potent peptide hormone inducing systemic vasoconstriction and aldosterone secretion.
- **Aldosterone**: Steroid mineralocorticoid promoting sodium and water retention in renal collecting ducts.
