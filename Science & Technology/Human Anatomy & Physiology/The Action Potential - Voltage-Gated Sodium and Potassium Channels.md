# The Action Potential - Voltage-Gated Sodium and Potassium Channels

## Overview
The Action Potential is a rapid, all-or-none electrical impulse propagating along neuronal and muscle membranes, driven by sequential opening of voltage-gated ion channels.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
The Hodgkin-Huxley Model: Resting potential (-70 mV) maintained by $Na^+/K^+$ ATPase. Depolarization past threshold (-55 mV) opens voltage-gated $Na^+$ channels, causing rapid inward $Na^+$ influx to +30 mV. Inactivation of $Na^+$ channels and opening of delayed-rectifier $K^+$ channels repolarizes the membrane.

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Saltatory Conduction in Myelinated Axons: Myelin sheaths formed by oligodendrocytes and Schwann cells insulate internodes; action potentials hop between uninsulated Nodes of Ranvier, boosting conduction velocity up to 120 m/s.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Local anesthetic block mechanisms (lidocaine blocking $Na^+$ channels), neurotoxin research (tetrodotoxin), anti-epileptic pharmacology.

---

## Key Terms
- **Action Potential**: Transient reversal of membrane electrical potential propagating along excitable cells.
- **Refractory Period**: Time following an action potential during which an excitable cell cannot fire again.
- **Saltatory Conduction**: Rapid propagation of action potentials jumping between Nodes of Ranvier along myelinated axons.
