# Hemoglobin Oxygen Dissociation Curve and Bohr Effect

## Overview
Hemoglobin is a tetrameric allosteric metalloprotein in erythrocytes that binds up to four oxygen molecules, displaying a sigmoidal binding curve modulated by the **Bohr Effect**.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
Cooperativity & Sigmoidal Binding: Binding of the first $O_2$ shifts hemoglobin from low-affinity Tense (T) state to high-affinity Relaxed (R) state, producing positive cooperativity (Hill coefficient $n \approx 2.8$).

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
The Bohr Effect & Right Shifts: In metabolically active exercising tissues, elevated $CO_2$, lower pH (acidosis), higher temperature, and increased 2,3-BPG shift the curve to the right, promoting efficient offloading of oxygen to tissues.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Pulse oximetry calibration, carbon monoxide poisoning (locking hemoglobin in high-affinity R state), high-altitude athletic training.

---

## Key Terms
- **Bohr Effect**: Physiological phenomenon where elevated $CO_2$ and hydrogen ions decrease hemoglobin's oxygen affinity.
- **Allostery**: Regulation of a protein's activity through binding of an effector molecule at a distinct regulatory site.
- **2,3-Bisphosphoglycerate (2,3-BPG)**: Metabolic intermediate in red blood cells that stabilizes the low-affinity deoxygenated T-state.
