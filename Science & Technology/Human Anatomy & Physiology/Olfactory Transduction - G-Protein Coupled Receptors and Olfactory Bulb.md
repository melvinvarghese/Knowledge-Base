# Olfactory Transduction - G-Protein Coupled Receptors and Olfactory Bulb

## Overview
The Olfactory System detects volatile odorant molecules through hundreds of specialized G-protein coupled olfactory receptors (ORs), organized into a combinatoric spatial odor map in the olfactory bulb.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Mechanisms
cAMP Transduction Cascade: Volatile odorants bind GPCRs on olfactory cilia; activation of $G_{\text{olf}}$ stimulates adenylyl cyclase III to produce cyclic AMP (cAMP); cAMP opens cyclic nucleotide-gated channels, triggering $Na^+/Ca^{2+}$ influx and secondary calcium-activated chloride ($Cl^-$) efflux depolarization.

---

## 2. Structural & Pathological Analysis
Glomerular Combinatorial Coding: Each sensory neuron expresses a single olfactory receptor type; all axons expressing the identical receptor converge onto specific pairs of **glomeruli** in the olfactory bulb, creating unique combinatorial neural odor signatures.

---

## 3. Clinical Significance & Real-World Applications
Anosmia diagnostic testing, artificial electronic electronic-nose gas sensors, perfume chemistry.

---

## Key Terms
- **Olfactory Receptor (OR)**: G-protein coupled receptor protein expressed on olfactory sensory cilia detecting odorants.
- **Glomerulus**: Spherical neuropil structure in the olfactory bulb where primary olfactory sensory axons synapse.
- **Anosmia**: Complete loss of the sense of smell resulting from head trauma, viral infection, or neurodegeneration.
