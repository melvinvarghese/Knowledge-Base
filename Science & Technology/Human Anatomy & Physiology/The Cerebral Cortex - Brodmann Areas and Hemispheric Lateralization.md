# The Cerebral Cortex - Brodmann Areas and Hemispheric Lateralization

## Overview
The Cerebral Cortex is the folded outer layer of neural tissue in the brain responsible for higher-order sensory perception, conscious thought, and motor control.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Brodmann Areas & Cortical Cytoarchitecture: Korbinian Brodmann (1909) mapped 52 distinct histological regions based on cellular layer thickness: Area 4 (Primary Motor Cortex), Areas 1–3 (Primary Somatosensory Cortex), Area 17 (Primary Visual Cortex / V1 in the occipital lobe), and Areas 44–45 (Broca's speech area).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Hemispheric Specialization & Split-Brain Research: Roger Sperry and Michael Gazzaniga's studies on corpus callosotomy patients revealed lateralization: the left hemisphere typically specializes in sequential language syntax and analytical logic, while the right hemisphere excels in spatial processing, facial recognition, and prosody.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Neurosurgical tumor mapping, stroke rehabilitation, functional neuroimaging (fMRI) task localization.

## Key Terminology
- **Brodmann Areas**: Histologically defined cortical brain regions categorized according to cellular cytoarchitectural structure.
- **Corpus Callosum**: Thick band of nerve fibers connecting the left and right cerebral hemispheres, enabling interhemispheric communication.
- **Hemispheric Lateralization**: Tendency for some neural functions or cognitive processes to be specialized to one side of the brain.
