# Polymerization Mechanisms - Addition vs Condensation

## Overview
Polymerization is the chemical reaction uniting small repeating monomer units into giant macromolecular polymer chains, categorized into chain-growth (addition) and step-growth (condensation).

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
Chain-Growth (Addition) Mechanism: Requires initiator (free radical, cation, anion) attacking alkene double bonds; propagates rapidly to form high-MW polymer with no small-molecule byproduct (polyethylene, polypropylene).

---

## 2. Structural Mechanics & Scientific Analysis
Step-Growth (Condensation) Mechanism: Bifunctional monomers react step-by-step with the elimination of a small byproduct molecule ($H_2O, HCl$), such as polyamides (Nylon) and polyesters (Dacron).

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Technological Impact
Synthetic textiles, automotive plastic bumpers, lightweight aerospace composites, and biodegradable PLA bioplastics.

---

## Key Terms
- **Monomer**: Small molecule capable of combining chemically with others to form a polymer.
- **Addition Polymerization**: Polymer growth where monomers link without eliminating secondary molecules.
- **Condensation Polymerization**: Polymer growth accompanied by the loss of small byproduct molecules like water.
