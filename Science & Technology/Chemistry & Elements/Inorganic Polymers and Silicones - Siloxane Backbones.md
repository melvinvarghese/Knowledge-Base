# Inorganic Polymers and Silicones - Siloxane Backbones

## Overview
Inorganic polymers are macromolecules whose structural main-chain backbone contains no carbon atoms, predominated by polysiloxanes (silicones).

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Siloxane Backbone ($-Si-O-Si-$): Polysiloxanes (such as polydimethylsiloxane / PDMS) feature repeating alternating silicon and oxygen atoms. The $Si-O$ bond is exceptionally strong ($452\text{ kJ/mol}$, making it thermally stable and UV resistant) and possesses wide $Si-O-Si$ bond angles ($140\text{–}180^\circ$) with extremely low rotational barrier energy, rendering silicones hyper-flexible fluids or elastomeric rubbers even at $-100^\circ\text{C}$.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Hydrophobicity & Biocompatibility: Methyl side groups shield the polar siloxane backbone, conferring extreme surface hydrophobicity and chemical inertness. Silicones are non-toxic, gas-permeable, and physiologically inert.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Biomedical implants and catheters, high-temperature thermal gaskets, microfluidic lab-on-a-chip prototyping (PDMS soft lithography).

## Key Terminology
- **Siloxane**: Functional group in organosilicon chemistry with the $Si	ext{-}O	ext{-}Si$ linkage forming the backbone of silicones.
- **PDMS**: Polydimethylsiloxane; the most widely used silicon-based organic polymer elastomer.
- **Soft Lithography**: Family of micro-fabrication techniques that use elastomeric stamps (primarily PDMS) to print microscale patterns.
