# Graphene and Carbon Nanotubes - 2D Materials

## Overview
Graphene is a single atom-thick 2D honeycomb lattice of $sp^2$-hybridized carbon atoms; Carbon Nanotubes (CNTs) are rolled cylinders of graphene exhibiting phenomenal tensile strength and conductivity.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
Dirac Cones & Ballistic Electron Transport: Conduction and valence bands touch at discrete Dirac points; electrons behave as massless relativistic fermions with mobility exceeding $200,000\text{ cm}^2/(\text{V}\cdot\text{s})$.

---

## 2. Structural Mechanics & Scientific Analysis
Chirality & Nanotube Properties: Rolling vector indices $(n, m)$ determine whether a carbon nanotube is metallic or semiconducting; tensile strength exceeds structural steel by a factor of 100.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Technological Impact
Transparent conductive touch screens, lithium-ion battery conductive additives, aerospace carbon composites, and ultra-sensitive biosensors.

---

## Key Terms
- **Graphene**: Two-dimensional planar allotrope of carbon arranged in a hexagonal honeycomb lattice.
- **Carbon Nanotube (CNT)**: Cylindrical nanostructure composed of rolled single- or multi-layer graphene.
- **Ballistic Transport**: Unimpeded motion of charge carriers through a medium without scattering.
