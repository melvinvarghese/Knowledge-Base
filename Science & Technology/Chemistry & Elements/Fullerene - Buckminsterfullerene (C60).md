# Fullerene - Buckminsterfullerene (C60)

## Overview
Buckminsterfullerene ($C_{60}$) is a spherical carbon allotrope composed of 60 carbon atoms organized into 12 pentagonal and 20 hexagonal faces, resembling a soccer ball.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
Truncated Icosahedron Geometry: Isolated pentagon rule ensures pentagons do not share edges, maximizing resonance stabilization. Hybridization is intermediate between $sp^2$ and $sp^3$ due to curvature strain.

---

## 2. Structural Mechanics & Scientific Analysis
Superconducting Endohedral Fullerenes: Trapping metal atoms inside the hollow carbon cage ($La@C_{60}$) or doping with alkali metals ($K_3C_{60}$) induces high-temperature superconductivity up to 33 K.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Technological Impact
Organic photovoltaic solar cells as electron acceptors, targeted drug delivery cages, and antioxidant free-radical scavengers.

---

## Key Terms
- **Fullerene**: Molecular allotrope of carbon consisting of hollow spherical, ellipsoid, or tubular cages.
- **Isolated Pentagon Rule**: Geometric stability rule requiring pentagonal rings in fullerenes to be separated by hexagons.
- **Endohedral Complex**: Fullerene containing atomic or small molecular species trapped within its internal cavity.
