# Mass Spectrometry and Molecular Weight Determination

## Overview
Mass Spectrometry (MS) is an analytical technique that ionizes chemical compounds, separates resulting ions based on mass-to-charge ratios ($m/z$), and measures molecular weights at sub-atomic precision.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
Ionization and Lorentz Deflection: Analytes are ionized (ESI, MALDI, EI) and accelerated through magnetic or electric fields; flight trajectory curves according to mass-to-charge ratio ($m/z$):

$$r = \frac{mv}{qB} = \frac{1}{B}\sqrt{\frac{2Vm}{q}}$$

---

## 2. Structural Mechanics & Scientific Analysis
Mass Analyzers & Fragmentation Spectra: Quadrupole, Time-of-Flight (TOF), and Orbitrap mass analyzers achieve resolution separating isotope peaks and identifying structural fragmentation patterns.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Technological Impact
Proteomics protein sequencing, Olympic anti-doping drug detection, and atmospheric gas analysis on Mars rover exploration missions.

---

## Key Terms
- **Mass-to-Charge Ratio ($m/z$)**: The dimensionless quotient of an ion's mass in atomic units divided by its elementary charge.
- **Electrospray Ionization (ESI)**: Soft ionization technique producing intact gas-phase ions from large biological polymers.
- **Base Peak**: The most intense peak in a mass spectrum representing the most stable fragment ion.
