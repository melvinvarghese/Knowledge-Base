# Hydrogen Bonding and Anomalous Properties of Water

## Overview
Hydrogen Bonding is an electrostatic dipole attraction between a hydrogen atom covalently bound to a highly electronegative atom (N, O, F) and a neighboring electronegative lone pair.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
The Water Tetrahedron: Each $H_2O$ molecule forms up to four hydrogen bonds in a tetrahedral arrangement, creating high cohesion, surface tension, and a boiling point 150°C higher than comparable hydrides ($H_2S$).

---

## 2. Structural Mechanics & Scientific Analysis
Density Anomaly of Ice: Upon freezing, open hexagonal crystalline hydrogen-bonded cages expand the lattice volume by 9%, making solid ice less dense than liquid water at 4°C, insulating aquatic biospheres.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Technological Impact
DNA double-helix base pairing stability, protein secondary structure folding ($lpha$-helices), and planetary climate regulation.

## Key Terms
- **Hydrogen Bond**: Strong intermolecular dipole attraction involving hydrogen bonded to N, O, or F.
- **Surface Tension**: Cohesive energy required to increase the surface area of a liquid.
- **Enthalpy of Vaporization**: Energy required to transform a quantity of liquid substance into a gas.
