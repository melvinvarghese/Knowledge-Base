# Intermolecular Forces - London Dispersion and Dipole-Dipole

## Overview
Intermolecular Forces (Van der Waals forces) are the non-covalent attractive and repulsive electrodynamic forces acting between molecules, dictating condensed phase states and boiling points.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
London Dispersion Forces (LDF): Arise from instantaneous fluctuating multipole moments in electron clouds inducing complementary dipoles in neighboring atoms; potential energy scales as $V \propto -1/r^6$.

---

## 2. Structural Mechanics & Scientific Analysis
Dipole-Dipole & Keesom Forces: Electrostatic alignment between permanent molecular dipole moments ($H-Cl \cdots H-Cl$), stronger than dispersion but weaker than hydrogen bonds.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Technological Impact
Liquefaction of noble gases and non-polar hydrocarbons, gecko toe pad dry adhesion, and chromatography retention mechanisms.

---

## Key Terms
- **London Dispersion Force**: Weakest intermolecular attraction resulting from transient quantum electron fluctuations.
- **Polarizability**: Ease with which an external electric field distorts an electron cloud.
- **Van der Waals Radius**: Effective radius of an atom when interacting via non-bonded intermolecular contact.
