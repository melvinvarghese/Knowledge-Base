# Electrolysis and Faraday's Laws

## Overview
Electrolysis uses external direct electrical current to drive non-spontaneous chemical reactions, essential for industrial metal extraction and chemical synthesis.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
Faraday's Laws of Electrolysis: Mass ($m$) of substance liberated at an electrode is proportional to charge transferred ($Q = It$):

$$m = \frac{Q M}{z F} = \frac{I t M}{z F}$$

Where $M$ is molar mass, $z$ is valence number, and $F = 96,485\text{ C/mol}$ is Faraday's constant.

---

## 2. Structural Mechanics & Scientific Analysis
Overpotential and Electrode Kinetics: Non-ideal thermodynamic resistance requires applying voltage higher than theoretical $E^\circ$ to overcome activation barriers of gas evolution ($O_2, H_2$).

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Technological Impact
Hall-Héroult electrolytic aluminum smelting, chlor-alkali production of chlorine and NaOH, and green hydrogen water electrolysis.

---

## Key Terms
- **Electrolysis**: Chemical decomposition produced by passing an electric current through a liquid or solution.
- **Faraday's Constant ($F$)**: Total electric charge carried by one mole of electrons (~96,485 C).
- **Overpotential**: Voltage required to drive an electrochemical reaction beyond its thermodynamic potential.
