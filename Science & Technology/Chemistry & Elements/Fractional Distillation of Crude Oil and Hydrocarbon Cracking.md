# Fractional Distillation of Crude Oil and Hydrocarbon Cracking

## Overview
Fractional Distillation and Cracking are core petroleum refining operations separating raw crude oil into hydrocarbon fractions based on boiling points and thermally cracking heavy residues into light fuels.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
Fractionating Column Thermal Gradient: Crude oil vaporizes at 350°C; as vapors rise through bubble-cap trays, fractions condense at distinct boiling ranges (refinery gas <30°C, gasoline 40–205°C, diesel 250–350°C).

---

## 2. Structural Mechanics & Scientific Analysis
Fluidized Catalytic Cracking (FCC): Long-chain heavy gas oils pass through hot zeolite catalysts at 500°C, breaking $C-C$ bonds via carbocation mechanisms to yield high-octane gasoline and alkenes.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Technological Impact
Aviation jet fuel, automotive gasoline refining, petrochemical feedstocks for plastics, and bitumen asphalt for roadways.

---

## Key Terms
- **Fractional Distillation**: Separation of a mixture into component fractions based on differing boiling points.
- **Catalytic Cracking**: Breakdown of heavy long-chain hydrocarbons into lighter molecules over catalysts.
- **Zeolite**: Microporous aluminosilicate mineral catalyst used in petroleum refining.
