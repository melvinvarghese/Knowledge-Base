# Supramolecular Chemistry - Host-Guest Systems and Molecular Machines

## Overview
Supramolecular Chemistry, dubbed 'chemistry beyond the molecule' by Jean-Marie Lehn (Nobel Prize 1987), studies complex multi-molecular assemblies held together by non-covalent interactions.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Host-Guest Chemistry & Crown Ethers: Crown ethers (Charles Pedersen) and cryptands feature macrocyclic cavities lined with Lewis basic oxygen atoms that selectively bind specific alkali metal cations based on size-matching host-guest complementarity (e.g., 18-crown-6 selectively binding $K^+$). Cyclodextrins encapsule hydrophobic guest molecules inside their toroid interior.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Synthetic Molecular Machines: Jean-Pierre Sauvage, J. Fraser Stoddart, and Ben Feringa (Nobel Prize 2016) synthesized mechanically interlocked molecules (rotaxanes and catenanes) containing non-covalently linked components that undergo controlled mechanical motion. Feringa synthesized the first synthetic light-driven rotary molecular motor rotating unidirectionally $360^\circ$.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Targeted smart drug delivery nanocapsules, self-healing polymers, molecular electronic switches.

## Key Terminology
- **Host-Guest Chemistry**: Branch of supramolecular chemistry in which a host molecule forms a non-covalent complex with a guest molecule.
- **Rotaxane**: Mechanically interlocked molecular architecture consisting of a dumbbell-shaped molecule threaded through a macrocyclic ring.
- **Catenane**: Mechanically interlocked molecular structure consisting of two or more interlinked macrocyclic rings like chains.
