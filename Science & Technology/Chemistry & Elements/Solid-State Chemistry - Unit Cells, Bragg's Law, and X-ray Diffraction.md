# Solid-State Chemistry - Unit Cells, Bragg's Law, and X-ray Diffraction

## Overview
Solid-State Chemistry explores the structural synthesis, crystallographic symmetry, and electronic properties of solid crystalline materials.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Crystal Lattices & The 14 Bravais Lattices: Crystalline solids feature long-range periodic spatial order constructed by tiling a fundamental geometric volume—the **unit cell**. Auguste Bravais demonstrated that all 3D periodic crystals belong to one of 14 distinct Bravais lattices categorized into seven crystal systems (cubic, tetragonal, orthorhombic, etc.). Common metal packings include Face-Centered Cubic (FCC), Body-Centered Cubic (BCC), and Hexagonal Close-Packed (HCP).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Bragg's Law & X-ray Crystallography: William Henry and William Lawrence Bragg (Nobel Prize 1915) proved that crystalline planes with interplanar spacing $d$ constructively scatter X-rays of wavelength $\lambda$ at incident angle $\theta$ according to Bragg's Law: $n \lambda = 2 d \sin \theta$. Measuring diffraction spot angles and intensities allows reconstruction of electron density maps and atomic coordinates.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Discovery of the DNA double-helix structure (Rosalind Franklin, Watson & Crick), semiconductor wafer defect inspection, lithium battery cathode characterization.

## Key Terminology
- **Bragg's Law**: Condition for constructive interference of waves scattered by crystalline atomic lattice planes: $n\lambda = 2d\sin	heta$.
- **Unit Cell**: Smallest repeating structural unit of a crystal lattice that retains the overall geometric symmetry of the crystal.
- **Bravais Lattice**: Infinite array of discrete points in space generated by a set of discrete translation operations.
