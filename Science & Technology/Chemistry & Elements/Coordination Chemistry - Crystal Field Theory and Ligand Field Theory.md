# Coordination Chemistry - Crystal Field Theory and Ligand Field Theory

## Overview
Coordination Chemistry investigates transition metal complexes where central metal cations are surrounded by coordinate covalent ligands.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Crystal Field Theory (CFT) & $d$-Orbital Splitting: Models ligands as negative point charges electrostatically perturbing the degenerate $d$-orbitals of the transition metal. In an octahedral complex, electrostatic repulsion splits the five $d$-orbitals into higher-energy $e_g$ ($d_{z^2}, d_{x^2-y^2}$) and lower-energy $t_{2g}$ ($d_{xy}, d_{xz}, d_{yz}$) orbitals separated by crystal field splitting energy $\Delta_o$.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Spectrochemical Series & Colors of Coordination Complexes: Strong-field ligands ($CN^-, CO$) produce large $\Delta_o$ (low-spin diamagnetic complexes); weak-field ligands ($I^-, Br^-, F^-$) produce small $\Delta_o$ (high-spin paramagnetic complexes). Electron transitions between split $d$-orbitals absorb visible light, giving transition metal solutions brilliant vibrant colors.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Biological metalloenzymes (iron in hemoglobin heme, cobalt in vitamin B12), industrial homogeneous catalysts (Wilkinson's catalyst).

## Key Terminology
- **Crystal Field Splitting**: Energy difference between higher and lower $d$-orbital subsets resulting from ligand electrostatic fields ($\Delta_o$).
- **Ligand**: Ion or molecule that donates a pair of electrons to a central metal atom or ion to form a coordination complex.
- **Spectrochemical Series**: Empirically determined list of ligands ordered by increasing crystal field splitting energy.
