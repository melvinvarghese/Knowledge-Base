# Electrochemistry - The Nernst Equation and Galvanic Fuel Cells

## Overview
Electrochemistry studies the relationship between chemical reactions and electrical potential, governing batteries, corrosion, and hydrogen fuel cells.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Nernst Equation: Relates cell electrical reduction potential ($E$) to reaction quotient ($Q$) at temperature $T$: $E = E^\circ - \frac{RT}{nF} \ln Q = E^\circ - \frac{0.0592}{n} \log_{10} Q$ (at $25^\circ\text{C}$), linking Gibbs free energy ($\Delta G^\circ = -n F E^\circ$) to electrochemical driving force.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Hydrogen Proton-Exchange Membrane (PEM) Fuel Cells: Continuously converts chemical fuel directly into electricity without combustion. At the platinum anode, hydrogen gas is oxidized: $2 H_2 \to 4 H^+ + 4 e^-$. Protons pass through the acidic polymer membrane (Nafion); electrons travel through an external circuit, recombining at the cathode with oxygen: $O_2 + 4 H^+ + 4 e^- \to 2 H_2O$, producing pure water as the sole byproduct.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Lithium-ion battery charging cycles, grid energy storage, industrial electrolysis of aluminum (Hall-Héroult process).

## Key Terminology
- **Nernst Equation**: Equation relating the reduction potential of an electrochemical reaction to standard electrode potential, temperature, and activities.
- **Galvanic Cell**: Electrochemical cell that derives electrical energy from spontaneous redox reactions taking place within the cell.
