# Functional Groups in Organic Chemistry - Alcohols, Aldehydes, Ketones

## Overview
Functional Groups are specific groupings of atoms within molecules that dictate characteristic chemical reactivity regardless of the remainder of the carbon skeleton.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
Carbonyl Chemistry ($C=O$): Polar carbonyl carbon is electrophilic, readily attacked by nucleophiles. Aldehydes ($R-CHO$) are easily oxidized to carboxylic acids; ketones ($R-CO-R'$) resist oxidation.

---

## 2. Structural Mechanics & Scientific Analysis
Alcohol Reactivity: Hydroxyl groups ($-OH$) engage in hydrogen bonding, undergo nucleophilic substitution to alkyl halides, and dehydrate to form alkenes over acid catalysts.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Technological Impact
Industrial solvent production (acetone, ethanol), synthesis of synthetic perfumes and flavorings (vanillin, cinnamaldehyde), and biological carbohydrate metabolism.

---

## Key Terms
- **Functional Group**: Specific atom arrangement conferring predictable chemical behavior to organic molecules.
- **Carbonyl Group**: Divalent functional group consisting of a carbon atom double-bonded to oxygen ($C=O$).
- **Nucleophilic Addition**: Reaction where a nucleophile attacks an electrophilic carbonyl carbon.
