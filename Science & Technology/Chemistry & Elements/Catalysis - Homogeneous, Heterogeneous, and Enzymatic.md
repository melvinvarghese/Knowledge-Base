# Catalysis - Homogeneous, Heterogeneous, and Enzymatic

## Overview
Catalysis is the acceleration of a chemical reaction by a substance that is not consumed in the reaction, acting by providing alternative reaction pathways with lower activation energy.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
Thermodynamics vs Kinetics: Catalysts lower $E_a$, accelerating forward and reverse rates identically; they do not alter chemical equilibrium constants ($K_{\text{eq}}$) or Gibbs free energy ($\Delta G$).

---

## 2. Structural Mechanics & Scientific Analysis
The Three Catalytic Classes: Heterogeneous (solid metal surface, liquid reactants), Homogeneous (single phase, soluble organometallics), and Biological Enzymes (ultra-specific protein active sites with induced-fit binding).

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Technological Impact
Automotive catalytic converters (Pt/Pd/Rh reduction of NOx and CO), polymer synthesis (Ziegler-Natta), and biological metabolic pathways.

---

## Key Terms
- **Catalyst**: A substance increasing chemical reaction rates without being permanently consumed.
- **Active Site**: Specific molecular pocket of an enzyme or catalytic surface where substrate binding occurs.
- **Turnover Frequency (TOF)**: Number of catalytic reaction cycles executed per active site per unit time.
