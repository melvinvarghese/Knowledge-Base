# Photochemistry - Jablonski Diagrams, Fluorescence, and Phosphorescence

## Overview
Photochemistry investigates chemical reactions, luminescence, and non-radiative electronic state transitions induced by the absorption of ultraviolet or visible photons.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Jablonski Diagram & Electronic Relaxation: Upon photon absorption ($h\nu$), an electron transitions from ground singlet state $S_0$ to excited singlet states ($S_1, S_2$). Rapid non-radiative **Internal Conversion (IC)** and vibrational relaxation dissipate excess vibrational energy ($10^{-12}\text{ s}$) to the lowest vibrational level of $S_1$ (Kasha's Rule).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Fluorescence vs Phosphorescence: 1. Fluorescence: Spin-allowed radiative transition from $S_1 \to S_0$ with zero spin inversion ($\Delta S = 0$), emitting photons rapidly ($10^{-9}\text{–}10^{-7}\text{ s}$); 2. Phosphorescence: Non-radiative **Intersystem Crossing (ISC)** flips electron spin to populate a metastable triplet state ($T_1$). Radiative transition from $T_1 \to S_0$ is quantum-mechanically forbidden ($\Delta S \neq 0$), yielding long-lived afterglow luminescence persisting from milliseconds to hours.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Fluorescence microscopy in cell biology (GFP tagging), photodynamic cancer therapy, organic solar cells.

## Key Terminology
- **Jablonski Diagram**: Energy diagram illustrating the electronic states of a molecule and transitions between them.
- **Intersystem Crossing (ISC)**: Isoenergetic radiationless transition between electronic states of different spin multiplicity (e.g., singlet to triplet).
- **Fluorescence**: Emission of light by a substance that has absorbed light or other electromagnetic radiation, occurring almost instantaneously.
