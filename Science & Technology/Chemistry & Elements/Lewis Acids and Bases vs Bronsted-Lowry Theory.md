# Lewis Acids and Bases vs Bronsted-Lowry Theory

## Overview
Acid-Base Chemistry classifies chemical interactions based on proton exchange (Brønsted-Lowry) or electron-pair coordination (Lewis theory), unifying broad organic and inorganic reactions.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
Brønsted-Lowry (Proton Transfer) vs Lewis (Electron Pair): Brønsted acid is a proton donor ($HA \rightleftharpoons H^+ + A^-$); Lewis acid is an electron-pair acceptor ($AlCl_3, BF_3$), forming coordinate dative covalent adducts.

---

## 2. Structural Mechanics & Scientific Analysis
Hard-Soft Acid-Base (HSAB) Principle: Hard acids (small, high charge density) preferentially bind hard bases; soft polarizable acids coordinate soft polarizable ligands ($Pt^{2+}$ with phosphines).

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Technological Impact
Industrial Friedel-Crafts alkylation catalysts ($AlCl_3$), biological enzymatic zinc metalloenzymes, and pharmaceutical synthesis.

---

## Key Terms
- **Lewis Acid**: Chemical species capable of accepting an electron pair into an empty orbital.
- **Brønsted-Lowry Base**: Species capable of accepting a proton ($H^+$).
- **Coordinate Covalent Bond**: Covalent bond where both shared electrons originate from a single donor atom.
