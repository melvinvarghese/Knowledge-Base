# Infrared Spectroscopy and Molecular Vibrational Modes

## Overview
Infrared (IR) Spectroscopy identifies covalent functional groups in molecules by detecting the resonant absorption of infrared light by quantized molecular vibrations.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Harmonic Oscillator Model & Selection Rules: Covalent bonds vibrate as quantum mechanical harmonic oscillators with quantized energy levels: $E_v = (v + \frac{1}{2}) \hbar \omega$. The vibrational frequency depends on bond force constant $k$ and reduced mass $\mu$: $\nu = \frac{1}{2\pi c} \sqrt{\frac{k}{\mu}}$. A vibrational mode absorbs infrared radiation if and only if the vibration produces a net change in the molecule's electric dipole moment ($\frac{d\mu}{dq} \neq 0$).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Diagnostic Infrared Bands & Fourier Transform IR (FTIR): Characteristic diagnostic absorption peaks identify functional groups: Carbonyl ($C=O$ stretch at $\sim 1700\text{ cm}^{-1}$), Hydroxyl ($O-H$ broad stretch at $\sim 3300\text{ cm}^{-1}$), and Alkyne ($C\equiv C$ at $\sim 2150\text{ cm}^{-1}$). The complex 'fingerprint region' ($<1500\text{ cm}^{-1}$) confirms exact molecular identity.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Quality control in industrial polymer manufacturing, atmospheric greenhouse gas sensing ($CO_2, CH_4$ dipole vibrations), forensic paint analysis.

## Key Terminology
- **FTIR**: Fourier Transform Infrared spectroscopy; technique collecting high-resolution spectral data over a wide spectral range using an interferometer.
- **Dipole Moment**: Measurement of the separation of two opposite electrical charges across a chemical bond.
- **Fingerprint Region**: Spectral region below $1500	ext{ cm}^{-1}$ containing complex overlapping absorption bands unique to each chemical compound.
