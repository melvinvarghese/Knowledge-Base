# The Periodic Table - Trends in Electronegativity and Atomic Radius

## Overview
The Periodic Table organizes chemical elements by increasing atomic number ($Z$) and electron configurations, exhibiting recurring periodic trends in atomic radius, ionization energy, and electronegativity.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
Effective Nuclear Charge and Shielding: $Z_{\text{eff}} = Z - S$. As nuclear charge rises across a period without adding new outer electron shells, valence electrons are pulled closer, shrinking atomic radii and escalating electronegativity.

---

## 2. Structural Mechanics & Scientific Analysis
Periodic Periodic Trends: Atomic radii decrease left-to-right across periods and expand down groups. Electronegativity peaks at Fluorine (3.98 Pauling) and drops toward Francium (0.7 Pauling).

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Technological Impact
Predicting chemical reactivity, materials synthesis, semiconductor bandgap tuning, and catalysis design.

---

## Key Terms
- **Electronegativity**: Relative measure of an atom's ability to attract shared electron pairs in a bond.
- **Effective Nuclear Charge**: Net positive charge experienced by valence electrons accounting for shielding.
- **Ionization Energy**: Energy required to remove the most loosely bound electron from a gaseous atom.
