# Chemical Bonding - Ionic, Covalent, and Metallic Bonds

## Overview
Chemical Bonding represents the thermodynamic interactions binding atoms into stable polyatomic molecules and crystalline lattices, minimizing system free energy.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
The Bond Spectrum and Octet Rule: Large electronegativity differences ($\Delta\chi > 1.7$) produce ionic transfer; intermediate differences yield polar covalent sharing; zero difference yields nonpolar covalent sharing; delocalized electron seas define metallic bonding.

---

## 2. Structural Mechanics & Scientific Analysis
Lattice Energy vs Bond Dissociation: Ionic crystals are stabilized by electrostatic lattice enthalpy (Born-Haber cycle); covalent molecules depend on localized molecular orbital overlap ($\sigma$ and $\pi$ bonds).

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Technological Impact
Structural materials selection, electrical conductor vs insulator design, and pharmaceutical molecular docking.

---

## Key Terms
- **Ionic Bond**: Electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged cation-anion pairs.
- **Covalent Bond**: Bond formed by the mutual quantum sharing of valence electron pairs.
- **Metallic Bond**: Electrostatic attraction between a lattice of positive metal ions and delocalized conduction electrons.
