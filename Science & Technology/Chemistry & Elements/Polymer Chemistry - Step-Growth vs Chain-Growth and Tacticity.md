# Polymer Chemistry - Step-Growth vs Chain-Growth and Tacticity

## Overview
Polymer Chemistry synthesizes and characterizes high-molecular-weight macromolecules constructed from repeating covalent monomer units.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Chain-Growth vs Step-Growth Polymerization: Chain-growth (addition) polymerization requires an active initiator (free radicals, cations, anions) to open double bonds in monomers (e.g., ethylene to polyethylene) in rapid propagation without byproducts. Step-growth (condensation) polymerization occurs between bi-functional monomers (diacids and diamines to nylon), releasing small condensate molecules (such as $H_2O$ or $HCl$).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Stereochemistry & Tacticity: Karl Ziegler and Giulio Natta (1953) developed titanium/aluminum coordination catalysts (Ziegler-Natta catalysts) that control the spatial arrangement of pendant groups (**tacticity**): Isotactic (all groups on the same side), Syndiotactic (alternating sides), and Atactic (random orientation). Isotactic polymers pack tightly into crystalline, high-strength plastics.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Manufacturing synthetic fibers (Kevlar, Dacron), biomedical biodegradable sutures (polylactic acid), conductive conjugated polymers.

## Key Terminology
- **Ziegler-Natta Catalyst**: Catalyst composed of titanium halides and organoaluminum compounds enabling stereospecific alkene polymerization.
- **Tacticity**: Relative stereochemical arrangement of adjacent chiral centers within a polymer macromolecule.
- **Glass Transition Temperature ($T_g$)**: Temperature range over which an amorphous polymer transitions from a brittle glassy state into a rubbery flexible state.
