# Alkane, Alkene, and Alkyne Chemistry

## Overview
Alkanes, Alkenes, and Alkynes form the core aliphatic hydrocarbon classes, distinguished by carbon-carbon single ($sp^3$), double ($sp^2$), and triple ($sp$) covalent bonds.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
Hybridization and Bond Geometries: Alkanes ($C_n H_{2n+2}$, tetrahedral 109.5°), Alkenes ($C_n H_{2n}$, planar trigonal 120° with reactive $\pi$-bond), Alkynes ($C_n H_{2n-2}$, linear 180° with two orthogonal $\pi$-bonds).

---

## 2. Structural Mechanics & Scientific Analysis
Electrophilic Addition vs Radical Substitution: Alkanes undergo free-radical halogenation; alkenes readily undergo Markovnikov electrophilic addition across electron-rich $\pi$-bonds.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Technological Impact
Ethylene monomer production for polyethylene plastics, oxy-acetylene welding torches (3300°C flame), and motor fuel combustion.

---

## Key Terms
- **Alkene**: Unsaturated hydrocarbon containing at least one carbon-carbon double bond.
- **Markovnikov's Rule**: In addition to an asymmetrical alkene, hydrogen adds to the carbon with more hydrogens.
- **Pi ($\pi$) Bond**: Covalent bond formed by the lateral parallel overlap of adjacent p-orbitals.
