# Chirality and Stereochemistry - Enantiomers, Diastereomers, and Optical Activity

## Overview
Stereochemistry explores the three-dimensional spatial orientation of atoms within molecules, governed by geometric chirality.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Chiral Centers & Enantiomers: A chiral center is typically an $sp^3$-hybridized carbon atom bonded to four chemically distinct substituents. Enantiomers are non-superimposable mirror-image stereoisomers that share identical physical properties (boiling points, melting points, NMR spectra) but rotate plane-polarized light in equal and opposite directions (optical activity: dextrorotatory $[+]$ and levorotatory $[-]$).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Biological Asymmetry & The Thalidomide Tragedy: Biological enzymes and receptors are inherently chiral. While one enantiomer may produce a therapeutic drug effect, the mirror-image enantiomer can be inactive or toxic. The administration of racemic thalidomide in the 1950s led to catastrophic birth defects because the $(S)$-enantiomer is teratogenic, necessitating modern enantioselective asymmetric synthesis.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Asymmetric catalytic hydrogenation (Noyori/Knowles), chiral HPLC separation chromatography, drug discovery.

## Key Terminology
- **Chirality**: Geometric property of a molecule that makes it non-superimposable on its mirror image.
- **Enantiomer**: One of two stereoisomers that are mirror images of each other that are non-superimposable.
- **Racemic Mixture**: Equimolar mixture of left- and right-handed enantiomers that displays zero net optical rotation.
