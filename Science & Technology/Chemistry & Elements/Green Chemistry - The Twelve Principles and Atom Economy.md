# Green Chemistry - The Twelve Principles and Atom Economy

## Overview
Green Chemistry, codified by Paul Anastas and John Warner (1998), redesigns chemical processes and products to eliminate the generation and use of hazardous substances.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Atom Economy vs Percentage Yield: Traditional chemical efficiency measured percentage yield, ignoring byproduct waste. Barry Trost formulated **Atom Economy**: $\text{Atom Economy} = \frac{\text{Molecular Weight of Desired Product}}{\text{Total Molecular Weight of All Reactants}} \times 100\%$. Addition reactions achieve 100% atom economy, whereas substitution and elimination reactions generate stoichiometric waste.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Twelve Principles of Green Chemistry: Key mandates include: 1. Waste Prevention (better to prevent waste than treat it); 2. Safer Solvents (replacing chlorinated solvents with supercritical $CO_2$ or water); 3. Catalysis (preferring catalytic reagents over stoichiometric reagents); 4. Renewable Feedstocks (using agricultural biomass instead of petroleum); 5. Design for Degradation.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Sustainable pharmaceutical manufacturing, bio-plastics (polylactic acid from corn), industrial catalytic efficiency.

## Key Terminology
- **Green Chemistry**: Design of chemical products and processes that reduce or eliminate the generation of hazardous substances.
- **Atom Economy**: Metric measuring the conversion efficiency of a chemical process in terms of all atoms involved and desired products produced.
- **Supercritical $CO_2$**: Fluid state of carbon dioxide held at or above its critical temperature and pressure, serving as a non-toxic green industrial solvent.
