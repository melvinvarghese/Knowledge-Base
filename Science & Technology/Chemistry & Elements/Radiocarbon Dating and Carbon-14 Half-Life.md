# Radiocarbon Dating and Carbon-14 Half-Life

## Overview
Radiocarbon Dating is a radiometric method invented by Willard Libby (1949) that measures residual carbon-14 ($^{14}C$) decay to determine the age of carbon-bearing organic archaeological artifacts up to ~50,000 years.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
Atmospheric Production and Radioactive Decay: Cosmic ray neutrons strike nitrogen to form carbon-14: ${}^{14}\text{N} + n \rightarrow {}^{14}\text{C} + p$. Decay follows first-order kinetics with half-life $t_{1/2} = 5,730\text{ years}$:

$$N(t) = N_0 e^{-\lambda t} \implies t = -\frac{\ln(N/N_0)}{\lambda}$$

---

## 2. Structural Mechanics & Scientific Analysis
Biological Equilibrium & Death: Living organisms maintain equilibrium ratio ($^{14}C / ^{12}C \approx 10^{-12}$) via metabolic carbon exchange; upon biological death, uptake ceases and $^{14}C$ decays via beta emission.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Technological Impact
Archaeological dating of ancient human settlements, authentication of ancient parchment manuscripts (Dead Sea Scrolls), and paleoclimatic ice core dating.

---

## Key Terms
- **Carbon-14**: Radioactive isotope of carbon with 6 protons and 8 neutrons.
- **Half-Life ($t_{1/2}$)**: Time required for half the radioactive nuclei in a sample to undergo decay.
- **Accelerator Mass Spectrometry (AMS)**: High-precision analytical method directly counting individual $^{14}C$ atoms.
