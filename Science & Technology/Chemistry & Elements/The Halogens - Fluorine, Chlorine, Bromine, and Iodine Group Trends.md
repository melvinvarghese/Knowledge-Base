# The Halogens - Fluorine, Chlorine, Bromine, and Iodine Group Trends

## Overview
The Halogens (Group 17) are a family of highly reactive, electronegative non-metals with seven valence electrons ($ns^2 np^5$) that form ionic salts with alkali metals.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Periodic Trends & Physical States: Down Group 17, boiling points increase due to growing London dispersion forces, spanning all three standard states of matter at room temperature: Fluorine (pale yellow gas), Chlorine (greenish gas), Bromine (dense red-brown liquid), and Iodine (lustrous dark purple crystalline solid). Electronegativity decreases down the group (Fluorine is the most electronegative element: 3.98 Pauling scale).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Reactivity & Halide Chemistry: Halogens readily gain one electron to achieve stable noble-gas electron configurations ($X + e^- \to X^-$). Fluorine's tiny atomic radius and weak $F\text{-}F$ bond make it the most aggressively reactive element, capable of oxidizing noble gases (e.g., $XeF_2$) and reacting with water violently.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Water purification chlorination, halogen light bulbs, thyroid hormone biochemistry (iodine in thyroxine), fluoropolymer coatings (Teflon).

## Key Terminology
- **Halogen**: Group 17 chemical element characterized by high electronegativity and salt-forming reactivity.
- **Electronegativity**: Chemical property describing the tendency of an atom to attract a shared pair of electrons toward itself.
- **Teflon (PTFE)**: Polytetrafluoroethylene; fluoropolymer plastic with extreme chemical resistance and low friction coefficients.
