# Mass Spectrometry - Ionization, Time-of-Flight, and Fragmentation

## Overview
Mass Spectrometry (MS) is an analytical technique that measures the mass-to-charge ratio ($m/z$) of ionized molecules to determine chemical formulas, isotopic abundances, and structural fragments.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Ionization Techniques: Hard ionization (Electron Ionization / EI) uses a 70 eV electron beam to shatter molecules into reproducible diagnostic fragments. Soft ionization methods (Electrospray Ionization / ESI; Matrix-Assisted Laser Desorption / MALDI, Fenn and Tanaka, Nobel Prize 2002) ionize intact fragile biomolecules (proteins, DNA) without thermal fragmentation.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Mass Analyzers: 1. Quadrupole analyzers: Filter ions via oscillating RF and DC electric fields; 2. Time-of-Flight (TOF): Accelerates ions through fixed electric potentials; lighter ions travel faster through a flight tube, hitting detectors earlier: $t = d \sqrt{\frac{m}{2 q V}}$; 3. Orbitraps: Trap ions in electrostatic oscillations.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
High-throughput proteomics biomarker discovery, forensic toxicology screening, drug metabolite quantification.

## Key Terminology
- **Mass-to-Charge Ratio ($m/z$)**: Physical quantity obtained by dividing the mass of an ion by its elementary charge.
- **Electrospray Ionization (ESI)**: Soft ionization technique generating intact multiply-charged aerosol ions from liquid solutions.
- **Time-of-Flight (TOF)**: Mass spectrometry method that separates ions according to the time taken to travel through an electric-field-free flight tube.
