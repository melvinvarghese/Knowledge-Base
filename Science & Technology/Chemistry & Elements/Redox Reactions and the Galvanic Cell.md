# Redox Reactions and the Galvanic Cell

## Overview
Reduction-Oxidation (Redox) reactions involve the transfer of electrons between chemical species. Galvanic (voltaic) cells harness spontaneous redox reactions to generate direct electrical current.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
Standard Reduction Potentials and Cell Voltage: $E^\circ_{\text{cell}} = E^\circ_{\text{cathode}} - E^\circ_{\text{anode}}$. Spontaneous reactions have positive cell potential ($E^\circ > 0$), linked to Gibbs free energy via $\Delta G^\circ = -nFE^\circ$.

---

## 2. Structural Mechanics & Scientific Analysis
The Daniell Cell & Salt Bridge: Zinc anode oxidizes ($Zn \rightarrow Zn^{2+} + 2e^-$), copper cathode reduces ($Cu^{2+} + 2e^- \rightarrow Cu$). A porous salt bridge maintains electrical neutrality via ion migration.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Technological Impact
Lithium-ion vehicle battery cells, zinc-air hearing aid button cells, and sacrificial anode cathodic corrosion protection.

---

## Key Terms
- **Galvanic Cell**: Electrochemical cell deriving electrical energy from spontaneous redox reactions.
- **Anode**: Electrode where oxidation (electron loss) occurs.
- **Nernst Equation**: Calculates non-standard cell reduction potentials: $E = E^\circ - \frac{RT}{nF}\ln Q$.
