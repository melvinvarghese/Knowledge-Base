# Chirality and Optical Isomerism in Organic Molecules

## Overview
Chirality describes the geometric property of a molecule that is non-superimposable on its mirror image, giving rise to optical enantiomers with identical physical properties but distinct biological interactions.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
Stereocenters and Cahn-Ingold-Prelog (CIP) Rules: An $sp^3$ carbon bonded to four distinct substituents forms a stereocenter. Priority ranking assigns absolute configuration as $(R)$ (rectus, clockwise) or $(S)$ (sinister, counterclockwise).

---

## 2. Structural Mechanics & Scientific Analysis
Optical Activity: Enantiomers rotate plane-polarized light in equal and opposite directions ($[\alpha]_D$ dextrorotatory $(+)$ vs levorotatory $(-)$). Racemic mixtures (1:1 ratio) show zero optical rotation.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Technological Impact
Chiral drug safety (thalidomide tragedy: $(R)$-enantiomer was sedative, $(S)$-enantiomer teratogenic), L-amino acid biological exclusivity, and asymmetric chiral catalysis.

---

## Key Terms
- **Enantiomers**: Non-superimposable mirror-image stereoisomers with identical scalar physical properties.
- **Stereocenter**: Atom bearing groups such that interchanging any two produces a stereoisomer.
- **Racemic Mixture**: Equimolar mixture of two enantiomers exhibiting zero net optical rotation.
