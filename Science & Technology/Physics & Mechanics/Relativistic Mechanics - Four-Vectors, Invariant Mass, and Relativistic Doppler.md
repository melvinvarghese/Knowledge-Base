# Relativistic Mechanics - Four-Vectors, Invariant Mass, and Relativistic Doppler

## Overview
Relativistic Mechanics reformulates classical Newtonian mechanics using four-dimensional tensor vectors (four-vectors) that remain invariant under Lorentz coordinate transformations.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Spacetime Four-Vectors: Physical quantities are unified into four-vectors: Four-position $X^\mu = (ct, \mathbf{x})$, Four-velocity $U^\mu = \frac{dX^\mu}{d\tau} = \gamma (c, \mathbf{u})$, and Four-momentum $P^\mu = m_0 U^\mu = (E/c, \mathbf{p})$. The Minkowski inner product of four-momentum with itself yields the invariant scalar rest mass: $P_\mu P^\mu = -(E/c)^2 + \mathbf{p}^2 = -m_0^2 c^2$, valid for all observers.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Relativistic Doppler Effect: When light sources move at relativistic velocity $v$ at angle $\theta$ relative to an observer, time dilation combines with classical Doppler shifts: $f = f_0 \frac{\sqrt{1 - \beta^2}}{1 - \beta \cos \theta}$. For purely transverse motion ($\theta = 90^\circ$), light exhibits a pure relativistic redshift ($f = f_0 \sqrt{1 - \beta^2}$) with no classical counterpart.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
High-energy particle physics collision cross-section calculations, relativistic astrophysical jet beaming.

## Key Terminology
- **Four-Vector**: Four-component vector in Minkowski spacetime transforming contravariantly under Lorentz transformations.
- **Invariant Mass**: Characteristic rest mass of an isolated physical system that remains identical across all inertial reference frames.
- **Proper Time**: Time interval $	au$ measured by a clock traveling along a specific worldline path through spacetime.
