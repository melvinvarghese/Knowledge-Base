# Terminal Velocity and Atmospheric Drag

## Overview
Terminal velocity is the steady speed attained by a falling body when the upward aerodynamic drag force exactly equals the downward gravitational force.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
Equilibrium Drag Balance: $v_t = \sqrt{\frac{2mg}{\rho C_d A}}$. Derived by setting the Rayleigh drag equation ($F_d = \frac{1}{2}\rho v^2 C_d A$) equal to weight ($mg$).

---

## 2. Structural Mechanics & Scientific Analysis
Flow Regimes: Laminar Stokes flow ($Re < 1$) where drag scales linearly ($F_d \propto v$) versus turbulent wake flow ($Re > 1000$) scaling quadratically ($F_d \propto v^2$).

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Technological Impact
Parachute deceleration systems, ballistic re-entry aerodynamics, and raindrop terminal impact speeds.

---

## Key Terms
- **Terminal Velocity**: Constant speed where drag balances gravitational weight.
- **Drag Coefficient ($C_d$)**: Dimensionless index of aerodynamic drag resistance.
- **Reynolds Number**: Ratio of inertial forces to viscous forces in fluid mechanics.
