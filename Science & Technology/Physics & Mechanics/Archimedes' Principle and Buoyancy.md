# Archimedes' Principle and Buoyancy

## Overview
Archimedes' Principle dictates that any body immersed in fluid experiences an upward buoyant force equal to the gravitational weight of the displaced fluid.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
Hydrostatic Pressure Gradient: $F_b = \rho_{\text{fluid}} V_{\text{disp}} g$. Net upward force balances gravitational body weight $W = mg$ at neutral equilibrium.

---

## 2. Structural Mechanics & Scientific Analysis
Metacentric Stability: Center of Gravity ($G$) and Center of Buoyancy ($B$) alignment determines righting moments. Positive metacentric height ($GM > 0$) prevents capsizing.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Technological Impact
Submarine variable ballast systems, hydrometer specific gravity metering, and stratospheric helium aerostats.

---

## Key Terms
- **Buoyancy**: Upward fluid force counteracting gravitational weight.
- **Specific Gravity**: Density ratio relative to pure water at 4°C.
- **Metacenter**: Intersection point of buoyant force lines with the vessel vertical axis.
