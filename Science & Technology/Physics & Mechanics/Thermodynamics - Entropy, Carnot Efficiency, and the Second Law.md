# Thermodynamics - Entropy, Carnot Efficiency, and the Second Law

## Overview
Thermodynamics governs heat, work, and energy transformations, underpinned by the fundamental asymmetry of the Second Law of Thermodynamics.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Carnot Cycle & Maximum Theoretical Efficiency: Sadi Carnot (1824) proved that no heat engine operating between hot reservoir $T_H$ and cold reservoir $T_C$ can exceed the Carnot efficiency: $\eta_{\text{max}} = 1 - \frac{T_C}{T_H}$. All reversible engines operating between the same temperatures achieve identical efficiency.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Clausius Entropy & The Arrow of Time: Rudolf Clausius formalized entropy as $dS = \frac{\delta Q_{\text{rev}}}{T}$. The Second Law of Thermodynamics dictates that for any spontaneous process in an isolated system, total entropy must non-decrease: $\Delta S_{\text{total}} \ge 0$. Ludwig Boltzmann linked thermodynamic entropy to microscopic statistical state multiplicities: $S = k_B \ln \Omega$, providing a thermodynamic basis for the arrow of time.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Combustion engine design, refrigerator heat pumps, cosmic thermal equilibrium (Heat Death of the universe).

## Key Terminology
- **Second Law of Thermodynamics**: Law stating that the total entropy of an isolated system can never decrease over time.
- **Carnot Efficiency**: Maximum theoretical efficiency that any heat engine can achieve operating between two thermal reservoirs.
- **Microstate**: Specific detailed microscopic microscopic configuration of a thermodynamic system, denoted by $\Omega$.
