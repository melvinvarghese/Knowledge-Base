# Friction - Static, Kinetic, and Rolling Resistance

## Overview
Friction is the resistive tangential contact force opposing relative lateral motion between solid surfaces, governed by microscopic asperities and molecular adhesion.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
Coulomb Friction Laws: $f_s \le \mu_s N$ and $f_k = \mu_k N$. The static coefficient ($\mu_s$) generally exceeds kinetic ($\mu_k$) because surface bonds must be sheared.

---

## 2. Structural Mechanics & Scientific Analysis
Microscopic Tribology: Microscopic contact asperities cold-weld under local compressive pressure. Rolling resistance arises from inelastic material hysteresis in tires/tracks.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Technological Impact
High-grip automotive tire rubber compounds, anti-lock braking systems (ABS), and pressurized hydrodynamic oil bearings.

---

## Key Terms
- **Tribology**: Science of friction, lubrication, and wear between interacting surfaces.
- **Asperity**: Microscopic roughness peak on a solid surface.
- **Hysteresis Loss**: Energy dissipated as internal heat during cyclic elastic deformation.
