# The Compton Effect and Photon-Electron Scattering

## Overview
The Compton Effect demonstrates that when an X-ray or gamma-ray photon collides with a stationary electron, the scattered photon emerges with longer wavelength and lower energy.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
Compton Wavelength Shift Formula: $\Delta\lambda = \lambda' - \lambda = \frac{h}{m_e c}(1 - \cos\theta)$. Compton wavelength is $\lambda_C = h/(m_e c) \approx 2.43 \times 10^{-12}\text{ m}$.

---

## 2. Structural Mechanics & Scientific Analysis
Relativistic Momentum Conservation: Treating photons as particles with momentum $p = h/\lambda$, colliding elastically with free target electrons under relativistic conservation laws.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Technological Impact
Radiation oncology dosimetry, gamma-ray telescope detector shielding, and deep Compton camera imaging.

---

## Key Terms
- **Compton Shift**: Wavelength increase of a photon after inelastic scattering by a charged particle.
- **Compton Wavelength**: Characteristic quantum mechanical scale parameter of a particle ($h/mc$).
- **Klein-Nishina Formula**: Differential cross-section governing angular photon scattering probability.
