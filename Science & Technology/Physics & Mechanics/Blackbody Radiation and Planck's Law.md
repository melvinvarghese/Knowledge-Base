# Blackbody Radiation and Planck's Law

## Overview
Planck's Law describes the spectral energy density of electromagnetic radiation emitted by a blackbody in thermal equilibrium, resolving the catastrophic ultraviolet divergence of classical physics.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
Planck's Spectral Distribution: $u(\nu, T) = \frac{8\pi h \nu^3}{c^3} \frac{1}{e^{h\nu / k_B T} - 1}$. Bridges Wien's displacement law ($\lambda_{\text{max}} T = b$) and Stefan-Boltzmann total energy ($j^* = \sigma T^4$).

---

## 2. Structural Mechanics & Scientific Analysis
The Ultraviolet Catastrophe Resolution: Classical Rayleigh-Jeans law predicted infinite radiated power at high frequencies; Max Planck introduced energy quantization ($E = nh\nu$) to match observations.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Technological Impact
Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB) thermal mapping, infrared pyrometry thermometers, and stellar surface temperature classification.

---

## Key Terms
- **Blackbody**: An idealized physical body that absorbs all incident electromagnetic radiation.
- **Ultraviolet Catastrophe**: Failure of classical electrodynamics predicting infinite high-frequency radiation.
- **Stefan-Boltzmann Constant**: Fundamental constant linking blackbody radiant flux to $T^4$.
