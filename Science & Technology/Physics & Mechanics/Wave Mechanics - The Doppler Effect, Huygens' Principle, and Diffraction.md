# Wave Mechanics - The Doppler Effect, Huygens' Principle, and Diffraction

## Overview
Wave mechanics describes the propagation, interference, and frequency modulation of continuous wave disturbances across acoustic, mechanical, and optical media.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Doppler Effect Formulation: For a source moving at velocity $v_s$ and observer at velocity $v_o$ relative to medium sound speed $v$, the observed frequency shifts as: $f' = f_0 \left( \frac{v \pm v_o}{v \mp v_s} \right)$. In astrophysics, relativistic Doppler shifts of light waves reveal the cosmological expansion of the universe via galactic redshift ($z = \frac{\Delta \lambda}{\lambda_0}$).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Huygens-Fresnel Principle & Diffraction: Christiaan Huygens stated that every point on a advancing wavefront acts as a secondary source of spherical wavelets. Waves bending around obstacles or passing through narrow apertures of width $a \approx \lambda$ interfere to create Fraunhofer diffraction patterns with intensity minima at $a \sin \theta = m \lambda$.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Radar speed guns, Doppler ultrasound echocardiography, X-ray crystallography diffraction analysis.

## Key Terminology
- **Doppler Effect**: Change in frequency or wavelength of a wave in relation to an observer who is moving relative to the wave source.
- **Huygens' Principle**: Principle that every point on a wavefront is a source of wavelets that spread out in the forward direction at the same speed as the wave.
- **Diffraction**: Bending and spreading of waves around obstacles and openings whose dimensions are comparable to the wavelength.
