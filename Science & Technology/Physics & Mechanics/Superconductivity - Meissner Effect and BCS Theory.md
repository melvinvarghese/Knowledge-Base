# Superconductivity - Meissner Effect and BCS Theory

## Overview
Superconductivity is a quantum mechanical state characterized by zero electrical resistance and the complete expulsion of magnetic flux fields below a critical transition temperature ($T_c$).

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
The Meissner-Ochsenfeld Effect: Superconductors are perfect diamagnets ($B = 0$ internally). Penetrating magnetic flux is countered by persistent surface screening currents.

---

## 2. Structural Mechanics & Scientific Analysis
BCS Theory & Cooper Pairing: Below $T_c$, electron-phonon lattice interactions create attractive forces between electrons of opposite spin and momentum, condensing into a bosonic macroscopic ground state.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Technological Impact
Maglev train magnetic levitation, MRI superconducting magnets, and transmon qubits in quantum computers.

---

## Key Terms
- **Meissner Effect**: Complete expulsion of magnetic field lines from a superconductor interior.
- **Cooper Pair**: Bound pair of electrons interacting via crystal lattice phonon vibrations.
- **Critical Temperature ($T_c$)**: Temperature below which electrical resistance drops strictly to zero.
