# The Michelson-Morley Experiment and the Speed of Light

## Overview
The Michelson-Morley Experiment (1887) attempted to detect the relative velocity of Earth through the luminiferous aether, yielding a famous null result that paved the way for Special Relativity.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
Optical Interferometer Principle: Light split into two perpendicular arms of length $L$. Aether wind was expected to produce travel time differential $\Delta t \approx (L/c)(v^2/c^2)$ and fringe shifts.

---

## 2. Structural Mechanics & Scientific Analysis
The Null Result and Aether Rejection: No fringe displacement was detected within experimental accuracy, proving the speed of light ($c$) is invariant in all inertial reference frames.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Technological Impact
Foundation of Einstein's Special Relativity, development of ultra-stable laser cavities, and modern LIGO gravitational wave interferometry.

---

## Key Terms
- **Luminiferous Aether**: Hypothetical stationary medium once postulated to carry light waves.
- **Interferometry**: Superposition technique extracting measurements from wave interference fringes.
- **Invariance of $c$**: Postulate that light in vacuum travels at $c$ relative to all inertial observers.
