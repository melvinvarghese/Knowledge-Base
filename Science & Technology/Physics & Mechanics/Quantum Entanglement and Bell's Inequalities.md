# Quantum Entanglement and Bell's Inequalities

## Overview
Quantum Entanglement is a phenomenon where two or more particles become inextricably linked such that the physical state of one instantly dictates the state of the other, regardless of spatial separation.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
Bell's Inequality & Local Realism: John Bell proved that any local hidden-variable theory must satisfy $|E(a,b) - E(a,c)| \le 1 + E(b,c)$. Quantum mechanics violates this bound up to $2\sqrt{2}$ (Cirel'son bound).

---

## 2. Structural Mechanics & Scientific Analysis
Non-Locality and Wavefunction Collapse: Entangled pairs in Bell states ($|\Phi^+\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|00\rangle + |11\rangle)$) yield correlated measurements without violating relativistic causality (no faster-than-light signaling).

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Technological Impact
Quantum teleportation protocols, quantum computing entanglement gates, and Quantum Key Distribution (E91 protocol).

---

## Key Terms
- **Entanglement**: Non-separable quantum state where individual particles share a single wave function.
- **Bell's Theorem**: Proof that no physical theory of local hidden variables can reproduce quantum mechanics.
- **EPR Paradox**: Thought experiment challenging the completeness of quantum mechanics.
