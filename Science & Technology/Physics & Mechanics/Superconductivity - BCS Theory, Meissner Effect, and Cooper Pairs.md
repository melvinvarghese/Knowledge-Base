# Superconductivity - BCS Theory, Meissner Effect, and Cooper Pairs

## Overview
Superconductivity is a quantum condensed-matter state characterized by exactly zero electrical resistance and the total expulsion of interior magnetic fields below a critical temperature ($T_c$).

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Meissner-Ochsenfeld Effect: When a material transitions into a superconducting state under an applied magnetic field, surface screening currents spontaneously arise to generate an opposing interior field, resulting in complete internal magnetic flux expulsion ($B = 0$) and magnetic levitation.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The BCS Microscopic Theory (Bardeen, Cooper, Schrieffer, 1957): Below $T_c$, weak electron-phonon lattice vibrations overcome electron Coulomb repulsion, binding electrons into composite bosonic pairs called **Cooper Pairs**. Because Cooper pairs are bosons, they condense into a single macroscopic ground quantum state that flows through the crystal lattice without scattering or electrical resistance.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
MRI medical imaging superconducting magnets, maglev train levitation, transmon qubits in quantum computers.

## Key Terminology
- **Superconductivity**: State of zero electrical resistance and expulsion of magnetic flux occurring in certain materials cooled below a critical temperature.
- **Cooper Pair**: Pair of electrons bound together at low temperatures by electron-phonon interactions acting as a composite boson.
- **Meissner Effect**: Expulsion of a magnetic field from a superconductor during its transition to the superconducting state.
