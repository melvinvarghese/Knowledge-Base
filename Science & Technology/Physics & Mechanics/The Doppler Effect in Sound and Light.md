# The Doppler Effect in Sound and Light

## Overview
The Doppler Effect is the shift in observed wave frequency caused by relative motion between the emitter and the observer across acoustic and electromagnetic spectra.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
Acoustic vs Relativistic Doppler Shift: Acoustic medium shift $f = f_0 (v \pm v_o)/(v \mp v_s)$ versus relativistic vacuum shift $f = f_0 \sqrt{(1-\beta)/(1+\beta)}$.

---

## 2. Structural Mechanics & Scientific Analysis
Wavefront Compression: Moving sources compress wavefronts ahead (blueshift/higher pitch) and elongate them behind (redshift/lower pitch). At $v > c_s$, a Mach shock cone forms.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Technological Impact
Doppler meteorological radar, medical bloodflow ultrasonography, and astronomical measurement of galactic expansion.

---

## Key Terms
- **Doppler Shift**: Change in apparent wave frequency due to relative source motion.
- **Redshift**: Spectral line shift toward longer wavelengths in receding celestial bodies.
- **Mach Cone**: Conical pressure wavefront produced by supersonic motion.
