# The Stern-Gerlach Experiment and Quantum Spin

## Overview
The Stern-Gerlach Experiment (1922) revealed the spatial quantization of angular momentum by passing silver atoms through an inhomogeneous magnetic field, proving the existence of intrinsic quantum spin.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
Magnetic Deflection Force: In an inhomogeneous magnetic field $\partial B_z / \partial z$, magnetic dipole $\mu_z$ experiences force $F_z = \mu_z (\partial B_z / \partial z)$.

---

## 2. Structural Mechanics & Scientific Analysis
Discrete Spatial Splitting: Instead of a continuous classical smear, silver atoms split into exactly two discrete beams corresponding to quantized electron spin eigenvalues $S_z = \pm \frac{1}{2}\hbar$.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Technological Impact
Foundation of quantum measurement theory, magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) spin manipulation, and qubit states in quantum computing.

---

## Key Terms
- **Quantum Spin**: Intrinsic form of angular momentum carried by fundamental elementary particles.
- **Spatial Quantization**: Restriction of angular momentum projection vectors to discrete orientations.
- **Bohr Magneton ($\mu_B$)**: Physical constant representing electron magnetic dipole moment.
