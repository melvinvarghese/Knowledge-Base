# Electromagnetism - Maxwell's Equations and the Speed of Light

## Overview
Maxwell's Equations (1865) unified electricity and magnetism into a single coherent classical field theory, predicting electromagnetic waves traveling at the speed of light.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Four Differential Field Equations: 1. Gauss's Law: $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{E} = \frac{\rho}{\epsilon_0}$ (electric charges produce electric fields); 2. Gauss's Law for Magnetism: $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{B} = 0$ (no magnetic monopoles exist); 3. Faraday's Law of Induction: $\nabla \times \mathbf{E} = -\frac{\partial \mathbf{B}}{\partial t}$ (changing magnetic fields induce circulating electric fields); 4. Ampère-Maxwell Law: $\nabla \times \mathbf{B} = \mu_0 \mathbf{J} + \mu_0 \epsilon_0 \frac{\partial \mathbf{E}}{\partial t}$ (electric currents and changing electric fields induce magnetic fields).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Electromagnetic Wave Equation: In a vacuum with no charges or currents, combining the curl of curl yields the electromagnetic wave equation: $\nabla^2 \mathbf{E} = \mu_0 \epsilon_0 \frac{\partial^2 \mathbf{E}}{\partial t^2}$. The wave propagation velocity $c = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_0 \epsilon_0}} \approx 299,792,458\text{ m/s}$ matched measured light speeds, proving that light is an electromagnetic wave.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Radio and microwave telecommunications, electric power generators and transformers, foundational seed for Special Relativity.

## Key Terminology
- **Maxwell's Equations**: Set of coupled partial differential equations that form the theoretical foundation of classical electromagnetism.
- **Displacement Current**: Term added by Maxwell ($\mu_0 \epsilon_0 rac{\partial \mathbf{E}}{\partial t}$) accounting for changing electric fields in Ampère's Law.
- **Permittivity & Permeability**: Fundamental physical constants ($\epsilon_0, \mu_0$) characterizing vacuum resistance to electric and magnetic fields.
