# Quantum Electrodynamics (QED) - Feynman Diagrams and Renormalization

## Overview
Quantum Electrodynamics (QED) is the relativistic quantum field theory describing the electromagnetic interaction of charged subatomic particles via virtual photon exchanges.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Feynman Diagrams as Computational Perturbation: Richard Feynman introduced intuitive spacetime diagrams that graphically represent complex probability amplitude integrals in quantum perturbation series. Solid lines represent propagating fermions (electrons/positrons); wavy lines represent gauge bosons (photons); and vertices represent interaction couplings proportional to the fine-structure constant ($\alpha \approx 1/137$).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Problem of Infinities & Renormalization: Loop integrals in QED initially yielded infinite divergent self-energies. Tomonaga, Schwinger, and Feynman developed **renormalization**—systematically absorbing mathematical infinities into measurable physical quantities (bare mass and charge), predicting the electron's anomalous magnetic dipole moment ($g-2$) to twelve decimal places of experimental accuracy.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Most precisely tested physical theory in human history, theoretical template for the Standard Model of particle physics.

## Key Terminology
- **QED**: Quantum Electrodynamics; quantum field theory of electromagnetic interactions mediated by photon exchange.
- **Feynman Diagram**: Pictorial representation of the mathematical expressions governing the behavior of subatomic particles.
- **Renormalization**: Mathematical procedure in quantum field theory used to eliminate infinities appearing in calculated physical quantities.
