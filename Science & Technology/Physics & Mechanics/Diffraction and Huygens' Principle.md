# Diffraction and Huygens' Principle

## Overview
Huygens' Principle states that every point on a wavefront acts as a secondary source of spherical wavelets, explaining wave diffraction and bending around obstacles.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
Diffraction Angle and Wavelength Scaling: Angular spread scales as $\sin\theta \approx \lambda / a$, where $\lambda$ is wavelength and $a$ is aperture width.

---

## 2. Structural Mechanics & Scientific Analysis
Fraunhofer vs Fresnel Regimes: Far-field planar wavefront interference (Fraunhofer) versus near-field curved wavefront diffraction (Fresnel).

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Technological Impact
Diffraction grating spectrometers, optical microscope resolution limits (Abbe criterion), and X-ray crystallography.

---

## Key Terms
- **Diffraction**: Bending and spreading of waves around obstacles and through apertures.
- **Wavefront**: Continuous surface joining points of identical phase in a propagating wave.
- **Abbe Limit**: Physical resolution barrier set by light diffraction: $d = \lambda / (2\text{NA})$.
