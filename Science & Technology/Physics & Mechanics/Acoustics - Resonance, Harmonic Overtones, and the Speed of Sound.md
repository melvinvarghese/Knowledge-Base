# Acoustics - Resonance, Harmonic Overtones, and the Speed of Sound

## Overview
Acoustics is the branch of physics studying mechanical longitudinal pressure waves propagating through gases, liquids, and solids.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Wave Equation & The Newton-Laplace Formula: Acoustic sound propagates through compressions and rarefactions. The speed of sound $c$ depends on the medium's adiabatic bulk modulus $K_s$ and density $\rho$: $c = \sqrt{\frac{K_s}{\rho}} = \sqrt{\gamma \frac{R T}{M}}$, traveling at $\sim 343\text{ m/s}$ in dry air at $20^\circ\text{C}$ and $\sim 1,480\text{ m/s}$ in water.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Resonance & Standing Waves: Confined acoustic cavities (such as organ pipes or string instruments) generate standing waves when reflected waves interfere constructively at boundary nodes and antinodes. Fundamental resonant frequencies ($f_1 = \frac{v}{2L}$) produce integer harmonic overtones ($f_n = n f_1$), whose relative spectral amplitudes define the distinct acoustic **timbre** of musical instruments.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Architectural hall acoustic engineering, ultrasonic medical scanning, noise-cancelling acoustic interference.

## Key Terminology
- **Speed of Sound**: Distance travelled per unit time by a sound wave as it propagates through an elastic medium.
- **Resonance**: Phenomenon in which an acoustic system responds at maximum amplitude to an external driving frequency matching its natural frequency.
- **Timbre**: Perceived sound quality or acoustic tone color of a musical note that distinguishes different types of sound production.
