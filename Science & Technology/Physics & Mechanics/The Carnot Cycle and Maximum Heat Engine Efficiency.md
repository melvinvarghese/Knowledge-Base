# The Carnot Cycle and Maximum Heat Engine Efficiency

## Overview
The Carnot Cycle is an idealized thermodynamic cycle operating between two thermal reservoirs, establishing the theoretical maximum efficiency limit for any heat engine.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
Carnot Efficiency Theorem: No engine can exceed the efficiency of a reversible Carnot engine operating between $T_H$ and $T_C$:

$$\eta_{\text{Carnot}} = 1 - \frac{T_C}{T_H}$$

Derived directly from the Second Law of Thermodynamics (entropy cannot decrease).

---

## 2. Structural Mechanics & Scientific Analysis
The Four Cycle Phases: 1. Isothermal expansion at $T_H$; 2. Isentropic adiabatic expansion; 3. Isothermal compression at $T_C$; 4. Isentropic adiabatic compression back to start.

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Technological Impact
Theoretical performance benchmarks for steam turbine power stations, internal combustion engine design, and thermodynamic heat pump coefficient of performance (COP).

---

## Key Terms
- **Carnot Efficiency**: Upper theoretical thermodynamic limit on converting heat into mechanical work.
- **Isentropic Process**: An idealized reversible adiabatic thermodynamic process with constant entropy.
- **Thermal Reservoir**: Thermodynamic system with infinite heat capacity maintaining constant temperature.
