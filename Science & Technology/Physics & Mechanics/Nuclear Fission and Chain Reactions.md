# Nuclear Fission and Chain Reactions

## Overview
Nuclear Fission is the nuclear reaction where a heavy nucleus splits into smaller daughter nuclei with the release of massive kinetic energy, neutrons, and gamma radiation.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations & Governing Principles
Mass Defect and Energy Release: Fission of Uranium-235 ($n + {}^{235}\text{U} \rightarrow {}^{141}\text{Ba} + {}^{92}\text{Kr} + 3n + 200\text{ MeV}$) converts lost nuclear binding mass defect into energy via $\Delta E = \Delta m c^2$.

---

## 2. Structural Mechanics & Scientific Analysis
Neutron Multiplication Factor ($k$): Ratio of neutrons in generation $n+1$ to generation $n$. Subcritical ($k < 1$), critical ($k = 1$, steady power reactor), and supercritical ($k > 1$).

---

## 3. Real-World Applications & Technological Impact
Commercial pressurized and boiling water nuclear power reactors, naval nuclear submarine propulsion, and medical radioisotope synthesis.

---

## Key Terms
- **Fission**: Splitting of a heavy atomic nucleus into lighter fragments.
- **Critical Mass**: Minimum mass of fissile material required to sustain a chain reaction.
- **Control Rods**: Neutron-absorbing materials (boron, cadmium) regulating reactor power.
