### 🌌 Space & Astronomy
- [[The Sun - Anatomy of a Star]] — The 6 layers, nuclear fusion reactor, and magnetic storms powering our Solar System.
- [[The Solar System - Rocky Planets vs Gas Giants]] — The 8 planets, the frost line, asteroid belts, and frozen Kuiper debris.
- [[The Life Cycle of Stars]] — From stellar nebulae to red giants, white dwarfs, violent supernovae, and black holes.
- [[The Big Bang and the Universe]] — The origin of space and time 13.8 billion years ago, cosmic expansion, and dark matter.
- [[Black Holes and Spacetime]] — Infinite density singularities, event horizons, time dilation, and warped gravity.
- [[The Moon - Earth's Satellite]] — Earth’s only natural satellite, formed 4.5 billion years ago when a Mars-sized planet (Theia) collided with young Earth. The Moon stabilizes Earth's axial tilt and drives ocean tides.
- [[Mercury - The Iron Planet]] — The smallest planet in our solar system and the closest to the Sun. Mercury is essentially a giant iron core covered in a thin, cratered rocky crust with wild temperature swings.
- [[Venus - The Greenhouse Furnace]] — Earth's nearest neighbor and 'evil twin.' Venus suffered runaway greenhouse heating, creating a hellish surface hot enough to melt lead under crushing atmospheric pressure.
- [[Mars - The Red Planet and Past Water]] — The fourth planet from the Sun. Once warm, wet, and active, Mars lost its protective magnetic field to solar wind, leaving behind a frozen, oxidized red desert with signs of ancient rivers.
- [[Jupiter - The Gas Giant and Storm King]] — The undisputed heavyweight of our Solar System. A gas giant holding more mass than all other planets combined, with a swirling atmosphere of hydrogen and helium and dozens of moons.
- [[Saturn - Rings and Moons]] — The sixth planet from the Sun, world-famous for its majestic ring system made of billions of icy particles, and fascinating moons like Titan and Enceladus.
- [[Uranus - The Tilted Ice Giant]] — An ice giant composed of water, methane, and ammonia ices. Unique for spinning completely on its side, likely caused by a colossal planetary collision in early solar system history.
- [[Neptune - The Windy Outpost]] — The most distant major planet in our Solar System. A deep blue ice giant famous for supersonic supersonic winds howling at over 2,000 km/h and its captured moon Triton.
- [[Pluto and Dwarf Planets]] — Reclassified in 2006 as a dwarf planet, Pluto is an icy world in the Kuiper Belt featuring nitrogen glaciers, ice mountains, and a heart-shaped plain (Tombaugh Regio).
- [[Asteroids, Meteors, and Meteorites]] — The debris left over from the formation of the Solar System. Understanding the difference between rocks in space, streaks of light in the sky, and stones that land on Earth.
- [[Comets - Dirty Snowballs of the Solar System]] — Cosmic snowballs of frozen gases, rock, and dust orbiting the Sun in elongated ellipses. When nearing the Sun, solar radiation vaporizes ice into luminous glowing tails.
- [[The Milky Way Galaxy]] — Our cosmic home: a barred spiral galaxy containing 100 to 400 billion stars, stretching 100,000 light-years across, with a supermassive black hole at its core.
- [[Types of Galaxies - Spiral, Elliptical, and Irregular]] — Edwin Hubble’s classification of the universe's island universes: rotating Spirals, aged smooth Ellipticals, and chaotic colliding Irregulars.
- [[Nebulae - Stellar Nurseries]] — Vast interstellar clouds of dust, hydrogen, and helium gas. Nebulae are both the maternity wards where young stars are born and the glowing remnants of dead stars.
- [[Supernovae - The Deaths of Massive Stars]] — The most cataclysmic explosions in the cosmos. In a fraction of a second, a dying massive star unleashes as much energy as our Sun will emit over its entire 10-billion-year life.
- [[Neutron Stars and Pulsars]] — The collapsed core of an exploded massive star. So dense that protons and electrons are crushed into pure neutrons—packing the mass of two Suns into a sphere just 20 km across.
- [[Exoplanets - Worlds Beyond Our Solar System]] — Planets orbiting stars outside our Solar System. Over 5,500 exoplanets have been confirmed, ranging from boiling 'Hot Jupiters' to rocky worlds in the habitable Goldilocks zone.
- [[The James Webb Space Telescope]] — NASA's premier infrared observatory stationed 1.5 million kilometers from Earth at Lagrange Point 2. Its 6.5-meter gold-coated beryllium mirror peers back in time to the first stars.
- [[Space Exploration and Human Spaceflight]] — From Sputnik and Apollo 11 to the International Space Station and the Artemis lunar program—humanity's quest to leave Earth's gravity well and colonize the cosmos.
- [[The Fate of the Universe - Big Freeze to Big Rip]] — Cosmology's ultimate question: How will the universe end? Driven by Dark Energy, current observations point toward the 'Big Freeze'—an eternal, cold, silent expanse.

### 🌋 Earth & Geology
- [[Earth's Layers and Plate Tectonics]] — Crust, mantle, outer liquid core, continental drift, and the Ring of Fire.
- [[Volcanoes and Magma]] — Shield vs. composite volcanoes, pyroclastic flows, lahars, and magma chambers.
- [[Earthquakes and Tsunamis]] — Seismic P, S, and surface waves, the underwater displacement mechanics of tsunamis.
- [[The Atmosphere and the Water Cycle]] — The 5 atmospheric shields, the ozone layer, and the planet's closed water loop.
- [[Weather Systems and Extreme Storms]] — High vs. low pressure, the Coriolis effect, hurricane eyes, and supercell tornadoes.
- [[The Rock Cycle - Igneous, Sedimentary, and Metamorphic]] — Earth's crust is in a continuous state of recycling. Over millions of years, heat, pressure, melting, and weathering transform rocks between three fundamental classes.
- [[Minerals and Crystals]] — Minerals are naturally occurring, inorganic solids with a definite chemical formula and an orderly crystalline structure. Rocks are simply aggregates of one or more minerals.
- [[Fossils and the Geological Time Scale]] — Fossils are the preserved remains, impressions, or traces of ancient life buried in sedimentary rock. They act as a chronological tape recorder for Earth's 4.5-billion-year history.
- [[Mountains and Orogeny]] — Orogeny is the geological process of mountain building. Mountains form when tectonic plates collide, crumpling the crust into folded ridges or volcanic arcs.
- [[Caves and Karst Landscapes]] — Caves are subterranean cathedrals carved by acidic water slowly dissolving soluble bedrock like limestone over hundreds of thousands of years.
- [[Rivers and Drainage Basins]] — Rivers are the planetary veins that carry freshwater runoff from mountain peaks to the ocean, carving valleys and depositing fertile floodplains along their course.
- [[Glaciers and Ice Ages]] — Glaciers are massive rivers of compressed ice that flow slowly under their own weight. They are nature's bulldozers, carving iconic U-shaped valleys and fjords.
- [[Deserts and Wind Erosion]] — Covering one-third of Earth's land, deserts are defined by aridity (less than 25 cm of rainfall per year), where wind (Aeolian processes) shapes the terrain.
- [[Oceans - Currents and the Global Conveyor Belt]] — The oceans cover 71% of Earth's surface. Global thermohaline circulation acts as a colossal planetary conveyor belt, distributing heat and regulating world climates.
- [[The Deep Sea - Trenches and Hydrothermal Vents]] — The abyss is Earth's final frontier: pitch-black, freezing, and under bone-crushing pressure, yet thriving with alien-like life fueled by volcanic hydrothermal chemistry.
- [[The Carbon Cycle]] — Carbon is the chemical backbone of all organic life. Earth's carbon cycle shuttles carbon atoms continuously between the atmosphere, biosphere, oceans, and geosphere.
- [[The Nitrogen Cycle]] — Nitrogen makes up 78% of our air, but plants and animals cannot breathe it directly. Specialized bacteria must 'fix' nitrogen into usable soil fertilizers.
- [[Weathering and Erosion]] — The two relentless surface sculptors of Earth. Weathering breaks solid rock down into pieces where it sits; Erosion carries the broken pieces away.
- [[Soil - The Living Skin of the Earth]] — Soil is not dead dirt; it is a complex, living ecosystem of weathered rock minerals, organic humus, air, water, and billions of microorganisms essential for human food.
- [[The Ozone Layer and Stratospheric Chemistry]] — A delicate shield of triatomic oxygen ($O_3$) in the stratosphere that absorbs over 98% of the Sun's harmful ultraviolet (UV-B) radiation.
- [[The Greenhouse Effect and Global Warming]] — Earth's natural atmospheric blanket keeps our planet livable, but human emissions of greenhouse gases are trapping excess heat and altering the global climate.
- [[Earth's Magnetic Field and the Geodynamo]] — Generated by swirling molten iron in the outer core, Earth's magnetosphere extends tens of thousands of kilometers into space, shielding our atmosphere from solar wind.
- [[Tides and Gravitational Pull]] — The periodic rise and fall of ocean waters caused by the gravitational tug-of-war between the Moon, the Sun, and the rotating Earth.
- [[Coasts and Wave Dynamics]] — Coastlines are the ever-changing battleground between ocean hydraulic force and continental rock, shaped by longshore drift, sea cliffs, arches, and barrier islands.
- [[Geysers and Geothermal Energy]] — Geysers are rare natural geothermal springs that periodically erupt with boiling water and steam due to underground magma chambers and tight plumbing systems.

### 🧬 The Living World & Nature
- [[The Cell - How Life Works]] — The microscopic factories of life, prokaryotes vs. eukaryotes, and organelle machinery.
- [[Evolution and Natural Selection]] — Darwin’s 4 pillars, reproductive fitness, and the mechanisms of adaptation.
- [[The Tree of Life - Biological Classification]] — Taxonomy hierarchy (Dear King Philip), the 3 domains, and binomial naming.
- [[Photosynthesis - How Plants Power Life]] — Converting sunlight, water, and $CO_2$ into glucose and life-giving oxygen.
- [[Dinosaurs and the Mesozoic Era]] — Triassic, Jurassic, and Cretaceous periods, the Chicxulub asteroid impact, and avian evolution.
- [[The Six Kingdoms of Life]] — Biologists classify all organisms into six broad kingdoms based on cell structure, how they obtain nutrition, and their evolutionary history.
- [[Bacteria and Archaea - The Microscopic Pioneers]] — The earliest and most numerous organisms on the planet. Simple single-celled prokaryotes that drive global chemical cycles, digestion, and biotechnology.
- [[Fungi - The Decomposers and Mycelial Networks]] — Fungi are neither plants nor animals. Connected by underground fungal webs (**mycelium**), they are Earth's primary recyclers, breaking down dead wood and feeding ecosystems.
- [[Plant Anatomy - Roots, Stems, and Leaves]] — Plants are precision hydraulic systems designed to harvest sunlight and soil minerals through specialized tissues: Xylem pipelines, Phloem food delivery, and Stomata pores.
- [[Flowering Plants and Pollination]] — Angiosperms (flowering plants) revolutionized the planet 130 million years ago by partnering with insects, birds, and wind to achieve cross-pollination and seed dispersal.
- [[Carnivorous Plants]] — Plants that evolved to trap and digest insects and small animals to obtain vital nitrogen and phosphorus in acidic, nutrient-poor bogs.
- [[Invertebrates - The Spineless Majority]] — Over 95% of all animal species on Earth lack a backbone. From microscopic tardigrades to colossal squids, invertebrates dominate every ecosystem on the globe.
- [[Insects - The Most Successful Animals on Earth]] — With over 1 million identified species, insects represent more than half of all known living organisms. Their success stems from small size, exoskeletons, wings, and rapid reproduction.
- [[Metamorphosis - From Caterpillar to Butterfly]] — Complete metamorphosis is one of biology's most radical transformations: an insect completely dissolves its larval body into cellular soup inside a chrysalis to rebuild an adult.
- [[Spiders and Arachnids]] — Arachnids are 8-legged predatory arthropods. Spiders produce silk stronger than steel by weight to engineer webs, egg sacs, and draglines.
- [[Marine Invertebrates - Jellyfish, Octopuses, and Coral]] — The oceans harbor astonishing invertebrate diversity: jellyfish with no brain or heart, corals that build underwater cities, and octopuses with decentralized distributed intelligence.
- [[Fish - Anatomy of Aquatic Vertebrates]] — The earliest vertebrates on Earth. Fish mastered underwater life through gills for gas exchange, streamlined hydrodynamics, lateral line sensors, and swim bladders.
- [[Sharks and Rays - Cartilaginous Hunters]] — Chondrichthyes (cartilaginous fish) have ruled the oceans for over 400 million years—older than trees and dinosaurs. Their skeletons are made of flexible cartilage instead of bone.
- [[Amphibians - Life Between Water and Land]] — The evolutionary bridge from water to land. Frogs, toads, and salamanders lead a double life (*Amphi-Bios*), breathing through permeable skin and laying jelly eggs in water.
- [[Reptiles - Cold-Blooded Masters]] — Reptiles conquered dry land through two revolutionary evolutionary inventions: waterproof scaly skin to prevent dehydration, and shelled amniotic eggs that don't need water.
- [[Birds - The Feathers and Physics of Flight]] — Direct descendants of theropod dinosaurs, birds conquered the air with hollow honeycomb bones, powerful four-chambered hearts, high-efficiency air sacs, and aerodynamic feathers.
- [[Mammals - Warm Blood, Fur, and Milk]] — Mammals are warm-blooded vertebrates defined by hair or fur, specialized teeth, and mammary glands that produce milk to nurse their young.
- [[Predator-Prey Dynamics and Food Chains]] — Energy flows through ecosystems in trophic levels. As energy moves up from producers to apex predators, roughly 90% is lost at each step as metabolic heat.
- [[Symbiosis - Mutualism, Commensalism, and Parasitism]] — Symbiosis describes close, persistent biological interactions between different species: win-win partnerships, one-sided benefits, and harmful exploitation.
- [[Mass Extinctions - The Big Five in Earth's History]] — Over 99% of all species that ever lived are extinct. Five times in Earth's history, planetary catastrophes wiped out over 70% of life, resetting the evolutionary clock.

### 🫀 Human Body & Medicine
- [[The Human Brain and Nervous System]] — 86 billion neurons, the 4 cerebral lobes, synapses, and autonomic responses.
- [[The Circulatory System - The Heart and Blood]] — The 4-chambered pump, pulmonary vs. systemic circuits, and blood components.
- [[The Immune System - Defense Against Disease]] — Physical barriers, innate macrophage patrols, and adaptive antibody memory.
- [[Genetics and DNA - The Blueprint of Life]] — The double helix, A-T / C-G base pairing, transcription, and translation into proteins.
- [[The Skeletal System - Bones and Joints]] — The human skeleton is an internal architectural framework of 206 bones that protects vital organs, anchors muscles, manufactures red blood cells, and stores calcium.
- [[The Muscular System - Contraction and Biomechanics]] — The body contains over 600 muscles making up 40% of body weight. Muscles only pull; they never push—operating in antagonistic pairs to produce movement.
- [[The Respiratory System - Lungs and Gas Exchange]] — Your respiratory system exchanges vital gases across an astonishing surface area the size of a tennis court, bringing in oxygen and exhaling carbon dioxide waste.
- [[The Digestive System - From Food to Fuel]] — A 9-meter continuous gastrointestinal tube that mechanically crushes, chemically digests, and absorbs nutrients from food while expelling waste.
- [[The Liver - The Body's Chemical Processing Plant]] — The body’s largest internal organ, performing over 500 vital functions: filtering toxins, manufacturing bile, regulating blood sugar, and producing clotting factors.
- [[The Kidneys and Excretory System]] — Two bean-shaped filtration marvels that process 200 liters of blood every day, balancing fluids, blood pressure, and filtering urea waste into urine.
- [[The Endocrine System and Hormones]] — The body’s wireless chemical communication network. Endocrine glands release hormones into the bloodstream to regulate growth, metabolism, stress, and mood.
- [[The Human Eye and Vision]] — The eye is a living biological camera that focuses light onto a light-sensitive screen (the retina), translating photons into electrical nerve impulses for the brain.
- [[The Ear - Hearing and Balance]] — The ear does double duty: translating air vibrations into sound perceptions, and using fluid-filled semicircular canals to tell your brain which way is up.
- [[Skin - The Integumentary Shield]] — Your largest organ, covering roughly 2 square meters. A waterproof, self-healing shield that regulates temperature, senses touch, and prevents dehydration.
- [[The Five Senses and Sensory Receptors]] — How the human brain samples external reality: specialized sensory receptors translate light, vibration, chemical shapes, and temperature into neural code.
- [[The Lymphatic System]] — The drainage and filtration network of the body. Collects excess fluid leaking from tissues, filters it through lymph nodes for bacteria, and returns it to blood.
- [[Blood Types and the ABO System]] — Human blood is categorized into A, B, AB, and O based on inherited carbohydrate marker antigens sitting on the surface of red blood cells.
- [[Stem Cells and Tissue Regeneration]] — The body’s master cells. Undifferentiated cells that have the unique ability to divide indefinitely and transform into any specialized cell type in the body.
- [[Human Reproduction and Development]] — From the fusion of sperm and egg into a single zygote to a fully formed infant in 40 weeks—the biological stages of human embryonic development.
- [[Viruses - The Borderline of Life]] — Viruses are not technically alive. They are microscopic genetic packages (DNA or RNA wrapped in a protein coat) that hijack host cell machinery to reproduce.
- [[Vaccines and Herd Immunity]] — How community vaccination protects entire populations: when enough people are immunized against a contagious disease, chains of transmission break.
- [[Nutrition - Macronutrients and Micronutrients]] — The biochemical fuels that keep human metabolism running: energy-yielding Macronutrients (Carbs, Fats, Proteins) and catalytic Micronutrients (Vitamins, Minerals).
- [[Sleep and Circadian Rhythms]] — Your body is governed by an internal 24-hour biological clock (**Circadian Rhythm**). Sleep is not inactive downtime; it is an active period of brain cleaning and memory consolidation.
- [[The Human Microbiome]] — You are half-microbe. Your body hosts 38 trillion bacterial cells—primarily in the gut—that synthesize vitamins, train your immune system, and influence mental mood via the gut-brain axis.

### ⚛️ Physics, Chemistry & Energy
- [[The Atom and Matter]] — Protons, neutrons, electrons, chemical bonding, and the 4 states of matter.
- [[Newton's Laws of Motion]] — Inertia, F=ma, action-reaction, and the universal laws of gravitation.
- [[The Electromagnetic Spectrum]] — From long radio waves to visible colors, medical X-rays, and cosmic gamma rays.
- [[The Periodic Table of Elements]] — Dmitri Mendeleev’s masterwork. The 118 known chemical elements organized by atomic number and recurring chemical properties into rows (periods) and columns (groups).
- [[Metals, Nonmetals, and Metalloids]] — Elements divide into three broad classes based on electrical conductivity, malleability, and how readily they share or accept electrons.
- [[Acids, Bases, and the pH Scale]] — The chemical measure of hydrogen ion concentration. Acids donate protons ($H^+$), bases accept protons ($OH^-$), and the logarithmic pH scale runs from 0 to 14.
- [[Chemical Reactions and Catalysts]] — Chemical reactions rearrange atoms into new molecular configurations by breaking and forming chemical bonds without ever creating or destroying matter.
- [[States of Matter and Phase Transitions]] — Matter changes its physical state based on temperature and pressure: Melting, Freezing, Vaporization, Condensation, and direct phase leaps like Sublimation.
- [[Thermodynamics - Heat, Work, and Entropy]] — The universal laws governing heat and energy. They state that energy can never be created or destroyed, and that the disorder of the universe (**Entropy**) always increases.
- [[Electricity - Current, Voltage, and Resistance]] — Electricity is the flow of electric charge (electrons). Governed by **Ohm’s Law** ($V = I \times R$), circuits balance electrical pressure, flow rate, and resistance.
- [[Magnetism and Magnetic Fields]] — Magnetism is a fundamental physical force produced by the alignment of electron spins in materials and by moving electric charges.
- [[Electromagnetism - Motors and Generators]] — Electricity and magnetism are two sides of the same coin. An electric current creates a magnetic field, and a moving magnetic field generates electricity (Faraday's Law).
- [[Sound Waves - Frequency, Pitch, and Decibels]] — Sound is a mechanical longitudinal wave of compressed air molecules. Pitch depends on frequency (Hz), while loudness is measured on the logarithmic decibel (dB) scale.
- [[Optics - Reflection, Refraction, and Lenses]] — Optics is the branch of physics studying light behavior: how mirrors reflect rays, how lenses bend light (**Refraction**), and how optical glass powers microscopes and telescopes.
- [[Energy - Kinetic, Potential, and Conservation]] — Energy is the quantitative property transferred to an object to perform work. It exists in kinetic motion or stored potential forms, perfectly conserved in every process.
- [[Radioactivity and Nuclear Fission]] — Unstable heavy atomic nuclei decay naturally by releasing ionizing radiation. When struck by a neutron, Uranium-235 splits in two (**Nuclear Fission**), releasing staggering energy.
- [[Nuclear Fusion - Recreating the Sun on Earth]] — The Holy Grail of clean energy: smashing hydrogen isotopes together at 100 million degrees to create helium, producing zero carbon emissions and zero long-lived radioactive waste.
- [[Quantum Mechanics - The Bizarre Subatomic Realm]] — At the atomic scale, the classical rules of reality break down. Particles exist in clouds of probability, pass through solid walls, and communicate across distance instantaneously.
- [[Einstein's Theory of Special Relativity]] — Albert Einstein’s 1905 breakthrough: the speed of light is the universal cosmic speed limit ($c = 300,000 \text{ km/s}$). As you travel closer to the speed of light, time slows down and mass increases.
- [[Einstein's Theory of General Relativity]] — Gravity is not an invisible pulling force. Massive objects (like planets and stars) curve the 4-dimensional fabric of **Spacetime**; objects simply follow the curves.
- [[Semiconductors and the Microchip Revolution]] — Modern digital civilization is built on **Silicon**. Semiconductors can switch between conducting and insulating electricity, enabling billions of microscopic on/off transistors on a microchip.
- [[Renewable Energy - Solar, Wind, and Hydroelectric]] — Energy derived from natural resources that replenish faster than they are consumed. Clean alternatives to fossil fuels that produce zero direct greenhouse emissions.
- [[Artificial Intelligence and Computing]] — From Alan Turing’s computational theory to modern Deep Learning neural networks. How human beings engineered machines that can process language, recognize patterns, and reason.
