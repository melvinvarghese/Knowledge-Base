# Sanctions and Economic Statecraft

## Overview
Economic statecraft deploys trade tariffs, financial asset freezes, SWIFT banking bans, and semiconductor export controls to coerce rivals or signal moral resolve without firing missiles. Dominating 21st-century geopolitics, sanctions raise hard questions about effectiveness, civilian harm, and dollar weaponization.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Moving away from blunt 1990s comprehensive trade embargoes (which devastated Iraqi civilians while leaving Saddam Hussein's regime intact), modern sanctions rely heavily on 'smart/targeted' measures: freezing oligarchs' overseas yachts and bank accounts, cutting targeted banks off Western capital markets and SWIFT messaging, and imposing high-tech export controls (blocking advanced AI chips and lithography tools).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Empirical studies (Gary Hufbauer, Daniel Drezner) reveal a mixed record: sanctions work best in modest policy disputes between allies or as costly deterrence signals, yet rarely force authoritarian regimes to surrender core survival interests — often triggering 'rally-round-the-flag' nationalism, black-market smuggling, and 'weaponized interdependence' backlashes pushing rivals toward alternative payment rails.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Strategic geoeconomics now pairs targeted sanctions with explicit sunset off-ramps and humanitarian food/medicine carve-outs.

## Key Terminology
- **Targeted (Smart) sanctions**: Asset freezes, travel bans, and sectoral finance limits aimed at regime elites rather than whole populations.
- **Secondary sanctions**: Penalties threatening third-country firms that trade with a sanctioned target, leveraging US market access.
- **Export controls**: National-security restrictions barring transfer of dual-use semiconductors, aerospace, or AI tech.
- **Weaponized interdependence**: Henry Farrell and Abraham Newman's concept of exploiting central hubs (SWIFT, dollar clearing) for leverage.
- **Sanctions evasion**: Bypassing restrictions via shadow tanker fleets, shell companies, and transshipment hubs.
- **De-dollarization**: Rival efforts to settle bilateral trade in non-dollar currencies to insulate against Western financial sanctions.
