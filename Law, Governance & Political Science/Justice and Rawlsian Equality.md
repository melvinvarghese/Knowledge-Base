# Justice and Rawlsian Equality

## Overview
John Rawls's *A Theory of Justice* (1971) single-handedly revived normative political philosophy, asking what rules rational, self-interested people would choose for society behind a 'Veil of Ignorance' hiding their own wealth, race, gender, or talent.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Reviving social contract logic, Rawls imagined contractors in an 'Original Position' behind a Veil of Ignorance: knowing general economics and psychology yet blind to whether they will be born rich or poor, gifted or disabled. Reasoning conservatively against worst-case ruin, Rawls argued contractors would adopt two lexical principles of 'Justice as Fairness': (1) Equal Basic Liberties for all (speech, conscience, vote), taking absolute priority; and (2) the Difference Principle — social and economic inequalities are just *only* if attached to open equal opportunity *and* working to the greatest benefit of the least-advantaged members of society.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Rawls's egalitarianism sparked Robert Nozick's brilliant libertarian counterattack in *Anarchy, State, and Utopia* (1974): any 'patterned' redistribution violates self-ownership ('taxation of earnings is on a par with forced labor'), so only voluntary historical acquisition and transfer matter. Amartya Sen later pushed beyond Rawls's abstract 'ideal theory' toward practical 'Capabilities' reducing concrete injustices.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Rawlsian reasoning directly shapes progressive tax debates, healthcare equity, and global climate justice toward future generations.

## Key Terminology
- **Veil of Ignorance**: Rawls's thought experiment stripping away knowledge of personal class, talent, or identity to ensure impartial rules.
- **Original Position**: Hypothetical fair bargaining setup behind the Veil of Ignorance.
- **Difference Principle**: Rawls's rule permitting economic inequality only when it maximizes benefits for the worst-off.
- **Distributive justice**: Normative principles governing fair allocation of wealth, opportunities, and burdens across society.
- **Primary goods**: All-purpose social resources (rights, income, self-respect) any rational person wants regardless of life plan.
- **Entitlement theory**: Robert Nozick's libertarian alternative basing justice solely on fair acquisition and voluntary transfer.
