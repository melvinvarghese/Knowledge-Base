# Republicanism and Civic Virtue

## Overview
Often confused with modern partisan labels, classical and neo-republicanism form a distinct 2,000-year political tradition defining liberty not merely as non-interference, but as 'non-domination' — living free from anyone's arbitrary unchecked power, sustained by active civic virtue.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Rooted in the Roman Republic (Cicero, Polybius) and Renaissance Florence (Niccolò Machiavelli's *Discourses on Livy*), classical republicanism feared corruption and tyranny above all, advocating mixed government, rotating offices, citizen militias, and active public spirit (*virtù*) putting common good above private luxury. This Atlantic republican current deeply shaped the Dutch Republic, English Commonwealth, and American Founders.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Philosopher Philip Pettit revived 'neo-republicanism' by sharpening its core concept of freedom: whereas classical liberalism defines freedom as *non-interference* (a kindly master who never whips an enslaved person technically doesn't 'interfere'), republicanism defines freedom as *non-domination* — never being vulnerable to another's arbitrary whim. Even unchecked corporate monopolies or abusive spouses violate non-domination unless balanced by enforceable legal protections.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Neo-republicanism inspires modern campaign-finance reform, workplace protections, and civic education cultivating shared public responsibility.

## Key Terminology
- **Non-domination**: Pettit's republican definition of liberty as security against arbitrary, unchecked power by state or private masters.
- **Civic virtue**: Citizen willingness to prioritize shared public good and constitutional defense over narrow self-interest.
- **Mixed government**: Institutional balance combining executive energy, aristocratic/expert senate, and popular assembly.
- **Machiavellian *virtù***: Civic courage, adaptability, and public spirit sustaining a free republic against corruption (*corruzione*).
- **Contestatory democracy**: Republican mechanism enabling citizens to challenge arbitrary executive or administrative decisions.
- **Atlantic republicanism**: Early-modern intellectual current linking Machiavelli and Harrington to American constitutional thought.
