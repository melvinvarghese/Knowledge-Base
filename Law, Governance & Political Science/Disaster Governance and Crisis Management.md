# Disaster Governance and Crisis Management

## Overview
Natural hazards — hurricanes, earthquakes, wildfires, pandemics — only become catastrophic *disasters* when they collide with human vulnerability, weak building codes, and fragmented governance. Disaster governance coordinates mitigation, early warning, emergency response, and equitable recovery across federal, local, and civic tiers.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Emergency management follows four cyclical phases: Mitigation (flood buyouts, seismic building codes — yielding ~$6+ saved per $1 spent), Preparedness (evacuation drills, stockpiles), Response (Incident Command Systems unifying firefighters, military, and hospitals), and Recovery ('building back better' rather than rebuilding in floodplains).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Crucially, disaster sociology reveals disasters are never 'equalizers': poor, elderly, and carless residents suffer disproportionate mortality (as seen in Hurricane Katrina, 2005) and struggle longest with bureaucratic aid forms. Meanwhile, 'moral hazard' in subsidized federal flood insurance can perversely encourage repeated coastal rebuilding unless paired with climate-informed zoning.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
International Sendai Framework standards and community mutual-aid hubs increasingly shift crisis policy from reactive rescue toward proactive climate-resilient design.

## Key Terminology
- **Sendai Framework**: UN global blueprint (2015–2030) shifting disaster policy from post-event relief toward proactive risk reduction.
- **Incident Command System**: Standardized, scalable multi-agency hierarchy coordinating emergency responders at disaster scenes.
- **Build Back Better**: Recovery principle upgrading infrastructure and zoning resilience during post-disaster reconstruction.
- **Social vulnerability**: Pre-existing poverty, age, disability, and transit gaps magnifying disaster harm in marginalized areas.
- **Seismic / Flood mitigation**: Proactive engineering and land-use investments reducing future hazard damage before strikes occur.
- **Whole-of-society resilience**: Integrating municipal agencies, utilities, NGOs, and neighborhood mutual-aid networks.
