# Think Tanks, Interest Groups, and Lobbying

## Overview
Between elections, organized interest groups — business associations, labor unions, environmental coalitions, and policy think tanks — supply legislators with technical research, grassroots mobilization, and campaign funds. Balancing legitimate civic petitioning against dark-money oligarchy is a perennial democratic challenge.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Early pluralist Robert Dahl viewed competing interest groups as healthy checks ensuring diverse minorities win voice on issues they care intensely about. Yet E.E. Schattschneider famously countered: 'The flaw in the pluralist heaven is that the heavenly chorus sings with a strong upper-class accent' — because wealthy corporations outspend citizen groups dramatically on full-time lobbyists, tax lawyers, and strategic think tanks.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Campaign finance rules diverge sharply across democracies: the US Supreme Court (*Citizens United*, 2010) equated independent corporate political spending with protected First Amendment speech, fueling billion-dollar Super PACs, whereas Canada, the UK, and much of Europe cap total election spending, ban corporate donations, and grant free public broadcast airtime.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Mandatory lobbying registries, 'democracy vouchers' (Seattle's public small-donor credits), and real-time donor disclosures aim to amplify ordinary citizens' voices.

## Key Terminology
- **Interest group**: Organized association seeking to influence public policy without running candidates directly on ballots.
- **Lobbying**: Direct advocacy presenting research, constituent pressure, and legislative drafts to policymakers.
- **Think tank**: Policy research institute producing white papers, media commentary, and future cabinet talent.
- **Citizens United (2010)**: Landmark US Supreme Court ruling striking down bans on independent corporate/union election spending.
- **Astroturfing**: Deceptive corporate or political PR campaign disguised as spontaneous grassroots citizen activism.
- **Democracy vouchers**: Publicly funded small-donor credits distributed to residents to finance local candidates competitively.
