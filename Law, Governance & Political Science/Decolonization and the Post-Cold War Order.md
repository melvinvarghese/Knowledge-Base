# Decolonization and the Post-Cold War Order

## Overview
Between 1945 and 1975, anti-colonial movements dismantled European empires, tripling the number of sovereign states from ~51 UN founders to nearly 200 today. Tracing the shift from Cold War bipolarity and the Non-Aligned Movement through brief US unipolarity into today's contested multipolar order explains modern geopolitics.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Exhausted by WWII and confronted by Asian and African independence struggles (India 1947, Indonesia, Ghana 1957, Algeria 1962), European colonial powers retreated rapidly, yet often left arbitrary straight-line borders cutting across ethnic homelands. Seeking autonomy from both Washington and Moscow, newly independent nations led by Nehru, Nasser, Sukarno, and Tito convened the 1955 Bandung Conference and founded the Non-Aligned Movement (NAM), championing anti-imperialism and Global South economic equity.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The 1991 Soviet collapse briefly inaugurated a 'unipolar moment' of US primacy, NATO expansion, and hyper-globalization — prematurely dubbed 'the end of history' by Francis Fukuyama. By the 2010s–2020s, China's rapid industrial rise, Russia's revisionism, and assertive 'middle powers' (India, Brazil, Turkey, Saudi Arabia) ushered in a fragmented multipolar order marked by selective non-alignment.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Understanding this post-colonial arc clarifies why Global South nations frequently resist taking binary sides in great-power rivalries.

## Key Terminology
- **Bandung Conference (1955)**: Historic Asian-African summit laying foundations for the Non-Aligned Movement against Cold War blocs.
- **Non-Aligned Movement (NAM)**: Coalition of postcolonial states refusing formal military alignment with either Cold War superpower.
- **Unipolar moment**: Post-1991 era of unmatched US military, economic, and institutional primacy.
- **End of History thesis**: Fukuyama's 1989 claim that liberal democracy represented humanity's final ideological evolution.
- **Middle powers**: Non-superpower states wielding decisive regional influence and diplomatic hedging power.
- **Global South**: Broad geopolitical designation encompassing developing postcolonial nations across Africa, Asia, and Latin America.
