# Administrative Law and the Regulatory State

## Overview
Modern states govern daily life — clean air, drug safety, aviation, banking, and telecommunications — largely through executive agencies rather than line-by-line parliamentary statutes. Administrative law sets the procedural guardrails, public notice rules, and judicial oversight keeping unelected regulators accountable.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Because legislatures lack technical expertise and bandwidth to specify every chemical threshold or banking ratio, statutes delegate rulemaking authority to specialized agencies. To prevent arbitrary decrees, laws like the US Administrative Procedure Act (1946) require 'notice-and-comment' rulemaking (publishing proposed rules, soliciting public and industry feedback, and responding rationally) alongside formal adjudicative hearings.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Courts police administrative boundaries via doctrines of statutory interpretation and nondelegation — deciding how much discretion legislatures may hand agencies and how strictly judges defer to agency expertise (marked in the US by the 40-year reign and 2024 overturning of *Chevron* deference in *Loper Bright*). Globally, administrative tribunals and ombudsmen provide accessible citizen recourse against bureaucratic error.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Balancing nimble scientific expertise with democratic accountability remains one of contemporary governance's central legal battlegrounds.

## Key Terminology
- **Notice-and-comment rulemaking**: Transparent administrative procedure requiring agencies to publish draft regulations and address public input.
- **Arbitrary and capricious review**: Judicial standard invalidating agency rules lacking reasoned explanation or ignoring key evidence.
- **Chevron deference**: Former US doctrine deferring to reasonable agency interpretations of ambiguous statutes (overturned 2024).
- **Nondelegation doctrine**: Constitutional principle limiting how broadly a legislature can transfer lawmaking power to agencies.
- **Ombudsman**: Independent public official investigating citizen complaints against administrative maladministration.
- **Ultra vires**: Action taken by an agency or official beyond the scope of its legally granted statutory authority.
