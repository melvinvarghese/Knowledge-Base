# Tax Law and Public Finance

## Overview
Tax law funds public infrastructure, defense, courts, and social insurance while actively shaping economic inequality and behavior. Balancing equity, administrative efficiency, and global capital mobility, modern fiscal systems mix progressive income, consumption, corporate, and property taxes.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Public finance distinguishes *vertical equity* (higher earners paying higher shares via progressive marginal brackets) from *horizontal equity* (similarly situated taxpayers bearing equal burdens), alongside *regressive* taxes (flat sales taxes consuming a larger share of low-income budgets). Major revenue engines include personal income and payroll taxes, Value-Added Tax (VAT — a multi-stage consumption tax favored across Europe and Asia for low evasion), corporate income taxes, and local property taxes.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Distinguishing lawful 'tax avoidance' (structuring transactions within statutory loopholes) from illegal 'tax evasion' (concealing income or falsifying records) drives endless statutory complexity. Globalization exacerbated multinational profit-shifting into low-tax havens via transfer pricing and intangible IP licensing — prompting the 2021 OECD/G20 global minimum corporate tax (15%) framework.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Tax law also acts as social policy via 'Pigouvian taxes' on carbon or tobacco and refundable credits like the Earned Income Tax Credit.

## Key Terminology
- **Progressive vs. Regressive tax**: Tax rate rising with income ability to pay versus flat consumption levies hitting lower incomes harder.
- **Value-Added Tax (VAT)**: Incremental consumption tax collected at each stage of production and distribution.
- **Tax avoidance vs. evasion**: Legal minimization using statutory rules versus criminal concealment or fraud.
- **Base Erosion and Profit Shifting**: Multinational corporate strategies shifting taxable profits artificially into low-tax jurisdictions.
- **Pigouvian tax**: Corrective levy priced to internalize negative externalities like carbon emissions or pollution.
- **Marginal vs. Effective tax rate**: Tax rate on the next dollar earned versus total tax paid divided by total income.
