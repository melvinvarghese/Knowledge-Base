# Labor Law and Employment Rights

## Overview
Labor and employment law recognizes that individual workers rarely bargain on equal footing with large employers. Spanning collective union bargaining, minimum-wage floors, workplace safety, and anti-discrimination statutes, labor law balances managerial flexibility against human dignity.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Individual employment law sets baseline non-waivable protections — minimum wages, overtime limits, workplace safety (OSHA), family leave, and bans on race, sex, age, or disability discrimination — contrasting US 'at-will' employment against European and Latin American 'just-cause' dismissal protections. Collective labor law (e.g., US National Labor Relations Act, 1935; International Labour Organization conventions) safeguards workers' rights to unionize, bargain collectively, and strike.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
In the 21st century, fissured workplaces, global supply chains, and algorithmic gig-economy platforms (Uber, DoorDash) blurred employee vs. independent-contractor boundaries — triggering fierce legal battles over misclassification, algorithmic wage-setting, and remote-work 'right to disconnect' laws.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Comparative studies show strong sectoral collective bargaining (common in Nordic countries) compresses wage inequality while maintaining high productivity.

## Key Terminology
- **Collective bargaining**: Good-faith negotiation between employer groups and recognized labor unions over wages and conditions.
- **At-will vs. Just-cause dismissal**: US default permitting termination for any non-illegal reason versus requiring documented cause.
- **Employee vs. Independent contractor**: Crucial legal classification determining eligibility for minimum wage, overtime, and benefits.
- **Freedom of association**: Fundamental ILO human right protecting workers' freedom to form and join unions.
- **Anti-discrimination law**: Statutes prohibiting disparate treatment and unjustified disparate impact in hiring and pay.
- **Right to disconnect**: Statutory protection shielding employees from after-hours work emails and calls.
