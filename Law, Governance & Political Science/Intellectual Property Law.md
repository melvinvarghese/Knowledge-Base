# Intellectual Property Law

## Overview
Intellectual property (IP) law creates temporary exclusive rights over intangible creations of the mind — inventions, literary works, brand symbols, and trade secrets. Designed to solve the 'free-rider' problem of non-rivalrous ideas, IP balances innovation incentives against public access and cumulative creativity.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Four core pillars cover distinct domains: Patents grant ~20-year monopolies over novel, non-obvious, useful inventions in exchange for public technical disclosure; Copyright protects original creative expression fixed in a medium (not raw facts or underlying ideas) for the author's life plus 50–70+ years; Trademarks prevent consumer confusion over brand names and logos indefinitely while used in commerce; and Trade Secrets guard confidential formulas (like Coca-Cola's recipe) against corporate espionage without public registration.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Because ideas build cumulatively on prior work, IP embeds vital public-interest exceptions: Fair Use / Fair Dealing permits commentary, parody, news, and scholarship; patent terms expire into the public domain (enabling cheap generic drugs); and open-source licenses (GPL, MIT, Creative Commons) invert copyright to guarantee shared collaboration.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Today, generative AI training on copyrighted books and artwork, pharmaceutical patent evergreening, and TRIPS global trade rules place IP at the center of digital and biomedical politics.

## Key Terminology
- **Copyright**: Exclusive legal right controlling reproduction, adaptation, and distribution of original expressive works.
- **Patent**: Time-limited exclusive right granted for novel, useful, non-obvious inventions in exchange for public disclosure.
- **Fair use**: US doctrine permitting limited unlicensed use of copyrighted material for criticism, parody, teaching, or transformation.
- **Public domain**: Body of creative works and inventions free of exclusive IP restrictions and open to all.
- **Trademark**: Distinctive sign, name, or logo identifying the commercial source of goods or services.
- **Trade secret**: Confidential business information deriving economic value from secrecy protected against misappropriation.
