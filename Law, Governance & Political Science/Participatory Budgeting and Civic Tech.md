# Participatory Budgeting and Civic Tech

## Overview
Originating in Porto Alegre, Brazil in 1989, Participatory Budgeting (PB) invites ordinary neighborhood residents to propose, deliberate, and vote directly on how to spend a real share of municipal capital budgets — revitalizing local trust from Paris and Madrid to New York and Seoul.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Unlike toothless town-hall complaints, PB transfers binding fiscal authority directly to citizens through annual neighborhood assemblies, volunteer budget delegates refining technical feasibility with city engineers, and public ballots prioritizing parks, street lighting, clinics, or school repairs. In Porto Alegre, PB dramatically redirected sewer and water investments toward neglected low-income favelas while slashing clientelist patronage.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Modern 'civic tech' scales deliberation digitally via open-source platforms like *Decidim* (Barcelona) and *vTaiwan* (pioneered by Audrey Tang using *Pol.is* clustering to surface consensus compromises across polarized online debates rather than amplifying outrage).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
While PB covers modest slices of city budgets (typically 1–10%), empirical evaluations link sustained PB to higher tax compliance, lower infant mortality in Brazilian municipalities, and stronger civic efficacy.

## Key Terminology
- **Participatory Budgeting**: Democratic process enabling residents directly to propose and vote on municipal public spending priorities.
- **vTaiwan / Pol.is**: Taiwanese digital deliberation pipeline visualizing consensus clusters to draft tech regulation.
- **Civic tech**: Software platforms built to deepen citizen participation, transparency, and local problem-solving.
- **Clientelism**: Exchanging targeted local favors or jobs for political votes, countered by transparent PB rules.
- **Equity-weighted allocation**: PB formula directing extra capital funds toward historically underserved neighborhoods.
- **Liquid democracy**: Hybrid voting model allowing citizens either to vote directly or delegate their proxy to trusted domain peers.
