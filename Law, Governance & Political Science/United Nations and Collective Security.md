# United Nations and Collective Security

## Overview
Founded in 1945 from the ashes of World War II to 'save succeeding generations from the scourge of war,' the United Nations embodies global collective security — yet its Security Council veto structure reflects 1945 power politics, creating recurring tension between sovereign paralysis and global legitimacy.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Unlike the toothless League of Nations, the UN Charter (Article 2(4)) outlawed unilateral aggressive war, concentrating binding enforcement power (Chapter VII sanctions and military authorization) in the 15-member UN Security Council. However, granting permanent seats and unilateral vetoes to the five WWII victors (US, UK, France, USSR/Russia, China) meant the Council freezes whenever permanent members clash directly.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Around the Council, the UN system evolved indispensable functional agencies: the General Assembly (one-nation-one-vote moral forum), WHO, UNICEF, UNHCR, UNESCO, and the World Food Programme. When Cold War vetoes blocked Chapter VII wars, Secretary-General Dag Hammarskjöld improvised neutral 'Blue Helmet' peacekeeping ('Chapter Six-and-a-Half').

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Persistent calls to reform Security Council permanent seats (adding India, Brazil, Germany, Japan, and African representation) clash with P5 veto resistance, leaving the UN imperfect yet irreplaceable for humanitarian aid and treaty coordination.

## Key Terminology
- **UN Security Council (UNSC)**: 15-member UN organ holding primary binding authority over international peace, sanctions, and force.
- **P5 Veto power**: Unilateral power of China, France, Russia, UK, and US to block any substantive UNSC resolution.
- **Collective security**: System where all states pledge to unite against any member committing aggression.
- **Chapter VII authority**: UN Charter provisions authorizing binding economic sanctions or military enforcement against threats to peace.
- **UN General Assembly**: Universal 193-member deliberative body passing non-binding resolutions and managing UN budgets.
- **Multilateralism**: Coordinated diplomatic problem solving across three or more states via shared rules.
