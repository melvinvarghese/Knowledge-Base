# Anticorruption and Transparency

## Overview
Corruption — the abuse of entrusted public power for private gain — drains trillions from schools, hospitals, and infrastructure while eroding civic trust. Modern anticorruption architecture pairs sunlit transparency laws and e-procurement portals with independent prosecutors and whistleblower shields.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Political scientists distinguish petty bribery (street-level payments to police or clerks) from 'grand corruption' (rigged defense contracts, resource-extraction kickbacks) and 'state capture' (oligarchs rewriting statutes for private monopolies). Transparency tools like Freedom of Information Acts (FOIA — pioneered by Sweden in 1766), mandatory asset disclosures for officials, beneficial-ownership registries unmasking anonymous shell companies, and open digital public-procurement platforms dramatically shrink hidden discretion.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Yet transparency alone fails without enforcement: independent Anticorruption Agencies (such as Hong Kong's ICAC or Singapore's CPIB), protected investigative journalism, and legal shields encouraging internal whistleblowers (providing anonymous reporting channels and anti-retaliation remedies) turn sunlight into real accountability.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
International conventions like the UN Convention Against Corruption (UNCAC) and US Foreign Corrupt Practices Act (FCPA) criminalize cross-border bribery even when paid abroad.

## Key Terminology
- **Grand vs. Petty corruption**: High-level elite embezzlement and rigged state contracts versus everyday low-level facilitation bribes.
- **Freedom of Information (FOIA)**: Statutory right empowering citizens and journalists to inspect government records unless narrowly exempt.
- **Beneficial ownership registry**: Public or law-enforcement database identifying the real human owners behind anonymous shell corporations.
- **Whistleblower protection**: Legal safeguards shielding insiders who report fraud, safety threats, or abuse from retaliation.
- **E-procurement**: Transparent online government bidding systems reducing human discretion and kickbacks in contracting.
- **FCPA / UK Bribery Act**: Extraterritorial laws prosecuting domestic corporations that bribe foreign public officials.
