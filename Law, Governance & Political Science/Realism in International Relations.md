# Realism in International Relations

## Overview
Realism is the oldest and most influential school of International Relations, viewing world politics as a tragic, competitive struggle for survival and power among self-interested states operating under 'anarchy' — the absence of any overarching world government.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Tracing back to Thucydides's Peloponnesian War ('the strong do what they can and the weak suffer what they must'), Niccolò Machiavelli, and Thomas Hobbes, Classical Realism (Hans Morgenthau, E.H. Carr) rooted conflict in human lust for power. In 1979, Kenneth Waltz launched Structural Realism (Neorealism): regardless of whether leaders are noble or wicked, international *anarchy* forces every state into 'self-help,' creating the 'security dilemma' — one nation's defensive military buildup looks threatening to neighbors, triggering arms races.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Neorealism splits into 'defensive realism' (Waltz: states seek enough security to survive and balance against aggressors) and John Mearsheimer's 'offensive realism' (great powers maximize relative power relentlessly toward regional hegemony, predicting inevitable US-China friction).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
While critics fault realism for overlooking domestic politics, economics, and normative change, realism remains an indispensable sober check on idealistic foreign-policy overreach.

## Key Terminology
- **International anarchy**: Absence of a supreme global sovereign above nation-states, not chaos itself.
- **Security dilemma**: Tragic spiral where defensive measures taken by one state alarm rivals into counter-arming.
- **Self-help system**: Realist condition requiring states to rely ultimately on their own military and alliances for survival.
- **Relative vs. Absolute gains**: Realist focus on who gains *more* from cooperation rather than mutual Absolute benefit.
- **Offensive vs. Defensive realism**: Debate over whether states seek regional hegemony relentlessly or merely sufficient security.
- **Thucydides Trap**: Graham Allison's term for heightened war risk when a rising power threatens an established hegemon.
