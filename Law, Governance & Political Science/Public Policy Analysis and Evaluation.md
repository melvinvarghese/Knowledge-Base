# Public Policy Analysis and Evaluation

## Overview
Public policy analysis applies economics, statistics, and institutional political science to design, cost, and empirically evaluate government programs — asking not merely what laws sound noble on paper, but what actually works on the ground.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Classic policy textbooks map a cyclical process: agenda setting, policy formulation, legislative adoption, implementation, and evaluation. John Kingdon's 'Multiple Streams Framework' explained why good ideas sit ignored for decades until three streams converge during a brief 'policy window': a visible Problem (crisis/data), a ready Policy proposal, and favorable Politics (new election/mood).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
To measure real causal impact rather than wishful correlation, modern evaluation relies heavily on Cost-Benefit Analysis (CBA — discounting future lives, carbon, and dollars) and quasi-experimental econometrics or Randomized Controlled Trials (RCTs, pioneered in development policy by Abhijit Banerjee, Esther Duflo, and Michael Kremer) testing interventions like school deworming or cash transfers before national scaling.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Understanding 'implementation gaps' — why top-down laws fail amid local resistance or underfunded agencies — turns abstract statutes into effective public outcomes.

## Key Terminology
- **Multiple Streams Framework**: Kingdon's model explaining policy breakthroughs when Problem, Policy, and Politics streams align.
- **Policy window**: Brief fleeting opportunity when political crisis or turnover allows major policy adoption.
- **Cost-Benefit Analysis**: Systematic quantitative comparison of monetized social benefits against compliance and fiscal costs.
- **Randomized Controlled Trial (Policy)**: Field experiment randomly assigning communities or households to test causal policy impact.
- **Implementation gap**: Divergence between legislative intent on paper and actual street-level delivery outcomes.
- **Overton window**: Range of policy ideas considered politically mainstream and acceptable in public discourse at a given time.
