# Representation and Electoral Systems

## Overview
How votes translate into legislative seats profoundly shapes party competition, coalition government, and minority representation. Contrasting single-member 'winner-take-all' districts against proportional representation explains why some democracies sustain two giant parties while others host vibrant multi-party coalitions.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Under First-Past-the-Post (FPTP) plurality systems (US, UK, Canada, India), whichever candidate wins the most votes in a geographic district takes the sole seat. Political scientist Maurice Duverger formalized 'Duverger's Law': single-member plurality districts mechanically crush third parties and encourage tactical voting, reliably producing two-party systems — while remaining vulnerable to partisan 'gerrymandering' (drawing crooked district maps to waste rival votes).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Conversely, Proportional Representation (PR — used across most of Europe, Latin America, and New Zealand's mixed-member MMP) allocates legislative seats matching each party's national vote share, yielding multi-party parliaments, higher turnout, and broader women/minority representation, at the cost of post-election coalition bargaining and occasional small kingmaker parties. Ranked-Choice Voting (Instant Runoff) lets voters rank candidates so minor picks don't 'spoil' races.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Electoral engineering is a primary tool of constitutional peacemaking in divided societies.

## Key Terminology
- **First-Past-the-Post (FPTP)**: Single-member district plurality rule awarding the seat solely to the top vote-getter.
- **Proportional Representation (PR)**: Electoral system allocating legislative seats in proportion to each party's total vote share.
- **Duverger's Law**: Sociological rule linking single-member plurality districts to stable two-party competition.
- **Gerrymandering**: Manipulating electoral district boundaries to artificially advantage one party or dilute minority votes.
- **Ranked-Choice Voting**: Ballot system allowing voters to rank preferences, eliminating lowest candidates via instant runoff.
- **Mixed-Member Proportional**: Hybrid electoral system combining local constituency MPs with compensatory proportional party lists.
