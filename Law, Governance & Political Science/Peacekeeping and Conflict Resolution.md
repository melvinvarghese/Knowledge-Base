# Peacekeeping and Conflict Resolution

## Overview
Ending civil wars and keeping fragile ceasefires from reigniting requires patient third-party mediation, impartial peacekeeping troops, and post-war disarmament. Empirical conflict studies prove UN peacekeeping dramatically lowers civilian deaths and prevents war recurrence despite high-profile failures.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Conflict resolution scholars (I. William Zartman) highlight 'ripeness' — belligerents rarely compromise until reaching a 'mutually hurting stalemate' where neither side can win militarily. Once ceasefires are brokered, traditional UN peacekeeping operated under three bedrock principles: host-party consent, strict impartiality, and minimal force solely in self-defense. Modern 'multidimensional' missions add civilian protection mandates, election monitoring, and Disarmament, Demobilization, and Reintegration (DDR) of ex-combatants.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Statistical evaluations by Virginia Page Fortna and Barbara Walter prove that when Blue Helmets deploy after civil wars, peace holds far longer because peacekeepers solve 'credible commitment' fears during vulnerable disarmament phases — even as tragic failures like Srebrenica (1995) and sexual-exploitation scandals forced stricter accountability reforms.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Regional bodies like the African Union increasingly co-lead robust peace-enforcement missions alongside UN logistics.

## Key Terminology
- **Mutually hurting stalemate**: Zartman's condition where warring sides perceive continued fighting as painful and unwinnable, opening negotiation.
- **Three principles of peacekeeping**: Traditional UN bedrock norms: consent of parties, impartiality, and non-use of force except self-defense.
- **DDR**: Disarmament, Demobilization, and Reintegration programs transitioning ex-fighters into civilian economies.
- **Credible commitment problem**: Fear that disarming first leaves a faction vulnerable to betrayal, overcome by third-party peacekeepers.
- **Spoiler problem**: Radical factions using targeted violence to sabotage moderate peace agreements.
- **Peacebuilding**: Long-term post-conflict institutional reconstruction addressing root grievances, courts, and livelihoods.
