# Civil-Military Relations

## Overview
Every state confronts a fundamental paradox: the armed forces must be strong enough to defend the nation from external threats, yet never so autonomous that they overthrow or dictate policy to elected civilian leaders. Civil-military relations studies how democracies maintain durable civilian supremacy.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
In *The Soldier and the State* (1957), Samuel Huntington contrasted 'subjective civilian control' (politicizing the officer corps with partisan loyalists) against 'objective civilian control': cultivating a fiercely professional, politically neutral military focused strictly on combat expertise while deferring unconditionally to elected civilian strategy. Peter Feaver later framed civil-military friction as a continuous 'agency' tug-of-war where civilians monitor military 'working vs. shirking.'

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Where civilian institutions weaken, economic crises deepen, or militaries run sprawling commercial empires (as in Egypt, Pakistan, or Myanmar), coups d'état and 'tutelary regimes' recur. Conversely, mature democracies reinforce civilian supremacy through civilian defense ministries, legislative defense-budget control, non-partisan military academies, and strict norms barring active-duty officers from partisan campaigning.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Modern challenges test these boundaries via domestic disaster/border deployments, retired generals endorsing candidates, and private military contractors.

## Key Terminology
- **Civilian supremacy**: Constitutional principle placing ultimate authority over war, budgets, and defense policy in elected civilians.
- **Objective civilian control**: Huntington's model ensuring military obedience by maximizing non-partisan military professionalism.
- **Coup d'état**: Sudden extra-legal seizure of state power by elements of the military or security apparatus.
- **Tutelary regime**: System where elected civilians govern formally under behind-the-scenes military veto power.
- **Military-industrial complex**: Eisenhower's warning of self-reinforcing lobbying ties among defense contractors, military brass, and legislators.
- **Posse Comitatus**: US legal tradition restricting domestic law-enforcement deployment of federal military troops.
