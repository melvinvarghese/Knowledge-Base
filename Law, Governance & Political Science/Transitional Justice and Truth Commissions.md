# Transitional Justice and Truth Commissions

## Overview
When nations emerge from dictatorship, apartheid, or civil war, they face an agonizing dilemma: how to hold perpetrators accountable for mass atrocities without provoking military coups or tearing fragile peace apart. Transitional justice balances criminal trials, Truth and Reconciliation Commissions, reparations, and institutional reform.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Pioneered at the 1945 Nuremberg Trials establishing individual criminal responsibility for crimes against humanity, transitional justice expanded dramatically during Latin America's 1980s democratic transitions and post-apartheid South Africa. Where outgoing generals still hold guns, South Africa's Truth and Reconciliation Commission (TRC, chaired by Archbishop Desmond Tutu) innovated 'conditional amnesty': perpetrators gained immunity *only* by publicly confessing full political crimes before victims, building an undeniable shared historical record.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Elsewhere, Argentina and Chile gradually overturned military self-amnesties decades later to prosecute junta commanders; Rwanda paired UN tribunals with community *gacaca* courts; and post-communist states used 'lustration' (vetting secret-police collaborators out of public office).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Comprehensive transitional justice pairs victim reparations and memorials with structural police and judicial reform so atrocities never recur.

## Key Terminology
- **Transitional justice**: Judicial and non-judicial mechanisms addressing legacies of mass human rights abuses after conflict or tyranny.
- **Truth and Reconciliation Commission**: Official non-judicial investigative body documenting past atrocities and centering survivor testimony.
- **Conditional amnesty**: Granting immunity from prosecution strictly in exchange for full, truthful public disclosure (South African TRC).
- **Lustration**: Vetting and barring former authoritarian regime officials or secret-police agents from public posts.
- **Reparations**: Material compensation, medical care, and symbolic apologies owed to survivors of state atrocities.
- **Guarantees of non-recurrence**: Institutional reforms of military, police, and courts preventing future systemic abuse.
