# Space Law and Global Commons

## Overview
As private companies launch mega-constellations and nations race back to the Moon, space law adapts Cold War treaties to govern orbital debris, asteroid mining, and celestial commons.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Anchored in the 1967 Outer Space Treaty (OST) — drafted at the height of the US-Soviet space race — outer space is declared the 'province of all mankind,' open to peaceful exploration by all states and strictly immune from national sovereignty or territorial annexation (Article II). The treaty bans nuclear weapons and weapons of mass destruction in orbit, holds launching states strictly liable for damage caused by falling space objects, and mandates avoiding harmful contamination.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
However, 1960s treaties left commercial resource extraction ambiguous: while nations cannot claim the Moon itself, the US-led Artemis Accords and national laws argue private firms may extract and own lunar water ice or asteroid minerals once mined — contested by advocates of the 1979 Moon Agreement's stricter 'common heritage' sharing regime.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Urgent regulatory gaps now center on 'Kessler syndrome' orbital debris collisions, radio-spectrum/orbital-slot crowding by mega-constellations, and anti-satellite missile norms.

## Key Terminology
- **Outer Space Treaty (1967)**: Foundational international space treaty banning national appropriation of celestial bodies and orbital WMD.
- **Non-appropriation principle**: OST Article II ban prohibiting any nation from claiming sovereignty over the Moon or planets.
- **Artemis Accords**: US-led multilateral principles for peaceful lunar exploration, safety zones, and space resource extraction.
- **Kessler syndrome**: Cascading chain reaction of orbital debris collisions threatening to render low-Earth orbit unusable.
- **Liability Convention (1972)**: Treaty holding launching states strictly liable for surface damage caused by falling space debris.
- **Global commons**: Resource domains lying outside national jurisdiction (high seas, Antarctica, outer space, atmosphere).
