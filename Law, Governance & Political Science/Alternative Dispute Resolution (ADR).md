# Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR)

## Overview
Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR) resolves legal conflicts outside formal courtroom trials through negotiation, mediation, and binding arbitration. Prized for speed, confidentiality, and specialized expertise in commercial and international disputes, ADR also sparks criticism when forced onto consumers and workers.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
ADR forms a spectrum of rising third-party control: Negotiation lets parties settle directly; Mediation brings a neutral facilitator to help parties craft mutually acceptable, non-binding solutions (widely successful in family and workplace disputes); and Arbitration empowers a private neutral arbitrator (or panel) to hear evidence and issue a legally binding, court-enforceable award with very narrow appeal grounds.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
In cross-border commerce, the 1958 New York Convention makes international commercial arbitration awards enforceable in 170+ countries — far easier than enforcing foreign national court judgments. Conversely, widespread US corporate use of mandatory pre-dispute arbitration and class-action waivers in consumer and employment fine print drew sharp criticism for privatizing civil justice and shielding systemic misconduct from public precedent.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Modern courts increasingly integrate court-annexed mediation and online dispute resolution (ODR) to clear backlogged dockets affordably.

## Key Terminology
- **Mediation**: Voluntary, confidential dispute resolution guided by a neutral facilitator lacking power to impose outcomes.
- **Arbitration**: Private adjudicative process where a neutral decision-maker issues a binding, enforceable award.
- **New York Convention (1958)**: Global treaty ensuring mutual recognition and enforcement of international arbitral awards.
- **Mandatory arbitration clause**: Standard-form contract term requiring consumers or workers to waive court trials and class actions.
- **BATNA**: Best Alternative to a Negotiated Agreement — key benchmark determining leverage in Fisher & Ury negotiation theory.
- **Online Dispute Resolution (ODR)**: Digital platforms resolving high-volume e-commerce, traffic, or small-claims disputes remotely.
