# Urban Governance and Municipal Law

## Overview
Cities house over half of humanity and generate the vast share of global GDP, yet municipal governments often occupy the weakest legal rung in national constitutions. Urban governance shapes zoning, transit, policing, and housing affordability — balancing local democratic responsiveness against regional coordination.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
In federal and unitary states alike, cities vary sharply in legal autonomy: under 'Dillon's Rule' (common in many US states), municipalities hold only powers explicitly granted by state legislatures, whereas 'Home Rule' charters grant broader local self-governance. Local governments fund schools, water, and streets primarily through property taxes, intergovernmental grants, and municipal bonds.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
One of municipal law's most consequential tools is land-use zoning. Originating in early-20th-century efforts to separate smoky factories from homes, exclusionary single-family zoning later drove suburban sprawl, racial segregation, and severe urban housing shortages — sparking modern 'YIMBY' state-level upzoning reforms near transit corridors.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Innovative city networks (C40 Cities) increasingly bypass national gridlock on climate adaptation, congested-pricing transit, and participatory neighborhood budgeting.

## Key Terminology
- **Dillon's Rule vs. Home Rule**: Narrow interpretation limiting city powers to explicit state grants versus broader local charter autonomy.
- **Exclusionary zoning**: Land-use restrictions (large minimum lots, single-family-only rules) limiting affordable multifamily housing.
- **Inclusionary zoning**: Municipal requirement or incentive reserving a share of new developments for below-market affordable units.
- **Municipal bond**: Debt security issued by cities or local authorities to finance schools, bridges, and water systems.
- **Congestion pricing**: Dynamic tolling charged to vehicles entering dense urban cores to cut traffic and fund public transit.
- **Metropolitan fragmentation**: Split of a single economic metro region across dozens of competing suburban municipal jurisdictions.
