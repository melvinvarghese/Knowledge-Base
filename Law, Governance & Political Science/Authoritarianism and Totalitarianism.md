# Authoritarianism and Totalitarianism

## Overview
Political science sharply distinguishes traditional authoritarian regimes — which monopolize power while leaving private family and religious life partly intact — from 20th-century totalitarianism's total mobilization of society. Modern comparative research explains how dictators survive through elite 'selectorate' payoffs and informational control.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Hannah Arendt's *The Origins of Totalitarianism* (1951) and Juan Linz contrasted ordinary dictatorships against Nazi Germany and Stalinist USSR: totalitarian regimes deploy an all-encompassing utopian ideology, single mass party, pervasive secret-police terror, and state monopoly over media and education to atomize citizens and erase private conscience.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Contemporary comparative politics (Bruce Bueno de Mesquita's Selectorate Theory, Milan Svolik) analyzes modern non-democracies — personalist, military juntas, dominant-party, and monarchies — through twin survival dilemmas: 'authoritarian power-sharing' (buying off a small 'winning coalition' of generals and oligarchs with rents) and 'authoritarian control' (preventing mass protests via targeted censorship and 'informational autocracy' mimicking democratic competence rather than crude terror).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Understanding autocratic resilience clarifies why economic sanctions often backfire by shrinking elite coalitions while independent civil society and elite splits drive democratic transitions.

## Key Terminology
- **Totalitarianism**: Extreme regime type seeking total control over public and private life via mass ideology and secret-police terror.
- **Selectorate Theory**: Formal model showing leaders survive by rewarding a small 'winning coalition' with private rents versus public goods.
- **Informational autocracy**: Sergei Guriev and Daniel Treisman's term for modern dictatorships ruling via media spin and fake competence over mass terror.
- **Coup-proofing**: Dictatorial tactics (fragmenting security agencies, ethnic stacking) to prevent military overthrow at the cost of combat skill.
- **Preference falsification**: Timur Kuran's concept of citizens publicly praising a hated regime out of fear, masking sudden revolutionary cascades.
- **Personalist dictatorship**: Autocracy where power concentrates around one individual leader rather than an institutional party or junta.
