# Liberalism and Individual Rights

## Overview
Liberalism is the foundational political tradition centering individual liberty, moral equality, religious toleration, private property, and constitutional limits on state power. Evolving from 17th-century anti-absolutism through John Stuart Mill's free-speech defense to modern welfare liberalism, it underpins contemporary liberal democracy.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Emerging from Europe's Wars of Religion and Lockean contract theory, classical liberalism advocated religious toleration, free markets (Adam Smith), freedom of the press, and representative government. In *On Liberty* (1859), John Stuart Mill warned against not only state tyranny, but 'the tyranny of the majority' crushing unconventional thinkers — formulating the 'Harm Principle': society may coerce an individual *only* to prevent harm to others, never for their own moral good.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
In the late 19th and 20th centuries, 'modern/social liberalism' (T.H. Green, L.T. Hobhouse, John Maynard Keynes, John Rawls) argued formal legal rights ring hollow amid severe poverty or monopoly power, justifying public education, labor protections, and social safety nets to ensure *positive* effective freedom.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Critics from communitarians, Marxists, and conservatives debate whether liberal individualism erodes civic solidarity — yet liberal rights remain the global benchmark against authoritarian censorship.

## Key Terminology
- **Harm Principle**: J.S. Mill's rule that state coercion is justified solely to prevent tangible harm to others.
- **Negative vs. Positive liberty**: Isaiah Berlin's distinction between freedom *from* interference and effective capacity *to* act.
- **Tyranny of the majority**: Tocqueville and Mill's warning that democratic majorities can oppress unpopular minorities.
- **Value pluralism**: Berlin's recognition that ultimate human goods (liberty vs. equality) can genuinely clash without one single rank.
- **Classical vs. Social liberalism**: Market-centered minimal-state liberalism versus welfare-state liberalism supporting equal opportunity.
- **Marketplace of ideas**: Millian justification for free speech arguing open debate tests and refines truth.
