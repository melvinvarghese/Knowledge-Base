# Refugee and Asylum Law

## Overview
Forged from Europe's post-WWII displacement crisis, the 1951 Refugee Convention defines who qualifies for international sanctuary when a home state persecutes or fails to protect its citizens. Today, record global displacement driven by war, authoritarianism, and climate shocks strains asylum systems worldwide.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Under the 1951 Convention and 1967 Protocol, a legal 'refugee' is someone outside their country owing to a well-founded fear of persecution based on race, religion, nationality, political opinion, or particular social group. Its linchpin rule is *non-refoulement* — forbidding states from returning asylum seekers to territories where their lives or freedom face threat.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
However, 1951 legal categories exclude massive modern realities: Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs who never crossed an international border) and people fleeing generalized gang violence, economic collapse, or rising sea-level climate disasters. Meanwhile, wealthy nations increasingly outsource asylum processing to third countries (EU border compacts, UK/Australian offshore schemes), shifting roughly 75%+ of global refugee hosting onto neighboring developing countries.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
UNHCR and refugee advocates champion expanded labor-mobility pathways, regional responsibility sharing, and legal protection for climate-displaced communities.

## Key Terminology
- **1951 Refugee Convention**: Foundational UN treaty defining refugee status and the binding non-refoulement obligation.
- **Asylum seeker vs. Refugee**: Person awaiting legal status adjudication after requesting protection versus formally recognized refugee.
- **Non-refoulement**: Jus cogens-level bar against expelling refugees back into persecution or torture.
- **Internally Displaced Person (IDP)**: Person forced to flee home by war or disaster who remains within national borders.
- **Safe third country**: Legal doctrine transferring asylum seekers to transit nations deemed safe for claim processing.
- **Climate refugee gap**: Lack of formal 1951 Convention protection for cross-border displacement driven purely by climate change.
