# European Union Governance

## Overview
Born from post-WWII Franco-German coal and steel integration to make European war 'not merely unthinkable, but materially impossible,' the European Union evolved into history's deepest supranational experiment — uniting 27 sovereign nations in a single market, customs union, and shared parliament.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Neither an ordinary treaty club nor a full federal superstate, EU governance blends intergovernmentalism and supranationalism across four core institutions: the European Commission (proposing legislation and enforcing competition/digital law), the Council of the EU and European Council (representing national ministers and prime ministers), the directly elected European Parliament (co-legislating budgets and laws), and the Court of Justice (CJEU), whose landmark *Costa v. ENEL* (1964) established supremacy of EU law over conflicting national statutes.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Deepening via the 1992 Maastricht Treaty and Schengen border-free travel, the EU achieved immense peace and regulatory clout ('Brussels Effect'), yet exposed structural strains during the 2010s Eurozone debt crisis (sharing one currency without a unified fiscal treasury), migration disputes, rule-of-law backsliding in member states, and Britain's 2016 Brexit exit.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Remaining the world's premier laboratory for pooled sovereignty, the EU continually negotiates the boundary between national democracy and continental scale.

## Key Terminology
- **Supranationalism**: Delegating binding legislative or judicial authority above individual nation-states to shared institutions.
- **Single Market (Four Freedoms)**: EU internal market guaranteeing free movement of goods, capital, services, and people.
- **European Commission**: Executive and legislative-initiative arm of the EU enforcing treaties and single-market rules.
- **Eurozone monetary union**: 20 EU states sharing the Euro currency under European Central Bank monetary policy.
- **Schengen Area**: Passport-free travel zone eliminating routine internal border controls across most European countries.
- **Democratic deficit**: Critique that EU decision-making feels distant from national voters compared to domestic parliaments.
