# Privacy, Data Protection, and AI Regulation

## Overview
In an era of ubiquitous smartphones, data brokers, and generative AI, privacy and data-protection law defines legal limits on corporate surveillance and state monitoring — led globally by the European Union's General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and AI Act.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Early privacy law evolved from Samuel Warren and Louis Brandeis's 1890 'right to be let alone' and Fourth Amendment physical search limits toward Fair Information Practice Principles (FIPPs): purpose limitation, data minimization, transparency, and user access. Enforced in 2018, the EU's GDPR transformed global compliance ('the Brussels Effect') by requiring explicit lawful basis (consent or legitimate interest) for processing personal data, granting rights to erasure and portability, and levying fines up to 4% of global revenue.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Following the GDPR, the EU AI Act (2024) pioneered risk-tiered AI governance: banning unacceptable-risk social scoring and untargeted facial scraping, imposing strict audits on high-risk hiring/medical/judicial AI, and mandating transparency for general-purpose models.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Key legal tensions persist around encryption vs. law-enforcement access, cross-border data transfers, and algorithmic discrimination.

## Key Terminology
- **GDPR**: EU's landmark 2018 General Data Protection Regulation establishing strict global data-privacy rights and fines.
- **Brussels Effect**: Anu Bradford's concept describing how EU regulations become de facto global standards for multinational firms.
- **Data minimization**: Legal principle requiring organizations to collect only personal data strictly necessary for a stated purpose.
- **Right to be forgotten**: Data subject's qualified right to demand erasure of outdated or unlawful personal records.
- **EU AI Act**: Risk-based regulatory framework classifying AI applications from banned to high-risk and minimal-risk.
- **Informed consent**: Freely given, specific, unambiguous user agreement to data processing without dark-pattern coercion.
