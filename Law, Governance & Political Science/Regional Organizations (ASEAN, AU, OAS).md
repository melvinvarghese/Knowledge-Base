# Regional Organizations (ASEAN, AU, OAS)

## Overview
Beyond the European Union's deep supranational integration, regional organizations across Asia, Africa, and the Americas — notably ASEAN, the African Union (AU), and the Organization of American States (OAS) — manage local trade, diplomatic norms, and regional security in markedly different ways.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Founded in 1967 amid Southeast Asian Cold War tensions, the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) practices the 'ASEAN Way': informal consensus decision-making, strict non-interference in domestic politics, and quiet face-saving diplomacy — keeping regional peace among ten diverse members while struggling to curb internal crises like Myanmar's military coup.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Conversely, the African Union (reconstituted in 2002 from the OAU) broke sharply with passive non-interference under Article 4(h), authorizing collective intervention in member states during genocide or unconstitutional coups while leading African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA) integration. In the Americas, the OAS combines diplomatic democracy charters with the Inter-American Human Rights system alongside regional rivals like Mercosur.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
As global UN consensus stalls, regional organizations increasingly serve as front-line responders for trade corridors, pandemic alerts, and election monitoring.

## Key Terminology
- **ASEAN Way**: Southeast Asian diplomatic norm prioritizing consensus, quiet consultation, and strict domestic non-interference.
- **African Union (AU)**: 55-member continental body advancing economic integration and peacekeeping with anti-coup/atrocity mandates.
- **AfCFTA**: African Continental Free Trade Area uniting Africa into the world's largest free-trade zone by member count.
- **Inter-American System**: OAS human-rights architecture linking Commission investigation with binding Inter-American Court rulings.
- **Open regionalism**: Trade blocs lowering internal barriers without erecting hostile walls against outside global partners.
- **Subsidiarity (Regional)**: Principle preferring nearby regional mediators to resolve local disputes before global UN escalation.
