# Rule of Law and Constitutionalism

## Overview
The Rule of Law holds that no ruler, official, or citizen stands above impartial, publicly known, and consistently enforced laws. Paired with constitutionalism — binding government power within fundamental written or customary limits — it anchors stable democracy and economic trust.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Tracing back to Aristotle's maxim 'the law should govern' and England's 1215 Magna Carta curbing royal arbitrary seizure, A.V. Dicey defined the Rule of Law through legal supremacy over arbitrary discretion, equality before ordinary courts, and rights grounded in judicial remedies. Lon Fuller later specified eight 'inner morality' criteria of legality: laws must be general, public, prospective (non-retroactive), clear, non-contradictory, possible to obey, stable, and faithfully administered.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Scholars distinguish 'thin' (procedural predictability enabling commerce even in authoritarian states) from 'thick' Rule of Law (incorporating human rights, due process, and democratic accountability). Constitutionalism entrenches higher-order rules requiring supermajorities to amend, preventing transient legislative majorities from abolishing minority rights or cancelling elections.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Empirical indices (World Justice Project) show strong Rule of Law correlates tightly with lower corruption, higher foreign investment, and peaceful political transitions.

## Key Terminology
- **Rule of Law**: Principle that all persons and state officials are accountable to publicly promulgated, equally enforced laws.
- **Constitutionalism**: Doctrine limiting government authority through entrenched fundamental law.
- **Due process**: Procedural fairness guarantees including notice, impartial hearing, and defense rights before depriving liberty or property.
- **Ex post facto prohibition**: Ban on retroactively criminalizing actions that were legal when committed.
- **Thin vs. Thick Rule of Law**: Procedural predictability and stability versus substantive integration of human rights and justice.
- **Magna Carta (1215)**: English charter establishing that even the monarch is subject to law and due process.
