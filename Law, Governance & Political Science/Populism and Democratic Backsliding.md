# Populism and Democratic Backsliding

## Overview
Populism pits a supposedly 'pure, ordinary people' against a 'corrupt elite,' claiming the populist leader alone embodies the true popular will. While populist energy can challenge unresponsive oligarchies, modern political science shows how elected populists frequently drive gradual 'democratic backsliding' from within.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Cas Mudde defines populism as a 'thin-centered ideology' capable of attaching to either left-wing anti-corporate programs or right-wing nativist platforms. Unlike pluralist democracy — which treats political opponents as legitimate rivals — populism frames opposition parties, independent judges, and critical journalists as illegitimate traitors obstructing the people.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Whereas 20th-century democracies died suddenly via military coups, Steven Levitsky and Daniel Ziblatt (*How Democracies Die*, 2018) show 21st-century 'democratic backsliding' unfolds legally step-by-step: capturing constitutional courts and election commissions, starving independent media of state ads, gerrymandering districts, and tilting rules into 'competitive authoritarianism' where elections still occur on a rigged playing field.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Preventing backsliding requires rebuilding 'institutional forbearance' (restraint in using legal technicalities to crush rivals), broad pro-democratic coalitions, and addressing genuine economic and cultural grievances fueling distrust.

## Key Terminology
- **Populism**: Thin ideology dividing society into 'the pure people' versus 'the corrupt elite' and claiming sole moral representation.
- **Democratic backsliding**: Gradual, often legalistic erosion of democratic checks, electoral fairness, and civil liberties by elected leaders.
- **Competitive authoritarianism**: Hybrid regime holding real multiparty elections heavily skewed by state media control, court capture, and harassment.
- **Institutional forbearance**: Unwritten democratic norm of self-restraint against weaponizing legal prerogatives to destroy rivals.
- **Mutual toleration**: Norm recognizing political opponents as legitimate citizens rather than existential enemies.
- **Illiberal democracy**: Regime holding popular elections while dismantling minority rights, independent courts, and press freedom.
