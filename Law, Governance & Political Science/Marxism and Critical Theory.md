# Marxism and Critical Theory

## Overview
Developed by Karl Marx and Friedrich Engels, Marxism analyzes history through class struggle, economic modes of production, and alienation. In the 20th century, Western Marxists and the Frankfurt School pivoted from deterministic economics toward cultural hegemony, mass media, and Critical Theory.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Marx's 'historical materialism' posited that society's economic 'base' (forces and relations of production) shapes its political, legal, and ideological 'superstructure.' Under capitalism, workers separated from owning productive tools suffer alienation and generate 'surplus value' extracted as profit by capitalists — driving recurring crises and eventual proletarian revolution.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
When expected revolutions failed in wealthy Western democracies, Antonio Gramsci introduced 'cultural hegemony': ruling classes maintain consent not merely via police force, but by saturating schools, churches, and media with 'common-sense' ideology. Simultaneously, the Frankfurt School (Max Horkheimer, Theodor Adorno, Herbert Marcuse, later Jürgen Habermas) founded Critical Theory to critique instrumental rationality, authoritarian psychology, and the 'culture industry' pacifying citizens with standardized consumer entertainment.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
While Soviet and Maoist state command economies collapsed or transformed into state capitalism amid horrific authoritarian costs, Marxist class analysis and Critical Theory remain influential tools across sociology, history, and media studies.

## Key Terminology
- **Historical materialism**: Marxist theory viewing material economic production and class conflict as primary engines of historical change.
- **Base and Superstructure**: Metaphor linking economic production relations (base) to law, religion, and culture (superstructure).
- **Surplus value**: Difference between total value produced by labor and wages paid, forming capitalist profit in Marx's theory.
- **Cultural hegemony**: Gramsci's concept of ruling-class dominance maintained via cultural consent rather than overt coercion.
- **Frankfurt School**: Interdisciplinary German neo-Marxist institute pioneering Critical Theory and mass-culture critique.
- **Alienation (Entfremdung)**: Psychological estrangement of workers from their products, labor process, species-being, and peers.
