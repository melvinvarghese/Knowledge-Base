# Tort Law and Civil Liability

## Overview
Tort law addresses non-contractual civil wrongs — physical injuries, medical malpractice, defamation, and defective products — shifting the financial cost of harm from innocent victims onto responsible actors while deterring careless risk-taking.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Torts divide into three tiers of fault: intentional torts (battery, trespass, fraud), negligence (unintentional failure to exercise reasonable care), and strict liability (imposing liability regardless of care for abnormally dangerous activities or defective consumer products). To win a negligence claim, a plaintiff must prove four elements: Duty of care, Breach of reasonable standard, factual and proximate Causation, and actual Damages.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Judge Learned Hand famously formalized breach economically in *United States v. Carroll Towing* (1947): negligence occurs when the burden of precaution (*B*) is less than the probability (*P*) multiplied by injury severity (*L*) — seeding modern Law and Economics. Later product-liability breakthroughs (MacPherson, Traynor) held manufacturers strictly liable for unsafe designs even without direct retail contracts.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Mass tort litigation (asbestos, tobacco, opioids) and class actions act as vital private regulatory complements to government agencies, though critics debate frivolous suits and liability caps.

## Key Terminology
- **Negligence**: Breach of a legal duty of reasonable care causing foreseeable harm to another.
- **Strict liability**: Civil or criminal responsibility imposed regardless of fault or careful precautions (e.g., defective products).
- **Proximate cause**: Legal boundary limiting liability to harms reasonably foreseeable from the wrongful act.
- **Learned Hand formula**: Economic negligence test comparing precaution cost (B) against expected accident loss (P × L).
- **Vicarious liability**: Holding an employer legally responsible for torts committed by employees within the scope of work.
- **Class action**: Procedural lawsuit enabling one or more plaintiffs to sue on behalf of a large group sharing common injuries.
