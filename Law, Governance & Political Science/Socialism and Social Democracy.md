# Socialism and Social Democracy

## Overview
Socialism encompasses political and economic theories advocating social or democratic ownership of key productive resources and reduction of class inequality. Splitting historically from revolutionary Marxism, democratic Social Democracy forged Europe's postwar mixed-economy welfare states through universal suffrage and labor unions.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Early 19th-century 'utopian socialists' (Robert Owen, Charles Fourier) built cooperative industrial communities to counter brutal early factory conditions. Following Marx and Engels's 1848 *Communist Manifesto*, European socialist parties split sharply around 1900–1917: revolutionary communists (Lenin) pursued vanguard party seizure and state command economies, whereas 'revisionists' (Eduard Bernstein) and British Fabians argued universal suffrage allowed workers to win reforms peacefully via parliament.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
After 1945, Social Democratic parties (especially in Sweden, Norway, Germany, and the UK) abandoned wholesale nationalization in favor of Keynesian demand management, strong union collective bargaining, progressive taxation, and universal healthcare, pensions, and higher education — proving capitalist productivity can coexist with low poverty and high social mobility.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Contemporary democratic socialists debate worker-cooperative models, sovereign wealth funds, and green industrial transitions amid aging demographics and globalized capital.

## Key Terminology
- **Social Democracy**: Reformist democratic politics combining regulated market economies with universal welfare states and labor rights.
- **Fabian Society**: Late-19th-century British intellectual movement advocating gradual democratic transition toward socialism.
- **Mixed economy**: Economic system blending private enterprise and markets with public infrastructure and state regulation.
- **Decommodification**: Esping-Andersen's metric of how well citizens can survive (healthcare, old age) independently of market wages.
- **Means of production**: Factories, land, machinery, and capital infrastructure used to produce economic goods.
- **Nordic model**: Scandinavian social-democratic synthesis of open trade, strong unions, and universal public services.
