# State Capacity and Fragile States

## Overview
Before debating how big or small government should be, a state must actually exist functionally: Max Weber defined the state as the human community successfully claiming a monopoly on legitimate physical violence within a territory. 'State capacity' — the ability to tax, keep peace, and deliver services — separates thriving nations from fragile states.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Historian Charles Tilly famously observed 'war made the state, and the state made war': early modern European monarchs built permanent tax bureaucracies, censuses, and courts primarily to finance rival armies, inadvertently forging modern fiscal capacity. Francis Fukuyama distinguishes 'state scope' (how many functions government claims) from 'state strength' (actual ability to enforce rules impartially): many developing states suffer over-ambitious scope paired with weak capacity.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Conversely, 'fragile' or 'failed' states — often trapped by colonial artificial borders, ethnic exclusion, and 'resource curse' oil rents (removing rulers' need to bargain with citizens for taxes) — lose territorial control to militias, fueling civil war and humanitarian collapse.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Building durable state capacity requires broad-based tax-for-services social contracts, impartial courts, and meritocratic civil services rather than top-down foreign military imposition.

## Key Terminology
- **State capacity**: Administrative, fiscal, and coercive ability of a government to implement policies and enforce law across territory.
- **Monopoly on legitimate violence**: Max Weber's defining criterion of sovereign statehood.
- **Resource curse**: Paradox where abundant oil or mineral wealth weakens tax accountability, democracy, and economic diversification.
- **Bellicist theory (Tilly)**: Account explaining modern European state formation through fiscal-military competition.
- **Fiscal contract**: Bargain exchanging citizen tax compliance for representative voice and reliable public services.
- **Fragile state**: State struggling to maintain security, basic infrastructure, and legal legitimacy across its borders.
