# Libertarianism and Minimal State

## Overview
Libertarianism elevates individual self-ownership, voluntary exchange, and strict limits on state coercion above collective social engineering. Spanning F.A. Hayek's epistemic market arguments and Robert Nozick's 'night-watchman state' to anarcho-capitalism, it challenges paternalism and regulatory expansion.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Drawing on John Locke's self-ownership and Adam Smith's spontaneous order, modern libertarians argue individuals own their bodies and labor justly, leaving the state strictly as a 'night-watchman' ('minarchism') protecting citizens against force, theft, and contract fraud — opposing minimum wages, occupational licensing, drug prohibition, and wealth redistribution. More radical Murray Rothbard and David Friedman push into 'anarcho-capitalism,' proposing private arbitration and security agencies replace the state entirely.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Epistemically, F.A. Hayek's *The Road to Serfdom* and 'The Use of Knowledge in Society' (1945) argued central planners can never gather dispersed local tacit knowledge encoded automatically by free-market price signals — warning welfare bureaucracy drifts toward authoritarian control. Critics counter that unchecked markets generate monopolies, unpriced carbon pollution, and extreme inherited inequality destroying genuine equal opportunity.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Libertarian critiques reshaped deregulation, school vouchers, drug decriminalization, and decentralized cryptocurrency movements.

## Key Terminology
- **Self-ownership**: Foundational libertarian principle that each individual holds exclusive moral property rights over their body and labor.
- **Night-watchman state**: Minimal state (minarchism) restricted solely to courts, police, and national defense.
- **Spontaneous order**: Hayek's concept of complex, beneficial social institutions (prices, language, common law) emerging without central design.
- **Non-Aggression Principle**: Libertarian ethical axiom forbidding initiation of physical force or fraud against persons or property.
- **Anarcho-capitalism**: Stateless market theory proposing private firms supply law, courts, and defense.
- **Paternalism**: Restricting an individual's liberty supposedly for their own personal good, strongly opposed by libertarians.
