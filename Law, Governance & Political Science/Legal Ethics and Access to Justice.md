# Legal Ethics and Access to Justice

## Overview
Legal ethics governs lawyers' dual obligations as zealous client advocates and officers of the court, while 'access to justice' confronts the stark reality that legal complexity and high fees lock millions of low- and middle-income citizens out of civil remedies.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Core professional duties include strict confidentiality and attorney-client privilege (enabling candid legal advice without fear of compelled disclosure), avoiding conflicts of interest, competence, and candor toward the tribunal (never fabricating evidence or misleading judges — a duty tested recently by lawyers filing hallucinated AI-generated case citations).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Yet formal legal rights mean little without practical access. Studies consistently show ~70–85% of civil legal needs among low-income households — evictions, domestic violence orders, wage theft, debt collection — go unrepresented by counsel because civil cases lack a constitutional *Gideon* guarantee of free lawyers. Legal aid clinics, pro bono mandates, plain-language court forms, and calibrated non-lawyer legal paraprofessionals seek to close this justice gap.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Balancing strict professional self-regulation with innovative, affordable legal-tech and community navigators defines 21st-century access-to-justice reform.

## Key Terminology
- **Attorney-client privilege**: Evidentiary rule shielding confidential legal-advice communications between client and lawyer from compelled disclosure.
- **Duty of candor**: Ethical obligation prohibiting lawyers from knowingly offering false evidence or misstating law to a court.
- **Conflict of interest**: Ethical bar against representing clients whose interests clash with another client or the lawyer's own interests.
- **Access to justice gap**: Disparity between citizens' civil legal needs and their ability to afford or navigate legal representation.
- **Pro bono publico**: Voluntary free legal services provided by attorneys for the public good.
- **Legal paraprofessionals**: Licensed non-lawyer specialists authorized in select jurisdictions to assist with routine family or housing matters.
