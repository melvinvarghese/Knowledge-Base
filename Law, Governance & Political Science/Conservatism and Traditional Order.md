# Conservatism and Traditional Order

## Overview
Conservatism is a political philosophy emphasizing evolutionary institutional continuity, accumulated cultural wisdom, social order, and skepticism toward sweeping rationalist blueprints. Articulated classically by Edmund Burke in response to the French Revolution, conservatism favors cautious reform over ideological upheaval.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
In *Reflections on the Revolution in France* (1790), Edmund Burke contrasted Britain's gradual 1688 constitutional evolution against France's violent dismantling of monarchy, church, and custom in the name of abstract 'Rights of Man.' Burke argued society is an organic intergenerational partnership among 'those who are living, those who are dead, and those who are to be born' — warning that inherited traditions encode tacit practical wisdom too complex for any single generation's abstract reason to redesign from scratch.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
In the 20th century, Michael Oakeshott defined the conservative disposition as preferring the familiar to the unknown and practical experience over ideological dogma, while modern conservatism branched into distinct national currents: Anglo-American fusionism (blending free-market capitalism with traditional family/religious norms), European Christian Democracy (supporting strong social market safety nets), and nationalist populism.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Conservatism's core insight — Chesterton's Fence ('never tear down a fence until you understand why it was built') — remains a vital check on hubristic social engineering.

## Key Terminology
- **Burkean conservatism**: Tradition emphasizing gradual incremental reform, inherited institutions, and skepticism of utopian blueprints.
- **Chesterton's Fence**: Principle cautioning against abolishing traditional rules before understanding their original protective function.
- **Organic society**: Conservative view of community as an inherited web of interdependent families, churches, and local associations.
- **Little platoons**: Burke's term for local civic, family, and neighborhood institutions anchoring moral life.
- **Christian Democracy**: Post-WWII European center-right tradition uniting traditional social values with social-market welfare.
- **Fusionism**: Mid-20th-century American conservative alliance joining free-market libertarianism with social traditionalism.
