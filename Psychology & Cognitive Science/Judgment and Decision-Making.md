# Judgment and Decision-Making

## Overview
Judgment and decision-making (JDM) studies how individuals evaluate uncertain outcomes, weigh trade-offs over time, and choose among competing options. Moving beyond idealized expected-utility axioms, psychological models like Bounded Rationality and Prospect Theory capture how loss aversion, framing, and emotion govern real-world choice.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Classical economic theory assumed humans maximize Expected Utility rationally across all available options. In the 1950s, Herbert Simon introduced 'bounded rationality,' arguing that limited time, information, and working memory force people to 'satisfice' — choosing the first option meeting an acceptable threshold rather than optimizing exhaustively. Building on this, Kahneman and Tversky's Prospect Theory (1979) demonstrated that people evaluate outcomes relative to a neutral reference point rather than absolute wealth, and feel losses roughly twice as intensely as equivalent gains (loss aversion).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Prospect Theory's S-shaped value function is concave for gains (encouraging risk aversion to lock in sure profits) and convex for losses (encouraging risk-seeking gambles to avoid locking in a sure loss). Consequently, logically identical choices reverse depending on wording — the 'framing effect' (e.g., describing surgery as having a '90% survival rate' vs. a '10% mortality rate'). Temporal choices also exhibit hyperbolic discounting: people disproportionately prefer smaller immediate rewards over larger future ones, yet reverse preferences when both options lie far ahead.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
In neuroscience, Antonio Damasio's Somatic Marker Hypothesis showed that patients with ventromedial prefrontal damage — stripped of emotional gut signals — make catastrophic real-life decisions despite intact IQ, proving emotion is indispensable to rational choice. JDM insights now inform public health messaging, retirement savings defaults, and clinical risk communication.

## Key Terminology
- **Prospect Theory**: Descriptive decision model showing people weigh gains and losses asymmetrically relative to a reference point.
- **Loss aversion**: Psychological asymmetry where the pain of losing a given amount outweighs the pleasure of gaining the same amount.
- **Framing effect**: Reversal of choice preferences when identical outcomes are described in terms of gains versus losses.
- **Bounded rationality**: Herbert Simon's principle that cognitive, informational, and time constraints limit optimization, leading to satisficing.
- **Hyperbolic discounting**: Tendency to devalue future rewards steeply in the near term, fueling procrastination and impulsivity.
- **Somatic Marker Hypothesis**: Damasio's theory that bodily emotional signals guide and accelerate complex real-world decision-making.
