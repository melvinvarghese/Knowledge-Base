# Adult Romantic Attachment and Close Relationships

## Overview
Relationship science applies longitudinal observation, attachment theory, and psychophysiology to understand attraction, intimacy, conflict, and enduring romantic stability. Moving beyond folklore, researchers map how early attachment templates and daily micro-interactions predict whether partnerships thrive or dissolve.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
In 1987, Cindy Hazan and Phillip Shaver extended Bowlby's infant attachment framework to adult romance, organizing relational styles along two continuous dimensions: Attachment Anxiety (hyper-vigilance over abandonment) and Attachment Avoidance (discomfort with intimacy and dependence). Securely attached adults balance autonomy and closeness comfortably, whereas anxious-avoidant 'pursuer-distancer' pairings often lock into self-reinforcing conflict loops. Arthur Aron also demonstrated how mutual escalating self-disclosure rapidly accelerates interpersonal closeness.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Observing hundreds of couples in his 'Love Lab,' John Gottman identified four destructive conflict patterns — the 'Four Horsemen of the Apocalypse': Criticism (attacking character rather than specific behavior), Contempt (superiority, eye-rolling, mockery — the single strongest predictor of divorce), Defensiveness (victim-playing counterattacks), and Stonewalling (physiological flooding leading to emotional shutdown). Stable, happy couples maintain roughly a 5:1 ratio of positive to negative interactions during conflict and consistently 'turn toward' everyday bids for connection.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Relationship science directly underpins Emotionally Focused Therapy (Sue Johnson) and Gottman Method couples counseling, shifting therapeutic work from surface negotiation toward underlying attachment fears and physiological co-regulation.

## Key Terminology
- **Attachment anxiety vs. avoidance**: Two orthogonal dimensions measuring fear of abandonment and discomfort with emotional closeness in adult relationships.
- **Four Horsemen (Gottman)**: Four toxic conflict behaviors predicting relationship breakdown: criticism, contempt, defensiveness, and stonewalling.
- **Bids for connection**: Small verbal or non-verbal gestures seeking attention, affection, or engagement from a partner.
- **Stonewalling**: Shutting down communication and withdrawing emotionally during conflict due to physiological overwhelm.
- **Self-disclosure**: Voluntary sharing of personal feelings and vulnerabilities, driving intimacy when reciprocated.
- **Companionate vs. Passionate love**: Hatfield's distinction between intense erotic longing and deep affectionate trust.
