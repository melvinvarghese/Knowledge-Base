# Attachment Theory and Early Relationships

## Overview
Attachment theory, founded by John Bowlby and empirically tested by Mary Ainsworth, explains how infants form deep emotional bonds with primary caregivers as an evolved survival system. Early caregiving responsiveness shapes 'internal working models' of self-worth and trust that echo across friendships, stress coping, and adult romance.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Challenging both psychoanalytic drive theory and behaviorist 'cupboard love' (the notion infants love mothers merely for milk), Harry Harlow's 1950s rhesus monkey experiments proved infant primates overwhelmingly preferred soft cloth 'mothers' offering contact comfort over wire mothers dispensing milk. Synthesizing ethology and clinical observation, John Bowlby proposed that human infants possess an innate attachment system seeking proximity to a protective caregiver ('safe haven' in distress, 'secure base' for exploration).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Mary Ainsworth operationalized attachment quality in the 1970s through the 'Strange Situation' — observing one-year-olds through brief separations and reunions with mothers. She identified three core patterns: Secure (distressed at departure, quickly comforted on return — ~60% in low-risk samples), Insecure-Avoidant (suppressing distress, ignoring caregiver upon return), and Insecure-Ambivalent/Resistant (clingy yet inconsolable and angry). Later, Mary Main added Disorganized attachment, marked by contradictory, frightened behaviors often tied to caregiver trauma or maltreatment.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Longitudinal studies show secure early attachment predicts stronger emotion regulation, peer competence, and resilience, though attachment styles remain open to 'earned security' through supportive later relationships or therapy. Attachment science reformed hospital pediatric visitation, foster-care stability policies, and couples counseling (Emotionally Focused Therapy).

## Key Terminology
- **Secure base**: Role of a responsive caregiver enabling an infant to explore the environment confidently while knowing safety is nearby.
- **Strange Situation**: Mary Ainsworth's structured laboratory separation-reunion procedure classifying infant attachment patterns.
- **Internal working model**: Mental blueprint of self-worth and others' trustworthiness formed in early caregiving relationships.
- **Contact comfort**: Innate mammalian need for physical warmth and soothing touch demonstrated by Harry Harlow's experiments.
- **Disorganized attachment**: Attachment pattern marked by frozen or contradictory approach-avoidance when a caregiver is simultaneously a source of fear.
- **Earned security**: Adult transition from an insecure childhood attachment history to secure relational functioning via reflection or therapy.
