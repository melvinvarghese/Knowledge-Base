# Anxiety, OCD, and Trauma-Related Disorders

## Overview
Anxiety, obsessive-compulsive, and trauma-related disorders hijack evolutionarily adaptive threat-detection circuits, locking individuals into false alarms, hypervigilance, and self-reinforcing avoidance. Understanding how avoidance maintains fear made these conditions among the most treatable in clinical psychology.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Normal fear responds to immediate danger, whereas anxiety anticipates uncertain future threats. Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD) traps sufferers in chronic, uncontrollable worry; Panic Disorder centers on sudden surges of autonomic terror misinterpreted as impending heart attacks or madness; and Social Anxiety Disorder magnifies fear of humiliation. Across all anxiety conditions, O.H. Mowrer's Two-Factor Theory explains persistence: classical conditioning pairs a cue with fear, and operant negative reinforcement rewards avoidance — bringing instant relief while preventing the brain from learning the cue is actually safe.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder (OCD) involves intrusive, ego-dystonic thoughts or images (obsessions — contamination, harm, symmetry) paired with repetitive mental or physical rituals (compulsions) performed to neutralize dread, linked to hyperactive cortico-striato-thalamo-cortical (CSTC) loops. Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD) arises after life-threatening trauma, manifesting across intrusion symptoms (flashbacks, nightmares), avoidance, negative mood/beliefs, and hyperarousal.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Exposure-based CBT (including ERP for OCD and Prolonged Exposure or EMDR for PTSD) achieves high remission rates by breaking avoidance cycles and updating fear memories. SSRIs provide valuable pharmacological adjuncts when distress blocks engagement.

## Key Terminology
- **Two-Factor Theory (Mowrer)**: Model explaining how fears are acquired via classical conditioning and maintained via operant avoidance.
- **Ego-dystonic**: Intrusive thoughts or impulses experienced as alien, distressing, and contrary to one's values (classic in OCD).
- **Panic attack**: Abrupt surge of intense autonomic fear peaking within minutes with palpitations, dizziness, and dread.
- **Hypervigilance**: Heightened sensory scanning for threat characteristic of PTSD.
- **Safety behaviors**: Subtle avoidance crutches (carrying pills, checking exits) that block full disconfirmation of anxiety.
- **EMDR**: Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing; trauma therapy pairing memory recall with bilateral eye movements.
