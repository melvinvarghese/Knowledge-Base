# Sleep Disorders and Circadian Misalignment

## Overview
Sleep disorders disrupt the timing, continuity, or restorative depth of sleep, impairing daytime cognition, metabolic health, and psychiatric stability. Spanning chronic insomnia, obstructive sleep apnea, and autoimmune narcolepsy, clinical sleep medicine pairs polysomnography with behavioral and physiological therapies.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Chronic Insomnia Disorder — affecting roughly 10% of adults — involves persistent difficulty initiating or maintaining sleep despite adequate opportunity, fueled by conditioned bed-arousal (associating the mattress with frustration and clock-watching). Rather than relying indefinitely on sedative hypnotics (which fragment natural sleep architecture), Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Insomnia (CBT-I) uses stimulus control, temporary sleep restriction, and cognitive reframing to restore natural sleep drive.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Breathing and neurological sleep disorders involve distinct mechanisms. Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA) causes repeated upper-airway collapses during sleep, triggering oxygen drops and micro-arousals that leave sufferers exhausted and raise stroke risk, treated effectively via CPAP positive airway pressure. Narcolepsy Type 1 stems from autoimmune destruction of hypothalamic orexin (hypocretin) neurons stabilizing wake-REM boundaries, causing irresistible daytime sleep attacks and sudden emotional muscle weakness (cataplexy). Circadian misalignment — common in jet lag, rotating shift work, and Delayed Sleep Phase Syndrome — desynchronizes internal SCN clocks from social schedules.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Diagnosed via overnight polysomnography measuring EEG, eye movements, muscle tone, and airflow, treating hidden sleep disorders frequently resolves co-occurring depression, hypertension, and attention deficits.

## Key Terminology
- **Polysomnography**: Comprehensive overnight sleep study recording brainwaves, eye movements, muscle tone, heart rhythm, and breathing.
- **Obstructive Sleep Apnea**: Sleep-disordered breathing marked by repeated upper-airway collapse, oxygen desaturation, and fragmented sleep.
- **Narcolepsy (Orexin deficiency)**: Neurological sleep disorder caused by loss of hypothalamic orexin neurons, producing daytime sleep attacks and cataplexy.
- **Cataplexy**: Sudden bilateral loss of voluntary muscle tone triggered by strong emotion (often laughter) in narcolepsy.
- **Sleep restriction therapy**: Core CBT-I technique temporarily limiting time in bed to consolidate fragmented sleep and rebuild homeostatic drive.
- **Social jetlag**: Chronic circadian mismatch between biological sleep timing on free days and early work/school schedules.
