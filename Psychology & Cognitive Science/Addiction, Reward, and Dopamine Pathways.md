# Addiction, Reward, and Dopamine Pathways

## Overview
Modern addiction science views substance use and behavioral addictions not as simple moral weakness, but as a learned hijacking of midbrain dopamine reward-prediction circuits. As tolerance builds and withdrawal pain deepens, compulsive 'wanting' decouples sharply from conscious 'liking.'

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
All addictive drugs — cocaine, opioids, nicotine, alcohol, amphetamines — converge on the mesolimbic dopamine pathway projecting from the ventral tegmental area (VTA) to the nucleus accumbens. Rather than encoding pure pleasure ('liking,' mediated largely by opioid and endocannabinoid hotspots), Wolfram Schultz proved dopamine neurons fire on 'reward prediction error': surging when rewards exceed expectations and dipping when expected rewards vanish. Drugs trigger artificial dopamine spikes far exceeding natural food or social rewards, stamping drug cues into basal ganglia habit loops.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Kent Berridge and Terry Robinson's Incentive-Sensitization Theory explains the tragic paradox of chronic addiction: repeated drug exposure permanently hypersensitizes dopamine 'wanting' circuits toward paraphernalia, places, and stress triggers, even as hedonic 'liking' plummets due to tolerance. George Koob complements this with 'allostasis': chronic use recruits brain stress systems (CRF, dynorphin), shifting motivation from positive euphoria toward desperate escape from dysphoric withdrawal ('the dark side of addiction').

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Effective treatment pairs pharmacological tools (methadone/buprenorphine for opioids, naltrexone for alcohol) with contingency management, motivational interviewing, and community recovery, recognizing relapse as a chronic medical vulnerability rather than treatment failure.

## Key Terminology
- **Mesolimbic dopamine pathway**: Core midbrain-to-striatum circuit (VTA to nucleus accumbens) driving motivational salience and reinforcement learning.
- **Reward prediction error**: Difference between received and expected reward driving dopamine bursts and synaptic updating.
- **Incentive sensitization**: Berridge and Robinson's theory that addiction hyper-sensitizes cue-triggered 'wanting' independently of hedonic 'liking.'
- **Tolerance and Withdrawal**: Neuroadaptation requiring escalating doses for effect and producing physical/emotional distress upon cessation.
- **Contingency management**: Evidence-based behavioral therapy awarding tangible vouchers for verified drug-free urine screens.
- **Harm reduction**: Pragmatic public health strategy (e.g., naloxone, needle exchange) minimizing fatal overdose and infection without demanding immediate abstinence.
