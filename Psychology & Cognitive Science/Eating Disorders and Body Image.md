# Eating Disorders and Body Image

## Overview
Eating disorders — including Anorexia Nervosa, Bulimia Nervosa, and Binge-Eating Disorder — are severe biopsychosocial illnesses marked by distorted body evaluation, rigid food control or loss of control, and dangerous medical complications. Far from shallow vanity, they carry the highest mortality rate among psychiatric diagnoses.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Anorexia Nervosa combines intense fear of weight gain and distorted body perception with severe energy restriction, triggering life-threatening bradycardia, bone loss, and cognitive starvation effects (famously documented in Ancel Keys's 1944 Minnesota Starvation Experiment, proving starvation itself induces food obsession and rigidity). Bulimia Nervosa pairs recurrent binge episodes with compensatory purging (vomiting, laxatives, extreme exercise), while Binge-Eating Disorder involves distressing loss-of-control eating without purging.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Etiology blends genetic temperament (perfectionism, harm avoidance, and cognitive rigidity), metabolic vulnerability, emotion dysregulation, and cultural thin-ideal or muscularity pressures amplified by algorithm-driven social media. Enhanced CBT (CBT-E, developed by Christopher Fairburn) targets the shared transdiagnostic core: overvaluation of shape and weight, dismantling rigid dietary rules that paradoxically trigger binges.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
For adolescents with anorexia, Family-Based Treatment (FBT / Maudsley method) empowers parents temporarily to restore safe nutrition before handing autonomy back, achieving substantially higher recovery rates than individual insight therapy alone.

## Key Terminology
- **Anorexia Nervosa**: Eating disorder characterized by severe caloric restriction, dangerously low weight, and intense fear of weight gain.
- **Bulimia Nervosa**: Cycle of discrete binge-eating episodes followed by compensatory behaviors like purging or fasting.
- **Binge-Eating Disorder**: Recurrent episodes of rapid, distressing overeating accompanied by loss of control without regular purging.
- **Minnesota Starvation Experiment**: Landmark 1944 study proving prolonged caloric restriction itself generates obsessive food preoccupation and mood decline.
- **CBT-E**: Fairburn's Enhanced Cognitive Behavioral Therapy targeting dietary restraint and overvaluation of body shape.
- **Family-Based Treatment (FBT)**: First-line adolescent anorexia therapy mobilizing parents as active allies in nutritional weight restoration.
