# Auditory Perception and Psychoacoustics

## Overview
Psychoacoustics bridges physical sound waves with subjective auditory experience, explaining how tiny hair cells in the inner ear and cortical networks extract pitch, timbre, spatial location, and musical consonance from overlapping air-pressure vibrations.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Sound waves funneled into the cochlea vibrate the basilar membrane tonotopically — high frequencies peak near the stiff base, low frequencies near the flexible apex — bending stereocilia on hair cells into neural spikes. Pitch perception combines 'place coding' (where the membrane peaks) and 'temporal phase-locking' (firing synchronized to wave cycles). Strikingly, the auditory cortex reconstructs the 'missing fundamental': even if a phone speaker strips away a bass note's lowest frequency, the brain infers the pitch from its upper harmonic spacing.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Spatial localization exploits tiny binaural cues: Interaural Time Differences (microsecond arrival gaps between left and right ears for low frequencies) and Interaural Level Differences (head-shadow volume drops for high frequencies), plus pinna ear-fold filtering for elevation. In music cognition, Albert Bregman's Auditory Scene Analysis parallels visual Gestalt rules, grouping simultaneous harmonics into single instrument timbres and rapid alternating notes into melodic streams — while octave equivalence and simple integer ratios (2:1, 3:2) ground cross-cultural musical consonance.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Psychoacoustics underpins MP3/AAC perceptual audio compression (discarding masked frequencies inaudible to human ears), cochlear implants restoring speech to deaf children, noise-canceling headphones, and immersive spatial audio.

## Key Terminology
- **Tonotopic organization**: Spatial mapping of sound frequencies from high to low along the cochlea and primary auditory cortex.
- **Missing fundamental**: Perceptual phenomenon where the brain infers a fundamental pitch from its upper harmonics alone.
- **Interaural Time Difference (ITD)**: Microsecond delay between a sound reaching the closer versus farther ear, used to localize horizontal direction.
- **Timbre**: Perceptual tone quality distinguishing instruments (e.g., oboe vs. violin) playing the exact same pitch and loudness.
- **Auditory masking**: Psychoacoustic effect where a louder sound renders a nearby quieter frequency inaudible, exploited in MP3 compression.
- **Auditory Scene Analysis**: Bregman's framework explaining how the brain segregates overlapping sound mixtures into distinct sources.
