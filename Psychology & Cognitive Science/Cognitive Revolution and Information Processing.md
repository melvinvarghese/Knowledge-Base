# Cognitive Revolution and Information Processing

## Overview
The Cognitive Revolution of the 1950s–1960s overturned behaviorism's strict focus on observable stimulus-response pairs, restoring mental representations, memory, language, and internal computation to scientific psychology. Modeling the mind as an information-processing system laid the foundation for modern cognitive psychology and cognitive science.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
By the 1940s, radical behaviorism under B.F. Skinner treated the brain as an unobservable 'black box,' insisting psychology study only external behavior. Cracks appeared when Edward Tolman demonstrated 'cognitive maps' in rats navigating mazes without immediate reward, and Alan Turing, Claude Shannon, and Norbert Wiener formalized computation, information theory, and cybernetics. In 1956, a landmark symposium at MIT featured George Miller's 'The Magical Number Seven, Plus or Minus Two' on working memory limits, Allen Newell and Herbert Simon's Logic Theorist AI program, and Noam Chomsky's critique of Skinner's Verbal Behavior — arguing that children's generative grammar cannot arise from reinforcement alone.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Ulric Neisser's 1967 textbook Cognitive Psychology unified perception, pattern recognition, attention, and memory under the information-processing metaphor: sensory input is encoded, transformed, stored, and retrieved much like data in a digital computer. Researchers measured reaction times (Saul Sternberg's memory-scanning paradigm) and mental rotation speeds (Roger Shepard) to infer hidden mental operations quantitatively. Eventually, strict serial computer analogies gave way to parallel distributed processing (connectionism) and embodied cognition, recognizing that human thought relies on neural networks and bodily interaction with the environment.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
The Cognitive Revolution birthed interdisciplinary cognitive science — uniting psychology, linguistics, computer science, neuroscience, and philosophy of mind. Its frameworks directly shaped human-computer interaction (HCI), educational design, cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), and modern artificial intelligence.

## Key Terminology
- **Information-processing model**: Framework comparing human cognition to computational encoding, storage, transformation, and retrieval of mental representations.
- **Cognitive map**: Mental spatial layout of an environment learned without explicit reinforcement, demonstrated by Edward Tolman.
- **Chunking**: George Miller's mechanism for grouping discrete items into meaningful units to expand working memory capacity.
- **Universal Grammar**: Noam Chomsky's theory that humans possess innate structural constraints enabling rapid language acquisition.
- **Mental rotation**: Cognitive operation of turning a visual representation in mind, whose time scales linearly with angular disparity.
- **Connectionism**: Cognitive modeling approach simulating thought as parallel activation across interconnected neuron-like nodes.
