# Sports and Performance Psychology

## Overview
Sports and performance psychology applies cognitive and motivational science to help athletes, musicians, surgeons, and astronauts execute elite skills under high stakes. Investigating why experts 'choke under pressure' versus entering effortless 'flow,' it develops mental routines that stabilize focus and arousal.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Early arousal models like the Yerkes-Dodson inverted-U curve noted that moderate arousal optimizes performance while excessive anxiety degrades precision — especially on complex tasks. Sian Beilock later uncovered two distinct mechanisms behind 'choking under pressure': working memory depletion (anxiety steals mental workspace needed for math or strategy) and 'explicit monitoring' (self-consciously micromanaging automated procedural steps — like thinking about wrist angle during a free throw — which disrupts smooth cerebellar execution).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
To prevent choking and cultivate Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi's flow state, performers train four evidence-based mental skills: attentional cueing (anchoring focus on external targets rather than internal mechanics), pre-performance routines (consistent breathing and physical triggers signaling automatic execution), vivid motor imagery (mentally rehearsing movements activates overlapping premotor and cerebellar circuits), and instructional/motivational self-talk.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Performance psychology now extends well beyond Olympic arenas into surgical simulation, conservatory music anxiety, military aviation, and esports, optimizing resilience under scrutiny.

## Key Terminology
- **Choking under pressure**: Acute drop in well-learned performance beneath high stakes due to anxiety-driven distraction or over-control.
- **Explicit monitoring**: Counterproductive conscious micromanagement of automated procedural skills under pressure.
- **Yerkes-Dodson law**: Empirical relationship showing performance rises with physiological arousal up to an optimal peak before declining.
- **Motor imagery**: Vivid mental rehearsal of physical movement activating neural motor circuits without overt muscle action.
- **Quiet eye**: Joan Vickers's finding that elite performers fixate gaze steadily on a target longer just before initiating a critical action.
- **Pre-performance routine**: Consistent behavioral and cognitive sequence preparing optimal focus prior to execution.
