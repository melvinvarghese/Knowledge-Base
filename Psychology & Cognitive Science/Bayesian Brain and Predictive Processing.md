# Bayesian Brain and Predictive Processing

## Overview
The Bayesian Brain and Predictive Processing frameworks unify perception, action, and learning under a single computational principle: the brain is a hierarchical prediction engine constantly guessing incoming sensory causes and updating internal models only when reality delivers surprising 'prediction errors.'

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Drawing on Thomas Bayes's probability theorem and Helmholtz's unconscious inference, predictive coding models reverse traditional bottom-up flow. Rather than waiting passively for retinal pixels to climb up sensory hierarchies, higher cortical layers continuously send top-down predictions downward; lower layers compare predictions against actual sensory input and pass upward *only* the unexplained residual mismatch (prediction error). When priors are strong and sensory data noisy, perception bends toward expectation — explaining illusions, placebo relief, and phantom sensations.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Karl Friston expanded this into the Free Energy Principle and 'Active Inference': biological agents minimize long-term surprise (free energy) either by updating internal beliefs (perception) *or* by moving muscles to make sensory input match predictions (action). Crucially, 'precision weighting' (modulated by dopamine and acetylcholine) acts as a volume knob deciding whether to trust sensory errors over prior beliefs — offering compelling computational accounts of autism (excessively high sensory precision weighting) and psychosis (aberrant precision triggering runaway delusions).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Predictive processing bridges neuroscience, clinical psychiatry, and machine learning (hierarchical variational autoencoders), though critics note its grand scope risks unfalsifiability unless anchored to concrete circuit tests.

## Key Terminology
- **Predictive coding**: Hierarchical neural architecture transmitting top-down predictions downward and bottom-up prediction errors upward.
- **Bayesian inference**: Updating prior probability beliefs mathematically in light of new sensory likelihood evidence.
- **Prediction error**: Mismatch between expected and actual sensory signals driving learning and perceptual updating.
- **Precision weighting**: Neuromodulatory gain setting how heavily sensory prediction errors override prior expectations.
- **Active inference**: Minimizing prediction error by physically acting on the world to fulfill expected sensory states.
- **Free Energy Principle**: Karl Friston's unified information-theoretic principle proposing living systems resist entropy by bounding sensory surprise.
