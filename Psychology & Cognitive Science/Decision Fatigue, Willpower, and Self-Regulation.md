# Decision Fatigue, Willpower, and Self-Regulation

## Overview
Self-regulation enables humans to override short-term impulses for long-term health, study, and cooperation. Revisiting classic 'marshmallow test' and 'ego depletion' findings, contemporary psychology shows sustainable self-control relies less on white-knuckle willpower than on environmental design and automated habits.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
In the 1960s, Walter Mischel's 'Marshmallow Test' observed preschoolers choosing between one immediate treat or two after waiting 15 minutes — finding that children who distracted themselves or reframed the marshmallow ('cool' cognitive strategies vs. 'hot' focus) showed stronger SAT scores and coping decades later. However, Tyler Watts and Greg Duncan's 2018 replication revealed much of that predictive link reflected family socioeconomic stability and trust that promised future rewards will actually arrive.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Similarly, Roy Baumeister's 'ego depletion' model originally claimed willpower behaves like a tiring muscle fueled by blood glucose. Yet massive multi-lab replications in the 2010s found ego-depletion effects were far smaller and less reliable than advertised, while Michael Inzlicht reframed mental fatigue as shifting *motivation* (from 'have-to' drudgery toward 'want-to' leisure) rather than literal energy exhaustion. Moreover, Brian Galla and Angela Duckworth proved people with high trait self-control actually use *less* effortful willpower daily — because they structure environments and routines into effortless habits.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Modern habit science (Wendy Wood) emphasizes 'implementation intentions' ('If X happens, I will do Y') and friction engineering (making healthy choices easy and distractions inconvenient) over fragile grit.

## Key Terminology
- **Delay of gratification**: Willingness to forego an immediate smaller reward in favor of a larger delayed outcome.
- **Hot vs. Cool systems**: Mischel's distinction between impulsive emotional trigger focus and calm abstract distraction strategies.
- **Ego depletion**: Contested hypothesis that exerting self-control temporarily exhausts a limited willpower resource.
- **Implementation intentions**: Peter Gollwitzer's 'if-then' action plans linking situational cues directly to desired responses.
- **Habit loop**: Automated cue-routine-reward association stored in basal ganglia circuits requiring minimal executive effort.
- **Situational self-control**: Proactively removing temptations from one's environment rather than relying on in-the-moment resistance.
