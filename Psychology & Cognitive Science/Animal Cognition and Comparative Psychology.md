# Animal Cognition and Comparative Psychology

## Overview
Comparative psychology studies perception, memory, tool manufacture, communication, and social intelligence across non-human species. Overturning anthropocentric assumptions, research on corvids, cetaceans, cephalopods, and great apes reveals convergent evolution of complex cognition in radically different brains.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Early comparative work suffered either naive anthropomorphism or rigid Morgan's Canon skepticism. Modern paradigms test ecological intelligence tailored to each species' sensory world. Jane Goodall shattered human uniqueness by documenting wild chimpanzees crafting termite-fishing sticks; New Caledonian crows surpass many primates by bending hooked wire tools and solving multi-step sequential traps; and octopuses demonstrate individual personality, play, and jar-opening problem solving despite decentralized nervous systems evolved independently from vertebrates.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
In social cognition, Western scrub-jays cache food selectively only when competitors aren't watching — and re-hide stashes later if a thief observed them, suggesting rudimentary perspective-taking ('it takes a thief to know a thief'). Mirror self-recognition tests (Gordon Gallup) are passed by great apes, dolphins, elephants, and Eurasian magpies, though critics note scent-oriented animals like dogs fail visual mirrors while passing 'olfactory mirror' tests. Frans de Waal documented empathy, consolation, and inequity aversion ('fairness protests' over cucumber vs. grape rewards) across primates.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Comparative cognition reshapes animal welfare ethics, conservation, and AI benchmarks, proving intelligence takes many non-human forms across avian, mammalian, and cephalopod lineages.

## Key Terminology
- **Morgan's Canon**: Parsimony rule stating animal behavior should not be attributed to higher mental processes if simpler mechanisms suffice.
- **Convergent cognitive evolution**: Independent evolution of complex problem solving and tool use in distant lineages (e.g., corvids and apes).
- **Mirror self-recognition**: Gallup's mark test assessing bodily self-awareness via response to a spot seen in a mirror.
- **Inequity aversion**: Negative behavioral reaction when a social partner receives a superior reward for equal effort.
- **Episodic-like memory**: Animal memory integrating *what*, *where*, and *when* details (demonstrated in food-caching scrub-jays).
- **Umwelt**: Jakob von Uexküll's term for the unique species-specific sensory world experienced by an organism.
