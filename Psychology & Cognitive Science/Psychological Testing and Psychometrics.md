# Psychological Testing and Psychometrics

## Overview
Psychometrics is the statistical and methodological science of measuring invisible psychological constructs — intelligence, personality, depression, and aptitude — with rigor. Without strict standards of reliability, validity, and bias auditing, psychological tests risk mistaking noise or cultural prejudice for objective truth.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Every sound psychological instrument rests on two foundational pillars. Reliability measures consistency: test-retest stability over time, internal consistency across items (Cronbach's alpha), and inter-rater agreement. Validity measures accuracy — whether a test actually captures the intended construct rather than something else: content validity, criterion validity (predicting real-world grades or clinical diagnosis), and construct validity (demonstrating expected convergent and discriminant correlations).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Classical Test Theory models any observed score as True Score plus Random Error. Modern Item Response Theory (IRT) goes further by modeling individual test items along difficulty and discrimination curves — powering Computerized Adaptive Testing (CAT) in exams like the GRE and PROMIS medical scales, which dynamically select the next question based on prior answers to achieve high precision in half the time. Crucially, Differential Item Functioning (DIF) analyses audit whether equally able test-takers from different demographic groups score differently due to hidden cultural wording bias.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Psychometric rigor safeguards clinical neuropsychology, educational placement, and hiring compliance, while exposing popular unvalidated pop-quizzes (like binary corporate personality typologies) as statistically unstable.

## Key Terminology
- **Reliability**: Consistency and repeatability of a psychological measurement across time, items, or raters.
- **Construct validity**: Degree to which a test genuinely measures the theoretical psychological attribute it claims to assess.
- **Cronbach's alpha**: Widely used statistic indexing internal consistency among items within a multi-item scale.
- **Item Response Theory (IRT)**: Probabilistic psychometric framework modeling how latent ability interacts with individual item difficulty and discrimination.
- **Computerized Adaptive Testing**: Dynamic testing algorithm tailoring each item's difficulty in real time to the examinee's estimated ability.
- **Differential Item Functioning**: Statistical test detecting whether an item behaves unfairly across demographic groups matched on underlying ability.
