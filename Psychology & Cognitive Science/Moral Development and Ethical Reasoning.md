# Moral Development and Ethical Reasoning

## Overview
Moral psychology investigates how humans distinguish right from wrong across the lifespan and why moral convictions trigger fierce social conflict. Shifting from Lawrence Kohlberg's rationalist developmental stages to Jonathan Haidt's intuitionist Moral Foundations Theory, the field reveals how gut feelings, cultural norms, and deliberate reasoning intertwine.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Extending Piaget's work on children's game rules, Lawrence Kohlberg (1958) presented moral dilemmas (like the Heinz drug-theft dilemma) to chart six stages across three levels: Preconventional (avoiding punishment and seeking self-interest), Conventional (upholding social approval, duty, and law), and Postconventional (reasoning from universal human rights and social contracts). Carol Gilligan famously critiqued Kohlberg's male-only sample for undervaluing an 'ethics of care' grounded in relational responsibility alongside abstract justice.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Around 2001, Jonathan Haidt challenged pure rationalism with the Social Intuitionist Model: moral judgments arise rapidly from automatic emotional flashes ('the emotional dog and its rational tail'), while conscious reasoning mostly constructs post-hoc justifications — demonstrated by 'moral dumbfounding' when people condemn harmless taboos without logical reasons. Haidt's Moral Foundations Theory identifies evolutionary intuitions — Care/Harm, Fairness/Cheating, Loyalty/Betrayal, Authority/Subversion, Sanctity/Degradation, and Liberty — showing political liberals prioritize Care and Fairness whereas conservatives weight all six more evenly.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Neuroscience experiments using 'trolley problems' (Joshua Greene) show impersonally flipping a switch engages cool utilitarian prefrontal calculation, whereas pushing a person off a footbridge triggers medial emotional alarm. Moral psychology illuminates political polarization, legal culpability, and AI ethical alignment.

## Key Terminology
- **Kohlberg's stages**: Three-level, six-stage model of moral reasoning progressing from self-interest and social conformity to universal principles.
- **Social Intuitionist Model**: Haidt's framework proposing rapid emotional intuitions drive moral judgment while reasoning justifies it afterward.
- **Moral Foundations Theory**: Pluralistic model identifying innate evolutionary moral intuitions (Care, Fairness, Loyalty, Authority, Sanctity, Liberty).
- **Moral dumbfounding**: Stubborn insistence that an act is morally wrong despite inability to articulate a coherent harm-based reason.
- **Ethics of care**: Carol Gilligan's moral framework emphasizing interpersonal responsiveness, empathy, and context over abstract rules.
- **Trolley dilemma**: Philosophical and neurocognitive thought experiment contrasting utilitarian harm-minimization against deontological prohibitions.
