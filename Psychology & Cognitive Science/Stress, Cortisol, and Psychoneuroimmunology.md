# Stress, Cortisol, and Psychoneuroimmunology

## Overview
Health psychology and psychoneuroimmunology (PNI) investigate the bidirectional highways linking psychological stress, nervous system arousal, endocrine hormones, and immune defense. While acute stress mobilizes survival energy, chronic 'allostatic load' accelerates cardiovascular disease, inflammation, and cellular aging.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Walter Cannon first mapped the fast sympathetic-adrenal-medullary ('fight-or-flight') surge of adrenaline, while Hans Selye outlined the General Adaptation Syndrome (alarm, resistance, exhaustion) via the slower HPA axis releasing cortisol. Bruce McEwen later replaced static homeostasis with 'allostasis' (achieving stability through dynamic physiological change) and 'allostatic load' — the cumulative wear-and-tear on blood vessels, metabolism, and hippocampus when stress switches fail to shut off.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Psychoneuroimmunology proved the brain and immune system speak a shared chemical language. Sheldon Cohen's viral-challenge studies demonstrated that chronically stressed adults exposed to rhinovirus catch colds at roughly twice the rate of low-stress peers, while Janice Kiecolt-Glaser showed caregiver burnout slows wound healing and blunts vaccine responses. Crucially, stress appraisal matters: Jeremy Jamieson and Alia Crum showed that reframing pounding heartbeats as performance-enhancing ('stress-is-enhancing' mindset) shifts vascular physiology from constricted threat toward energized challenge.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
PNI integrates behavioral medicine directly into oncology, cardiology, and autoimmune care — proving social support, aerobic exercise, sleep, and cognitive stress management measurably reduce inflammatory markers like IL-6 and CRP.

## Key Terminology
- **Psychoneuroimmunology (PNI)**: Interdisciplinary field studying bidirectional interactions among psychological processes, nervous system, and immune function.
- **Allostatic load**: Cumulative physiological wear and tear caused by repeated or chronic activation of stress-response systems.
- **Fight-or-flight response**: Rapid sympathetic nervous system mobilization elevating heart rate, blood pressure, and glucose for immediate threat.
- **Cortisol**: Primary glucocorticoid stress hormone released by the adrenal cortex under HPA-axis regulation.
- **Inflammatory cytokines**: Immune signaling molecules (e.g., IL-6, TNF-alpha) elevated by chronic psychological stress and linked to depression.
- **Threat vs. Challenge appraisal**: Lazarus's distinction between perceiving stressors as exceeding resources versus manageable opportunities for growth.
