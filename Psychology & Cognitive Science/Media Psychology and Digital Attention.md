# Media Psychology and Digital Attention

## Overview
Media psychology examines how screens, algorithms, video games, and social networks interact with human attention, emotion, social comparison, and memory. As smartphones became ubiquitous external extensions of mind, researchers separate genuine cognitive offloading benefits from variable-ratio distraction and adolescent social vulnerability.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Modern digital platforms engineer engagement using behavioral psychology: infinite scroll removes natural stopping cues, pull-to-refresh mirrors Skinnerian variable-ratio slot machines, and algorithmic feeds prioritize high-arousal moral outrage and social validation ('likes'). Meanwhile, 'transactive memory' and 'cognitive offloading' (Daniel Wegner, Evan Risko) reveal how outsourcing facts to search engines ('Google effect') frees working memory while creating illusions of personal knowledge — mistaking internet access for internal mastery.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Debate rages over smartphone and social media impacts on youth mental health. Researchers like Jean Twenge and Jonathan Haidt correlate rising adolescent depression and self-harm after 2012 with smartphone saturation, sleep displacement, and upward social comparison on visual platforms. Conversely, large-scale longitudinal studies (Amy Orben, Andrew Przybylski) find small average statistical effects overall, emphasizing that *how* media is used — passive doomscrolling vs. active creative connection — and individual vulnerability matter far more than raw screen-time hours.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Media psychology guides digital well-being tools, age-appropriate platform regulation, educational game design, and media literacy curricula helping citizens resist algorithmic manipulation.

## Key Terminology
- **Cognitive offloading**: Using physical tools, notes, or smartphones to reduce internal working-memory and recall demands.
- **Variable-ratio feed**: Algorithmic delivery of unpredictable social rewards (likes, viral clips) driving compulsive checking.
- **Doomscrolling**: Compulsive consumption of distressing news feeds fueled by threat vigilance and algorithmic retention.
- **Social comparison**: Evaluating one's appearance, status, or happiness against curated peers, amplified on visual social media.
- **Transactive memory**: Shared division of knowledge across people or digital tools where one remembers *where* to find facts.
- **Parasocial relationship**: One-sided emotional bond formed by media audiences with celebrities, streamers, or fictional characters.
