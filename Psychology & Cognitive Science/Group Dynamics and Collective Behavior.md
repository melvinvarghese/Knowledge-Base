# Group Dynamics and Collective Behavior

## Overview
Group dynamics studies how individuals behave differently inside teams, crowds, and organizations than when alone. While groups unlock division of labor and collective intelligence, they also suffer predictable traps — including social loafing, group polarization, and catastrophic groupthink.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Norman Triplett observed in 1898 that cyclists raced faster against peers than alone, introducing 'social facilitation': the mere presence of others elevates arousal, boosting performance on simple, well-learned tasks yet impairing performance on novel or complex ones (Robert Zajonc). Conversely, when individual contributions merge into an unmeasured pool — such as group tug-of-war or ungraded group projects — 'social loafing' reduces per-person effort unless individual accountability and task meaningfulness are restored.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Group decision-making introduces deeper structural hazards. Group polarization occurs when like-minded members deliberate and shift toward more extreme positions than their initial average (driven by persuasive arguments and social comparison). Irving Janis coined 'groupthink' after analyzing fiascos like the 1961 Bay of Pigs invasion: highly cohesive, insulated groups led by directive bosses suppress dissent under an illusion of invulnerability and unanimity. Additionally, groups suffer the 'common knowledge effect,' spending far more time discussing information everyone already knows rather than surfacing unique expertise held by one member.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Organizational psychology counters these traps with psychological safety (Amy Edmondson's finding that high-performing teams allow candid error reporting), structured devil's advocacy, anonymous pre-meeting idea generation (nominal group technique), and diverse cognitive perspectives.

## Key Terminology
- **Groupthink**: Irving Janis's term for flawed group decision-making driven by excessive desire for harmony and suppression of dissent.
- **Group polarization**: Tendency for group discussion to amplify members' pre-existing inclinations toward greater extremity.
- **Social loafing**: Reduction in individual effort when working collectively compared to working alone.
- **Social facilitation**: Enhanced performance on simple tasks and impaired performance on complex tasks in the presence of observers.
- **Psychological safety**: Shared belief that a team is safe for interpersonal risk-taking, candid disagreement, and admitting mistakes.
- **Hidden profile**: Decision scenario where critical clues are distributed unevenly among members, often overlooked due to common-knowledge bias.
