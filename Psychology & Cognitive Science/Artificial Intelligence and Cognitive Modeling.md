# Artificial Intelligence and Cognitive Modeling

## Overview
Cognitive modeling builds formal computer simulations to test theories of human memory, language, and reasoning. From early symbolic production systems and connectionist neural networks to modern transformer Large Language Models, AI and cognitive science have coevolved through mutual inspiration and sharp divergence.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
In the 1970s–80s, John Anderson's ACT-R and Allen Newell's SOAR modeled human cognition as symbolic production rules ('IF goal is X, THEN retrieve Y'), accurately predicting reaction times and memory decay curves. In 1986, David Rumelhart and James McClelland's Parallel Distributed Processing (connectionism) shifted modeling toward brain-like layered networks learning distributed statistical weights via backpropagation — igniting decades of debate with Jerry Fodor and Steven Pinker over whether human grammar requires explicit symbolic rules or emerges from sub-symbolic statistics.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Modern deep learning transformers and reinforcement learning (directly inspired by dopamine reward-prediction errors) achieve superhuman pattern recognition and fluent text generation. Yet cognitive scientists (Gary Marcus, Melanie Mitchell, Joshua Tenenbaum) highlight stark remaining gaps: human children acquire concepts from one or two examples using causal 'intuitive physics' and social shared intentionality, whereas LLMs require billions of tokens, lack grounded bodily experience, and struggle with out-of-distribution causal reasoning.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Hybrid neuro-symbolic models and developmental AI now look back to infant curiosity, intrinsic motivation, and active embodiment to build more sample-efficient, trustworthy machine intelligence.

## Key Terminology
- **ACT-R**: John Anderson's hybrid symbolic-subsymbolic cognitive architecture simulating human memory, attention, and reaction times.
- **Connectionism**: Cognitive modeling paradigm representing knowledge as weighted connections across distributed neuron-like units.
- **Symbol grounding problem**: Challenge of explaining how abstract internal symbols acquire real-world meaning without circular definitions.
- **Neuro-symbolic AI**: Hybrid computational approach combining deep neural pattern recognition with explicit structured causal reasoning.
- **Sample efficiency**: Ability of human learners to generalize new concepts from very few examples compared to data-hungry deep networks.
- **Intuitive physics**: Innate or early-developing human causal expectations about object solidity, gravity, and support.
