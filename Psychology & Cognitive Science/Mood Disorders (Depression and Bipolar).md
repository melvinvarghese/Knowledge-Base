# Mood Disorders (Depression and Bipolar)

## Overview
Mood disorders disrupt emotional equilibrium across sustained episodes of crushing sadness, loss of pleasure, or dangerously elevated mania. Encompassing Major Depressive Disorder, Persistent Depressive Disorder, and Bipolar I and II, mood conditions represent leading causes of global disability shaped by neurobiology, stress, and cognitive patterns.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Major Depressive Disorder (MDD) is defined not by ordinary grief, but by at least two weeks of pervasive depressed mood or anhedonia (inability to feel pleasure), accompanied by sleep/appetite shifts, psychomotor slowing, fatigue, worthlessness, or suicidal ideation. Beck's cognitive triad, learned helplessness (Seligman), chronic HPA-axis inflammation, and disrupted hippocampal-prefrontal regulation all contribute beyond simplistic 'serotonin deficiency' myths — explaining why antidepressants alter monoamines within hours yet require weeks of neuroplastic adaptation to relieve symptoms.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Bipolar disorders alternate between depressive lows and episodes of mania (Bipolar I: elevated or irritable mood, grandiosity, racing thoughts, decreased need for sleep, and impulsive high-risk spending or psychosis) or milder hypomania (Bipolar II, frequently misdiagnosed initially as unipolar depression). Bipolar spectrum conditions carry some of the highest heritability estimates in psychiatry (~70–80%) alongside heightened circadian rhythm sensitivity.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Effective care combines pharmacotherapy (SSRIs/SNRIs for severe depression; lithium, valproate, or atypical antipsychotics as mood stabilizers in bipolar) with targeted psychotherapies — CBT, Interpersonal Therapy (IPT), and Social Rhythm Therapy stabilizing sleep-wake clocks.

## Key Terminology
- **Anhedonia**: Marked loss of interest or capacity to experience pleasure from normally rewarding activities.
- **Mania vs. Hypomania**: Severely elevated, energetic mood state causing functional impairment or psychosis versus a milder non-psychotic form.
- **Learned helplessness**: Resignation and passivity acquired after repeated exposure to uncontrollable aversive stress.
- **HPA axis**: Hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal neuroendocrine stress system frequently hyperactive in melancholic depression.
- **Lithium**: Naturally occurring mineral salt serving as the gold-standard mood stabilizer and suicide-preventive agent in Bipolar I.
- **Bipolar II disorder**: Mood disorder characterized by recurring major depressive episodes alternating with hypomania without full mania.
