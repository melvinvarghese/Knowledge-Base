# Personality Psychology and Psychodynamic Views

## Overview
Beyond descriptive trait inventories, personality psychology seeks to explain *why* individuals develop coherent life narratives, unconscious conflicts, and characteristic coping styles. Classical psychodynamic theories — initiated by Sigmund Freud and expanded by Carl Jung, Alfred Adler, and Karen Horney — framed personality as a dynamic negotiation among biological drives, early relationships, and ego defenses.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Freud's structural model divided the psyche into the impulsive pleasure-seeking Id, the reality-mediating Ego, and the moralizing internalized Superego. To shield conscious awareness from anxiety, the Ego deploys unconscious defense mechanisms (systematized by Anna Freud): repression, projection (attributing disowned impulses to others), displacement, rationalization, reaction formation, and mature sublimation. Neo-Freudians quickly rejected Freud's exclusive focus on libido: Carl Jung proposed a 'collective unconscious' of universal archetypes (Shadow, Anima/Animus) and coined introversion/extraversion; Alfred Adler emphasized overcoming inferiority feelings and striving for social belonging.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Modern personality science integrates psychodynamic insight with empirical rigor through Dan McAdams's three-level framework: Level 1 covers dispositional traits (Big Five); Level 2 covers characteristic adaptations (personal goals, values, coping defenses); and Level 3 covers narrative identity — the evolving internalized life story giving unity and purpose to an adult life. Empirical studies confirm unconscious defenses (measured via projective or behavioral markers) and show how 'redemption narratives' predict psychological resilience.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
While strict Freudian metapsychology lacks falsifiability, psychodynamic concepts — implicit motivation, transference, defense mechanisms, and narrative identity — remain central to clinical insight, literary criticism, and contemporary social-cognitive personality research.

## Key Terminology
- **Defense mechanisms**: Unconscious psychological strategies protecting the ego from anxiety, conflict, or threats to self-esteem.
- **Projection**: Defense mechanism attributing one's own unacceptable impulses or feelings onto another person.
- **Sublimation**: Mature defense channeling socially unacceptable drives into constructive artistic, athletic, or intellectual pursuits.
- **Narrative identity**: McAdams's internalized, evolving life story integrating reconstructed past and imagined future into personal meaning.
- **Archetypes (Jung)**: Universal inherited symbolic motifs in Jungian theory such as the Persona, Shadow, and Hero.
- **Locus of control**: Julian Rotter's personality construct measuring whether people attribute outcomes to personal agency (internal) or luck/fate (external).
