# Neurodevelopmental Conditions

## Overview
Neurodevelopmental conditions — including Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD), Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD), and Specific Learning Disorders — arise from early variations in brain connectivity, synaptic maturation, and sensory-cognitive processing. Increasingly framed through the 'neurodiversity' paradigm, science balances supportive accommodation with targeted clinical care.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Autism Spectrum Disorder is characterized by persistent differences in social communication and reciprocity alongside restricted interests, repetitive motor/sensory behaviors, and heightened or dampened sensory sensitivity. Rather than a single deficit, autism reflects heterogeneous genetic architecture interacting with altered local-versus-long-range cortical connectivity. Contemporary researchers highlight the 'double empathy problem' (Damian Milton): communication breakdowns occur mutually between autistic and non-autistic people rather than solely inside autistic minds.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
ADHD affects executive regulation of attention, working memory, and impulse control — not an inability to pay attention, as seen in intense 'hyperfocus' on high-interest tasks, but difficulty *regulating* attention toward low-stimulation demands, tied to delayed prefrontal maturation and altered catecholamine signaling. Dyslexia stems primarily from phonological processing deficits making sound-to-letter decoding effortful despite intact general intelligence.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Evidence-based supports combine stimulant or non-stimulant medications and behavioral coaching for ADHD, structured phonics remediation for dyslexia, and sensory-friendly environmental accommodations and respectful communication supports for autism.

## Key Terminology
- **Neurodiversity**: Paradigm viewing neurological variations like autism and ADHD as natural human diversity alongside disabling barriers.
- **Double empathy problem**: Insight that social misunderstandings between autistic and neurotypical people stem from mutual perspective differences.
- **Executive dysfunction**: Impairment in planning, task initiation, working memory, and impulse regulation common in ADHD.
- **Stimming**: Self-stimulatory repetitive movements or sounds used by autistic individuals to regulate sensory input or emotion.
- **Phonological deficit**: Core difficulty segmenting and manipulating speech sounds underlying developmental dyslexia.
- **Masking (Camouflaging)**: Effortful suppression of natural autistic traits to appear neurotypical, often causing exhaustion and burnout.
