# Cognitive Biases in Science and Medicine

## Overview
Even highly trained physicians and scientists fall prey to systematic cognitive biases under time pressure, fatigue, and ambiguity. Diagnostic error research identifies anchoring, premature closure, and base-rate neglect in clinical reasoning — driving structured debiasing checklists and decision-support tools.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Jerome Groopman and Pat Croskerry cataloged dozens of cognitive traps in emergency and internal medicine. 'Anchoring bias' locks a clinician onto an initial impression (assuming a young patient's chest pain is anxiety or acid reflux), 'premature closure' halts diagnostic search once a plausible label fits, and 'availability bias' over-diagnoses rare diseases seen last week while missing atypical presentations of common illnesses. Similarly, 'diagnostic momentum' passes an unverified ER label unquestioned from shift to shift.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
In scientific research, confirmation bias and 'belief perseverance' lead investigators to scrutinize unexpected results far more harshly than welcome ones. Bayesian base-rate neglect also plagues screening tests: even a 99%-accurate test yields mostly false positives when screening a rare 1-in-1,000 disease, unless clinicians calculate positive predictive value.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
To counter System 1 traps without slowing urgent care, hospitals deploy cognitive 'timeouts' asking 'what else could this be?', structured differential checklists, natural-frequency probability displays, and collaborative AI diagnostic second-opinions.

## Key Terminology
- **Premature closure**: Diagnostic error of accepting an early plausible explanation and halting further differential investigation.
- **Diagnostic momentum**: Tendency for an unverified initial clinical label to stick unchallenged across multiple handoffs.
- **Positive predictive value**: Probability that a positive test result reflects true disease, heavily governed by underlying base-rate prevalence.
- **Natural frequencies**: Expressing probabilities as concrete counts ('10 out of 1,000') rather than percentages to cure base-rate neglect.
- **Cognitive forcing strategy**: Deliberate mental pause or checklist prompting clinicians to generate alternative diagnoses.
- **Sutton's slip**: Focusing exclusively on the obvious textbook explanation while missing rare or atypical presentations.
