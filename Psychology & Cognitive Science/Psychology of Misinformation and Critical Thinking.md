# Psychology of Misinformation and Critical Thinking

## Overview
Why do debunked falsehoods continue shaping judgment even after people accept a correction? Misinformation psychology maps cognitive bottlenecks — from the Continued Influence Effect and illusory truth to partisan echo chambers — and develops scalable 'prebunking' and accuracy-nudge interventions.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Stephan Lewandowsky and Ullrich Ecker documented the 'Continued Influence Effect': once a false causal claim (e.g., 'arson caused the warehouse fire') integrates into a person's mental model, simply stating 'that was false' leaves a causal gap, so people keep relying on the retracted claim unless given a clear alternative explanation. Furthermore, the 'illusory truth effect' ensures mere repetition boosts perceived credibility even for implausible claims — meaning careless debunking that repeats myths prominently can inadvertently reinforce familiarity.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Crucially, Gordon Pennycook and David Rand challenged claims that partisan 'motivated reasoning' explains all fake-news sharing: in many experiments, people share false headlines simply because social media cues prioritize outrage and engagement over truth, distracting System 2 reflection. Simply asking users to rate the accuracy of an unrelated headline ('accuracy nudge') slashes false sharing across political lines.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Effective communication follows the 'Truth Sandwich' (state truth first, briefly warn about the myth, reinforce truth) alongside gamified inoculation tools like *Bad News* teaching citizens to spot emotional manipulation and false dichotomies.

## Key Terminology
- **Continued Influence Effect**: Persistence of retracted misinformation in reasoning when no alternative causal explanation replaces it.
- **Illusory truth effect**: Increased belief in repeated statements solely due to cognitive processing fluency.
- **Accuracy nudge**: Brief prompt reminding social media users to consider truthfulness, significantly reducing misinformation sharing.
- **Truth Sandwich**: Communication framing placing verified fact before and after any brief mention of a debunked myth.
- **Lateral reading**: Fact-checking strategy of opening new tabs to verify a source's reputation externally rather than judging its layout.
- **Motivated reasoning**: Biased evaluation of evidence steered by desired emotional or political conclusions.
