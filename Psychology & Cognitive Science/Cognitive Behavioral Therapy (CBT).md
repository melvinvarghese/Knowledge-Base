# Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT)

## Overview
Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT), pioneered by Aaron Beck and Albert Ellis in the 1960s, is the most extensively researched form of psychotherapy. Grounded in the principle that maladaptive thoughts, core beliefs, and avoidance habits maintain emotional suffering, CBT teaches practical skills to test distorted appraisals and reshape behavior.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Drawing from Stoic philosophy ('people are disturbed not by things, but by their views of things'), Aaron Beck noticed depressed patients experienced rapid streams of 'automatic thoughts' colored by cognitive distortions — catastrophizing, all-or-nothing thinking, mind reading, and personalization — rooted in deeper negative core schemas about self, world, and future. Simultaneously, Albert Ellis developed Rational Emotive Behavior Therapy (REBT), challenging rigid 'musts' and 'shoulds.' Combining cognitive restructuring with behavioral activation and exposure created modern CBT.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
CBT operates collaboratively and time-limitedly via 'collaborative empiricism': therapist and client act as co-scientists testing beliefs against real-world evidence through thought records and behavioral experiments. For anxiety disorders, OCD, and PTSD, Exposure and Response Prevention (ERP) is CBT's sharpest engine — systematically confronting feared cues without safety behaviors so inhibitory learning overwrites catastrophic predictions.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Decades of meta-analyses establish CBT as a first-line treatment for depression, panic disorder, OCD, bulimia, and insomnia (CBT-I). Digital internet-delivered CBT (iCBT) now scales structured modules globally.

## Key Terminology
- **Cognitive distortions**: Systematic habitual thinking errors such as catastrophizing, black-and-white thinking, and overgeneralization.
- **Collaborative empiricism**: Therapeutic partnership testing a client's distressing beliefs against real-world evidence.
- **Behavioral activation**: Structured re-engagement with rewarding, value-aligned activities to break depressive withdrawal.
- **Exposure and Response Prevention (ERP)**: Gold-standard OCD/anxiety intervention confronting triggers while refraining from compulsive rituals.
- **Inhibitory learning**: Modern exposure mechanism teaching new safety associations that outcompete rather than erase old fear memories.
- **Core beliefs (Schemas)**: Deep, unconditional assumptions about self and others shaping moment-to-moment automatic thoughts.
