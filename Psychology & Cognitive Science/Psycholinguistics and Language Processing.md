# Psycholinguistics and Language Processing

## Overview
Psycholinguistics explores the cognitive and neural mechanisms enabling humans to comprehend, produce, and acquire language in real time. Operating effortlessly at 150+ words per minute, language processing integrates acoustic phonemes, lexical meanings, syntactic parsing, and social pragmatics within fractions of a second.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Classic 19th-century lesion studies mapped core language hubs in the left hemisphere: Paul Broca identified the inferior frontal gyrus (Broca's area), damage to which yields effortful, telegraphic speech despite intact comprehension, while Carl Wernicke identified the posterior superior temporal gyrus (Wernicke's area), damage to which produces fluent but meaningless speech ('word salad'). Modern dual-stream neurobiology refines this into a ventral stream mapping sound to meaning and a dorsal stream mapping sound to articulatory motor production.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Real-time comprehension is incremental rather than waiting for a sentence to finish. Readers and listeners immediately predict upcoming syntactic structures, stumbling over 'garden-path sentences' ('The horse raced past the barn fell') when initial expectations must be re-parsed. In reading, eye-tracking studies reveal that the eye jumps in discrete saccades, fixating ~250 ms on content words while skipping short predictable function words. Willem Levelt's speech-production model traces speaking in reverse: from conceptualizing an idea to selecting lemmas (meaning/grammar), retrieving phonological sounds, and executing rapid motor articulation.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Psycholinguistics informs dyslexia remediation (targeting phonological awareness deficits), stroke aphasia speech therapy, bilingual education, and natural-language processing (NLP) — where human predictive parsing echoes next-token prediction in modern large language models, albeit grounded in embodied human experience.

## Key Terminology
- **Broca's aphasia**: Non-fluent, effortful speech disorder with impaired grammar but relatively preserved comprehension, linked to left frontal damage.
- **Wernicke's aphasia**: Fluent speech disorder marked by impaired comprehension and nonsensical word substitutions, linked to left temporal damage.
- **Garden-path sentence**: Grammatically valid sentence that lures readers into an incorrect initial syntactic parse requiring reanalysis.
- **Phoneme vs. Morpheme**: Smallest contrastive sound unit in a language versus smallest unit carrying meaning.
- **Saccade**: Rapid, ballistic eye movement jumping between fixation points during reading.
- **Lexical access**: Mental retrieval of a word's meaning, grammatical role, and pronunciation from the internal mental lexicon.
