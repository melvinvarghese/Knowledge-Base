# Moral Machines and Human-AI Psychology

## Overview
As autonomous algorithms enter medicine, hiring, driving, and companionship, human-AI psychology studies how people trust, anthropomorphize, blame, or defer to machine intelligence. Understanding asymmetric biases — from blind automation bias to harsh algorithm aversion — is vital for safe AI deployment.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Humans naturally anthropomorphize responsive systems: Clifford Nass's 'Computers Are Social Actors' experiments proved users apply politeness norms, gender stereotypes, and reciprocity even to simple text interfaces. With fluent Large Language Models and companion chatbots, users increasingly attribute genuine empathy, sentience, and moral agency to statistical next-token predictors ('the ELIZA effect' at scale).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
When AI makes decisions alongside humans, two opposite errors emerge. 'Automation bias' occurs when fatigued doctors or drivers over-trust algorithmic recommendations without checking raw evidence. Conversely, Berkeley Dietvorst documented 'algorithm aversion': after watching a statistical model make a single visible mistake, people abandon it for inferior human judgment — even though humans err far more often — unless given slight control to tweak the model. Meanwhile, MIT's global Moral Machine study revealed broad cross-cultural consensus (saving more lives) alongside sharp regional variations on age and status trade-offs.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Human-AI psychology guides calibrated trust interfaces (displaying calibrated uncertainty rather than false confidence), human-in-the-loop oversight, and ethical safeguards against manipulative AI companionship.

## Key Terminology
- **ELIZA effect**: Tendency to project human understanding, emotion, and intentionality onto conversational computer programs.
- **Automation bias**: Over-reliance on automated alerts or AI recommendations leading humans to miss contradictory evidence.
- **Algorithm aversion**: Disproportionate loss of trust in predictive algorithms after witnessing a single error compared to human mistakes.
- **Anthropomorphism**: Attributing human-like mental states, motives, and feelings to non-human objects or software.
- **Calibrated trust**: Aligning human reliance on an AI system accurately with its true domain reliability.
- **Uncanny valley (AI/Robotics)**: Eerie revulsion triggered when synthetic faces or voices appear nearly human yet subtly off.
