# Motor Control and Sensorimotor Integration

## Overview
Catching a flying ball or playing a violin concerto requires coordinating hundreds of muscles across noisy neural delays in milliseconds. Motor neuroscience reveals how the motor cortex, basal ganglia, and cerebellum fuse proprioception with internal 'forward models' to execute fluid, adaptive movement.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Because sensory feedback from muscles takes 50–100+ milliseconds to reach the brain, waiting passively for feedback would make fast sports or speech jerky and unstable. Daniel Wolpert and Mitsuo Kawato proved the brain uses internal 'forward models' (primarily computed in the cerebellum): whenever motor cortex issues a movement command, an 'efference copy' predicts the exact sensory outcome milliseconds ahead — explaining why we cannot tickle ourselves (predicted touch is canceled out).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Distinct subcortical loops specialize in motor control: the Basal Ganglia select, initiate, and sequence learned habits via competing direct ('go') and indirect ('no-go') dopamine pathways (disrupted in Parkinson's bradykinesia and Huntington's chorea), while the Cerebellum fine-tunes timing, balance, and error-based motor adaptation. Proprioception — receptors inside muscles and joints reporting limb position — continuously calibrates body schema; patients losing proprioceptive nerves must consciously watch every step to avoid collapsing.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Motor control science guides prosthetic brain-computer interfaces (BCIs translating motor-cortex intent into robotic arms), Parkinson's deep-brain stimulation, and athletic skill acquisition.

## Key Terminology
- **Forward model**: Internal cerebellar simulation predicting the sensory consequences of a planned motor command before feedback arrives.
- **Efference copy**: Internal duplicate of a motor command sent to sensory predictors to cancel self-generated sensation.
- **Proprioception**: Unconscious sensory awareness of limb position, joint angle, and muscle tension.
- **Basal ganglia**: Subcortical nuclei governing action selection, habit formation, and smooth movement initiation.
- **Motor adaptation**: Rapid cerebellar recalibration of movement after external perturbations (e.g., prism goggles or heavy shoes).
- **Fitts's Law**: Mathematical relation predicting movement time from target distance and width, foundational in HCI and ergonomics.
