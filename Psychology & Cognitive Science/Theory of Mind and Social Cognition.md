# Theory of Mind and Social Cognition

## Overview
Theory of Mind (ToM) is the cognitive capacity to attribute beliefs, desires, intentions, and knowledge to oneself and others — recognizing that other people hold mental states different from one's own and sometimes false relative to reality. It forms the bedrock of human empathy, deception, teaching, and cooperative culture.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Premack and Woodruff coined 'Theory of Mind' in 1978 while asking whether chimpanzees understand human goals. In developmental psychology, Heinz Wimmer and Josef Perner created the classic 'false-belief task' (often enacted as the Sally-Anne test): Sally places a marble in a basket and leaves; Anne moves it to a box; where will Sally look upon returning? Most 3-year-olds predict Sally will look in the box (where *they* know it is), whereas by age 4–5 children reliably grasp Sally holds an outdated false belief and will search the basket.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Modern implicit eye-tracking studies (Kristine Onishi and Renée Baillargeon) suggest infants as young as 15 months already anticipate others' false beliefs visually before they can articulate them verbally — pointing to an early intuitive system complemented later by explicit linguistic and executive control. Neuroimaging consistently maps ToM reasoning to the temporoparietal junction (TPJ), medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC), and precuneus. Baron-Cohen, Leslie, and Frith famously linked delayed false-belief performance in many autistic children to distinct social-cognitive processing differences.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Theory of Mind undergirds irony, white lies, courtroom intent judgments, and narrative fiction. Research across cultures shows universal developmental trajectories with subtle timing variations shaped by family mental-state talk and sibling interactions.

## Key Terminology
- **False-belief task**: Experimental test assessing whether a person understands someone can hold an inaccurate belief about reality.
- **Sally-Anne test**: Canonical visual false-belief task tracking where a character will search for a secretly moved object.
- **Temporoparietal junction (TPJ)**: Key cortical region consistently activated when reasoning about others' beliefs, perspectives, and moral intent.
- **Shared intentionality**: Michael Tomasello's concept of uniquely human collaborative focus on joint goals and mutual mental states.
- **Mindreading (mentalizing)**: Everyday cognitive process of inferring others' hidden intentions, feelings, and knowledge.
- **Perspective-taking**: Cognitive ability to adopt another person's spatial, perceptual, or emotional viewpoint.
