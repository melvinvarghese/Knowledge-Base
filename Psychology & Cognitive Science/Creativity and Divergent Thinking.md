# Creativity and Divergent Thinking

## Overview
Creativity is the capacity to generate ideas or artifacts that are both novel and useful. Cognitive science investigates creativity not as mystical inspiration, but as an interplay between divergent idea generation, convergent evaluation, associative memory networks, and dynamic switching between the brain's Default Mode and Executive Control networks.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
In 1950, J.P. Guilford distinguished convergent thinking (narrowing down to a single correct answer, measured by standard IQ tests) from divergent thinking (generating multiple varied solutions to open prompts like 'list unusual uses for a brick,' scored for fluency, flexibility, and originality). Ellis Paul Torrance operationalized these into the Torrance Tests of Creative Thinking. Importantly, creativity and IQ correlate moderately up to around IQ 120 ('threshold hypothesis'), above which personality openness, persistence, and domain mastery matter far more.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Neuroimaging reveals that creative ideation recruits a rare collaboration between ordinarily antagonistic large-scale brain networks: the Default Mode Network (DMN — active during mind-wandering, daydreaming, and spontaneous association), the Executive Control Network (ECN — evaluating relevance and inhibiting clichés), and the Salience Network (switching between them). Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi also described 'flow' — an absorbed state of effortless concentration where skill meets optimal challenge — alongside a systems view emphasizing that creative breakthroughs require both individual novelty and field validation.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Creativity research informs organizational R&D, educational curricula (balancing rote mastery with open-ended play), and computational creativity in AI. Importantly, structured constraints often boost creative output more than blank slates by forcing unexpected associative leaps.

## Key Terminology
- **Divergent vs. Convergent thinking**: Generating many varied possibilities from one prompt versus narrowing toward a single optimal solution.
- **Remote Associates Test**: Mednick's creativity task requiring participants to find a fourth word linking three seemingly unrelated words.
- **Flow state**: Csikszentmihalyi's state of deep, intrinsically rewarding immersion where challenge and skill align.
- **Default Mode Network (DMN)**: Medial brain network active during spontaneous thought, memory retrieval, and imaginative simulation.
- **Threshold hypothesis**: Finding that intelligence supports creativity up to ~IQ 120, beyond which non-IQ factors dominate.
- **Conceptual blending**: Cognitive process combining elements from distinct mental spaces to yield novel metaphors or inventions.
