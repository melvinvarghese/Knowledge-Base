# Embodied and Situated Cognition

## Overview
Embodied, embedded, enacted, and extended ('4E') cognition challenges the classic computer metaphor of mind as disembodied symbol manipulation inside the skull. Instead, it argues cognition emerges dynamically from bodily morphology, sensorimotor action loops, and external tools.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Early cognitive science treated the body merely as an input-output peripheral for a central CPU brain. Embodied theorists (Francisco Varela, Eleanor Rosch, Lawrence Barsalou) countered that abstract concepts remain 'grounded' in sensorimotor simulations: reading action verbs like 'kick' or 'lick' measurably activates leg or tongue motor cortex! Gesture studies (Susan Goldin-Meadow) prove hand gestures do not merely decorate speech — restricting speakers' hands slows word retrieval and mathematical problem solving.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Andy Clark and David Chalmers pushed further with the 'Extended Mind' thesis (1998): if a notebook, smartphone, or abacus functions reliably as part of an ongoing cognitive loop (like Otto with Alzheimer's using his notebook), external artifacts literally constitute part of the mind's machinery ('epistemic actions' like rotating Tetris pieces on-screen rather than mentally). Ecological psychologists following J.J. Gibson similarly emphasize direct perception of bodily 'affordances' without heavy internal reconstruction.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
4E cognition reshaped robotics (moving from fragile top-down planners toward morphological computation in soft robots), mathematics education (using physical manipulatives and gestures), and human-AI collaboration.

## Key Terminology
- **4E Cognition**: Umbrella framework viewing mind as Embodied, Embedded, Enacted, and Extended beyond the skull.
- **Extended Mind thesis**: Clark and Chalmers's claim that external notebooks or devices integrated into cognitive loops form part of mind.
- **Grounded cognition**: Barsalou's view that conceptual thought recruits modal perceptual and motor simulation systems.
- **Epistemic action**: Physical movement (e.g., sorting Scrabble tiles) performed to simplify mental computation rather than change world state.
- **Sensorimotor contingency**: Lawful coupling between bodily movement and resulting sensory changes underlying active perception.
- **Morphological computation**: Offloading control complexity onto physical body geometry and material elasticity (used in robotics).
