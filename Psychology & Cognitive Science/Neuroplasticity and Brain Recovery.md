# Neuroplasticity and Brain Recovery

## Overview
Neuroplasticity is the brain's lifelong capacity to reorganize synaptic strength, dendritic branching, and functional cortical maps in response to experience, learning, or injury. Overturning century-old dogma that adult brains are hardwired after childhood, plasticity research powers stroke recovery and sensory rehabilitation.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Formulated by Donald Hebb in 1949 ('neurons that fire together, wire together'), synaptic plasticity strengthens active pathways via Long-Term Potentiation (LTP) and prunes unused ones via Long-Term Depression (LTD). Michael Merzenich proved adult sensory maps remain strikingly malleable: training monkeys on fine fingertip tasks expanded their corresponding somatosensory cortex representation, while losing a finger caused neighboring digit maps to invade the silent cortex.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Structural plasticity also includes experience-driven myelination (wrapping axons in insulating myelin to speed signal conduction during skill mastery, such as piano practice) and limited adult neurogenesis — birth of new neurons primarily within the hippocampal dentate gyrus, boosted by aerobic exercise and depressed by chronic stress. Following stroke, surviving peri-infarct tissue and opposite-hemisphere networks can gradually assume lost speech or motor functions.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Clinical translation produced Constraint-Induced Movement Therapy (Edward Taub: binding the healthy arm to force intensive retraining of a stroke-weakened limb), cochlear implants, and bilingual cognitive reserve, proving targeted effort reshapes neural hardware across the lifespan.

## Key Terminology
- **Hebbian learning**: Principle that repeated co-activation of pre- and postsynaptic neurons strengthens their synaptic connection.
- **Cortical remapping**: Reorganization of sensory or motor cortical territory following amputation, sensory loss, or intensive skill training.
- **Constraint-Induced Movement Therapy**: Stroke rehabilitation binding the unaffected limb to overcome 'learned non-use' and drive motor-cortex recovery.
- **Myelination**: Wrapping axons in glial myelin sheaths, accelerating conduction velocity across practiced neural circuits.
- **Adult neurogenesis**: Generation of functional new neurons in the adult hippocampal dentate gyrus.
- **Critical vs. Sensitive period**: Strict developmental window required for normal wiring versus broader eras of heightened plasticity.
