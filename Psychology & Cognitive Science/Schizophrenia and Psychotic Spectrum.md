# Schizophrenia and Psychotic Spectrum

## Overview
Schizophrenia is a complex neurodevelopmental disorder affecting roughly 1% of the global population, characterized by disruptions in reality testing, thought organization, motivation, and social cognition. Moving past stigmatizing myths, modern psychiatry integrates polygenic risk, synaptic pruning, and aberrant dopamine salience.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Clinically, symptoms cluster into three domains: Positive symptoms (excesses distorting reality, including auditory hallucinations and fixed false beliefs/delusions), Negative symptoms (deficits in normal functioning such as flat affect, avolition, anhedonia, and poverty of speech), and Cognitive symptoms (impairments in working memory, attention, and processing speed — often the strongest predictors of long-term disability). Onset typically emerges in late adolescence or early adulthood, often preceded by a subtle prodromal decline.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Neurobiologically, early observations that antipsychotic drugs block D2 dopamine receptors led to the Dopamine Hypothesis: elevated striatal dopamine creates 'aberrant salience,' causing neutral background sights or whispers to feel urgently meaningful, which cortex weaves into paranoid delusions. Complementing dopamine, NMDA glutamate hypofunction and excessive adolescent synaptic pruning (linked to C4 immune gene variants) explain negative and cognitive deficits alongside enlarged cerebral ventricles.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Early-intervention 'coordinated specialty care' combining low-dose second-generation antipsychotics, family psychoeducation, supported employment, and CBT for psychosis dramatically improves recovery, helping many individuals live independent, meaningful lives.

## Key Terminology
- **Positive vs. Negative symptoms**: Added perceptual/thought distortions (hallucinations, delusions) versus diminished emotional expression and motivation.
- **Aberrant salience**: Dopamine-driven attribution of urgent personal significance to random neutral stimuli, seeding delusions.
- **Delusion**: Fixed false belief maintained despite clear contradictory evidence and cultural norms.
- **Hallucination**: Vivid sensory perception (most commonly auditory voices) occurring without an external stimulus.
- **Avolition**: Severe lack of drive or initiation to pursue goal-directed everyday activities.
- **Prodrome**: Early subtle period of social withdrawal and perceptual oddity preceding a first full psychotic episode.
