# False Memories and Eyewitness Reliability

## Overview
Human memory is not a video recorder replaying intact files, but a reconstructive process vulnerable to suggestion, post-event misinformation, and schema distortion. Pioneered by Elizabeth Loftus, research on false memories has exposed severe flaws in eyewitness testimony and transformed legal standards.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
In the 1970s, Elizabeth Loftus demonstrated the 'misinformation effect': participants watching a car crash video estimated higher vehicle speeds when asked how fast the cars were going when they 'smashed' rather than 'hit' each other, and weeks later falsely remembered seeing broken glass that never existed. Subsequent studies using the 'Lost in the Mall' paradigm showed roughly a quarter of participants could be induced to recall entirely fabricated childhood events when suggested by trusted relatives.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Laboratory paradigms like Deese-Roediger-McDermott (DRM) reveal how routine semantic association generates false recall effortlessly: after hearing 'bed, rest, awake, tired, dream,' participants confidently insist they heard 'sleep.' At the neural level, constructive memory shares overlapping hippocampal and prefrontal networks with future imagination (Daniel Schacter) — memory evolved to simulate future scenarios flexibly rather than archive verbatim transcripts. Source-monitoring errors occur when imagined details, external suggestions, or media images are misattributed to direct personal experience.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
False-memory science sparked sweeping reforms in criminal justice: DNA exonerations (via the Innocence Project) revealed mistaken eyewitness identification as the leading factor in wrongful convictions. Police departments now adopt double-blind sequential lineups, non-leading cognitive interviews, and judicial instructions warning juries that witness confidence poorly predicts accuracy.

## Key Terminology
- **Misinformation effect**: Distortion of an original memory caused by misleading information encountered after the event.
- **Source-monitoring error**: Mistaking the origin of a memory — such as confusing an imagined scenario or heard story for a lived event.
- **DRM paradigm**: Word-list experiment reliably inducing false recall of an unpresented, semantically linked 'lure' word.
- **Constructive memory**: Principle that remembering actively rebuilds past events from fragments, schemas, and present context.
- **Flashbulb memory**: Vivid, confident memory of learning shocking public news, which nevertheless degrades and distorts over time.
- **Confabulation**: Unintentional fabrication of plausible memories to fill gaps without intent to deceive, common in certain brain injuries.
