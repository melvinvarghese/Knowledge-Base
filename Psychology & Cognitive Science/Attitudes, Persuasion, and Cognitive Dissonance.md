# Attitudes, Persuasion, and Cognitive Dissonance

## Overview
Attitude and persuasion research explains why people change their minds — or fiercely double down when confronted with contradicting facts. Leon Festinger's Cognitive Dissonance Theory revealed that human beings are not rationalizing machines driven solely by truth, but consistency-seekers who alter beliefs to justify past actions.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
In 1957, Leon Festinger proposed that holding two psychologically inconsistent cognitions (e.g., 'I smoke' and 'smoking causes cancer') generates uncomfortable arousal — cognitive dissonance — motivating people to reduce tension by changing behavior, adding excuses, or distorting reality. In his classic $1 vs. $20 experiment, participants paid only $1 to lie that a boring task was fun rated it genuinely more enjoyable afterward than those paid $20, because $1 gave insufficient external justification, forcing internal attitude change. Similarly, 'effort justification' explains why harsh hazing increases loyalty to a group.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
How external messages persuade listeners depends on motivation and ability, captured in Petty and Cacioppo's Elaboration Likelihood Model (ELM). Under the Central Route, motivated audiences scrutinize argument quality, producing durable attitude shifts; under the Peripheral Route, distracted or unmotivated audiences rely on superficial cues like speaker attractiveness, celebrity status, or length. Robert Cialdini synthesized field persuasion into six universal levers: reciprocity, commitment/consistency, social proof, authority, liking, and scarcity.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Cognitive dissonance and persuasion science underpin public health campaigns, clinical Motivational Interviewing (evoking clients' own dissonance between values and habits), political campaigning, and consumer marketing.

## Key Terminology
- **Cognitive dissonance**: Psychological discomfort triggered by inconsistency between beliefs, values, or behavior, motivating rationalization or change.
- **Insufficient justification**: Counterintuitive finding that smaller external rewards produce greater internal attitude change after counter-attitudinal behavior.
- **Elaboration Likelihood Model**: Petty and Cacioppo's dual-route persuasion model contrasting careful central scrutiny with superficial peripheral cues.
- **Confirmation bias**: Selective seeking and weighting of evidence that protects existing beliefs from dissonance.
- **Inoculation theory**: McGuire's strategy building resistance to future misinformation by pre-exposing people to weakened counterarguments.
- **Effort justification**: Tendency to value a goal or group more highly when significant pain, cost, or effort was endured to attain it.
