# Decision Neuroscience and Neuroeconomics

## Overview
Neuroeconomics unites behavioral economics, cognitive psychology, and neuroimaging to uncover how the brain computes subjective value, weighs risk and ambiguity, and resolves clashes between immediate temptation and long-term goals.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Using fMRI and primate electrophysiology, Paul Glimcher, Antonio Rangel, and Samuel McClure demonstrated that the brain translates wildly different options — apples, money, social praise — into a 'common neural currency' encoded primarily in the ventromedial prefrontal cortex (vmPFC) and ventral striatum. Activity in these valuation hubs tracks subjective willingness-to-pay automatically even when participants aren't actively choosing.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
When choices involve time delays, McClure and Jonathan Cohen observed competing neural dynamics: limbic-striatal circuits spike selectively for *immediate* rewards ('right now'), whereas lateral prefrontal executive circuits evaluate delayed options more evenly across time — explaining hyperbolic discounting and self-control lapses. Similarly, the anterior insula activates strongly during unfair ultimatum-game offers and anticipated financial loss, mediating loss aversion. Crucially, patients with bilateral amygdala lesions fail to show normal loss aversion in gambles.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Neuroeconomics clarifies why fatigue and cognitive load erode self-control, informs addiction and compulsive gambling interventions, and models how social trust (modulated via oxytocin and striatal reputation tracking) sustains cooperative markets.

## Key Terminology
- **Common neural currency**: Shared valuation signal in vmPFC and ventral striatum enabling comparison across disparate rewards.
- **Ventral striatum**: Subcortical reward hub integrating dopamine prediction errors and motivational value.
- **Ultimatum game**: Behavioral economics task testing fairness norms where responders reject unfair splits at personal cost.
- **Ambiguity vs. Risk**: Known outcome probabilities (risk) versus unknown, uncertain odds (ambiguity), recruiting distinct frontal circuits.
- **Self-control conflict**: Dynamic competition between immediate limbic valuation and dlPFC-modulated long-term goal weights.
- **Neuroimaging valuation**: Predicting aggregate population choices (e.g., music hits or microloans) from neural reward activity.
