# Forensic and Legal Psychology

## Overview
Forensic psychology applies clinical and cognitive science to legal proceedings, criminal investigations, and correctional rehabilitation. From exposing false confessions and eyewitness errors to evaluating competency to stand trial and recidivism risk, forensic psychologists guard empirical rigor inside the courtroom.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
In the criminal justice system, forensic psychologists perform court-ordered evaluations distinct from confidential therapy: assessing a defendant's present competency to understand charges and assist counsel (Dusky standard), evaluating criminal responsibility at the time of an offense (insanity defenses), and conducting structured violence-risk assessments (such as the HCR-20) — which consistently outperform unaided clinical intuition.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Cognitive and social researchers have also revolutionized police investigation standards. Saul Kassin demonstrated how confrontational, accusatory 'Reid technique' interrogations — involving isolation, prolonged exhaustion, and fabricated evidence claims — induce innocent suspects (especially juveniles and cognitively impaired individuals) to internalize or deliver false confessions. Similarly, Elizabeth Loftus and Gary Wells proved that simultaneous lineups and confirmatory feedback inflate mistaken eyewitness certainty. Consequently, modern best practices mandate video-recorded interrogations, non-coercive information-gathering interviews (the PEACE model), and double-blind sequential lineups.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Forensic psychology also informs jury selection, implicit bias instructions, sentencing alternatives, and trauma-informed correctional rehabilitation, bridging empirical behavioral science with constitutional due process.

## Key Terminology
- **Competency to stand trial**: Legal determination of whether a defendant currently understands court proceedings and can rationally assist counsel.
- **False confession**: Admission of guilt to a crime the suspect did not commit, often induced by coercive interrogation tactics or vulnerability.
- **Reid technique**: Traditional confrontational police interrogation method criticized by psychologists for elevating false-confession risk.
- **PEACE model**: Evidence-based, non-accusatory investigative interviewing framework prioritizing open-ended recall over coercion.
- **Structured professional judgment**: Risk assessment combining validated actuarial risk factors with individualized clinical case formulation.
- **Double-blind lineup**: Eyewitness identification procedure where neither witness nor administrator knows who the suspect is, preventing subtle cueing.
