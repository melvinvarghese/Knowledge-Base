# Spatial Cognition and Wayfinding

## Overview
Spatial cognition investigates how humans mentally represent, rotate, and navigate physical environments. Balancing egocentric body-centered routes with allocentric bird's-eye cognitive maps, spatial reasoning predicts STEM achievement and informs architecture and GPS interface design.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Navigation relies on two complementary reference frames: egocentric ('turn left at the bakery, then right at the oak' — route knowledge anchored in striatal stimulus-response habits) and allocentric ('the library lies north of the station' — survey map knowledge anchored in the hippocampus). Path integration (dead reckoning) continuously updates position using optic flow and inner-ear vestibular cues, though errors accumulate rapidly without visual landmarks.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
In 1971, Roger Shepard and Jacqueline Metzler showed reaction times to judge whether two 3D block figures match scale linearly with angular rotation difference, proving continuous analog spatial transformation. Spatial skills — mental rotation, spatial visualization, and perspective-taking — strongly predict engineering, chemistry, and surgical mastery, and show remarkable malleability through targeted training (such as spatial play, sketching, and action video games).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Overreliance on turn-by-turn smartphone GPS measurably suppresses hippocampal map engagement compared to paper maps or active landmark attention, prompting urban designers to preserve clear sightlines, distinctive landmarks, and legible street grids.

## Key Terminology
- **Egocentric vs. Allocentric**: Body-centered navigation ('left/right') versus environment-centered map coordinates ('north/south').
- **Path integration**: Updating estimated position continuously from self-motion cues (stride, optic flow, vestibular sense).
- **Route vs. Survey knowledge**: Sequential landmark-turn memories versus integrated bird's-eye spatial layout understanding.
- **Mental rotation**: Analog cognitive transformation of 2D or 3D object representations across orientation angles.
- **Landmark salience**: Visual distinctiveness and structural placement making objects effective anchors for wayfinding.
- **Vestibular system**: Inner-ear semicircular canals and otoliths sensing head acceleration, tilt, and gravity.
