# Moral Courage, Altruism, and Prosocial Behavior

## Overview
Alongside conformity and prejudice, social psychology studies humanity's capacity for everyday kindness, emergency rescue, and costly moral courage. Distinguishing emotional empathy from actionable compassion clarifies when bystanders freeze versus when individuals risk everything to help strangers.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Following the 1964 murder of Kitty Genovese, John Darley and Bibb Latané uncovered the 'bystander effect': individuals are less likely to intervene in emergencies when others are present due to diffusion of responsibility ('someone else will call'), pluralistic ignorance (looking at calm faces and assuming nothing is wrong), and evaluation apprehension. Yet modern CCTV meta-analyses by Richard Philpot show real-world street fights actually see intervention over 90% of the time when someone is present.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Why do people help strangers anonymously? C. Daniel Batson's Empathy-Altruism Hypothesis demonstrated experimentally that evoking genuine empathic concern motivates helping even when observers can easily escape the situation unseen — challenging pure 'egoistic relief' accounts. Paul Bloom further distinguishes emotional empathy (mirroring another's pain, which can cause burnout and parochial in-group bias) from rational compassion (caring about well-being across strangers without drowning in distress).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Training active bystander skills (making eye contact, assigning specific roles like 'you in the red jacket, call 911') dramatically raises intervention rates in medical emergencies, bullying, and workplace harassment.

## Key Terminology
- **Bystander effect**: Reduced likelihood of an individual helping in an emergency when multiple passive observers are present.
- **Pluralistic ignorance**: Collective misreading where everyone privately doubts a norm or calm facade yet conforms because others look calm.
- **Empathy-Altruism Hypothesis**: Batson's model showing genuine empathic concern produces selfless motivation to relieve another's need.
- **Compassion vs. Emotional empathy**: Warm, motivational care for another's flourishing versus vicariously absorbing their acute distress.
- **Moral courage**: Willingness to speak up or intervene ethically despite personal social, financial, or physical risk.
- **Kin vs. Reciprocal altruism**: Helping genetic relatives versus helping non-relatives with expectation of future mutual aid.
