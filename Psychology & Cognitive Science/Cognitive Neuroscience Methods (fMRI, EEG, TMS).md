# Cognitive Neuroscience Methods (fMRI, EEG, TMS)

## Overview
Cognitive neuroscience maps how physical brain activity gives rise to perception, memory, language, and decision-making. Because no single tool captures both millimeter anatomy and millisecond timing, researchers triangulate across fMRI, EEG/MEG, lesion studies, and causal brain stimulation.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Functional Magnetic Resonance Imaging (fMRI) tracks Blood-Oxygen-Level-Dependent (BOLD) signals: active neurons consume oxygen, triggering localized blood-flow surges peaking 4–6 seconds later. fMRI offers crisp millimeter spatial resolution across deep subcortical structures, yet suffers sluggish temporal resolution and only establishes correlation. Conversely, Electroencephalography (EEG) and Magnetoencephalography (MEG) record electrical scalp potentials or magnetic fields at millisecond precision — capturing rapid Event-Related Potentials (ERPs) like the N400 semantic-surprise wave — yet struggle to pinpoint deep sources precisely.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
To move beyond correlation to causation, Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation (TMS) pulses focused magnetic coils over the scalp to temporarily excite or disrupt targeted cortical patches ('virtual lesions'), proving whether a region is genuinely necessary for a task. Meanwhile, intracranial single-unit recordings in neurosurgical patients (discovering 'Jennifer Aniston' concept neurons) and modern multivoxel pattern analysis (MVPA) decode distributed mental representations across voxels.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Awareness of statistical pitfalls — like 'dead salmon' multiple-comparisons false positives — spurred strict neuroimaging standardization, open brain repositories (OpenNeuro), and multi-site consortia.

## Key Terminology
- **BOLD signal**: Blood-Oxygen-Level-Dependent MRI contrast measuring localized hemodynamic changes coupled to neural activity.
- **Spatial vs. Temporal resolution**: Precision in locating *where* brain activity occurs (mm) versus *when* it occurs (ms).
- **Event-Related Potential (ERP)**: Averaged EEG waveform locked to a specific sensory or cognitive event (e.g., P300, N400).
- **TMS (Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation)**: Non-invasive magnetic pulse technique temporarily modulating cortical excitability to test causal necessity.
- **Reverse inference**: Logical fallacy of assuming a specific mental state occurred solely because an associated brain region lit up.
- **MVPA (Pattern decoding)**: Machine-learning analysis reading distributed voxel activation patterns rather than isolated blob averages.
