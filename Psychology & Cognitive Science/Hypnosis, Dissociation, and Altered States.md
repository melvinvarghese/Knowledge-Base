# Hypnosis, Dissociation, and Altered States

## Overview
Hypnosis and dissociation represent striking shifts in normal conscious integration, attention, and sense of agency. Shedding stage-magic myths, modern neuroimaging proves hypnotic suggestion alters genuine sensory cortex processing, pain perception, and executive monitoring in susceptible individuals.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
First popularized controversially by Franz Anton Mesmer ('animal magnetism') and later studied clinically by Jean-Martin Charcot and Sigmund Freud, hypnosis was standardized scientifically by Ernest Hilgard's Stanford Hypnotic Susceptibility Scales. Hypnosis is not sleep; it combines focused absorption, reduced peripheral awareness, and heightened responsiveness to suggestion — distributed normally across the population, with ~10–15% 'high hypnotizables' experiencing dramatic perceptual shifts.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Two rival frameworks explain hypnosis: Hilgard's Neo-Dissociation Theory proposes suggestion splits executive control so a 'hidden observer' registers pain subconsciously while conscious awareness feels none, whereas Socio-Cognitive Theory (Nicholas Spanos) emphasizes role enactment, expectation, and strategic compliance. Functional MRI scans (David Spiegel, Amir Raz) bridge these views by showing genuine brain-network decoupling — reduced connectivity between the Dorsal Anterior Cingulate (salience/conflict) and Default Mode Network, allowing hypnotic suggestion to block Stroop word-reading interference or blunt pain cortex activity.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Clinically, hypnotherapy serves as an evidence-based adjunct for surgical pain, irritable bowel syndrome, and smoking cessation. Pathological dissociation (depersonalization, derealization, and Dissociative Identity Disorder) similarly reflects extreme compartmentalization under severe trauma.

## Key Terminology
- **Hypnotic suggestibility**: Stable individual trait measuring responsiveness to perceptual, motor, and cognitive suggestions during hypnosis.
- **Dissociation**: Disruption in the normal integration of consciousness, memory, identity, emotion, or bodily perception.
- **Neo-dissociation theory**: Ernest Hilgard's model proposing hypnosis divides executive control systems behind an amnesic barrier.
- **Depersonalization / Derealization**: Dissociative sensations of feeling detached from one's own body or viewing the external world as dreamlike.
- **Stroop effect**: Classic cognitive interference task (naming ink color of mismatched color words) notably reduced in high hypnotizables under suggestion.
- **Clinical hypnotherapy**: Evidence-based use of focused hypnotic suggestion as an adjunct for pain, IBS, and anxiety management.
