# Behavioral Genetics and Twin Studies

## Overview
Behavioral genetics investigates how genetic variation and environmental factors jointly shape individual differences in intelligence, personality, mental health, and behavior. Moving past naive 'nature vs. nurture' binaries, modern twin, adoption, and genome-wide studies reveal that genes and environments constantly co-act.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Classic behavioral genetics compares identical (monozygotic, sharing ~100% of segregating DNA) and fraternal (dizygotic, sharing ~50%) twins reared together or apart, alongside adoption designs. Reviewing fifty years of findings, Eric Turkheimer summarized 'Three Laws of Behavior Genetics': (1) all human behavioral traits are heritable (typically 30–70% of population variance); (2) the effect of being raised in the same family ('shared environment') is surprisingly smaller than genetic variance for most adult traits; and (3) a substantial share of variance stems from the 'non-shared environment' (unique peers, luck, illness, differential experiences).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Crucially, 'heritability' is a population statistic, not an individual destiny or genetic determinism: height is ~80–90% heritable, yet improved nutrition raised average human height dramatically across generations. Moreover, genes operate through Gene-Environment Correlation (rGE — active children seek niches matching predispositions, and evocative infants elicit distinct parenting) and Gene-Environment Interaction (GxE — genetic vulnerabilities manifest primarily under severe childhood stress). Modern Genome-Wide Association Studies (GWAS) show psychological traits are massively polygenic, shaped by thousands of tiny-effect variants rather than single 'genius' or 'warrior' genes.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Behavioral genetics reframes parenting (reducing misplaced parental guilt over fixed child temperaments while highlighting unique supportive niches) and guides precision psychiatry toward polygenic risk screening paired with early environmental protection.

## Key Terminology
- **Heritability (h²)**: Proportion of observable trait variation within a specific population attributable to genetic differences, not individual destiny.
- **Monozygotic vs. Dizygotic twins**: Identical twins sharing 100% of nuclear DNA versus fraternal twins sharing ~50% like ordinary siblings.
- **Gene-Environment Correlation**: Process whereby genetic tendencies influence the environments individuals evoke or actively select.
- **Gene-Environment Interaction**: Differential sensitivity to environmental stress or enrichment depending on an individual's genotype.
- **Polygenic score**: Aggregate index summing tiny statistical effects of thousands of genetic variants to estimate trait propensity.
- **Shared vs. Non-shared environment**: Family-wide influences making siblings alike versus unique individual experiences making siblings differ.
