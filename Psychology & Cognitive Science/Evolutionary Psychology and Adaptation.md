# Evolutionary Psychology and Adaptation

## Overview
Evolutionary psychology applies Darwinian natural and sexual selection to human cognition, emotion, and social behavior. Pioneered by Leda Cosmides, John Tooby, and David Buss, it views the brain as an ensemble of evolved computational adaptations shaped to solve ancestral survival and reproductive challenges.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Extending ethology and E.O. Wilson's sociobiology, evolutionary psychologists argue the human mind is not an all-purpose blank slate, but houses domain-specific adaptations — such as innate fear preparedness toward snakes and heights, incest avoidance (Westermarck effect), cheater-detection modules in social exchange (demonstrated via Cosmides's Wason selection task), and parent-offspring bonding. W.D. Hamilton's Kin Selection theory explained altruism toward relatives via 'inclusive fitness,' while Robert Trivers's Reciprocal Altruism explained cooperation among non-kin.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
In mating and social life, Trivers's Parental Investment Theory predicts sex differences where the sex investing greater biological effort in offspring becomes choosier, while intense competition shapes traits via sexual selection. David Buss's 37-culture survey found broad cross-cultural patterns alongside immense ecological flexibility. Critics — notably Stephen Jay Gould and cultural psychologists — caution against unprovable 'just-so stories,' reminding researchers that many traits are non-adaptive byproducts ('spandrels') or products of rapid cultural evolution.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Rigorous evolutionary psychology now pairs comparative primatology, paleoanthropology, and computational modeling to distinguish genuine biological adaptations from cultural plasticity, illuminating mismatches between ancestral environments and modern hyper-palatable foods, chronic loneliness, and digital media.

## Key Terminology
- **Inclusive fitness**: Total genetic success combining personal reproduction and support for the reproduction of biological relatives.
- **Reciprocal altruism**: Evolutionarily stable cooperation among non-relatives based on tracked mutual aid and cheater punishment.
- **Environment of Evolutionary Adaptedness (EEA)**: Composite ancestral selection pressures under which a given human psychological adaptation evolved.
- **Spandrel**: Non-adaptive evolutionary byproduct arising alongside a genuinely selected trait.
- **Westermarck effect**: Innate sexual aversion developing between individuals raised closely together in early childhood, preventing inbreeding.
- **Evolutionary mismatch**: Discrepancy between ancestral conditions an adaptation evolved for and modern environments (e.g., sugar cravings, chronic screen stress).
