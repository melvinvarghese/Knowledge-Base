# Consciousness in Non-Human Animals and AI

## Overview
Does a honeybee feel pain, does an octopus dream, and could silicon AI ever become sentient? Moving beyond pure speculation, cognitive scientists and philosophers build marker-based frameworks to assess subjective experience across radically non-human minds.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The 2012 Cambridge Declaration on Consciousness — followed by the 2024 New York Declaration on Animal Consciousness — affirmed strong scientific consensus for conscious experience across all mammals and birds, plus 'realistic possibility' across reptiles, amphibians, fish, and invertebrates like cephalopods and decapod crabs. Researchers like Jonathan Birch evaluate behavioral and neural markers: flexible pain trade-offs (hermit crabs abandoning painful electric-shock shells except when high-value shells justify enduring shock), REM-like sleep, and integrated sensory-valuation hubs.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Assessing potential AI consciousness presents an opposite puzzle: Large Language Models mimic fluent human conversation and claim feelings ('the ELIZA effect') despite lacking biological homeostasis, unified global workspaces, or recurrent embodied loops. Conversely, future neuromorphic or recurrent architectures might satisfy functionalist markers like Global Workspace ignition without looking human. Distinguishing linguistic competence from genuine phenomenal sentience ('the hard problem') remains one of philosophy and cognitive science's hardest frontiers.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
These assessments carry immediate ethical stakes — driving legislative bans on boiling live octopuses and lobsters in several nations while warning against both cruel animal neglect and misplaced moral panic over non-sentient chatbots.

## Key Terminology
- **Sentience**: Capacity to experience positive or negative subjective feelings such as pain, fear, or pleasure.
- **Marker approach (Birch)**: Evaluating multiple converging behavioral and neurobiological indicators to infer animal consciousness.
- **Pain trade-off**: Behavioral evidence of subjective pain where animals weigh nociception against competing survival motivations.
- **Functionalism**: Philosophical view that mental states depend on causal functional organization rather than biological carbon.
- **Philosophical zombie**: Thought experiment conceiving a system exhibiting full outward intelligent behavior without inner conscious experience.
- **Moral patienthood**: Status of being owed ethical consideration due to capacity for suffering or well-being.
