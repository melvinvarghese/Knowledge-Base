# The Sorrows of Young Werther - Johann Wolfgang von Goethe, Sturm und Drang, and Romantic Subjectivity

## Overview
Johann Wolfgang von Goethe's *The Sorrows of Young Werther* (*Die Leiden des jungen Werthers*, 1774) is a seminal epistolary novel that ignited the German *Sturm und Drang* (Storm and Stress) movement and European Romanticism.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Epistolary Subjectivity & Unrequited Passion: Written as a series of intimate letters from Werther, a sensitive young artist, to his friend Wilhelm. In the idyllic village of Wahlheim, Werther falls hopelessly in love with **Lotte** (Charlotte), an intelligent, compassionate young woman who is already engaged to the stable, pragmatic bourgeois official Albert. Werther's passionate, subjective emotional world clashes violently with cold social conventions and the rationalist Enlightenment.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Werther Effect & Romantic Cult: As Werther's obsessive passion drives him to despair, he borrows Albert's hunting pistols under the pretext of a journey and shoots himself at his desk, dressed in his iconic blue coat and yellow vest. The novel became an unprecedented international sensation: young men across Europe dressed in 'Werther costume' and adopted his brooding melancholy. It also provoked moral panic over a wave of copycat suicides (birthing the modern sociological term **The Werther Effect**).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Established Goethe as a global literary star at age 24 and defined Romantic emotionalism and alienation.

## Key Terminology
- **Sturm und Drang**: Proto-Romantic German literary and artistic movement of the late 18th century emphasizing intense individual emotion, nature, and rebellion.
- **Werther Effect**: Sociological phenomenon where publicized suicides trigger an increase in copycat suicides across a population.
- **Epistolary Novel**: A novel written entirely in the form of a series of letters or diary entries between characters.
