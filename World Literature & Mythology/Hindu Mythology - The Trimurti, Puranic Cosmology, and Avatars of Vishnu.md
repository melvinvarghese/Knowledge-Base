# Hindu Mythology - The Trimurti, Puranic Cosmology, and Avatars of Vishnu

## Overview
Hindu mythology is an expansive, ancient tradition preserved across the Vedas, Upanishads, Epics, and 18 Mahapuranas, articulating cyclic cosmic time, karma, and divine manifestation.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Trimurti & Cosmic Cycles (Yugas): Cosmic balance is personified by the **Trimurti**: **Brahma** (the creator), **Vishnu** (the preserver), and **Shiva** (the destroyer and transformer), alongside their female consorts, the **Tridevi** (Saraswati, Lakshmi, and Parvati/Durga/Kali). Cosmic time is strictly cyclic, repeating across endless **Kalpas** (days of Brahma lasting 4.32 billion human years), each containing 14 Manvantaras and cycling through four descending moral ages (**Yugas**): Satya Yuga (the golden age of truth), Treta Yuga, Dvapara Yuga, and the current dark age of moral decline, **Kali Yuga**.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Dashavatara (Avatars of Vishnu): To restore cosmic moral order (**Dharma**) whenever evil threatens creation, Lord Vishnu descends into the mortal realm via ten canonical incarnations (**Dashavatara**): Matsya (fish), Kurma (tortoise), Varaha (boar), Narasimha (man-lion), Vamana (dwarf), Parashurama (warrior), Rama (ideal king), Krishna (divine teacher and statesman), Buddha, and the prophesied apocalyptic warrior Kalki.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Serves as the philosophical and narrative foundation for classical Indian dance, temple architecture, and contemporary cultural identity across South and Southeast Asia.

## Key Terminology
- **Trimurti**: The Hindu cosmic triad of supreme divinity consisting of Brahma the creator, Vishnu the preserver, and Shiva the destroyer.
- **Kali Yuga**: The fourth and final age in the Hindu cycle of cosmic time, marked by moral decay, discord, and spiritual ignorance.
- **Avatar**: The material manifestation or bodily incarnation of a deity on Earth to restore dharma.
