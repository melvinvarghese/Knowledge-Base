# Orlando Furioso - Ludovico Ariosto, High Chivalric Romance, and Epic Irony

## Overview
Ludovico Ariosto's *Orlando Furioso* ('Mad Orlando', definitive 46-canto edition 1532) is the crowning epic poem of the Italian High Renaissance, weaving chivalric adventure, romance, and irony.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Plot Strands & Orlando's Descent into Madness: Set against the legendary war between Charlemagne's Christian paladins and King Agramante's Saracens. Ariosto weaves three main plotlines: (1) The war for Europe; (2) The dynastic romance of the female warrior **Bradamante** and Ruggiero (ancestors of Ariosto's patrons, the House of Este); (3) The tragic madness of **Orlando** (Roland): when the pagan princess Angelica rejects him for the lowly foot soldier Medoro, Orlando is driven into feral madness, stripping naked and uprooting trees barehanded.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Astolfo's Voyage to the Moon: The English knight Astolfo flies on a **Hippogriff** (half-horse, half-griffin) to the terrestrial paradise, where Saint John the Apostle flies him in Elijah's chariot to the **Moon**. On the Moon, all things lost on Earth—broken vows, wasted time, forgotten prayers, and Orlando's lost sanity (contained in a labeled glass flask)—are stored, which Astolfo retrieves.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Directly influenced Edmund Spenser's *The Faerie Queene*, William Shakespeare's *As You Like It*, and modern magical realism.

## Key Terminology
- **Hippogriff**: A legendary winged creature with the front half of an eagle and the hindquarters of a horse, ridden by Astolfo.
- **Entrelacement**: A narrative technique of interweaving multiple simultaneous plot threads, switching back and forth between characters.
- **Epic Irony**: A narrative stance where the epic narrator gently mocks the chivalric delusions of his own heroic characters.
