# Jerusalem Delivered (Gerusalemme Liberata) - Torquato Tasso and Christian Counter-Reformation Epic

## Overview
Torquato Tasso's *Gerusalemme Liberata* (1581) is a 20-canto Italian epic poem recounting the historical First Crusade (1099) and the siege of Jerusalem led by Godfrey of Bouillon.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Counter-Reformation Piety vs Sensual Temptation: Composed during the Catholic Counter-Reformation. Tasso sought to write an epic that adhered to Aristotle's unity of action while dramatizing the spiritual conflict between Christian duty and sensual passion. The crusader champion **Rinaldo** is seduced by the beautiful Saracen sorceress **Armida**, who lures him to an enchanted garden of sensual oblivion on the Fortunate Islands, paralyzing the siege until fellow knights rescue him with a magic shield.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Tragedy of Tancred and Clorinda: The epic's tragic emotional peak is the duel between the Christian knight **Tancred** and the fierce Saracen warrior-maiden **Clorinda**. Clorinda wears armor concealing her identity; they battle in nocturnal darkness until Tancred mortally wounds her. Dying, Clorinda asks for baptism; lifting her visor to administer the sacrament with water in his helmet, Tancred discovers he has slain his own beloved, collapsing in agony.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Set the benchmark for late Renaissance Christian heroic epic, inspiring operas by Monteverdi, Handel, and Rossini.

## Key Terminology
- **Ottava Rima**: An Italian rhyming stanza form of eight eleven-syllable lines (abababcc) used by Tasso and Ariosto.
- **Tancred and Clorinda**: The celebrated tragic episode where a knight unwittingly slays the woman he loves in combat and baptizes her as she dies.
- **Armida's Garden**: An idyllic enchanted landscape symbolizing seductive sensual pleasure that diverts warriors from spiritual duty.
