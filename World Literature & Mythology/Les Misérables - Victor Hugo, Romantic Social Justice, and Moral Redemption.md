# Les Misérables - Victor Hugo, Romantic Social Justice, and Moral Redemption

## Overview
Victor Hugo's *Les Misérables* (1862) is a monumental French epic novel of social injustice, spiritual grace, and revolutionary sacrifice, centering on the redemption of an ex-convict.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Jean Valjean & The Bishop's Candlesticks: **Jean Valjean** is sentenced to nineteen years of hard labor in the galleys for stealing a loaf of bread to feed his sister's starving children. Hardened into a bitter beast, Valjean is sheltered by the saintly **Bishop Myriel**, whom Valjean robs of silver plate. When the police capture him, the Bishop lies to protect Valjean, handing him two silver candlesticks and proclaiming: *'Jean Valjean, my brother, you belong no longer to evil, but to good. It is your soul that I am buying for you.'* Transformed by this grace, Valjean reinvents himself as the philanthropic industrialist Monsieur Madeleine.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Javert, Fantine, and the Paris Barricades: Valjean is pursued across decades by the fanatical police inspector **Javert**, the embodiment of unbending, merciless statutory law. Valjean rescues **Cosette**, the abused orphan of the dying factory worker Fantine. Their lives intersect with the radical student revolutionaries of the ABC Friends (led by Enjolras and Marius Pontmercy) at the 1832 June Rebellion barricades in Paris. Valjean carries the wounded Marius through the labyrinthine sewers of Paris. When Valjean spares Javert's life, Javert's moral universe fractures—unable to reconcile mercy with the law, Javert leaps to his death into the Seine.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Hugo declared the novel was written to illuminate the three great problems of the century: 'the degradation of man by the proletariat, the subjection of woman by hunger, the atrophy of the child by darkness.'

## Key Terminology
- **Jean Valjean**: The central protagonist whose journey from embittered convict to saintly protector embodies Christian moral redemption.
- **Javert**: The rigid, fanatical police inspector who personifies unyielding legalism devoid of mercy.
- **June Rebellion (1832)**: The Parisian anti-monarchist student uprising dramatized at the barricades in Les Misérables.
