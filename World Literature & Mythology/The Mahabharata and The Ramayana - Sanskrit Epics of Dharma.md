# The Mahabharata and The Ramayana - Sanskrit Epics of Dharma

## Overview
The *Mahabharata* and *Ramayana* are the foundational Sanskrit epics (*Itihasa*) of ancient India, exploring duty, righteousness, and cosmic moral order (*Dharma*).

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Ramayana (Valmiki): Traces Prince Rama (an avatar of Vishnu), who accepts exile into the Dandaka forest with his wife Sita and brother Lakshmana. When the demon king Ravana abducts Sita to Lanka, Rama allies with the vanara king Sugriva and Hanuman to slay Ravana, presenting Rama as the ideal king (*Maryada Purushottama*).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Mahabharata (Vyasa) & The Bhagavad Gita: The longest epic poem in world literature (over 200,000 verses), narrating the dynastic feud between the Pandava brothers and Kauravas culminating in the Kurukshetra War. On the battlefield, Krishna counsels Prince Arjuna in the *Bhagavad Gita*, resolving his moral despair by teaching selfless action (*Karma Yoga*), devotion (*Bhakti*), and universal duty (*Dharma*).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Ethical, philosophical, and devotional foundation of Hinduism; influential narrative tradition across South and Southeast Asia (Wayang puppetry, Angkor Wat).

## Key Terminology
- **Dharma**: Cosmic order, righteousness, moral duty, and ethical conduct central to Indian philosophical epics.
- **Bhagavad Gita**: 700-verse Hindu scripture within the Mahabharata consisting of a dialogue between Prince Arjuna and Lord Krishna.
- **Hanuman**: Devoted vanara deity in the Ramayana celebrated as the embodiment of supreme selfless service and devotion.
