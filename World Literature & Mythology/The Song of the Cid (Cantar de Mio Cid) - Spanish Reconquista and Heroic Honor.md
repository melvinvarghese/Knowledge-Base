# The Song of the Cid (Cantar de Mio Cid) - Spanish Reconquista and Heroic Honor

## Overview
The *Cantar de Mio Cid* (c. 1200) is the oldest preserved Spanish epic poem (cantar de gesta), chronicling the heroic deeds, exile, and moral restoration of the Castilian knight Rodrigo Díaz de Vivar (El Cid Campeador).

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Realism & Three Cantares: Unlike the supernatural, magical French *Song of Roland*, the *Cantar de Mio Cid* is grounded in historical realism and military pragmatism. The narrative unfolds across three cantares: (1) **Cantar del Destierro** (Canto of Exile): El Cid is falsely accused by envious courtiers and exiled by King Alfonso VI of Castile, forced to leave his wife Doña Jimena and daughters in a monastery. El Cid wages military campaigns against Moorish and Christian lords, dutifully sending rich royal gifts of war booty to the king; (2) **Cantar de las Bodas** (Canto of the Weddings): El Cid captures the wealthy Islamic city of Valencia, winning the king's pardon and receiving royal permission for his daughters, Doña Elvira and Doña Sol, to marry the aristocratic Infantes de Carrión; (3) **Cantar de la Afrenta de Corpes** (Canto of the Affront of Corpes): Exposed as cowardly during a lion escape, the vengeful Infantes beat and strip El Cid's daughters in the oak forest of Corpes, leaving them for dead.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Justice over Blood Vengeance: Rather than initiating an illegal private blood feud, El Cid appeals to King Alfonso's court at Toledo, demanding legal restitution and judicial duel. His champions defeat the Infantes, and his daughters marry the princes of Navarre and Aragon, uniting El Cid's bloodline with the royal houses of Spain.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Celebrates the ideal Castilian virtue of honor (*honra*), martial brilliance, pragmatic chivalry, and steadfast loyalty.

## Key Terminology
- **El Cid Campeador**: Honorific title meaning 'The Lord, Champion of Battlefield Duels' awarded to Rodrigo Díaz de Vivar.
- **Honra**: Personal and familial honor in Spanish chivalric culture that must be defended and vindicated.
- **Cantar de Gesta**: Old Spanish epic poems performed orally by wandering minstrels (*juglares*) celebrating heroic deeds.
