# The Plays of William Shakespeare - Tragedy, Soliloquy, and the Globe

## Overview
William Shakespeare (1564–1616) revolutionized theatrical drama and the English language through his psychological tragedies, histories, and comedies staged at London's Globe Theatre.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Tragic Depth & The Soliloquy: Shakespeare transformed the soliloquy from a clumsy exposition device into a profound vehicle for internal psychological interiority and moral agonizing: Hamlet's existential paralysis ('To be, or not to be'), Macbeth's nihilistic descent ('Tomorrow, and tomorrow, and tomorrow'), and King Lear's stripping of royal ego on the blasted heath.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Iambic Pentameter & Linguistic Invention: Writing predominantly in unrhymed iambic pentameter (blank verse), Shakespeare coined over 1,700 new English words (eyeball, swagger, bedazzled) and hundreds of idioms still pervasive across the modern English language.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Archetypal exploration of power, betrayal, love, jealousy, and mortality, performed continuously worldwide for four centuries.

## Key Terminology
- **Blank Verse**: Unrhymed poetry written in regular iambic pentameter, the foundational meter of Elizabethan drama.
- **Soliloquy**: Dramatic monologue spoken by a character alone on stage expressing internal thoughts and emotional conflicts.
- **The Globe Theatre**: London playhouse built in 1599 by Shakespeare's playing company, the Lord Chamberlain's Men.
