# The Divine Comedy - Dante's Inferno, Purgatorio, and Paradiso

## Overview
Dante Alighieri's *The Divine Comedy* (1320) is the supreme poetic masterpiece of the late Middle Ages, synthesizing Christian theology, classical philosophy, and Florentine politics.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Terza Rima & The Three Realms: Composed in hendecasyllabic *terza rima* ($aba, bcb, cdc$), Dante journeys through the afterlife guided by the Roman poet Virgil (representing human reason) through the nine circles of Hell (*Inferno*) and the mountain of purification (*Purgatorio*), before his beloved Beatrice (divine grace) guides him through celestial Heaven (*Paradiso*).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Contrapasso & Moral Realism: Dante constructs divine justice through the principle of *contrapasso*—where eternal punishments mirror or contrast the earthly sins committed (e.g., fortune tellers have their heads twisted backward; lustful souls are swept along in an eternal hurricane).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Established the Tuscan vernacular as the modern Italian literary language, monumental synthesis of medieval scholasticism.

## Key Terminology
- **Terza Rima**: Three-line stanza rhyming scheme invented by Dante Alighieri with interlocking rhymes.
- **Contrapasso**: Principle that the punishment of a sinner in the afterlife corresponds directly to the nature of their sin on Earth.
- **Virgil**: Classical Roman poet who acts as Dante's guide through Hell and Purgatory as the embodiment of classical human reason.
