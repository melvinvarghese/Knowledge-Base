# Slavic Mythology - Perun, Veles, and the World Tree

## Overview
Slavic mythology encompasses the pre-Christian religious beliefs, cosmology, and ritual folklore of the Slavic peoples of Eastern, Central, and Southern Europe.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Cosmic Axis & The Dualistic Thunder-Serpent Battle: Slavic cosmology is structured around the **World Tree** (typically a colossal sacred oak): the heavens and celestial canopy are ruled by **Perun**, the supreme god of thunder, lightning, storms, and the warrior caste (wielding an axe or arrows). The roots of the tree, subterranean waters, and the moist underworld of **Nav** are ruled by his arch-rival **Veles** (or Volos), the horned god of cattle, lowlands, trickery, the dead, magic, and music. The central mythic drama is the eternal combat between them: Veles ascends from the roots to steal Perun's cattle, family, or rain clouds; Perun strikes Veles down with fiery lightning bolts, causing rain to fall upon the earth and restoring cosmic fertility.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Pantheon & The Sacred Seasons: Other major deities include **Dazhbog** (the solar deity), **Mokosh** (the Great Mother Earth and protectress of women's work and spinning), **Svarog** (the celestial blacksmith), and **Morana / Marzanna** (the winter goddess of death, whose effigy is ritually drowned in spring to usher in rebirth).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Reconstructed by linguists (Vyacheslav Ivanov, Vladimir Toporov) through comparative Indo-European mythology and surviving Eastern European seasonal folktales and songs.

## Key Terminology
- **Perun**: The supreme Slavic god of thunder, war, lightning, and heavenly fire, associated with the sacred oak tree.
- **Veles**: The Slavic underworld deity of earth, waters, cattle, magic, and wealth, perennial rival of Perun.
- **Nav**: The Slavic underworld where souls of the deceased dwell, conceptualized as a green meadow ruled by Veles.
