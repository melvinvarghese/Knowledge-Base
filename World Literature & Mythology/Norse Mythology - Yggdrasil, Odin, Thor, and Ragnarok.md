# Norse Mythology - Yggdrasil, Odin, Thor, and Ragnarok

## Overview
Norse Mythology comprises the pre-Christian cosmological beliefs of the Scandinavian peoples, preserved primarily in the 13th-century Icelandic *Poetic Edda* and Snorri Sturluson's *Prose Edda*.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Yggdrasil & The Nine Realms: The cosmos is structured around the colossal sacred ash tree Yggdrasil, connecting Nine Realms including Asgard (realm of the Aesir gods), Midgard (the mortal world of humans), and Helheim (underworld). Wisdom demands sacrifice: all-father Odin pierced himself with his spear Gungnir on Yggdrasil for nine nights to unlock the secret magic of the runes.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Inevitability of Ragnarök: Unlike classical mythologies where gods are immortal masters of fate, the Norse gods are mortal. The prophetic climax of the cosmos is Ragnarök ('Twilight of the Gods')—a cosmic battle preceded by the bitter three-year winter Fimbulwinter where Odin is slain by the wolf Fenrir, Thor falls to the Midgard Serpent Jörmungandr, and the world is consumed by fire and drowned before re-emerging renewed.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Inspiration for Germanic romantic literature, Wagnerian opera (*Der Ring des Nibelungen*), modern epic fantasy (J.R.R. Tolkien).

## Key Terminology
- **Yggdrasil**: Immense mythical sacred ash tree central to Norse cosmology connecting the Nine Realms.
- **Ragnarök**: Cataclysmic series of future events, including a great battle and natural disasters, leading to the death of gods and renewal of the world.
- **Aesir**: Principal pantheon of Norse deities, including Odin, Frigg, Thor, Baldr, and Tyr.
