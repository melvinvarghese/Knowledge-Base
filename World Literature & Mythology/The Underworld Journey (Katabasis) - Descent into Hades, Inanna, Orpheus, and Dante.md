# The Underworld Journey (Katabasis) - Descent into Hades, Inanna, Orpheus, and Dante

## Overview
Katabasis (Greek for 'descent') is a central mythic and literary trope describing a hero's voyage into the subterranean realm of the dead and their subsequent miraculous return to the world of the living.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Classical & Ancient Archetypes: (1) **Inanna's Descent** (Sumerian): Inanna strips off a piece of divine jewelry at each of the seven gates of the underworld, arriving naked before Ereshkigal, who turns her into a corpse hung on a meat hook until resurrected; (2) **Orpheus and Eurydice**: The divine musician charms Hades and Persephone with his lyre to retrieve his dead bride, but loses her on the threshold by looking back; (3) **Odysseus (Nekuia)**: Performs blood sacrifices at the edge of Oceanus to summon the ghost of the blind prophet Tiresias; (4) **Aeneas (Aeneid Book VI)**: Plucks the Golden Bough and crosses the River Styx with the Sibyl to consult his father Anchises regarding Rome's destiny.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Dante and Modern Psychological Interpretation: Dante Alighieri transformed katabasis into a comprehensive Christian spiritual pilgrimage through *Inferno*. Carl Jung interpreted the underworld descent as an encounter with the unconscious shadow—a necessary psychological crisis preceding spiritual individuation and rebirth.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Underpins Western narrative structure, from classical heroic epics to modern psychological fiction and cinema.

## Key Terminology
- **Katabasis**: A descent into the underworld or underworld-like space in mythological and literary narratives.
- **Nekuia**: The ancient Greek necromantic ritual of evoking ghosts of the dead to question them about the future.
- **Golden Bough**: The enchanted tree branch that granted the hero Aeneas safe passage across the River Styx into Hades.
