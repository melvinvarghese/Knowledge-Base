# Oedipus Rex and Antigone - Sophocles, Tragic Irony, and Divine vs Human Law

## Overview
Sophocles' Theban plays—chiefly *Oedipus Rex* (Oedipus Tyrannus, c. 429 BCE) and *Antigone* (c. 441 BCE)—stand as the pinnacle of ancient Athenian tragic irony, fate, and moral defiance.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Oedipus Rex & The Mechanics of Tragic Revelation: Aristotle (*Poetics*) celebrated *Oedipus Rex* as the flawless tragedy. Fleeing a prophecy that he would murder his father and marry his mother, Oedipus inadvertently fulfills it: slaying King Laius at a three-way crossroads, solving the riddle of the Sphinx, and marrying Queen Jocasta to rule plague-stricken Thebes. Oedipus' unyielding quest for truth leads to **Anagnorisis** (tragic recognition) and **Peripeteia** (reversal of fortune): discovering that the defiling murderer he curses is himself. Jocasta hangs herself, and Oedipus gouges out his own eyes with her golden brooches, embodying the blindness of mortal vision.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Antigone & The Conflict of Unwritten Laws: Following the civil war between Oedipus' sons, King Creon decrees that Polyneices' corpse remain unburied as food for dogs. Oedipus' daughter **Antigone** defies Creon's edict, performing funeral rites for her brother. Antigone appeals to the immutable, eternal, unwritten divine laws (*agraphoi nomoi*) against Creon's autocratic positive state law. Sentenced to be walled up alive in a cave, Antigone hangs herself, triggering the suicide of Creon's son and wife, leaving Creon a broken tyrant.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Explores the limits of human reason against fate, providing Sigmund Freud with the foundational psychoanalytic 'Oedipus Complex' and political theory with the archetype of civil disobedience.

## Key Terminology
- **Hamartia**: The tragic error, flaw, or misjudgment of a protagonist that brings about their downfall in classical tragedy.
- **Anagnorisis**: The moment in a drama where the protagonist discovers the true nature of their situation or identity.
- **Agraphoi Nomoi**: Unwritten, eternal divine moral laws that supersede the temporary statutory decrees of political rulers.
