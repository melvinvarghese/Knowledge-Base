# Gulliver's Travels - Jonathan Swift, Misanthropic Satire, and Political Parody

## Overview
Jonathan Swift's *Gulliver's Travels* (1726) is the supreme political and misanthropic satire in the English language, masquerading as a popular traveler's adventure journal.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Four Voyages of Lemuel Gulliver: (1) **Lilliput**: A society of six-inch-tall people engaged in absurd factional wars over which end of a boiled egg to break (Big-Endians vs Little-Endians, satirizing Catholic-Protestant wars and Whig-Tory politics); (2) **Brobdingnag**: A land of giants where Gulliver is tiny; the wise giant King laughs at European politics, concluding that humanity is 'the most pernicious race of little odious vermin that nature ever suffered to crawl upon the surface of the earth'; (3) **Laputa & The Academy of Lagado**: A floating island of detached intellectuals and scientists conducting absurd experiments (extracting sunbeams from cucumbers, softening marble into pillows), satirizing the Royal Society; (4) **The Land of the Houyhnhnms**: A society of hyper-rational, peaceful horses (**Houyhnhnms**) who govern degenerate, filthy, ape-like humans (**Yahoos**). Gulliver is so disgusted by his own humanity that upon returning to England, he cannot bear the smell of his wife and spends his days talking to horses in his stable.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Philosophical Meaning: Swift does not present Houyhnhnm rationality as a simple ideal, but as cold and sterile, creating a devastating critique of human pride (*animal rationale* vs *rationis capax*).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
The foundation of English satirical prose, shaping political commentary, science fiction, and modern dystopian writing.

## Key Terminology
- **Yahoo**: A crude, filthy, brutish creature in Swift's satire possessing human form, representing base human passions.
- **Houyhnhnm**: A race of rational, honorable horses who live in peaceful harmony and govern the feral Yahoos.
- **Lilliputian**: Extremely small or petty, derived from the diminutive inhabitants of Lilliput.
