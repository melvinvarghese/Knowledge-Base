# The Anabasis - Xenophon, The Persian Expedition, and the March of the Ten Thousand

## Overview
Xenophon's *Anabasis* ('The March Upcountry' or 'The Expedition of Cyrus', c. 370 BCE) is an ancient Greek eyewitness military memoir, chronicling the survival and epic retreat of 10,000 Greek mercenaries from deep within the Persian Empire.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Historical Context & The Battle of Cunaxa: In 401 BCE, Prince Cyrus the Younger hired an army of Greek hoplite mercenaries (the 'Ten Thousand') to seize the Achaemenid throne from his brother, King Artaxerxes II. At the Battle of Cunaxa (near Baghdad), the Greeks won tactical battlefield victory, but Cyrus was killed in combat, leaving the Greek army stranded in the heart of Mesopotamia without money, food, or allies. The Persian satrap Tissaphernes invited the senior Greek generals to a truce banquet and treacherously beheaded them all.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Democratic Military Leadership & The Cry of 'Thalatta!': The panicked Greek troops elected **Xenophon**, a young Athenian student of Socrates, as their new commander. Xenophon organized the army into a mobile, democratic voting community ('a marching polis'). For months, the Ten Thousand fought their way northward through blistering deserts, snow-covered Armenian mountain passes, and hostile tribal territories (Kurds, Carduchians). The emotional climax arrives when the vanguard reaches the summit of Mount Theches and bursts into the famous joyful collective cry: **'Thalatta! Thalatta!' ('The Sea! The Sea!')**, recognizing the Black Sea that connected them to home.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
The quintessential ancient military memoir of discipline, democratic consensus under crisis, and logistics; inspired Alexander the Great's subsequent invasion of Persia.

## Key Terminology
- **Anabasis**: Literally 'a journey upward from the coast'; classical term for an expedition into the interior of a continent.
- **Thalatta! Thalatta!**: The celebrated Greek exclamation ('The Sea! The Sea!') uttered by Xenophon's soldiers upon reaching the Black Sea.
- **Hoplite**: A heavily armed ancient Greek citizen-soldier equipped with bronze armor, round shield (hoplon), and thrusting spear.
