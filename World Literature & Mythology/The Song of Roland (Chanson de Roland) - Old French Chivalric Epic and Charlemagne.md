# The Song of Roland (Chanson de Roland) - Old French Chivalric Epic and Charlemagne

## Overview
The Song of Roland (*La Chanson de Roland*, c. 1100) is the oldest surviving major work of French literature and the supreme masterpiece of the *chansons de geste* (songs of heroic deeds).

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Historical Reality vs Crusader Legend: Based loosely on the historical Battle of Roncevaux Pass (778 CE), where Basque mountaineers ambushed Charlemagne's rearguard in the Pyrenees. Composed during the First Crusade (c. 1098), the poem transforms the historical ambush into a monumental, black-and-white ideological clash between Christianity and Islam.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Narrative Climax: Treason & The Olifant: Charlemagne's nephew **Roland**, the most valiant of the Twelve Paladins, commands the rearguard. He is betrayed by his jealous stepfather Ganelon, who coordinates with the Saracen King Marsile to ambush the Christians at Roncevaux. Outnumbered twenty-to-one, Roland's wise companion **Oliver** urges him to blow his ivory horn (**the Olifant**) to summon Charlemagne's main army. Blinding pride and chivalric vanity (*démesure*) drive Roland to refuse, insisting on glorious martyrdom. Wielding his unbreakable sword **Durendal**, Roland slaughters thousands until his rearguard is decimated. Only as he lies dying does Roland sound the Olifant with such superhuman force that his temples burst. Charlemagne returns to avenge the slaughter, capturing Saragossa and executing the traitor Ganelon.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Established the ideals of feudal loyalty, militant Christian crusading ideology, and tragic heroic chivalry in European literature.

## Key Terminology
- **Chanson de Geste**: An Old French epic poem of the 11th–14th centuries celebrating the heroic martial deeds of historical and legendary knights.
- **Olifant**: The ivory horn made from an elephant tusk used by Roland to summon Charlemagne's army at the cost of his life.
- **Durendal**: Roland's legendary indestructible sword said to contain sacred Christian relics in its golden hilt.
