# The Arthurian Legend - Camelot, the Round Table, and the Holy Grail

## Overview
The Arthurian Legend is a vast medieval cycle of Celtic folklore and French chivalric romance celebrating King Arthur, Camelot, and the quest for the Holy Grail.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Evolution from Celtic Warlord to Courtly Romance: Arthur first appears in pseudo-historical Welsh chronicles (Nennius, Geoffrey of Monmouth's *Historia Regum Britanniae*). French poet Chrétien de Troyes (12th century) introduced courtly love, Camelot, Lancelot, and the Holy Grail, transforming war chronicles into psychological chivalric romances.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Round Table & Malory's Synthesis: The Round Table symbolized egalitarian nobility among knights without hierarchical seating disputes. Sir Thomas Malory unified the sprawling French and English tales in *Le Morte d'Arthur* (1485), chronicling the tragic collapse of Camelot caused by the adulterous love of Lancelot and Guinevere and the treachery of Mordred.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Enduring ideal of chivalric honor, Christian mysticism in the Grail quest, national mythos of Britain.

## Key Terminology
- **Chivalry**: Medieval knightly code combining martial valor, Christian piety, courtly etiquette, and protection of the vulnerable.
- **Holy Grail**: Sacred chalice or vessel associated with the Last Supper and Christ's crucifixion, sought by Arthurian knights.
- **Le Morte d'Arthur**: Pivotal English prose work by Sir Thomas Malory compiling and refining the legends of King Arthur and his knights.
