# Ulysses and The Modernist Novel - James Joyce and Stream of Consciousness

## Overview
James Joyce's *Ulysses* (1922) is the defining landmark of Modernist literature, chronicling a single day in Dublin (June 16, 1904) through radical stylistic experimentation.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Homeric Parallels & The Everyday Epic: Joyce maps the events of Leopold Bloom's ordinary day onto Homer's *Odyssey*: Bloom is Odysseus, Stephen Dedalus is Telemachus, and Molly Bloom is Penelope. By elevating mundane bodily functions, wandering, and breakfast to mythic heights, Joyce transforms ordinary urban life into an epic.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Stream of Consciousness & Linguistic Innovation: Pioneers revolutionary narrative techniques—interior monologues capturing the erratic, unfiltered flow of human thought, polyphonic parodies of literary styles across English history (Oxen of the Sun), culminating in Molly Bloom's famous unpunctuated 45-page concluding soliloquy.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Shattered traditional 19th-century linear narrative structure, landmark legal battle against censorship (United States v. One Book Called Ulysses, 1933).

## Key Terminology
- **Stream of Consciousness**: Literary technique presenting the continuous, unfiltered flow of thoughts and feelings passing through a character's mind.
- **Bloomsday**: Annual June 16 celebration commemorating Leopold Bloom's fictional journey through Dublin in Ulysses.
- **Modernism**: Early 20th-century cultural movement breaking with traditional Victorian narrative conventions.
