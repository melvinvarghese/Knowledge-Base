# The Plays of Plautus and Terence - Roman Fabula Palliata, Stock Characters, and New Comedy

## Overview
Titus Maccius Plautus (c. 254–184 BCE) and Publius Terentius Afer (Terence, c. 195–159 BCE) established Roman dramatic comedy (*Fabula Palliata*), adapting Greek Hellenistic New Comedy into the foundation of Western comedic theater.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Plautus vs Terence: Contrast in Comedic Form: (1) **Plautus**: Energetic, bawdy, musical, and filled with wordplay, anachronistic Roman jokes, and slapstick. Plautus broke the fourth wall to pander directly to the boisterous Roman crowd. (2) **Terence**: An emancipated African-born former slave whose comedies featured psychological realism, refined conversational Latin, intricate dual-plots, and moral elegance. Terence coined the immortal humanist maxim in *Heauton Timorumenos*: *'Homo sum, humani nihil a me alienum puto'* ('I am human; nothing human is alien to me').

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Stock Characters & Dramatic Architecture: Both dramatists perfected classical comedic stock characters: the clever slave (*servus callidus*, who drives the plot and outsmarts his master), the braggart soldier (*miles gloriosus*), the stingy father (*senex iratus*), the young lover in despair (*adulescens*), the parasitic flatterer, and the greedy pimp (*leno*).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Plautus's *Menaechmi* (The Twin Brothers) directly inspired Shakespeare's *The Comedy of Errors*; Plautus's *Miles Gloriosus* birthed Sir John Falstaff; Roman comedy formed the DNA of modern television sitcoms.

## Key Terminology
- **Fabula Palliata**: Ancient Roman comedy written in Latin but set in Greece, featuring actors dressed in Greek cloaks (*pallium*).
- **Servus Callidus**: The 'clever slave' stock character in Roman comedy who orchestrates schemes and manipulates masters to achieve a romantic or comedic resolution.
- **Miles Gloriosus**: The 'braggart soldier' stock character who boastfully claims heroic battlefield exploits while being fundamentally cowardly.
