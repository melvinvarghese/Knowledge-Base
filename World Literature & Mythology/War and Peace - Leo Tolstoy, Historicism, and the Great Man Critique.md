# War and Peace - Leo Tolstoy, Historicism, and the Great Man Critique

## Overview
Leo Tolstoy's *War and Peace* (1869) is an epic panorama of Russian society during the Napoleonic Wars, interwoven with a radical philosophical critique of military historiography.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Panorama of War & Society: Follows the intertwined fates of aristocratic families (Bolkonsky, Bezukhov, Rostov) through peacetime ballrooms, court intrigues, and the cataclysms of Austerlitz (1805) and Borodino (1812) during Napoleon's disastrous invasion of Russia.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Tolstoy's Philosophy of History: Tolstoy radically rejects the 'Great Man' theory of history (championed by Carlyle and Hegel), demonstrating that historical events are not directed by generals like Napoleon or Kutuzov, but result from the infinitesimal, collective momentum of millions of uncoordinated individual actions.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Unsurpassed summit of psychological realism, microscopic human intimacy paired with panoramic macro-historical canvas.

## Key Terminology
- **Great Man Theory**: Historiographical theory attributing major historical events and progress to individual decisive leaders, rejected by Tolstoy.
- **Battle of Borodino**: Massive, bloody 1812 battle outside Moscow between Napoleon's Grande Armée and Russian forces under Kutuzov.
- **Pierre Bezukhov**: Searching, socially awkward protagonist of War and Peace who undergoes profound philosophical and spiritual growth.
