# Norse Eddas - The Poetic Edda, Prose Edda, and Skaldic Poetry of Snorri Sturluson

## Overview
The Norse Eddas—the *Poetic Edda* and Snorri Sturluson's *Prose Edda*—constitute the primary textual sources for our knowledge of Scandinavian mythology and Germanic heroic legend.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Poetic Edda (Elder Edda): Preserved in the 13th-century Icelandic manuscript *Codex Regius* (c. 1270). A collection of anonymous alliterative verse poems divided into mythological and heroic lays. The masterwork is **Völuspá** (Prophecy of the Seeress), where an ancient seeress recounts the creation of the cosmos out of Ginnungagap, the ash tree Yggdrasil, the golden age of the gods, the tragic slaying of Baldr, and the apocalyptic doom of **Ragnarök**. Other crucial poems include **Hávamál** (Sayings of the High One, Odin's wisdom poem on hospitality, runes, and magic) and **Þrymskviða** (Thor cross-dressing as a bride to recover his stolen hammer Mjölnir).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Prose Edda (Younger Edda): Written around 1220 by Icelandic chieftain and historian **Snorri Sturluson** as a poet's handbook. In its main section, *Gylfaginning* (The Deluding of Gylfi), the Swedish king Gylfi questions three enthroned kings (High, Just-as-High, and Third) about the gods, systematizing the Norse pantheon.

Skaldic Poetry & Kennings: Skaldic verse deployed complex, rigid meters (such as *dróttkvætt*) and elaborate figurative metaphors known as **Kennings** (e.g., 'whale-road' for the sea, 'wound-dew' for blood, 'slaughter-wolf' for a warrior).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Directly provided the mythology, characters, and linguistic structures for Richard Wagner's *Ring Cycle* and J.R.R. Tolkien's *The Lord of the Rings*.

## Key Terminology
- **Poetic Edda**: Collection of Old Norse alliterative poems preserved in the Codex Regius detailing Norse myth and heroic sagas.
- **Kenning**: A compound figurative poetic expression used in Old Norse and Anglo-Saxon poetry in place of a simple noun.
- **Völuspá**: The premier poem of the Poetic Edda detailing the creation, destruction, and rebirth of the Norse cosmos.
