# Frankenstein - Mary Shelley, Promethean Ambition, and Scientific Responsibility

## Overview
Mary Shelley's *Frankenstein; or, The Modern Prometheus* (1818) is the foundational text of modern science fiction, blending Gothic horror with a profound ethical interrogation of scientific hubris.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Frame Narrative & The Creation Scene: Written when Mary Shelley was only 18 during the 'Year Without a Summer' at Lake Geneva with Percy Bysshe Shelley and Lord Byron. Structured as an epistolary frame narrative: Captain Robert Walton's letters from an Arctic expedition frame the confession of the Swiss scientist **Victor Frankenstein**. Driven by Enlightenment scientific vanity and alchemical obsession, Victor animates a synthetic human creature assembled from charnel house cadavers using galvanism. Terrified by the creature's monstrous yellow eyes and convulsive breath, Victor flees in disgust, abandoning his creation.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Creature's Tragedy & Miltonic Allegory: The Creature is not born malevolent; he is an innocent, articulate, noble savage who learns language and literacy by reading Milton's *Paradise Lost*, Plutarch's *Lives*, and Goethe's *Werther*. Universally rejected, stoned, and shunned solely due to his horrifying physical deformity, the Creature's desire for love turns into murderous rage. He demands that Victor build him a female companion; when Victor destroys the half-made female out of fear of a race of monsters, the Creature swears: *'I will be with you on your wedding night'*, murdering Victor's bride Elizabeth.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
The ultimate modern myth of technological responsibility, scientific hubris, and the catastrophic consequences of societal prejudice.

## Key Terminology
- **The Modern Prometheus**: The subtitle of the novel referencing the Greek Titan who stole fire and suffered eternal torment for defying cosmic limits.
- **Galvanism**: The 18th-century scientific theory of inducing muscle spasms in dead tissue via electric currents, inspiring Shelley's reanimation concept.
- **Creature's Soliloquy**: The eloquent, tragic speeches wherein the monster articulates his longing for human empathy and moral companionship.
