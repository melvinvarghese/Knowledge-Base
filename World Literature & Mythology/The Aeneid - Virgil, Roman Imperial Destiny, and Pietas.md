# The Aeneid - Virgil, Roman Imperial Destiny, and Pietas

## Overview
Virgil's *Aeneid* (29–19 BCE) is Rome's national epic, connecting the mythological fall of Troy to the imperial foundation and moral destiny of the Roman Empire under Augustus.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Plot & The Roman Virtues: The Trojan prince Aeneas flees burning Troy, carrying his aging father Anchises and holding his son Ascanius. Journeying to Italy, Aeneas embodies *pietas*—devotion to duty, ancestral gods, and destiny above personal desire, exemplified when he abandons his passionate love for Queen Dido of Carthage to fulfill Rome's founding.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Imperial Teleology & The Underworld: In Book VI, the Sibyl guides Aeneas to the Underworld, where Anchises reveals a vision of Rome's glorious future: *'To spare the conquered and wage war down the proud'* (*parcere subiectis et debellare superbos*), validating the Augustan Pax Romana.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Supreme model of literary Latin epic hexameter, foundational inspiration for Dante's Divine Comedy.

## Key Terminology
- **Pietas**: Roman virtue of duty, religious devotion, and filial piety subordinating individual will to divine destiny.
- **Dido**: Legendary Queen of Carthage whose tragic suicide upon Aeneas's departure mythologized the historical Punic Wars.
- **Golden Bough**: Mythical bough that allowed Aeneas to travel through the underworld safely in Book VI of the Aeneid.
