# Inuit and Circumpolar Shamanic Mythology - Sedna, the Northern Lights, and Animal Souls

## Overview
Inuit mythology is a profound circumpolar animistic tradition born from extreme Arctic survival, where human existence requires maintaining delicate spiritual equilibrium with animal souls and the mistress of the sea.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Myth of Sedna (Nuliajuk / Taleelayuk): The central cosmological figure is **Sedna**, the Sea Mother. Courted by a deceitful petrel seabird disguised as a handsome hunter, Sedna was taken to a barren island. When her father attempted to rescue her in a kayak, the seabird stirred up a terrifying Arctic storm. Terrified for his own life, the father threw Sedna overboard. When she clung desperately to the gunwale, her father chopped off her finger joints with an axe. As her severed fingers fell into the freezing sea, they transformed into the first seals, walruses, and whales. Sedna sank to the bottom of the ocean to rule as mistress of all marine life.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Shaman's Trance (Angakkuq): When humans break taboos, dirt and moral pollution accumulate in Sedna's hair; in her anger, she traps sea mammals in her underwater home, starving human villages. The Inuit shaman (**Angakkuq**) enters a deep ecstatic trance, journeying to the ocean floor to comb out the tangles in Sedna's hair and appease her wrath, releasing game back to hunters.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Reflects a hunting philosophy where animals possess immortal souls (*Inua*) requiring profound respect and moral taboos.

## Key Terminology
- **Sedna**: The supreme Inuit marine deity whose severed fingers became sea mammals and who controls the abundance of game.
- **Angakkuq**: An Inuit shaman who acts as an intermediary between the human community and the powerful spirits of nature.
- **Inua**: The spiritual soul, person, or animating essence believed by the Inuit to dwell in all animals, lakes, and natural forces.
