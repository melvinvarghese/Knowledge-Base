# Greek Mythology - The Olympian Pantheon, Titanomachy, and Fate

## Overview
Greek Mythology comprises the sacred polytheistic narratives of the ancient Hellenic world, exploring cosmic creation, divine caprice, and human fate (*Moira*).

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Cosmogony & The Titanomachy: Hesiod's *Theogony* recounts the emergence of the cosmos from Chaos, Gaia, and Uranus. Cronus castrated Uranus; Zeus and his siblings rebelled against their father Cronus in the ten-year war known as the Titanomachy, casting the Titans into Tartarus and establishing the Twelve Olympians on Mount Olympus.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Hubris, Nemesis, & Inescapable Destiny: Human existence in Greek myth is governed by unavoidable fate. Mortality's greatest transgression is *hubris* (arrogant defiance of the gods), which inevitably summons *Nemesis* (divine retribution), exemplified in myths of Icarus, Prometheus, Oedipus, and Sisyphus.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Psychological archetypes (Jungian archetypes, Freud's Oedipus complex), enduring motifs across Western art and literature.

## Key Terminology
- **Titanomachy**: Ten-year mythological battle fought between the older generation of Titans and the younger Olympian gods.
- **Hubris**: Dangerous overconfidence, excessive pride, or arrogance towards the gods resulting in retribution.
- **Nemesis**: Goddess personifying divine retribution against individuals who succumb to hubris.
