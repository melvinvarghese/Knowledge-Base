# Hesiod's Theogony and Works and Days - The Genealogy of Gods and the Ages of Man

## Overview
Hesiod (fl. c. 700 BCE) stands alongside Homer as the co-founder of classical Greek literature, articulating systematic genealogies of the gods in *Theogony* and agrarian moral philosophy in *Works and Days*.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Theogony: The Birth of the Gods: Hesiod's *Theogony* provides the standard Greek cosmogony: in the beginning was Chaos (void), followed by Gaia (Earth), Tartarus (abyss), and Eros (desire). Gaia births Uranus (Sky), who imprisons their monstrous offspring inside the earth. Gaia crafts a jagged adamantine sickle for her youngest Titan son, **Cronus**, who ambushes and castrates his father, throwing his severed genitals into the sea (from whose foam Aphrodite is born). Cronus swarms his own children until Rhea tricks him with a swaddled stone, concealing infant **Zeus** in Crete. Zeus leads the Olympians in a ten-year war (**The Titanomachy**) against the Titans, casting them into Tartarus.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Works and Days & The Five Ages of Man: Addressed to his quarrelsome brother Perses. Hesiod recounts: (1) The myth of **Prometheus** stealing fire and Zeus's retaliatory creation of **Pandora**, whose jar releases plagues, sorrow, and disease into the world, leaving only Hope (*Elpis*) trapped inside; (2) The **Five Ages of Mankind**: Golden Age, Silver Age, Bronze Age, Heroic Age (Trojan heroes), and the current decadent, toil-filled **Iron Age**.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Formed the bedrock of Greek religious canon, memorized by schoolchildren across antiquity alongside Homer.

## Key Terminology
- **Titanomachy**: The ten-year mythological war fought between the older Titans based on Mount Othrys and the Olympians led by Zeus.
- **Pandora's Jar (Box)**: The mythological vessel containing all human evils and miseries released when Pandora opened the lid out of curiosity.
- **Dike**: The Greek concept and personification of moral justice, cosmic balance, and fair judgment.
