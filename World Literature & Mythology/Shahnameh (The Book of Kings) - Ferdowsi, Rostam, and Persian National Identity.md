# Shahnameh (The Book of Kings) - Ferdowsi, Rostam, and Persian National Identity

## Overview
The Shahnameh ('Book of Kings') is the national epic of the Persian-speaking world, a monumental poetic masterpiece of over 50,000 rhyming couplets written by Abolqasem Ferdowsi between 977 and 1010 CE.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Linguistic Preservation & Three Epochs: Following the Arab conquest of Iran and centuries of Arabization, Ferdowsi spent 30 years composing the Shahnameh in nearly pure Classical Persian (*Farsi-ye Darbari*), deliberately avoiding Arabic loanwords to resurrect Iranian cultural pride. Ferdowsi famously wrote: 'I have suffered much during these thirty years; with Persian, I have revived the Ajam [Persians].' The epic traverses three distinct historical-mythological ages: (1) **The Mythological Age**: The creation of the world, early kings (Keyumars, Jamshid), and the monstrous dragon-king Zahhak (who grew snakes from his shoulders); (2) **The Heroic Age**: The golden age of heroes dominated by the invincible champion **Rostam** and the endless wars between Iran and Turan (ruled by the evil king Afrasiab); (3) **The Historical Age**: The fall of the Achaemenid dynasty to Alexander the Great, the rise of the Sasanian Empire, and its final collapse under the Arab Islamic conquest.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Tragedy of Rostam and Sohrab: The emotional climax is the Greek-tragedy-like duel between Rostam and his unknown son, **Sohrab**. Ignorant of each other's identities in the fog of war, father and son battle fiercely on the desert sands. Rostam mortally stabs Sohrab, only to discover the onyx arm-amulet he had given Sohrab's mother, learning in devastating grief that he has slain his own son.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
The bedrock of modern Iranian, Afghan, and Tajik national identity, literature, and miniature painting.

## Key Terminology
- **Shahnameh**: The monumental Persian national epic written by Ferdowsi chronicling the history of kings from creation to the Arab conquest.
- **Rostam**: The mythical champion of ancient Iran, celebrated for superhuman strength, honor, and his loyal steed Rakhsh.
- **Rostam and Sohrab**: The tragic episode where the hero Rostam unwittingly slays his own warrior son in combat.
