# Celtic Fairy Faith and the Otherworld - Sídhe, Banshees, and Changeling Lore

## Overview
The Celtic Fairy Faith represents the deep, persistent belief in an ancient, parallel supernatural race inhabiting the mounds, bogs, and coastal islands of Ireland, Scotland, and Wales.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Sídhe & The Otherworld: When the godlike Tuatha Dé Danann were defeated by mortal Celts, they retreated beneath the earth into fairy mounds (*sídhe*), becoming the **Aos Sí** (People of the Mounds). They are not tiny winged Victorian sprites, but aristocratic, tall, beautiful, and dangerous supernatural beings who exist in a parallel dimension—the **Otherworld** (Tír na nÓg, Avalon)—where time flows unnaturally (a single day in fairyland equals centuries in the mortal world).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Supernatural Entities & Lore: (1) **The Banshee (Bean Sídhe)**: The ancestral fairy woman whose chilling, mournful keening outside a home foretells the imminent death of a family member; (2) **Changelings**: Fairies abducting human babies and replacing them with sickly, elderly fairy substitutes, an explanation for childhood illness and developmental divergence in pre-modern folklore; (3) **Púca**: A mischievous shapeshifting spirit taking the form of a dark horse, goat, or eagle that leads night travelers on wild rides.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Documented anthropologically by W.Y. Evans-Wentz (*The Fairy-Faith in Celtic Countries*, 1911) and championed by W.B. Yeats and Lady Gregory during the Irish Literary Revival.

## Key Terminology
- **Aos Sí**: The supernatural inhabitants of fairy mounds in Irish and Scottish Celtic folklore.
- **Banshee**: A supernatural spirit in Irish folklore whose wailing shriek heralds a death in an aristocratic family.
- **Changeling**: A fairy child left secretly in place of an abducted human child in European folkloric belief.
