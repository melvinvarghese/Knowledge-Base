# Native American Oral Traditions - The Raven, Spider Woman, and Sacred Creation Sagas

## Overview
Native American oral literature encompasses the sacred cosmogonies, animal transformation tales, and philosophical emergence narratives of the diverse Indigenous nations of North America.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Major Regional Mythological Traditions: (1) **Pacific Northwest (Raven Tales)**: Among the Haida, Tlingit, and Coast Salish, **Raven** is a shapeshifting trickster and creator deity who steals the sun, moon, and stars from a greedy chief's bentwood boxes to illuminate the world, and opens a clam shell on the beach to release the first human beings; (2) **Southwest (Navajo/Diné & Hopi Emergence)**: The universe is organized across successive vertical underworlds; ancestral insect beings and holy people climb through four colored worlds to emerge onto the present Fifth World. **Spider Woman (Na'ashjé'íí Asdzáá)** teaches the Diné the sacred art of loom weaving and protects heroes from monsters; (3) **Plains (White Buffalo Calf Woman)**: The Lakota sacred tradition of the celestial woman who gifted the Sacred White Buffalo Calf Pipe (*Chanunpa*) and the Seven Sacred Rites.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Ecological Reciprocity & Kinship: Unlike Western narratives centering human dominion over nature, Indigenous cosmogonies view animals, plants, rivers, and mountains as sentient kin bound by sacred duties of ecological reciprocity and balance (*Hózhǫ́* in Navajo).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Preserved through ceremony, winter hearth storytelling, and contemporary Indigenous poetry (Joy Harjo, Leslie Marmon Silko).

## Key Terminology
- **Emergence Myth**: A creation narrative where humanity and ancestral beings ascend through successive underworld levels into the present earth.
- **Hózhǫ́**: The foundational Navajo philosophical concept representing beauty, balance, harmony, and cosmic order.
- **Spider Woman**: A revered Navajo and Hopi creator and protector figure associated with weaving and protective guidance.
