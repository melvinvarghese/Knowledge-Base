# Mesopotamian Descent of Inanna - The Seven Underworld Gates and Ereshkigal

## Overview
The *Descent of Inanna* (c. 1900–1600 BCE) is an ancient Sumerian myth inscribed on cuneiform tablets, recounting goddess Inanna's ambitious descent into the netherworld, her death, and her resurrection through seasonal substitution.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Seven Gates of the Kur: Inanna, Queen of Heaven, goddess of love, beauty, fertility, and warfare, decides to visit the Underworld (**Kur**), ruled by her dark, grieving older sister **Ereshkigal**. Neti, chief gatekeeper of the underworld, requires Inanna to submit to the ancient laws of the dead. At each of the seven underworld gates, Inanna is stripped of an insignia of divine power: (1) the crown; (2) lapis lazuli beads; (3) breastplate; (4) gold bracelet; (5) rod of measurement; (6) pectorals; and (7) royal robe. Stripped naked and powerless, Inanna stands before the seven underworld judges (the Anunnaki), whose gaze turns her into a corpse that is hung on a peg.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Resurrection & Dumuzid's Sacrifice: Inanna's loyal servant Ninshubur appeals to the wisdom god Enki, who creates two asexual beings from the dirt of his fingernails (Kurgarra and Galatur) to sympathize with Ereshkigal's labor pains. Ereshkigal gifts them Inanna's corpse, which they revive with the plant and water of life. However, ancient underworld law dictates that no one leaves the underworld without providing a substitute. Returning to Earth, Inanna finds her husband, the shepherd-king **Dumuzid**, luxuriating on his throne rather than mourning her death. In furious grief, she hands Dumuzid over to the underworld demons. Dumuzid's sister Geshtinanna agrees to split the sentence, spending half the year in the underworld, explaining the seasonal agricultural cycle.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
The archetypal myth of psychological descent, ego-stripping, death, and seasonal vegetative renewal.

## Key Terminology
- **Inanna**: The Sumerian Queen of Heaven and supreme goddess of love, political power, warfare, and fertility.
- **Ereshkigal**: The solitary, fierce Queen of the Great Below (the Sumerian underworld of the dead).
- **Dumuzid**: Sumerian shepherd king and husband of Inanna, condemned to spend half the year in the underworld, symbolizing dying and rising vegetation.
