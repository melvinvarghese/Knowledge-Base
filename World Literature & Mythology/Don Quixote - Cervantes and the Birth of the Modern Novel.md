# Don Quixote - Cervantes and the Birth of the Modern Novel

## Overview
Miguel de Cervantes' *Don Quixote* (Part I: 1605, Part II: 1615) is widely regarded as the first modern psychological novel, brilliantly parodying medieval chivalric romance.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Delusion of Chivalry: Alonso Quixano, an aging country gentleman, reads so many chivalric romances that he loses his sanity, rebranding himself as knight-errant 'Don Quixote de la Mancha'. Armed in rusty armor on his nag Rocinante, he sets forth to right wrongs in the name of his idealized peasant maiden Dulcinea del Toboso, charging windmills he perceives as monstrous giants.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Quixote-Sancho Dynamic & Metafiction: The novel balances Quixote's soaring idealism against his pragmatic, proverb-spewing peasant squire, Sancho Panza. In Part II, Cervantes engages in revolutionary metafiction: characters have read Part I and recognize Quixote, exploring the porous boundary between illusion, reality, and identity.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Invented the polyphonic novel form, permanent entry of 'quixotic' into global language, foundational critique of romantic delusion.

## Key Terminology
- **Quixotic**: Exceedingly idealistic, unrealistic, and impractical in pursuit of romantic ideals.
- **Metafiction**: Fiction that self-consciously addresses the devices of fiction and explores the relationship between fiction and reality.
- **Sancho Panza**: Don Quixote's pragmatic peasant squire serving as the comedic and grounded foil to Quixote's madness.
