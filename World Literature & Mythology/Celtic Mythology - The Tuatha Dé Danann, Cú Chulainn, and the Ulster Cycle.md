# Celtic Mythology - The Tuatha Dé Danann, Cú Chulainn, and the Ulster Cycle

## Overview
Celtic mythology encompasses the polytheistic religious lore, heroic sagas, and supernatural traditions of the Celtic peoples, preserved primarily in medieval Irish and Welsh manuscript cycles.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Four Irish Manuscript Cycles: Irish Celtic literature is structured into four major cycles: (1) **The Mythological Cycle**: Preserved in the *Lebor Gabála Érenn* (Book of Invasions), detailing the arrival of the magical, godlike **Tuatha Dé Danann** (People of Danu)—including the Dagda, Lugh the Long-Armed, and the Morrígan (goddess of war). When defeated by the mortal Milesians, they retreat underground into the fairy mounds (*sídhe*), becoming the Aos Sí. (2) **The Ulster Cycle**: Heroic sagas centered on King Conchobar and the demigod warrior **Cú Chulainn** (The Hound of Ulster), renowned for his terrifying battle-frenzy (*ríastrad* or warp-spasm) and tragic defense of Ulster in the *Táin Bó Cúailnge* (Cattle Raid of Cooley). (3) **The Fenian Cycle**: Stories of Fionn mac Cumhaill and his warrior band, the Fianna. (4) **The Historical / King's Cycle**: Dynastic chronicles.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Themes & Supernatural Cosmology: Prominent motifs include the **Otherworld** (Tír na nÓg, the land of eternal youth), sacred geasa (magical taboos whose violation brings doom), shapeshifting water spirits, and the thin boundary between life and death at Samhain.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Profoundly influenced Arthurian romance, J.R.R. Tolkien's Middle-earth elven lore, and William Butler Yeats' Celtic Twilight literary revival.

## Key Terminology
- **Tuatha Dé Danann**: The supernatural race of godlike beings in Irish mythology who ruled Ireland before retreating into fairy mounds.
- **Cú Chulainn**: Preeminent heroic demigod of the Ulster Cycle, armed with the barbed spear Gáe Bulg and subject to the terrifying battle-rage ríastrad.
- **Táin Bó Cúailnge**: The epic centerpiece of the Ulster Cycle recounting Queen Medb's invasion of Ulster to steal the Brown Bull of Cooley.
