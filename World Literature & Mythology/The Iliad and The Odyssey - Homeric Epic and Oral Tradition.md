# The Iliad and The Odyssey - Homeric Epic and Oral Tradition

## Overview
Homer's *Iliad* and *Odyssey* (8th century BCE) are the cornerstone epic poems of classical antiquity, originating in oral poetic performance.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Iliad & The Rage of Achilles: Set during the tenth year of the Trojan War, the *Iliad* explores the devastating consequences of wrath (*menis*), martial honor (*kleos*), and human mortality through Achilles, Agamemnon, Hector, and Priam.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Odyssey & The Nostos Archetype: The *Odyssey* narrates the ten-year journey of cunning (*metis*) Odysseus returning home (*nostos*) to Ithaca, surviving mythical perils (the Cyclops Polyphemus, Circe, the Sirens) to reclaim his house and queen Penelope.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
The Parry-Lord oral formulaic hypothesis, foundational archetypes of Western storytelling and Greek heroic ethics.

## Key Terminology
- **Kleos**: Immortal glory and renown achieved through heroic deeds, celebrated in epic poetry.
- **Nostos**: Theme of homecoming or journey home, central to the structure of the Odyssey.
- **Formulaic Diction**: Repetitive rhythmic phrases (e.g., 'rosy-fingered Dawn') aiding memory in oral epic composition.
