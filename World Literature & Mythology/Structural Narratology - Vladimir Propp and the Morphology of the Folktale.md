# Structural Narratology - Vladimir Propp and the Morphology of the Folktale

## Overview
Vladimir Propp's *Morphology of the Folktale* (*Morfologiya skazki*, 1928) is a foundational text of Russian Formalism and structural narratology, decoding the universal structural mechanics of fairy tales.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Functions of the Dramatis Personae: Propp analyzed 100 Russian fairy tales from Afanasyev's collection, demonstrating that while the names and specific attributes of characters change infinitely across tales, their fundamental actions and plot **Functions** are finite, strictly sequential, and structural.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The 31 Narrative Functions & Seven Character Spheres: Propp identified **31 universal narrative functions**, beginning with Absentation, Interdiction, Violation, Reconnaissance, Villany/Lack, and progressing through Departure, the First Function of the Donor, the Hero's Reaction, Acquisition of a Magical Agent, Spatial Transference, Struggle, Victory, Liquidation of Lack, Return, Pursuit, Rescue, Unrecognized Arrival, Difficult Task, Solution, Recognition, Exposure, Punishment, and Wedding. Furthermore, Propp categorized all characters into **Seven Spheres of Action**: (1) The Villain; (2) The Donor (provider); (3) The Helper; (4) The Princess and her Father; (5) The Dispatcher; (6) The Hero; (7) The False Hero.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Pioneered structuralism, directly influencing Claude Lévi-Strauss, Roland Barthes, Algirdas Greimas, and modern computational narrative theory and game design.

## Key Terminology
- **Narrative Function**: An act of a character, defined from the point of view of its significance for the course of the action in a folktale.
- **Morphology**: The study of the forms, component parts, and structural relationships that compose a literary system.
- **Seven Spheres of Action**: Propp's structural classification of roles (Hero, Villain, Donor, Helper, etc.) distributing narrative functions across a tale.
