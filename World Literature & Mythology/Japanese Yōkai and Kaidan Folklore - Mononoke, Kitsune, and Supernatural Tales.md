# Japanese Yōkai and Kaidan Folklore - Mononoke, Kitsune, and Supernatural Tales

## Overview
Yōkai (supernatural monsters, spirits, and demons) and Kaidan (ghost stories) represent Japan's intricate tradition of paranormal folklore, rooted in animism, Shinto, and Buddhist moral cosmology.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Taxonomy of Yōkai: Japanese folklore categorizes hundreds of distinctive supernatural entities: (1) **Kitsune & Tanuki**: Shapeshifting animals; multi-tailed foxes (Kitsune) possess magical wisdom and illusions, while racoon dogs (Tanuki) use magical bellies and trickery to fool travelers; (2) **Tengu**: Bird-like mountain guardians with long noses or beaks, skilled in martial arts; (3) **Oni**: Horned ogres with iron clubs (*kanabō*) associated with underworld torture and thunder; (4) **Tsukumogami**: Household objects (umbrellas, sandals, kettles) that acquire a living soul and animate after reaching 100 years of service; (5) **Yūrei**: Restless human ghosts bound to the earth by vengeful grudges (*Onryō*), popularized in the white kimono and long black hair of Oiwa.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Edo Period Kaidan Boom & Lafcadio Hearn: During the Edo period, samurai and townsmen played *Hyakumonogatari Kaidankai* (A Gathering of One Hundred Ghost Stories), extinguishing one blue lantern after each tale until total darkness summoned a real spirit. In 1904, Western expatriate Lafcadio Hearn published *Kwaidan*, preserving classic tales like *Yuki-onna* (the Snow Woman) and *Mimi-nashi Hōichi* (Earless Hoichi).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Drives modern Japanese anime, manga (Studio Ghibli's *Spirited Away*, *Princess Mononoke*), and J-horror cinema (e.g., *The Ring*, *The Grudge*).

## Key Terminology
- **Yōkai**: Class of supernatural monsters, spirits, and bizarre entities in Japanese folklore.
- **Onryō**: A vengeful Japanese ghost capable of inflicting physical harm or curses upon the living to avenge a wrong.
- **Tsukumogami**: Everyday tools and household objects that acquire self-awareness and life after surviving for a century.
