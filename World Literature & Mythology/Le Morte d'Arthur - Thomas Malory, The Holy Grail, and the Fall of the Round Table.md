# Le Morte d'Arthur - Thomas Malory, The Holy Grail, and the Fall of the Round Table

## Overview
Sir Thomas Malory's *Le Morte d'Arthur* ('The Death of Arthur', completed c. 1470, printed by William Caxton in 1485) is the definitive English prose synthesis of the Arthurian legend, tracing the rise and tragic collapse of the Round Table.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Arthur's Ascent & Chivalric Fellowship: Malory translated and condensed centuries of French and English Arthurian romances into a single monumental narrative. Beginning with the miraculous sword in the stone, Arthur's conception via Merlin's magic, his receipt of Excalibur from the Lady of the Lake, and the founding of the **Round Table** at Camelot: a fellowship based on the Pentecostal Oath, commanding knights to flee treason, protect women, and do battle only in just causes.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Quest for the Holy Grail (Sangreal): The apex of spiritual chivalry is the quest for the **Holy Grail** (the cup of the Last Supper). Flawed secular knights (such as Sir Lancelot) fail due to earthly sin, while pure spiritual knights (**Sir Galahad**, Percival, Bors) achieve mystical communion with the Grail in Sarras, after which Galahad's soul ascends directly to heaven.

The Tragic Fall: Adultery and Treason: The tragedy unfolds through internal moral contradictions: Lancelot's adulterous love for Queen **Guinevere** creates an irreparable rift in Camelot. When Sir Mordred (Arthur's incestuous son) exposes their love, civil war erupts. At the catastrophic Battle of Camlann, Arthur and Mordred slay each other; Sir Bedivere casts Excalibur back into the lake, and the dying Arthur is borne away in a barge by weeping queens to the vale of Avalon (*Rex Quondam Rexque Futurus*—'The Once and Future King').

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Established English prose style on the cusp of the Renaissance and inspired Alfred Lord Tennyson's *Idylls of the King* and modern Arthurian literature.

## Key Terminology
- **Pentecostal Oath**: The moral oath sworn annually by the Knights of the Round Table to uphold justice, mercy, and chivalric honor.
- **Sangreal**: The Holy Grail, the sacred chalice of Christ sought as the ultimate quest of spiritual chivalry.
- **Rex Quondam Rexque Futurus**: The Latin inscription on Arthur's legendary tomb meaning 'The Once and Future King.'
