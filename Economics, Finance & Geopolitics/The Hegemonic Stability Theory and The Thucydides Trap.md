# The Hegemonic Stability Theory and The Thucydides Trap

## Overview
Hegemonic Stability Theory and the Thucydides Trap examine the lifecycle of global superpowers and the structural risks of war between rising and ruling powers.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Hegemonic Stability Theory (Charles Kindleberger): Argues that an open, liberal global economy requires a single dominant superpower (hegemon) willing and able to provide global public goods: open sea lanes, an international reserve currency, a market for distress goods, and emergency lending during panics. When the hegemon declines and no successor steps up (as during the 1930s transition from Britain to the US), the global system collapses into protectionism.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Thucydides Trap (Graham Allison): Derived from Thucydides' historical diagnosis of the Peloponnesian War: 'It was the rise of Athens and the fear that this inspired in Sparta that made war inevitable.' Historical analysis of 16 past cases where a rising power challenged a ruling hegemon found that 12 ended in catastrophic war.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Contemporary US-China strategic rivalry analysis, global trade governance resilience.

## Key Terminology
- **Hegemonic Stability Theory**: Theory that the international economic system is more likely to remain stable when a single nation-state is the dominant world power.
- **Thucydides Trap**: Severe structural stress caused when a rising power threatens to displace an established ruling hegemon.
- **Global Public Goods**: Non-excludable, non-rivalrous resources benefiting all states (e.g., freedom of maritime navigation, financial stability).
