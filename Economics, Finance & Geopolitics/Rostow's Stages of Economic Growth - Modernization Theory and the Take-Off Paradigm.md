# Rostow's Stages of Economic Growth - Modernization Theory and the Take-Off Paradigm

## Overview
W.W. Rostow's Stages of Economic Growth (1960) is a foundational linear-stages model of modernization theory, conceptualizing economic development as a universal sequence of structural transitions.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Five Linear Stages: Rostow posited that all societies transition through five distinct evolutionary phases: (1) **The Traditional Society**: Agrarian-dominated, pre-Newtonian technology, rigid social hierarchies, and subsistence output capped by agricultural productivity ceilings. (2) **Preconditions for Take-off**: Agricultural productivity surplus emerges, transport infrastructure (canals, railways) is built, and an entrepreneurial commercial elite emerges. (3) **The Take-off**: Critical inflection point where the investment rate rises from 5% to over 10% of national income, modern manufacturing industries expand rapidly, and political institutions commit to sustained industrialization. (4) **Drive to Maturity**: Technological sophistication diffuses across all sectors, domestic production shifts from heavy capital goods to refined consumer goods and services, and the economy integrates into global trade. (5) **Age of High Mass Consumption**: Real per-capita incomes exceed basic needs, leading to widespread consumer durables (automobiles, appliances), expansive social welfare states, and service-sector dominance.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Critiques & Teleological Limitations: Heavily criticized for assuming a rigid, Western-centric teleology that ignores post-colonial structural traps, institutional divergence, and the environmental sustainability constraints of high mass consumption.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Influenced Cold War US foreign aid strategy (Alliance for Progress) as a non-communist developmental manifesto during decolonization.

## Key Terminology
- **Take-off Stage**: Brief period of rapid industrial growth where productive investment increases dramatically, making growth self-sustaining.
- **Modernization Theory**: Sociological and economic theory asserting that traditional societies develop into modern ones through Western-style industrialization.
- **High Mass Consumption**: Final stage in Rostow's model where consumers enjoy discretionary purchasing power and modern durable goods.
