# The Tragedy of the Commons and Elinor Ostrom's Common-Pool Resources

## Overview
The Tragedy of the Commons describes the depletion of shared, open-access resources by rational self-interested individuals.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Hardin's Dilemma (1968): Garrett Hardin argued that shared pastures suffer inevitable overgrazing: each herder receives 100% of the benefit of adding an animal, but bears only a fraction of the cost of pasture destruction. Traditional solutions were state nationalization or private property privatization.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Elinor Ostrom & Polycentric Governance: Ostrom (Nobel Prize 2009) disproved Hardin by documenting thousands of sustainable self-governing community institutions (irrigation systems, Alpine pastures, fisheries). Successful commons rely on clear boundaries, participatory rulemaking, local monitoring, graduated sanctions, and low-cost conflict resolution.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Global climate policy negotiations, oceanic overfishing management, shared orbital satellite debris governance.

## Key Terminology
- **Tragedy of the Commons**: Situation where individuals with access to a shared resource act in their own interest and deplete the resource.
- **Common-Pool Resource**: Resource that is rivalrous in consumption but non-excludable (difficult to prevent access to).
- **Polycentric Governance**: Governance system with multiple governing bodies interacting to make and enforce rules within a domain.
