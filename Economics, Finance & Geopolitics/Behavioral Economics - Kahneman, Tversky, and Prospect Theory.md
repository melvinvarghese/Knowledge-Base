# Behavioral Economics - Kahneman, Tversky, and Prospect Theory

## Overview
Behavioral Economics integrates psychological insights with economics, proving that human decision-making systematically diverges from classical rational actor models (*Homo economicus*).

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Heuristics & Cognitive Biases: Daniel Kahneman and Amos Tversky identified fast heuristic mental shortcuts that produce systematic cognitive biases: Anchoring (over-relying on initial numbers), Availability (judging likelihood by ease of recall), and Representativeness (judging by stereotypical similarity).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Prospect Theory & Loss Aversion: Developed in 1979, Prospect Theory proved that people evaluate outcomes as gains and losses relative to a reference point rather than total wealth. The value function is asymmetric and S-shaped: people exhibit **loss aversion**, feeling the psychological pain of a loss roughly twice as intensely as the pleasure of an equivalent gain ($V(-\$100) \approx 2 \times |V(+\$100)|$).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Behavioral 'Nudge' theory (Thaler & Sunstein) applied to automatic retirement savings enrollments, consumer finance regulation.

## Key Terminology
- **Prospect Theory**: Behavioral economic theory describing how individuals make choices between probabilistic alternatives involving risk.
- **Loss Aversion**: Cognitive bias where the pain of losing is psychologically twice as powerful as the pleasure of gaining.
- **Nudge Theory**: Policy approach advocating indirect suggestions and positive reinforcement to influence decision-making without mandates.
