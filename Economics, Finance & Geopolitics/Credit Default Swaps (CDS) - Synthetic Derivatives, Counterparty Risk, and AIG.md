# Credit Default Swaps (CDS) - Synthetic Derivatives, Counterparty Risk, and AIG

## Overview
A Credit Default Swap (CDS) is a bilateral financial derivative contract wherein the buyer of credit protection pays a periodic fee to a seller in exchange for a payoff if a specified reference entity suffers a credit event.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Contract Mechanics & Synthetic Derivatives: The protection buyer pays a quarterly spread (expressed in basis points per year of notional value) to the protection seller. If a **Credit Event** occurs (bankruptcy, failure to pay, debt restructuring), the contract settles either via physical delivery (delivering the defaulted bond in exchange for par value) or cash settlement (paying the difference between par value and post-auction recovery value). In a **Synthetic CDO**, the portfolio consists entirely of CDS contracts rather than real physical loans, creating unlimited speculative leverage that severed derivative contracts from underlying physical capital.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Counterparty Risk & The AIG Bailout: CDS contracts were historically traded over-the-counter (OTC) without centralized clearing or mandatory capital reserves. American International Group (AIG) Financial Products sold over $400 billion in CDS protection on AAA-rated subprime CDO tranches without posting collateral. When ratings agencies downgraded the underlying CDOs, AIG faced tens of billions in immediate collateral margin calls, requiring an emergency $182 billion federal bailout to prevent the collapse of counterparties like Goldman Sachs and Deutsche Bank.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Post-crisis reforms mandated Central Counterparty (CCP) clearing, standardized documentation under ISDA master agreements, and margin requirements for non-cleared derivatives.

## Key Terminology
- **Credit Default Swap (CDS)**: Financial contract functioning like insurance against the default of an underlying sovereign or corporate debt issuer.
- **Counterparty Risk**: The risk that one party in a financial derivative transaction will default on its contractual obligations before settlement.
- **Naked CDS**: Speculative strategy of buying credit default swap protection on a company or sovereign bond without owning the underlying debt instrument.
