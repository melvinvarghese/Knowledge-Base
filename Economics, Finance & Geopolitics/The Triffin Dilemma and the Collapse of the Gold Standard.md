# The Triffin Dilemma and the Collapse of the Gold Standard

## Overview
The Triffin Dilemma describes the inherent conflict between short-term domestic monetary policy and long-term international currency reserve stability under a gold-pegged standard.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Theoretical Contradiction: Formulated by Belgian-American economist Robert Triffin in 1960: As global trade expands, the world demands more US dollars as foreign exchange reserves. To supply this liquidity, the United States must run persistent balance-of-payments deficits. However, accumulating foreign-held dollars eventually outstrips US gold reserves, undermining faith in dollar-gold convertibility.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Nixon Shock (1971): By the late 1960s, European central banks (led by France) began redeeming excess paper dollars for gold. On August 15, 1971, President Richard Nixon unilaterally suspended the dollar's convertibility into gold, ending the Bretton Woods system and inaugurating the modern era of floating fiat currencies.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Shift to pure fiat petrodollar system, emergence of floating foreign exchange (Forex) markets.

## Key Terminology
- **Triffin Dilemma**: Paradox stating that the issuer of a global reserve currency must run chronic deficits to provide global liquidity, eventually undermining confidence in the currency.
- **Nixon Shock**: Unilateral 1971 decisions by President Nixon ending the dollar's direct gold convertibility and imposing price freezes.
- **Fiat Currency**: Government-issued currency that is not backed by a physical commodity like gold but by the declaring government's decree.
