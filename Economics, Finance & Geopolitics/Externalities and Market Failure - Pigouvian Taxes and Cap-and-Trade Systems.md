# Externalities and Market Failure - Pigouvian Taxes and Cap-and-Trade Systems

## Overview
An externality is a cost or benefit incurred by a third party as an unintended consequence of an economic transaction between others, causing unregulated market allocations to become inefficient.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Market Failures & Welfare Divergence: In the presence of a **negative externality** (industrial pollution, carbon emissions), the Marginal Social Cost (MSC) exceeds Marginal Private Cost (MPC): $MSC = MPC + MEC$ (Marginal External Cost). Free markets overproduce at private equilibrium ($P_{mkt}, Q_{mkt}$), creating deadweight loss. In a **positive externality** (vaccinations, R&D education), Marginal Social Benefit (MSB) exceeds Marginal Private Benefit (MPB), leading to market underproduction.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Policy Remedies: (1) **Pigouvian Taxes**: Arthur Pigou proposed levying a per-unit tax exactly equal to the marginal external damage ($t = MEC$) at the social optimum, forcing firms to internalize the external cost. (2) **Cap-and-Trade (Tradeable Permits)**: The government sets an absolute ceiling on total emissions and auctions permits that can be traded across firms, ensuring abatement is achieved at the lowest marginal cost across the economy.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Implemented globally in carbon pricing (EU Emissions Trading System), sulfur dioxide trading (US Clean Air Act 1990), and congestion pricing schemes (London, Singapore).

## Key Terminology
- **Negative Externality**: Cost imposed on an uninvolved third party resulting from an economic transaction or activity.
- **Pigouvian Tax**: A tax levied on market activities that generate negative externalities, set equal to the external cost to achieve allocative efficiency.
- **Cap-and-Trade**: Market-based regulatory mechanism setting an emissions limit and allowing polluters to buy and sell emission allowances.
