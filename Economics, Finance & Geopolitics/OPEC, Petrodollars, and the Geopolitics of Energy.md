# OPEC, Petrodollars, and the Geopolitics of Energy

## Overview
Energy geopolitics explores how control over fossil fuel reserves, pipelines, and pricing mechanisms shapes global diplomatic power and currency hegemony.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
OPEC & The 1973 Oil Embargo: The Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries (OPEC), formed in 1960, weaponized oil production during the 1973 Yom Kippur War. Arab members imposed an embargo on Western nations supporting Israel, causing global crude prices to quadruple overnight, igniting Western stagflation.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Petrodollar System: Following the end of the gold standard, US Secretary of State Henry Kissinger negotiated a 1974 agreement with Saudi Arabia: the Saudis agreed to price all crude oil sales exclusively in US dollars and invest excess revenues ('petrodollars') in US Treasury debt; in exchange, the US guaranteed the military defense of the Kingdom, sustaining global dollar reserve demand.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Modern energy transitions, Nord Stream pipeline geopolitics, strategic petroleum reserves.

## Key Terminology
- **OPEC**: Intergovernmental organization of 12 oil-exporting nations coordinating petroleum production policies to stabilize oil markets.
- **Petrodollar**: US dollars earned by oil-exporting countries from selling crude oil, historically recycled into Western financial assets.
- **1973 Oil Shock**: Quadrupling of global oil prices caused by the Arab oil embargo following the Yom Kippur War.
