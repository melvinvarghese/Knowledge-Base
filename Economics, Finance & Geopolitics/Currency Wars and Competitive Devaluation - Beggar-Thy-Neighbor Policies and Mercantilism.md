# Currency Wars and Competitive Devaluation - Beggar-Thy-Neighbor Policies and Mercantilism

## Overview
A currency war, or competitive devaluation, is a condition in international international economics where sovereign nations deliberately manipulate exchange rates to weaken domestic currencies and boost export competitiveness.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Mechanics & The Beggar-Thy-Neighbor Dilemma: A weaker domestic currency makes a country's exports cheaper on world markets while making foreign imports artificially expensive, temporarily stimulating domestic manufacturing, employment, and current account balances. However, this gain comes at the direct expense of trading partners ('beggar-thy-neighbor'). When major economies simultaneously attempt to weaken their currencies via ultra-low policy rates, foreign reserve accumulation, or capital controls, the resulting tit-for-tat race to the bottom creates global monetary instability, asset price bubbles, and retaliatory trade tariffs without generating net aggregate global growth.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Historical Cycles & Modern Surveillance: Prominent historical episodes include the 1930s competitive devaluations following the collapse of the Gold Standard. Modern friction surfaced in 2010 when Brazilian Finance Minister Guido Mantega warned of a global currency war driven by the Federal Reserve's Quantitative Easing (which flooded emerging markets with yield-seeking hot money, driving uncompetitive currency appreciation).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Monitored under IMF Article IV consultations and US Treasury Semi-Annual Reports on Macroeconomic and Foreign Exchange Policies of Major Trading Partners (designating currency manipulators).

## Key Terminology
- **Competitive Devaluation**: Deliberate state intervention to lower the value of a national currency to gain an unfair trading advantage over foreign competitors.
- **Beggar-Thy-Neighbor**: Economic policy that solves a domestic economic problem by directly worsening the economic problems of other nations.
- **Currency Manipulator**: Formal statutory designation applied to countries that artificially suppress exchange rates to amass excessive bilateral trade surpluses.
