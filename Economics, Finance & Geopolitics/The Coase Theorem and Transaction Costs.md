# The Coase Theorem and Transaction Costs

## Overview
The Coase Theorem, formulated by Ronald Coase (1960), establishes that in the presence of externalities, private parties will bargain to an efficient allocation of resources if transaction costs are zero and property rights are well-defined.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Mathematical & Legal Formulation: Consider a factory emitting pollution that damages a nearby farm. Coase proved that regardless of whether the initial legal right is assigned to the factory (pollute) or the farmer (clean air), costless bargaining yields identical efficient production levels: the party valuing the right most will purchase it from the other.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Significance of Positive Transaction Costs: In the real world, transaction costs (information search, negotiation, contract enforcement) are never zero. Therefore, Coase demonstrated that initial legal entitlements and liability rules (*The Problem of Social Cost*) matter crucially in determining economic efficiency.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Spectrum frequency allocation auctions (FCC), carbon emissions cap-and-trade markets, tort liability assignment.

## Key Terminology
- **Coase Theorem**: Proposition that private parties can solve externalities efficiently if property rights are defined and transaction costs are negligible.
- **Transaction Costs**: Costs incurred in making an economic exchange, including search, bargaining, and enforcement costs.
- **Externality**: Cost or benefit incurred by a third party as a consequence of an economic transaction.
