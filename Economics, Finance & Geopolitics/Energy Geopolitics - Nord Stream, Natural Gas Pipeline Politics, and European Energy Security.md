# Energy Geopolitics - Nord Stream, Natural Gas Pipeline Politics, and European Energy Security

## Overview
Energy geopolitics examines the strategic struggle among nations to control fossil fuel extraction, transit pipeline networks, liquefied natural gas (LNG) export terminals, and clean energy supply chains.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Pipeline Politics & Nord Stream: Unlike oil, which can be moved flexibly across oceans via supertankers, natural gas was historically bound to fixed terrestrial pipelines, creating intense bilateral geopolitical dependencies between producer states, transit nations, and consumer hubs. Russia's state-owned Gazprom built massive undersea pipelines—**Nord Stream 1 and 2** directly under the Baltic Sea to Germany, and TurkStream to Southern Europe—bypassing traditional overland transit routes through Ukraine and Poland. The 2022 sabotage of Nord Stream pipelines dramatically ended decades of cheap Russian piped gas feeding German heavy industry, forcing an immediate, massive continental pivot toward US and Qatari **Liquefied Natural Gas (LNG)** and floating regasification units (FSRUs).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Green Energy Transition Geopolitics: The global transition away from fossil fuels does not eliminate energy geopolitics; it shifts power from petrostates to **electrostates** that dominate solar photovoltaic manufacturing, wind turbine component supply, and critical mineral processing.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Directly influences inflation trajectories, industrial competitiveness, and sovereign strategic autonomy across Europe and Asia.

## Key Terminology
- **Liquefied Natural Gas (LNG)**: Natural gas cooled to -162°C (-260°F) into a liquid state, reducing its volume by 600 times to enable intercontinental oceanic shipping.
- **Pipeline Lock-In**: Economic and geopolitical vulnerability resulting from heavy capital investment in fixed, immobile physical energy pipelines.
- **Electrostate**: A country whose geopolitical power and economic influence are derived from renewable energy generation, battery storage, and green tech supply chains.
