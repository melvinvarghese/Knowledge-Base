# Foreign Exchange (Forex) Markets - Purchasing Power Parity and Covered Interest Parity

## Overview
The foreign exchange (Forex) market is the world's largest, most liquid financial market, where global currencies are traded continuously around the clock in decentralized over-the-counter transactions.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Equilibrium Parity Conditions: (1) **Purchasing Power Parity (PPP)**: The Law of One Price dictates that identical goods should sell for the same price across different countries when converted to a common currency: $S = P / P^*$. Deviations explain over- or undervaluation of real exchange rates. (2) **Covered Interest Parity (CIP)**: Eliminates riskless arbitrage between spot exchange rates $S$, forward rates $F$, and interest rates across two nations: $\frac{F}{S} = \frac{1 + i_d}{1 + i_f}$. When CIP holds, forward premiums offset nominal interest rate differentials. (3) **Uncovered Interest Parity (UIP)**: Relies on expected future spot rates, often violated empirically by the **Currency Carry Trade**, where investors borrow in low-yielding currencies (e.g., Japanese Yen) to invest in high-yielding currencies.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Exchange Rate Regimes: Operates across a continuum from free-floating (US dollar, Euro, Yen) and managed floats to pegged regimes and currency boards (Hong Kong dollar).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Daily turnover exceeds $7.5 trillion (BIS Triennial Survey), dominated by cross-border commercial trade hedging, central bank reserves management, and speculative algorithmic liquidity.

## Key Terminology
- **Purchasing Power Parity (PPP)**: Economic theory stating that exchange rates between currencies are in equilibrium when their purchasing power is equal.
- **Covered Interest Parity (CIP)**: Theoretical condition in which the interest rate differential between two countries is equal to the forward exchange premium.
- **Currency Carry Trade**: Financial strategy of borrowing funds in a low-interest-rate currency to invest in assets denominated in a high-interest-rate currency.
