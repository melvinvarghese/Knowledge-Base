# Alfred Thayer Mahan and Maritime Geostrategy - Command of the Sea and Naval Hegemony

## Overview
Alfred Thayer Mahan's doctrine of sea power is the foundational text of modern naval geostrategy, positing that control of sea lanes and maritime trade is the primary determinant of national greatness and global hegemonic power.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Elements of Sea Power: Articulated in his seminal work, *The Influence of Sea Power upon History, 1660–1783* (1890). Mahan analyzed how Great Britain rose to global preeminence by securing undisputed **Command of the Sea**. Mahan identified six fundamental geographic and national elements of sea power: geographical position, physical conformation, extent of territory, size of population, national character, and character of the government. True sea power requires a synergistic trinity: (1) A robust domestic merchant marine and commercial trade fleet; (2) A powerful blue-water combat navy capable of destroying enemy fleets in decisive fleet actions; (3) A global network of fortified naval bases, coaling stations, and strategic maritime chokepoints to sustain forward naval power projection.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Historical & Modern Influence: Mahan's writings triggered a worldwide naval arms race prior to WWI (Kaiser Wilhelm II ordered copies for every German naval officer). Theodore Roosevelt used Mahanian theory to justify building the Great White Fleet, annexing Hawaii and the Philippines, and constructing the Panama Canal.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Directly underpins modern US Navy freedom of navigation operations (FONOPs) and the rapid naval modernization and aircraft carrier expansion of China's People's Liberation Army Navy (PLAN).

## Key Terminology
- **Command of the Sea**: The military control of maritime communication and transportation routes, denying their use to an adversary while protecting them for one's own fleet.
- **Blue-Water Navy**: A maritime force capable of operating globally across deep ocean waters, projecting power far from domestic territorial waters.
- **Chokepoint**: A narrow strategic maritime waterway, such as a strait or canal, whose blockage can choke global commerce and naval transit.
