# The European Single Market and the Four Freedoms

## Overview
The European Single Market is the world's most integrated regional trade area, built upon the free movement of goods, services, capital, and labor.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Four Freedoms: Codified in the 1986 Single European Act and implemented in 1993: 1. Free movement of Goods (elimination of customs duties and non-tariff regulatory barriers); 2. Free movement of Services (cross-border service provision and professional credential recognition); 3. Free movement of Capital (unrestricted investment and currency transfers); 4. Free movement of Persons (citizens' right to live, work, and study in any member state).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Harmonization & The Brussels Effect: Replaced divergent national standards with mutual recognition and unified EU directives. Through the 'Brussels Effect' (Anu Bradford), the EU's massive market size compels global multinational corporations to adhere to strict European standards (GDPR privacy, environmental regulations) worldwide.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Economic foundation of European integration, central battleground in the UK Brexit referendum.

## Key Terminology
- **Four Freedoms**: Fundamental economic freedoms of the EU internal market: free movement of goods, services, capital, and people.
- **Brussels Effect**: Phenomenon whereby the European Union unilaterally sets global market regulations through market power.
- **Non-Tariff Barrier**: Trade restriction, such as a quota, embargo, or regulatory standard, used by countries to restrict imports.
