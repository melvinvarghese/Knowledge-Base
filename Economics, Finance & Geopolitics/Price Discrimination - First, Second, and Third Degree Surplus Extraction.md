# Price Discrimination - First, Second, and Third Degree Surplus Extraction

## Overview
Price discrimination is a microeconomic pricing strategy where a firm with market power sells identical goods or services to different buyers at different prices to extract consumer surplus.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Pigou's Three Degrees: Pioneered by Arthur Pigou (1920): (1) **First-Degree (Perfect) Price Discrimination**: The seller charges each individual buyer their exact maximum willingness to pay ($P = WTP$). All consumer surplus is converted into producer surplus; deadweight loss is eliminated, achieving allocative efficiency but extreme distributional inequality. (2) **Second-Degree (Menu Pricing / Versioning)**: Prices vary according to quantity consumed or product quality tiers (bulk discounts, airline business vs economy class), inducing consumers to self-select based on preferences. (3) **Third-Degree (Market Segmentation)**: The firm segments the market into distinct groups based on observable traits with differing price elasticities (student/senior discounts, geographic software pricing, international pharmaceutical pricing).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Prerequisites & Arbitrage Prevention: Successful price discrimination requires three conditions: market power, identifiable consumer elasticities, and strict prevention of **resale/arbitrage** (geoblocking, digital rights management, non-transferable tickets).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Central to modern algorithmic pricing on e-commerce platforms, airline yield management, streaming subscription tiers, and pharmaceutical pricing.

## Key Terminology
- **Consumer Surplus**: Difference between the total amount consumers are willing to pay for a good and the actual amount they pay.
- **First-Degree Price Discrimination**: Charging each customer the absolute maximum they are willing to pay, capturing 100% of consumer surplus.
- **Arbitrage**: Simultaneous buying in a low-price market and selling in a higher-price market to exploit price differentials, undermining price discrimination.
