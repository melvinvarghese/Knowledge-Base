# Classical Economics - Adam Smith, The Wealth of Nations, and The Invisible Hand

## Overview
Classical Economics, founded by Adam Smith in *The Wealth of Nations* (1776), established political economy as an empirical discipline grounded in free exchange and division of labor.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Division of Labor & Capital Accumulation: Smith analyzed pin manufacturing to demonstrate that decomposing complex production into specialized tasks dramatically multiplies labor productivity. Savings and capital accumulation drive investment, technological improvement, and economic expansion.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Invisible Hand & Spontaneous Order: Smith demonstrated that when individuals pursue their enlightened self-interest within competitive markets governed by the rule of law, they are led as if by an 'invisible hand' to allocate resources efficiently, maximizing aggregate social wealth without centralized state direction.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Theoretical foundation of free-market capitalism, critique of mercantilism, defense of competitive commerce.

## Key Terminology
- **Invisible Hand**: Metaphor introduced by Adam Smith describing the unintended social benefits resulting from individual self-interested actions.
- **Division of Labor**: Separation of tasks in any economic system so that participants specialize to increase productive efficiency.
- **Mercantilism**: Pre-classical economic policy where states accumulated bullion through trade surpluses and strict protectionist tariffs.
