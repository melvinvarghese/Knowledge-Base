# The Great Depression of 1929 - The Wall Street Crash, Bank Runs, and the New Deal

## Overview
The Great Depression (1929–1939) was the deepest, most widespread economic catastrophe of modern industrial history, fundamentally transforming economic theory, banking architecture, and government intervention.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Cascade of Contraction: Triggered by the Wall Street stock market crash of October 29, 1929 ('Black Tuesday'), which burst an asset bubble fueled by speculative margin lending. Over the next three years, US real GDP contracted by over 25%, industrial production dropped by nearly half, and unemployment skyrocketed to 24.9%. Milton Friedman and Anna Schwartz (*A Monetary History of the United States*) proved that the crisis turned catastrophic because the Federal Reserve failed as a lender of last resort: passive monetary policy allowed one-third of all US commercial banks to fail, shrinking the broad money supply ($M_2$) by over 30% and causing a brutal debt-deflation spiral.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Global Propagation & The New Deal: The Smoot-Hawley Tariff Act (1930) sparked retaliatory tariffs, collapsing world trade by 66%. Franklin D. Roosevelt's **New Deal** dismantled the classical laissez-faire consensus: establishing the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation (FDIC) to end bank runs, creating the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC), passing the Glass-Steagall Act separating commercial from investment banking, and severing the domestic link to the gold standard.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Provided the intellectual impetus for John Maynard Keynes' *General Theory* (1936), birthing the entire discipline of modern macroeconomics.

## Key Terminology
- **Bank Run**: Phenomenon where a large number of bank customers withdraw their deposits simultaneously due to fears of bank insolvency.
- **Glass-Steagall Act**: 1933 US federal statute that strictly separated commercial deposit banking from speculative Wall Street investment banking.
- **Smoot-Hawley Tariff Act**: Protectionist 1930 US legislation that triggered a global trade war, severely deepening the worldwide depression.
