# Venture Capital and Private Equity - Leveraged Buyouts (LBOs), Carried Interest, and Growth Equity

## Overview
Private equity (PE) and venture capital (VC) represent alternative asset classes that provide non-public equity and debt capital to corporations across stages ranging from early startup seed funding to mature corporate restructuring.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Structures & The GP-LP Governance Model: Both VC and PE operate as closed-end limited partnerships typically structured with a 10-year fund life. The **General Partner (GP)** manages the fund and sources deals, while **Limited Partners (LPs)** (pension funds, university endowments, sovereign wealth funds) supply capital. Compensation follows the standard **'2 and 20' model**: a 2% annual management fee on committed capital and a 20% **Carried Interest** (performance profit share) subject to achieving a hurdle rate (typically 8% IRR).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Venture Capital vs Leveraged Buyouts (LBO): **Venture Capital** finances high-risk, unproven technology startups characterized by exponential growth potential (power-law distribution, where one 'fund-returner' covers dozens of total write-offs). **Private Equity / Leveraged Buyouts (LBOs)** acquire mature, cash-flow-positive enterprises using high debt leverage (typically 60-80% debt via junk bonds and syndicated bank loans), using the target company's own cash flows to service the debt while executing operational restructuring, multiple expansion, or spin-offs.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Drives major technological commercialization (Silicon Valley innovation ecosystem) but faces political scrutiny over debt loading, aggressive cost-cutting, and tax treatment of carried interest as capital gains.

## Key Terminology
- **Leveraged Buyout (LBO)**: The acquisition of another company using a significant amount of borrowed money (bonds or loans) to meet the cost of acquisition.
- **Carried Interest**: Share of any profits made on an investment that is paid to the fund manager as compensation, often taxed at capital gains rates.
- **Limited Partner (LP)**: An investor in a private equity or venture capital fund who provides capital but has limited liability and no operational control.
