# The Bretton Woods System - IMF, World Bank, and the Gold Standard

## Overview
The Bretton Woods Conference (1944) established the post-World War II international monetary order, pegging global currencies to the US dollar backed by gold.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Architectural Design: Forty-four Allied nations gathered at Bretton Woods, New Hampshire, debating plans between John Maynard Keynes (advocating an international clearing union and global currency 'Bancor') and Harry Dexter White. White's plan prevailed: the US dollar was pegged to gold at $35 an ounce, and all other currencies pegged their exchange rates to the dollar.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Foundational Institutions: Created the International Monetary Fund (IMF) to manage short-term balance-of-payments crises and maintain exchange rate stability, and the International Bank for Reconstruction and Development (now the World Bank) to finance post-war reconstruction and global development.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Facilitated 25 years of rapid post-war global trade expansion and economic growth (the 'Golden Age of Capitalism').

## Key Terminology
- **Bretton Woods Agreement**: 1944 agreement establishing the post-WWII pegged exchange rate system centered on the gold-convertible US dollar.
- **IMF**: International Monetary Fund; global organization promoting financial stability and facilitating international trade.
- **World Bank**: International financial institution providing loans and grants to low- and middle-income countries for capital projects.
