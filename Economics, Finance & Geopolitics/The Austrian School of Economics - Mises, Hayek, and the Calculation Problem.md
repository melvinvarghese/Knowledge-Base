# The Austrian School of Economics - Mises, Hayek, and the Calculation Problem

## Overview
The Austrian School of Economics emphasizes methodological individualism, subjective value theory, spontaneous order, and the essential information-signaling role of market prices.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Socialist Calculation Problem: Ludwig von Mises (1920) argued that socialist central planning must inevitably fail because in the absence of private property in capital goods, there are no market prices, rendering rational economic calculation of scarce capital allocation impossible.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Hayek & Prices as Dispersed Knowledge: Friedrich Hayek (*The Use of Knowledge in Society*, 1945) demonstrated that relevant economic knowledge is fragmented across millions of individuals. The price mechanism operates as a telecommunication system, instantaneously aggregating localized, tacit information about supply and demand without any centralized coordinator.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Austrian Business Cycle Theory (credit expansion causes malinvestment bubbles), influence on libertarian philosophy and cryptocurrency design.

## Key Terminology
- **Economic Calculation Problem**: Critique asserting that a socialist economy cannot rationally allocate resources without market-determined capital prices.
- **Subjective Theory of Value**: Economic principle stating that value is determined by individual subjective preferences rather than labor time.
- **Spontaneous Order**: Self-organizing social structures that emerge as the unintended consequence of human action rather than design.
