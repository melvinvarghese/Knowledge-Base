# Public Goods and Club Goods - Non-Rivalry, Non-Excludability, and Free-Rider Dilemmas

## Overview
Goods in economic theory are classified into a four-way taxonomy based on two key physical and economic dimensions: rivalry in consumption and excludability in access.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Four-Sector Taxonomy: (1) **Private Goods**: Rivalrous and Excludable (food, clothing, housing). (2) **Common-Pool Resources**: Rivalrous and Non-Excludable (open ocean fisheries, clean air, timber; susceptible to tragedy of the commons). (3) **Club Goods**: Non-Rivalrous and Excludable (subscription software, toll bridges, satellite TV; can be provisioned by private firms via pricing). (4) **Pure Public Goods**: Non-Rivalrous and Non-Excludable (national defense, basic scientific research, lighthouses, clean atmosphere).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Free-Rider Problem: Because individuals cannot be excluded from consuming public goods once produced, and one person's consumption does not diminish another's, rational self-interested actors have no incentive to pay voluntarily (free-riding). Private markets collapse due to under-provision, necessitating sovereign state taxation, legal enforcement, or public subsidization (Samuelson condition: $\sum MRS = MRT$).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Core theoretical rationale for public funding of national defense, basic research (DARPA, CERN, NIH), disease surveillance, and open-source software maintenance.

## Key Terminology
- **Non-Rivalrous**: Characteristic of a good where one person's consumption does not diminish its availability for others to consume.
- **Non-Excludable**: Characteristic where it is technologically or economically impossible to prevent non-payers from accessing the good.
- **Free-Rider Problem**: Market failure where consumers benefit from non-excludable public resources without contributing to their cost.
