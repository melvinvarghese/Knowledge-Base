# Supply-Side Economics - The Laffer Curve and Deregulation

## Overview
Supply-Side Economics argues that economic growth is most effectively stimulated by lowering barriers to production through marginal tax cuts, deregulation, and free-market incentives.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Laffer Curve: Drawn on a napkin by Arthur Laffer in 1974, illustrating the relationship between tax rates and tax revenue: At tax rates of 0% and 100%, tax revenue is zero. Between these extremes lies a revenue-maximizing tax rate $T^*$. If rates exceed $T^*$, cutting taxes increases work and investment incentives enough to expand the tax base and increase total revenues.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Reaganomics & Deregulation: Adopted by President Ronald Reagan (1981 Economic Recovery Tax Act), slashing top marginal income taxes from 70% to 28% and deregulating energy, telecommunications, and finance to stimulate capital investment.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Modern corporate tax rate competitiveness, ongoing empirical disputes over revenue self-financing claims.

## Key Terminology
- **Laffer Curve**: Theoretical curve showing the relationship between tax rates and resulting government tax revenue.
- **Supply-Side Economics**: Macroeconomic theory arguing that economic growth is maximized by lowering taxes and decreasing regulation.
- **Reaganomics**: Neoliberal economic policies associated with U.S. President Ronald Reagan emphasizing tax cuts and free enterprise.
