# Market Structures - Perfect Competition, Monopolistic Competition, Oligopoly, and Monopoly

## Overview
Market structure defines the organizational and competitive environment in which firms operate, determined by the number of firms, barriers to entry, product differentiation, and pricing power.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Spectrum of Competition: (1) **Perfect Competition**: Infinite buyers and sellers, homogeneous products, perfect information, zero barriers to entry; firms are price-takers ($P = MC = \text{min } ATC$), earning zero economic profit in the long run. (2) **Monopolistic Competition** (Chamberlin): Many sellers, differentiated products, low barriers; downward-sloping demand curves, non-price competition (branding, marketing), zero long-run economic profit due to free entry. (3) **Oligopoly**: Few large dominant firms, high barriers; strategic interdependence modeled via game theory. (4) **Monopoly**: Single seller, unique product with no substitutes, insurmountable barriers; firm is a price-maker maximizing profits where $MR = MC$, producing deadweight loss and extracting consumer surplus.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Antitrust & Modern Digital Platforms: The rise of two-sided network platforms (Google, Amazon, Meta) has challenged traditional market definitions, where zero consumer pricing obscures monopolistic data extraction and monopsonistic labor market power.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Guides global antitrust enforcement (DOJ, FTC, European Commission DG COMP) using the Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (HHI) to review merger approvals.

## Key Terminology
- **Perfect Competition**: Ideal market state where identical goods, perfect information, and free entry ensure firms make zero economic profits.
- **Deadweight Loss**: Loss of total economic surplus (consumer plus producer) caused by market inefficiencies, monopolies, or taxes.
- **Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (HHI)**: Quantitative measure of market concentration calculated by summing the squares of individual firm market shares.
