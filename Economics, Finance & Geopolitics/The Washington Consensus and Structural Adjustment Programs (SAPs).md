# The Washington Consensus and Structural Adjustment Programs (SAPs)

## Overview
Structural Adjustment Programs (SAPs) were conditionality-laden loans provided by the IMF and World Bank to developing nations facing economic debt crises during the 1980s and 1990s.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Conditionality Architecture: Loans required borrowing governments to enact Washington Consensus reforms: drastic public budget austerity, currency devaluation, trade tariff elimination, privatization of utilities, and dismantling of consumer food and fuel subsidies.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Developmental Critique: While stabilizing balance-of-payments crises, critics (Joseph Stiglitz) demonstrated that SAPs frequently precipitated devastating social consequences: collapse of public healthcare and education budgets, deep recessions, and rising inequality across sub-Saharan Africa and Latin America ('The Lost Decade').

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Transition toward Poverty Reduction Strategy Papers (PRSPs), ongoing debate over IMF fiscal surveillance.

## Key Terminology
- **Structural Adjustment**: Economic policies which countries must follow in order to qualify for World Bank and IMF loans.
- **Conditionality**: Policy and structural reforms that recipient countries must implement to access international emergency lending.
- **Lost Decade**: Period of economic stagnation and debt crises experienced by Latin America during the 1980s.
