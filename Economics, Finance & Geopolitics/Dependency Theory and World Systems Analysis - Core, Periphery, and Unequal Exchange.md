# Dependency Theory and World Systems Analysis - Core, Periphery, and Unequal Exchange

## Overview
Dependency Theory and Immanuel Wallerstein's World Systems Analysis offer structuralist critiques of modernization theory, arguing that underdevelopment in the Global South is actively produced by exploitation from the Global North.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Core, Periphery, and Semiperiphery: Emerging in Latin America in the 1960s (Raúl Prebisch, Fernando Henrique Cardoso, André Gunder Frank), Dependency Theory rejects the idea that underdeveloped nations are merely earlier stages of developed ones. Instead, global capitalism is structured into a dynamic hierarchy: (1) **The Core**: Wealthy industrialized nations characterized by high-wage labor, capital-intensive manufacturing, and advanced technological monopolies; (2) **The Periphery**: Developing agrarian/resource nations characterized by low-wage labor, extractive mining, and raw commodity export; (3) **The Semiperiphery**: Intermediate industrializing economies that buffer systemic tensions. **Unequal Exchange** funnels surplus value and capital outward from the periphery into the core, locking developing nations into permanent external debt and technological dependence.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Structural Delinking vs East Asian Counter-Evidence: Dependency theorists advocated for socialist revolution or national 'delinking' from global trade. However, the spectacular rise of the East Asian Tigers via export-oriented capitalism challenged the fatalism of permanent peripheral stagnation.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Foundational for post-colonial critique, structuralist economics, and contemporary analyses of neocolonial resource extraction in Africa and Latin America.

## Key Terminology
- **Core-Periphery Model**: Spatial and structural economic model dividing the world into dominant capitalist centers and exploited peripheral supplier nations.
- **Unequal Exchange**: Theoretical mechanism where trade between high-wage core nations and low-wage peripheral nations perpetually transfers economic surplus to the core.
- **World Systems Theory**: Macroscopic historical perspective viewing world history and social change as an integrated, evolving capitalist world-economy.
