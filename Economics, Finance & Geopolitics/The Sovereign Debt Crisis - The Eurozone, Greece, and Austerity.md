# The Sovereign Debt Crisis - The Eurozone, Greece, and Austerity

## Overview
The European Sovereign Debt Crisis (2010–2015) exposed the structural vulnerability of operating a unified monetary union (the Euro) without a unified fiscal union.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Structural Flaw of the Eurozone: Member nations adopted a single currency and single interest rate set by the European Central Bank (ECB) but maintained decentralized sovereign national budgets. Peripheral nations (Greece, Ireland, Portugal, Spain, Italy—the PIIGS) enjoyed low borrowing costs in the 2000s, accumulating massive public and private debt imbalances without sovereign exchange-rate devaluation mechanisms.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Bailouts & Draghi's 'Whatever It Takes': Greece's 2009 revelation of hidden deficits sparked bond yield spikes and banking contagion. The 'Troika' (ECB, EU Commission, IMF) imposed harsh austerity packages. The crisis was ultimately halted in 2012 when ECB President Mario Draghi famously declared that the ECB was prepared to do 'whatever it takes' to preserve the Euro.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Creation of the European Stability Mechanism (ESM), debate over austerity versus growth stimulus in depressed economies.

## Key Terminology
- **Sovereign Debt**: Bonds issued by a national government in foreign or domestic currencies to finance public expenditures.
- **The Troika**: Decision-making group composed of the European Commission, the European Central Bank, and the International Monetary Fund.
- **Whatever It Takes**: Mario Draghi's iconic 2012 speech committing the full monetary power of the ECB to defend the Euro against speculative collapse.
