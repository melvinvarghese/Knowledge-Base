# Terms of Trade and the Prebisch-Singer Hypothesis - Commodity Dependency in the Global South

## Overview
Terms of Trade (ToT) measures the relative price of a nation's exports compared to its imports, serving as a critical determinant of national purchasing power and long-term economic development trajectories.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Theoretical Foundations & Formulation: Net barter Terms of Trade is defined as the ratio of an export price index to an import price index: $ToT = \left(\frac{P_x}{P_m}\right) \times 100$. An increase in ToT means a nation can purchase more imported manufactured goods per unit of exports, improving real domestic welfare.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Prebisch-Singer Hypothesis: Formulated independently by Raúl Prebisch and Hans Singer (1950), the hypothesis posits that over the long term, the terms of trade between primary commodities (agricultural goods, raw mineral ores) and manufactured industrial goods deteriorate systematically against primary commodity exporters. This structural decline occurs because: (1) Industrial manufactured goods feature higher **Income Elasticity of Demand** (Engel's Law: as world income rises, the proportion spent on food and raw materials drops); (2) Industrialized core economies possess organized labor and monopoly pricing power that capture productivity gains as higher wages, whereas developing agrarian economies face intense commodity competition that forces productivity gains into lower global prices.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Provided the foundational intellectual justification for **Import Substitution Industrialization (ISI)** policies across Latin America, Africa, and post-colonial nations throughout the mid-20th century.

## Key Terminology
- **Terms of Trade (ToT)**: Ratio of a country's export prices to its import prices, indicating how many imports can be bought with a given volume of exports.
- **Prebisch-Singer Hypothesis**: Observation that the price of primary commodities declines over time relative to manufactured goods, disadvantaging developing nations.
- **Import Substitution Industrialization (ISI)**: Development policy advocating replacing foreign imports with domestic industrial production behind high protective tariff walls.
