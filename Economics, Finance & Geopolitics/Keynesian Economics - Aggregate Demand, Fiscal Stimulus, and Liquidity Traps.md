# Keynesian Economics - Aggregate Demand, Fiscal Stimulus, and Liquidity Traps

## Overview
Keynesian Economics, developed by John Maynard Keynes during the Great Depression, revolutionized macroeconomics by establishing that aggregate demand drives economic output and employment.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Sticky Wages & The Paradox of Thrift: Classical economics assumed prices and wages would instantly fall to clear markets. Keynes showed that wages are 'sticky downward' due to labor contracts and psychology. During recessions, aggregate demand collapses; individual attempts to save more (Paradox of Thrift) reduce overall consumer spending, deepening unemployment: $Y = C + I + G + (X - M)$.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Counter-Cyclical Fiscal Policy: Keynes argued that when the economy enters a 'liquidity trap' (where interest rates near zero fail to stimulate private investment), governments must step in with counter-cyclical deficit spending on public infrastructure to inject aggregate demand and activate the fiscal multiplier.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Foundational framework for modern fiscal stimulus packages, government automatic stabilizers (unemployment insurance), modern macroeconomic modeling.

## Key Terminology
- **Aggregate Demand**: Total demand for final goods and services in an economy at a given time and price level.
- **Liquidity Trap**: Condition where interest rates fall near zero and monetary policy becomes ineffective at stimulating borrowing.
- **Fiscal Multiplier**: Ratio of change in national income to the exogenous change in government spending that caused it.
