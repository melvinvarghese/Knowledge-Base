# Strategic Chokepoints - The Strait of Malacca, Hormuz, Bab-el-Mandeb, and Global Maritime Chokehold

## Overview
Strategic maritime chokepoints are narrow, highly congested waterways that connect major oceans and seas, where any military blockage, piracy, or geopolitical disruption threatens catastrophic shocks to global trade and energy markets.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Core Global Maritime Chokepoints: Over 80% of global commercial merchandise trade travels by sea. Five primary chokepoints concentrate the world's commercial vulnerability: (1) **Strait of Malacca**: Narrow waterway between Malaysia, Indonesia, and Singapore; carries over 25% of all global oil shipments and 60% of China's total energy imports (the 'Malacca Dilemma'); (2) **Strait of Hormuz**: Channel connecting the Persian Gulf to the open ocean; world's most critical oil transit chokepoint, carrying over 20 million barrels per day (one-fifth of global petroleum consumption); (3) **Bab-el-Mandeb & Suez Canal**: Strategic passage between the Horn of Africa and the Arabian Peninsula leading into the Red Sea and Suez Canal; vulnerable to regional insurgent missile and drone attacks (e.g., Houthi maritime interdiction 2023-2024); (4) **Panama Canal**: Connecting the Atlantic and Pacific oceans, constrained by freshwater reservoir droughts; (5) **Turkish Straits (Bosphorus and Dardanelles)**: Governed by the 1936 Montreux Convention, regulating naval access between the Black Sea and the Mediterranean.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Vulnerability & Economic Shockwaves: Closure of any primary chokepoint forces cargo container ships and oil supertankers to re-route thousands of miles around Africa's Cape of Good Hope, adding 10-14 transit days, burning millions in extra bunker fuel, and spiking global inflation.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Anchor priorities for naval deployment, maritime coalition task forces (Combined Maritime Forces, Operation Prosperity Guardian), and strategic energy reserves.

## Key Terminology
- **Malacca Dilemma**: Strategic anxiety of major Asian powers (especially China) regarding extreme vulnerability to a foreign naval blockade of the narrow Strait of Malacca.
- **Transit Chokepoint**: A narrow natural waterway where global traffic is concentrated, creating extreme systemic vulnerability to geopolitical or physical disruption.
- **Montreux Convention**: 1936 international treaty regulating maritime transit through the Turkish Straits, granting Turkey control over naval warship passage during conflicts.
