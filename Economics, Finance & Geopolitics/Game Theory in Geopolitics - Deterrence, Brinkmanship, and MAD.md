# Game Theory in Geopolitics - Deterrence, Brinkmanship, and MAD

## Overview
Strategic game theory models international geopolitical competition, analyzing how rational states make military and diplomatic moves under strategic interdependence.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Security Dilemma & Prisoner's Dilemma: In an anarchic international system without a global sovereign, state measures to increase their own security (e.g., military build-ups) inevitably threaten neighbors, provoking counter-measures that leave all states less secure—a classic multi-player Prisoner's Dilemma.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Thomas Schelling & The Strategy of Conflict: Schelling analyzed credibility in asymmetric deterrence: commitment devices ('burning your bridges' to eliminate retreat options) and 'the threat that leaves something to chance' (brinkmanship—deliberately pushing a crisis to the brink of disaster so that neither party can fully control the risk).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Nuclear deterrence doctrines, Taiwan Strait security balance, arms control treaty verification.

## Key Terminology
- **Security Dilemma**: Situation in which actions taken by a state to increase its own security cause reactions from other states that lead to a decrease in the original state's security.
- **Brinkmanship**: Practice of pursuing a dangerous policy to the limits of safety before stopping, especially in politics and diplomacy.
- **Credible Commitment**: Strategic move that restricts an actor's future options in a verifiable way to convince opponents of intent.
