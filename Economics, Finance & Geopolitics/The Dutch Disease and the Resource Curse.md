# The Dutch Disease and the Resource Curse

## Overview
The Dutch Disease describes the economic paradox where a sudden natural resource discovery (such as oil or gas) causes a boom that paradoxically harms the broader economy.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Causal Mechanics: Massive resource exports trigger massive capital inflows, driving rapid appreciation of the domestic real exchange rate. This currency overvaluation makes non-resource export sectors (manufacturing, agriculture) internationally uncompetitive, leading to deindustrialization and economic hollowing out.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Broader Resource Curse: Resource-rich nations frequently suffer from authoritarian rentier governance, corruption, volatile fiscal revenues tied to commodity price swings, and violent conflicts over resource rents.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Norway's Sovereign Wealth Fund (Government Pension Fund Global) as a counter-model sterilizing oil revenues abroad.

## Key Terminology
- **Dutch Disease**: Economic term for the negative consequences that can arise from a spike in the value of a nation's currency caused by natural resource exports.
- **Resource Curse**: Paradox that countries with an abundance of natural resources tend to have lower economic growth and worse developmental outcomes.
- **Sterilization**: Central bank financial intervention offsetting the effects of foreign exchange inflows on domestic money supply.
