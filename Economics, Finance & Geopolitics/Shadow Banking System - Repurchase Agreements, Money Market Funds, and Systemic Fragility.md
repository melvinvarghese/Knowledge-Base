# Shadow Banking System - Repurchase Agreements, Money Market Funds, and Systemic Fragility

## Overview
The shadow banking system comprises non-bank financial intermediaries that facilitate credit creation across global financial markets, operating without traditional banking licenses and explicit public safety nets.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Architecture & Credit Intermediation: Unlike commercial banks, which fund illiquid long-term loans with insured retail deposits protected by FDIC insurance and central bank discount window access, shadow banking relies on market-based wholesale funding. Key entities include **Money Market Mutual Funds (MMMFs)**, hedge funds, private credit funds, and special investment vehicles (SIVs). They engage in maturity transformation (borrowing short-term to invest long-term), liquidity transformation, and credit transformation through the **Repurchase Agreement (Repo)** market and asset-backed commercial paper (ABCP).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Modern 'Bank Run': In September 2008, the Reserve Primary Fund (a major money market fund) suffered losses on Lehman commercial paper, causing it to 'break the buck' (NAV falling below $1.00). This sparked an immediate institutional run across the entire money market fund sector, draining funding from Wall Street repo markets and forcing the Federal Reserve to guarantee all money market deposits.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
The Financial Stability Board (FSB) monitors global non-bank financial intermediation (NBFI), which now controls over $200 trillion in assets (nearly half of all global financial assets).

## Key Terminology
- **Shadow Banking**: System of non-bank financial institutions that conduct credit intermediation without traditional bank charters or central bank backstops.
- **Repurchase Agreement (Repo)**: Short-term secured transaction where one party sells securities to another and agrees to repurchase them at a higher price later.
- **Breaking the Buck**: Situation where a money market mutual fund's net asset value falls below its fixed $1.00 per share baseline.
