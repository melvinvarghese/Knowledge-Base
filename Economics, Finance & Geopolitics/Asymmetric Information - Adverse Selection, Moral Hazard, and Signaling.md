# Asymmetric Information - Adverse Selection, Moral Hazard, and Signaling

## Overview
Information Economics explores markets where buyers and sellers possess unequal information, causing market failure or collapse.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Adverse Selection & The Market for Lemons: George Akerlof (1970) demonstrated that when sellers know car quality better than buyers, buyers offer only average prices. Good car owners refuse to sell below value, leaving only defective 'lemons' on the market, precipitating complete market unraveling.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Moral Hazard & Signaling: Moral hazard occurs when an insured party takes excessive risks because another party bears the consequences (e.g., bank bailouts, health insurance). Michael Spence proved that high-ability workers overcome adverse selection by investing in costly, hard-to-fake 'signals' (such as elite college degrees).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Insurance underwriting, bank deposit safety net design, corporate governance executive compensation.

## Key Terminology
- **Adverse Selection**: Market dynamic where hidden characteristics lead to undesirable goods or high-risk participants dominating the market.
- **Moral Hazard**: Lack of incentive to guard against risk where one is protected from its consequences.
- **Signaling**: Action taken by an informed party to credibly reveal private information to an uninformed party.
