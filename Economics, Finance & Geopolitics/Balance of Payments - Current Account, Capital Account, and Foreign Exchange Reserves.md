# Balance of Payments - Current Account, Capital Account, and Foreign Exchange Reserves

## Overview
The Balance of Payments (BoP) is a comprehensive macroeconomic accounting statement that records all economic and financial transactions between a nation's residents and the rest of the world over a specified period.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Double-Entry Accounting Architecture: The BoP must mathematically balance to zero ($\text{Current Account} + \text{Capital & Financial Account} + \text{Official Reserves Change} = 0$). (1) **Current Account (CA)**: Records net trade in physical goods and services (Trade Balance $X - M$), net primary income (foreign dividends, interest payments, remittances), and secondary net unilateral transfers (foreign aid). A current account deficit implies a nation consumes and invests more than it produces ($CA = S - I$). (2) **Capital & Financial Account (FA)**: Records cross-border ownership changes in physical and financial assets, including Foreign Direct Investment (FDI), portfolio equity and bond flows, and commercial bank loans.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Twin Deficits Hypothesis & Balance of Payments Crises: When a country experiences persistent large current account deficits financed by volatile short-term 'hot money' portfolio inflows, an abrupt shift in global investor sentiment can trigger sudden capital flight. If central bank **Foreign Exchange Reserves** are exhausted attempting to defend a currency peg, the nation suffers a severe Balance of Payments crisis (e.g., 1997 Asian Financial Crisis), requiring emergency IMF structural lending.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Shapes sovereign credit ratings, currency vulnerability indices, and global macroeconomic surveillance under IMF Article IV consultations.

## Key Terminology
- **Current Account**: Record of a country's net trade in goods, services, net primary income from abroad, and net unilateral transfer payments.
- **Capital Flight**: Large-scale exodus of financial assets and capital out of a country due to geopolitical instability or imminent currency devaluation.
- **Foreign Exchange Reserves**: Foreign currency assets, gold, and SDRs held by a central bank to maintain confidence in sovereign monetary and exchange rate stability.
