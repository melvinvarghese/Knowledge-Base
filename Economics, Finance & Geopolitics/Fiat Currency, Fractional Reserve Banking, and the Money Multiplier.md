# Fiat Currency, Fractional Reserve Banking, and the Money Multiplier

## Overview
Modern economies operate on fiat currencies created through fractional reserve commercial banking systems where private credit issuance generates the majority of money supply.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Fractional Reserve Mechanics & The Multiplier: Commercial banks hold only a fraction of customer deposits as reserves, lending out the remainder. When Bank A lends $90 of a $100 deposit to a borrower who deposits it in Bank B, the money supply expands. Under theoretical reserve ratio $R$, the theoretical money multiplier is $m = 1/R$.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Endogenous Money Creation: Empirical central banking research (Bank of England, 2014) reveals that modern banks do not simply lend out pre-existing deposits. Rather, when a bank grants a loan, it simultaneously creates new deposit money out of nothing: credit expansion creates deposits endogenously, constrained by bank capital adequacy regulations (Basel III).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Money aggregates (M0, M1, M2), bank run dynamics, government deposit insurance schemes (FDIC).

## Key Terminology
- **Fractional Reserve Banking**: Banking system in which only a fraction of bank deposits are backed by actual cash on hand and available for withdrawal.
- **Money Multiplier**: Theoretical ratio of commercial bank money created to the amount of central bank reserves held.
- **Endogenous Money**: Monetary theory asserting that money is created within the economic system via bank lending rather than exogenous central bank printing.
