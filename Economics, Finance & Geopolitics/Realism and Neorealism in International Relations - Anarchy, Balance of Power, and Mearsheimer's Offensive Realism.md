# Realism and Neorealism in International Relations - Anarchy, Balance of Power, and Mearsheimer's Offensive Realism

## Overview
Realism and Neorealism (Structural Realism) form the dominant school of thought in International Relations theory, analyzing state behavior through the lens of power politics, international anarchy, and national survival.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Theoretical Foundations & Kenneth Waltz's Structural Realism: Classic realism (Thucydides, Machiavelli, Thomas Hobbes, Hans Morgenthau) viewed the lust for power as rooted in human nature. Kenneth Waltz (*Theory of International Politics*, 1979) reformulated realism into **Neorealism**: the driving force is not human psychology, but the **anarchic structure** of the international system (absence of a central sovereign authority). Because the international system is a self-help system where state survival cannot be guaranteed by international law, states must prioritize relative military power and maintain a **Balance of Power** through internal buildup or external alliances.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Offensive vs Defensive Realism: John Mearsheimer (*The Tragedy of Great Power Politics*, 2001) formulated **Offensive Realism**: because states can never know the future intentions of rivals, rational great powers maximize their share of world power, striving for regional hegemony. Conversely, defensive realists (Waltz, Jervis) argue that security is best achieved through balance, as aggressive expansion provokes counter-balancing coalitions (**Security Dilemma**).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Explains the geopolitical competition between the United States and a rising China, great-power spheres of influence, and structural resistance to world government.

## Key Terminology
- **International Anarchy**: The condition in world politics where there is no overarching global government or authority to enforce international laws and agreements.
- **Security Dilemma**: Paradox where measures taken by one state to increase its own security unintentionally make neighboring states feel less secure, prompting an arms race.
- **Offensive Realism**: Structural theory asserting that great powers must maximize relative power and pursue regional hegemony to ensure absolute survival.
