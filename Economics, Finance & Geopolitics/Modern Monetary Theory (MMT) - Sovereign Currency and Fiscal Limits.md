# Modern Monetary Theory (MMT) - Sovereign Currency and Fiscal Limits

## Overview
Modern Monetary Theory (MMT) is a heterodox macroeconomic framework positing that sovereign currency-issuing governments face no financial budget constraints, only real resource limits.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Chartalism & Operational Mechanics: Tracing back to Georg Friedrich Knapp's *State Theory of Money*, MMT notes that a government issuing its own floating, non-convertible fiat currency (e.g., US, UK, Japan) cannot go bankrupt or default involuntarily. Taxes do not finance government spending; rather, spending injects money, and taxation creates demand for the currency and drains liquidity to prevent inflation.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Inflation as the True Constraint: The limit on state expenditure is not debt or deficits, but real resource availability (labor, raw materials, industrial capacity). Beyond full employment, deficit spending triggers demand-pull inflation. MMT advocates a federal Job Guarantee as an automatic economic stabilizer.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Debates over pandemic deficit stimulus spending, funding mechanisms for green energy transitions.

## Key Terminology
- **MMT**: Modern Monetary Theory; macroeconomic model arguing sovereign currency issuers face resource constraints rather than financial solvency limits.
- **Chartalism**: Monetary theory asserting that money originates with the state and its legal authority to levy taxes payable in its currency.
- **Job Guarantee**: Policy proposal where the government acts as an employer of last resort, offering a job to anyone willing to work.
