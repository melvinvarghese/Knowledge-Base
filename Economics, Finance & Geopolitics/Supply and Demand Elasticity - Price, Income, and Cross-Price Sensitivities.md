# Supply and Demand Elasticity - Price, Income, and Cross-Price Sensitivities

## Overview
Elasticity measures the responsiveness of the quantity demanded or supplied of a good to changes in one of its determinants, such as price, consumer income, or the price of related goods.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Mathematical Formulations: **Price Elasticity of Demand (PED)** is defined as $\epsilon_d = \frac{\% \Delta Q_d}{\% \Delta P} = \frac{dQ}{dP} \frac{P}{Q}$. Demand is **inelastic** if $|\epsilon_d| < 1$ (necessities like insulin or gasoline; price increases raise total revenue), **unit elastic** if $|\epsilon_d| = 1$, and **elastic** if $|\epsilon_d| > 1$ (luxuries or commodities with close substitutes; price increases lower revenue). **Cross-Price Elasticity ($E_{xy}$)** distinguishes **substitutes** ($E_{xy} > 0$) from **complements** ($E_{xy} < 0$). **Income Elasticity ($YED$)** delineates **normal goods** ($YED > 0$) from **inferior goods** ($YED < 0$) and **luxury goods** ($YED > 1$).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Tax Incidence & Deadweight Loss: The economic burden of a tax does not depend on whether it is legally levied on buyers or sellers; rather, it falls disproportionately on whichever market side is more price-inelastic, determining the deadweight loss triangle of market distortion.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Foundational for corporate dynamic pricing algorithms (airline ticket yield management, rideshare surge pricing) and government excise tax design (tobacco, carbon).

## Key Terminology
- **Price Elasticity of Demand (PED)**: Percentage change in quantity demanded divided by percentage change in price.
- **Tax Incidence**: The relative division of a tax burden between consumers and producers determined by their relative price elasticities.
- **Cross-Price Elasticity**: Measure of how the demand for one good changes in response to price fluctuations in another related good.
