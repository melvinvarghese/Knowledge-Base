# The 2008 Global Financial Crisis - Subprime Mortgages, CDOs, and Systemic Meltdown

## Overview
The 2008 Global Financial Crisis (GFC) was the most severe worldwide economic and financial contraction since the Great Depression of 1929, triggered by the collapse of the US subprime housing bubble.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Securitization Machine & Structural Fragility: Driven by ultra-low post-dot-com interest rates and political mandates for homeownership, mortgage originators issued non-traditional 'subprime' loans with little to no documentation. Wall Street investment banks securitized these illiquid mortgages into **Mortgage-Backed Securities (MBS)** and sliced them into **Collateralized Debt Obligations (CDOs)** with multi-tiered tranches. Credit rating agencies (Moody's, S&P) assigned AAA ratings to mezzanine tranches using flawed mathematical correlation models (David Li's Gaussian Copula). Financial institutions like AIG issued hundreds of billions in uncollateralized **Credit Default Swaps (CDS)** insuring these toxic securities.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Systemic Contagion & The Lehman Collapse: When US nationwide home prices peaked in 2006 and subprime delinquencies soared, CDO values plummeted, rendering investment banks insolvent. In September 2008, the failure of Lehman Brothers froze the global interbank wholesale funding market (repo and commercial paper). Panic spread instantly across global financial networks, prompting the US Troubled Asset Relief Program (TARP), emergency liquidity backstops, and the Federal Reserve's unprecedented lender-of-last-resort interventions.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Directly catalyzed the Dodd-Frank Wall Street Reform and Consumer Protection Act (2010), the Basel III regulatory capital framework, and the birth of Bitcoin.

## Key Terminology
- **Subprime Mortgage**: A residential loan extended to borrowers with low credit ratings, high debt-to-income ratios, and elevated default risk.
- **Collateralized Debt Obligation (CDO)**: A structured financial derivative that pools cash-generating assets and repackages them into discrete risk tranches.
- **Systemic Risk**: The risk that the failure of an individual financial institution or sector could trigger widespread collapse across the entire financial system.
