# Tariffs, Non-Tariff Barriers, and Modern Protectionism - Welfare Losses and Trade Wars

## Overview
Protectionism refers to government economic policies designed to restrict international trade and shield domestic industries from foreign competition through tariffs, quotas, and regulatory barriers.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Tariff Mechanics & Welfare Analysis: A tariff is a customs duty or tax levied on imported goods (ad valorem percentage or specific per-unit fee). In economic welfare analysis of a small open economy, a tariff raises domestic consumer prices from the world price $P_w$ to $P_w + t$. This expands domestic producer surplus and generates government tariff revenue, but causes a net **deadweight loss** consisting of the production distortion triangle (wasteful production by inefficient domestic producers) and the consumption distortion triangle (consumers priced out of the market). In a large economy, an 'optimal tariff' can theoretically shift terms of trade in its favor, but inevitably provokes retaliatory counter-tariffs.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Non-Tariff Barriers (NTBs) & Neo-Protectionism: Beyond overt tariffs, modern trade protectionism deploys NTBs: import quotas, voluntary export restraints (VERs), burdensome sanitary and phytosanitary (SPS) regulations, local content requirements (LCRs), and national security trade designations (e.g., US Section 232 and Section 301).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Central to US-China trade friction (2018-present), industrial policy re-shoring (CHIPS Act, Inflation Reduction Act), and debates over strategic self-sufficiency.

## Key Terminology
- **Deadweight Loss of a Tariff**: Net economic loss in social surplus resulting from consumer distortion and inefficient domestic overproduction induced by trade taxes.
- **Non-Tariff Barrier (NTB)**: Trade restriction such as quotas, technical standards, or licensing requirements that impedes imports without direct taxes.
- **Trade War**: An escalating geopolitical conflict where countries retaliate against each other by raising mutual tariffs and trade restrictions.
