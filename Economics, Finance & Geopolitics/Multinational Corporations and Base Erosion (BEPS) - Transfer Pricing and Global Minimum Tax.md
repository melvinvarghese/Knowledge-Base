# Multinational Corporations and Base Erosion (BEPS) - Transfer Pricing and Global Minimum Tax

## Overview
Base Erosion and Profit Shifting (BEPS) refers to corporate tax planning strategies used by multinational enterprises (MNEs) to exploit gaps and mismatches in national tax rules to artificially shift profits to low- or zero-tax jurisdictions.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Mechanisms: Transfer Pricing & The Digital Economy: MNEs minimize effective tax liabilities through sophisticated tax engineering: (1) **Transfer Pricing Abuse**: Setting artificial internal prices for intra-company sales of goods, management fees, or intellectual property (IP) between subsidiaries located in different jurisdictions, violating the 'arm's length principle'; (2) **Tax Havens & Conduit Routing**: Channeling profits through shell companies located in jurisdictions like Bermuda, the Cayman Islands, Luxembourg, and Ireland (e.g., the infamous 'Double Irish with a Dutch Sandwich' structure); (3) **Digital Taxation Challenges**: Digital platform companies generate immense economic value from users in host countries without maintaining a physical 'permanent establishment', legally escaping local corporate income taxes.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The OECD/G20 Two-Pillar Solution: In 2021, over 135 countries agreed to historic global tax reform: **Pillar One** reallocates taxing rights over a portion of profits of the largest MNEs to market countries where users/consumers are located; **Pillar Two** enacts a landmark **Global Anti-Base Erosion (GloBE) 15% Global Minimum Corporate Tax**, neutralizing race-to-the-bottom tax havens.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Significantly restores national corporate tax bases and reduces multinational tax avoidance estimated historically at $100-$240 billion annually.

## Key Terminology
- **Base Erosion and Profit Shifting (BEPS)**: Tax avoidance strategies exploiting tax rule mismatches to shift profits from high-tax to low-tax jurisdictions.
- **Transfer Pricing**: The prices charged in transactions between related corporate entities (subsidiaries) within the same multinational conglomerate.
- **Global Minimum Corporate Tax**: International tax framework establishing a mandatory 15% floor on corporate income tax rates to prevent sovereign tax competition.
