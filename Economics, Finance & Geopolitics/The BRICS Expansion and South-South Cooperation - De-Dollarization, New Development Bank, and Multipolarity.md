# The BRICS Expansion and South-South Cooperation - De-Dollarization, New Development Bank, and Multipolarity

## Overview
The BRICS coalition (originally Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa) has expanded into a prominent multilateral platform championing South-South cooperation, global institutional reform, and economic de-dollarization.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Evolution from Acronym to Geopolitical Bloc: Coined as an investment thesis by Goldman Sachs economist Jim O'Neill in 2001, BRICS formalized into an annual diplomatic summit in 2009. In 2024, the bloc enacted a landmark expansion (BRICS+), admitting major energy exporters and regional powers including Iran, Egypt, Ethiopia, and the UAE. Collectively, BRICS+ represents over 45% of the world's population, over 30% of global land area, and over 35% of global GDP in Purchasing Power Parity (PPP) terms, surpassing the G7.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Financial Architecture & De-Dollarization: To counter Western dominance in the Bretton Woods institutions (IMF and World Bank), BRICS established the **New Development Bank (NDB)** in Shanghai and the Contingent Reserve Arrangement (CRA). The bloc actively promotes local-currency bilateral trade settlements (RMB, Ruble, Rupee, Dirham), digital currency bridges (mBridge), and physical gold reserve accumulation to reduce exposure to US dollar sanctions and SWIFT interdiction.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Highlights the growing influence of the 'Global South' demanding a multipolar world order and voting power redistribution in multilateral institutions.

## Key Terminology
- **Multipolarity**: A distribution of global power in which multiple major states or geopolitical blocs possess significant, comparable international influence.
- **De-Dollarization**: The strategic process where sovereign nations reduce their reliance on the US dollar as a global reserve currency and trade medium.
- **New Development Bank (NDB)**: Multilateral development bank established by BRICS nations to mobilize resources for infrastructure and sustainable development in emerging economies.
