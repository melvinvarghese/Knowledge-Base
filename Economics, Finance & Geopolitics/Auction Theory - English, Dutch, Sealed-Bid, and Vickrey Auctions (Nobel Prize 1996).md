# Auction Theory - English, Dutch, Sealed-Bid, and Vickrey Auctions (Nobel Prize 1996)

## Overview
Auction theory is an applied branch of game theory and mechanism design that analyzes how bidding rules, information asymmetry, and strategic behavior determine the allocation and price of goods.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Four Canonical Formats: (1) **English (Ascending-Price)**: Open ascending bids until only one bidder remains, who pays their final bid. (2) **Dutch (Descending-Price)**: Clock descends from a high price until the first bidder stops the clock and pays. (3) **First-Price Sealed-Bid (FPSB)**: Simultaneous confidential bids; highest bidder wins and pays their bid, incentivizing bidders to shade their bids below their true valuation. (4) **Second-Price Sealed-Bid (Vickrey Auction)**: Highest bidder wins, but pays the price submitted by the **second-highest bidder**. William Vickrey proved that bidding one's true valuation ($b_i = v_i$) is a **weakly dominant strategy**, eliminating bidding friction and strategic shading.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Revenue Equivalence & The Winner's Curse: The **Revenue Equivalence Theorem** (Myerson, Nobel 2007) proves that for risk-neutral bidders with independent private values, all four standard auction formats yield identical expected revenue to the seller. In **common-value auctions** (oil drilling tracts, spectrum rights), bidders suffer from the **Winner's Curse**: the auction winner is often the bidder with the most overly optimistic estimate of value, systematically overpaying unless bids are discounted for estimation error.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Paul Milgrom and Robert Wilson (Nobel 2020) designed the Simultaneous Multiple Round Auction (SMRA) for the FCC to allocate billions in telecom radio spectrum. Powers Google and Meta online ad auctions (Vickrey-Clarke-Groves variants).

## Key Terminology
- **Vickrey Auction**: Sealed-bid auction where the highest bidder wins but pays the second-highest bid price, making truthful bidding a dominant strategy.
- **Winner's Curse**: Tendency in common-value auctions for the winning bidder to overestimate the asset's true value and suffer an economic loss.
- **Revenue Equivalence Theorem**: Axiom stating that any standard auction format generates the same expected seller revenue under symmetric, independent private value conditions.
