# Gross Domestic Product (GDP) - Nominal, Real, and Purchasing Power Parity (PPP)

## Overview
Gross Domestic Product (GDP) is the standard monetary measure of the market value of all final goods and services produced within a country's borders in a specific time period.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Accounting Methodologies & Formulations: GDP is measured using three identical accounting approaches: Expenditure ($Y = C + I + G + (X - M)$), Income (wages, corporate profits, rents, net interest), and Value-Added (production gross output minus intermediate consumption). **Nominal GDP** measures production at current market prices, whereas **Real GDP** adjusts for inflation using a GDP deflator: $\text{Real GDP} = \frac{\text{Nominal GDP}}{\text{Deflator}} \times 100$. When comparing economic size across countries, **Purchasing Power Parity (PPP)** adjusts for the Balassa-Samuelson effect and local price levels of non-tradable goods, reflecting true domestic purchasing power.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Critiques and Limitations: Simon Kuznets, creator of national income accounting, warned against using GDP as a sole metric of national welfare. GDP fails to capture unpaid domestic labor, environmental degradation, income inequality, black market transactions, and consumer surplus generated by digital zero-cost goods.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Central to international multilateral benchmarking (IMF, World Bank), debt sustainability ratios (Debt/GDP), sovereign credit ratings, and UN Human Development Index adjustments.

## Key Terminology
- **Real GDP**: Inflation-adjusted measure reflecting the true physical volume of goods and services produced.
- **Purchasing Power Parity (PPP)**: Currency exchange rate adjustment that equalizes the price of a standard basket of goods across countries.
- **Expenditure Method**: Formulation calculating GDP as the sum of consumption, investment, government spending, and net exports.
