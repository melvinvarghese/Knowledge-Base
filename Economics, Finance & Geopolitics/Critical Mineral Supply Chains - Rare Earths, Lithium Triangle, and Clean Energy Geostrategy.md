# Critical Mineral Supply Chains - Rare Earths, Lithium Triangle, and Clean Energy Geostrategy

## Overview
Critical minerals—including rare earth elements (REEs), lithium, cobalt, nickel, and copper—form the indispensable physical foundation of modern electronics, electric vehicles, defense weapon systems, and renewable energy infrastructure.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Supply Concentration & Chinese Dominance: Unlike crude oil, which is distributed across dozens of global exporters, critical mineral processing exhibits extreme geographic concentration. China controls over 60% of global rare earth extraction and upwards of 85-90% of global rare earth refining and permanent magnet (NdFeB) production. It also refines over 65% of global lithium and cobalt. Key geographic bottlenecks include: (1) **The Lithium Triangle** (Chile, Argentina, Bolivia—holding over 50% of world lithium brine reserves); (2) **The Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC)** (supplying over 70% of global mined cobalt, largely financed by Chinese smelting operations).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
National Security Implications: Advanced defense applications—such as F-35 fighter jet radar and actuators, Virginia-class submarine electric motors, and precision-guided munitions—depend entirely on specialized rare earth magnets. Mineral weaponization (e.g., export restrictions on gallium, germanium, and antimony) has catalyzed Western industrial alliances (Minerals Security Partnership, US Inflation Reduction Act tax credits for domestic sourcing).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Centers global industrial policy debates, mining environmental litigation, and deep-sea mineral exploration treaties.

## Key Terminology
- **Rare Earth Elements (REEs)**: A group of 17 chemically similar metallic elements essential for high-performance permanent magnets, lasers, and defense electronics.
- **Lithium Triangle**: Geographic region encompassing the salt flats of Chile, Argentina, and Bolivia that contains the world's largest lithium reserves.
- **Permanent Magnet (NdFeB)**: High-strength neodymium-iron-boron magnet indispensable for electric vehicle traction motors and offshore wind turbine generators.
