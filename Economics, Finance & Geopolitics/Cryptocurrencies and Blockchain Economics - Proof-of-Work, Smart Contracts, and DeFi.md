# Cryptocurrencies and Blockchain Economics - Proof-of-Work, Smart Contracts, and DeFi

## Overview
Cryptocurrency and blockchain economics represent decentralized, cryptographic networks for trustless value transfer, programmatic contract execution, and decentralized financial intermediation.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Cryptographic Consensus & Tokenomics: Introduced by Satoshi Nakamoto (2008) via Bitcoin, blockchains solve the Byzantine Generals' problem and the double-spending dilemma without trusted central authorities. **Proof-of-Work (PoW)** couples thermodynamic computation (SHA-256 hashing) with economic incentives (block rewards and transaction fees) to secure ledger immutability. **Proof-of-Stake (PoS)** replaces physical energy expenditure with economic staking of capital, securing the network via programmatic slashing of dishonest validators. Smart contract platforms (Ethereum) execute Turing-complete code, enabling **Decentralized Finance (DeFi)**: automated market makers (AMMs like Uniswap utilizing constant-product invariants $x \cdot y = k$), decentralized lending protocols (Aave, Compound), and collateralized stablecoins (MakerDAO/DAI).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Economic & Regulatory Tension: Blockchains offer borderless censorship resistance and programmatic transparency, but struggle with scalability trilemmas (balancing decentralization, security, and throughput), extreme speculative price volatility, and regulatory compliance (AML/KYC, SEC securities classification).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Institutional adoption includes spot Bitcoin/Ethereum ETFs, stablecoins settling trillions in annual cross-border transaction volume, and corporate treasury diversification.

## Key Terminology
- **Proof-of-Work (PoW)**: Consensus mechanism requiring network participants to expend computational energy solving cryptographic puzzles to validate blocks.
- **Smart Contract**: Self-executing digital program stored on a blockchain that automatically executes contract terms when predetermined conditions are met.
- **Automated Market Maker (AMM)**: Decentralized exchange protocol relying on mathematical liquidity pools rather than traditional buyers and sellers order books.
