# The Gini Coefficient and Economic Inequality

## Overview
The Gini Coefficient is the standard statistical metric used to measure economic inequality and wealth or income distribution within a population.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Lorenz Curve Formulation: The Lorenz curve plots the cumulative percentage of total national income earned against the cumulative percentage of the population, from poorest to richest. The Gini coefficient is the ratio of the area between the 45-degree line of perfect equality and the Lorenz curve ($A$) to the total area under the equality line ($A + B$): $G = \frac{A}{A + B}$, ranging from 0 (perfect equality) to 1 (maximal inequality where one person earns all income).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Piketty's Capital in the Twenty-First Century: Thomas Piketty demonstrated that when the rate of return on capital ($r$) persistently exceeds the economic growth rate ($g$) ($r > g$), inherited wealth concentrates faster than labor income grows, leading to spiraling wealth inequality in the absence of progressive taxation.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Global developmental indicators, progressive income tax structuring, universal basic income (UBI) debates.

## Key Terminology
- **Gini Coefficient**: Statistical measure of economic inequality within a group, ranging from 0 (total equality) to 1 (total inequality).
- **Lorenz Curve**: Graphical representation of the distribution of income or wealth across an economy.
- **r > g Formula**: Piketty's proposition that when capital returns outpace economic growth, wealth inequality inevitably rises.
