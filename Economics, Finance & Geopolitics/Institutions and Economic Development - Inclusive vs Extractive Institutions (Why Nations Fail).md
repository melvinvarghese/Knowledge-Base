# Institutions and Economic Development - Inclusive vs Extractive Institutions (Why Nations Fail)

## Overview
Institutional economics posits that formal and informal rules of the game—such as property rights, the rule of law, and governance constraints—are the fundamental drivers of long-term economic prosperity.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Inclusive vs Extractive Framework: Formulated by Daron Acemoglu and James A. Robinson (*Why Nations Fail*, Nobel Prize 2024 with Simon Johnson). They argue that neither geography, culture, nor ignorance explains why some nations are rich and others poor; rather, it is their political and economic institutions. (1) **Inclusive Institutions**: Protect secure private property rights, enforce unbiased contracts, uphold the rule of law, provide public goods, and allow free entry for new businesses. Inclusive political institutions distribute political power broadly (pluralism) with constitutional checks and balances. This fosters innovation and creative destruction. (2) **Extractive Institutions**: Concentrate political power and wealth in the hands of a narrow ruling elite, designing economic rules to extract wealth from the majority, actively suppressing technological innovation to preserve their monopoly on political power.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Colonial Origins & Institutional Persistence: Utilizing colonial settler mortality data as an instrumental variable, Acemoglu, Johnson, and Robinson demonstrated that where Europeans faced low disease environments (US, Canada, Australia), they built inclusive institutions; where they faced high disease environments (sub-Saharan Africa, the Caribbean), they established extractive colonies designed for resource pillage, persisting to this day.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Directly refutes pure geographic determinism (Jared Diamond) and shapes modern World Bank governance and rule-of-law conditionalities.

## Key Terminology
- **Inclusive Institutions**: Legal and political structures that enable broad public participation, enforce contracts, and protect property rights.
- **Extractive Institutions**: Governance structures designed by a narrow elite to extract resources and wealth from the rest of society without accountability.
- **Institutional Persistence**: The historical tendency of political and economic institutions to self-reinforce and endure over centuries.
