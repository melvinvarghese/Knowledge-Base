# Modern Portfolio Theory - Markowitz Efficient Frontier and CAPM

## Overview
Modern Portfolio Theory (MPT), pioneered by Harry Markowitz (1952), mathematically optimizes asset allocation by balancing expected portfolio returns against variance risk.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Diversification & Covariance: Portfolio variance is not simply the weighted sum of individual variances, but depends on asset covariances: $\sigma_p^2 = \sum_i \sum_j w_i w_j \sigma_{ij}$. When asset returns have low or negative correlations ($\rho_{ij} < 1$), combining them reduces overall portfolio volatility without sacrificing expected returns.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Efficient Frontier & Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM): Markowitz identified the 'Efficient Frontier' of portfolios offering the maximum expected return for a given risk level. William Sharpe developed CAPM, pricing individual assets via their non-diversifiable systematic risk ($\beta$): $\mathbb{E}[R_i] = R_f + \beta_i (\mathbb{E}[R_m] - R_f)$, where $\beta_i = \text{Cov}(R_i, R_m) / \text{Var}(R_m)$.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Index funds, institutional pension fund asset allocation, passive ETF investment philosophies (Vanguard).

## Key Terminology
- **Efficient Frontier**: Set of optimal investment portfolios that offer the highest expected return for a defined level of risk.
- **Beta (Finance)**: Measure of a stock or portfolio's volatility and systematic risk in relation to the overall market.
- **Systematic Risk**: Market-wide, non-diversifiable risk inherent to the entire market or asset class.
