# Opportunity Cost, Sunk Cost Fallacy, and Economic Rent

## Overview
Economic decision-making rests on the fundamental concept of opportunity cost—the value of the next-best alternative forgone—distinguishing true economic calculations from simple accounting conventions.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Opportunity Cost & The Sunk Cost Fallacy: **Opportunity cost** includes both explicit out-of-pocket expenses and implicit costs (e.g., forgone interest, alternative wages). Economic Profit ($\Pi_{econ} = \text{Revenue} - \text{Explicit Costs} - \text{Implicit Costs}$) is always strictly less than Accounting Profit. The **Sunk Cost Fallacy** occurs when decision-makers irrationally factor past, unrecoverable expenditures into forward-looking decisions (e.g., continuing an unprofitable development project because millions have already been spent), violating the marginalist rule that only future marginal costs and revenues matter ($MC = MR$).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Economic Rent & Rent-Seeking: David Ricardo defined **Economic Rent** as any payment to a factor of production (land, talent, capital) exceeding the minimum payment required to keep it in its current employment (its opportunity cost). When supply is perfectly inelastic (e.g., prime Manhattan real estate, unique genius), all return is pure rent. **Rent-Seeking** (Gordon Tullock) occurs when individuals or firms expend scarce resources on political lobbying, tariffs, or regulatory capture to acquire government-granted monopolies or subsidies without producing any new wealth, creating pure deadweight economic loss.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Foundational for corporate capital budgeting, discounted cash flow (DCF) hurdle rates, urban land economics, and public choice critiques of regulatory capture.

## Key Terminology
- **Opportunity Cost**: The loss of potential gain from other alternatives when one alternative is chosen.
- **Sunk Cost**: A cost that has already been incurred and cannot be recovered by any future action, which rational decision-makers should ignore.
- **Rent-Seeking**: The pursuit of economic gain by manipulating public policy or economic conditions rather than by creating new wealth.
