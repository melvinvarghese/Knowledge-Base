# Economic Sanctions and Financial Statecraft - SWIFT Exclusion, Asset Freezes, and Secondary Sanctions

## Overview
Economic sanctions represent the strategic deployment of commercial, trade, and financial coercive measures by state actors to alter the political, security, or military behavior of target states or entities.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Weaponization of Global Financial Infrastructure: Modern financial statecraft has shifted from blunt trade embargoes to hyper-targeted financial sanctions exploiting the central dominance of the US dollar and Western financial infrastructure. Key instruments include: (1) **SWIFT Messaging Disconnection**: Expelling target banks from the Society for Worldwide Interbank Financial Telecommunication, severing automated cross-border transaction capabilities; (2) **Central Bank Reserve Freezes**: Immobilizing sovereign foreign exchange reserves deposited in foreign jurisdictions (e.g., the 2022 freezing of over $300 billion in Russian Central Bank reserves); (3) **Secondary Sanctions**: Penalizing non-US third-party corporate entities and banks that conduct business with sanctioned targets by threatening expulsion from the US dollar clearing system (CHIPS).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Unintended Consequences & De-Dollarization: Heavy reliance on sanctions incentivizes targeted and non-aligned states to build alternative financial bypass mechanisms, including bilateral local-currency trade settlements, non-SWIFT messaging rails (China's CIPS, Russia's SPFS), physical gold accumulation, and cryptocurrency rails, accelerating long-term multipolar de-dollarization.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Enforced by the US Treasury Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) and EU Sanctions Directorate across Russia, Iran, North Korea, and Venezuela.

## Key Terminology
- **Secondary Sanctions**: Penalties imposed by a sanctioning nation against third-country foreign entities that conduct trade with a primary sanctioned target.
- **SWIFT**: Secure messaging network utilized by global financial institutions to transmit instructions for international wire transfers.
- **OFAC**: The Office of Foreign Assets Control within the US Department of the Treasury that administers and enforces economic and trade sanctions.
