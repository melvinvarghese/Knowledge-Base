# National Debt and Fiscal Deficits - Sovereign Debt-to-GDP and Intergenerational Equity

## Overview
National debt represents the cumulative total of past annual budget deficits financed through the issuance of sovereign debt instruments such as treasury bills, notes, and bonds.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Debt Dynamics & The Domar Condition: The evolution of the debt-to-GDP ratio ($d = D/Y$) is governed by the sovereign budget identity: $\Delta d = (r - g)d_t - s_t$, where $r$ is the real interest rate on debt, $g$ is real economic growth rate, and $s_t$ is the primary budget surplus as a fraction of GDP. When $r < g$ (the 'Domar condition'), governments can sustain primary deficits without the debt ratio exploding. However, if $r > g$, compounding interest compounds debt trajectories, requiring structural fiscal austerity or risking a sovereign debt crisis.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Ricardian Equivalence vs Intergenerational Burden: Robert Barro's Ricardian Equivalence proposition argues that rational forward-looking consumers recognize that debt-financed tax cuts today imply higher taxes tomorrow, saving the windfall rather than stimulating consumption. Critics note that borrowing transfers capital burdens to future generations unless invested in high-return public infrastructure.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Informs debt ceiling negotiations, Maastricht convergence criteria (60% debt-to-GDP limit), and sovereign default risk spreads in international debt capital markets.

## Key Terminology
- **Primary Deficit**: Government budget deficit excluding net interest payments on accumulated debt.
- **Debt-to-GDP Ratio**: Metric comparing a country's total public debt to its annual gross domestic product, indicating debt sustainability.
- **Ricardian Equivalence**: Economic hypothesis stating that government debt financing has the same economic impact as direct taxation.
