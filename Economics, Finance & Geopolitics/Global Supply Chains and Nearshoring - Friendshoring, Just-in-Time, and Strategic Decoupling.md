# Global Supply Chains and Nearshoring - Friendshoring, Just-in-Time, and Strategic Decoupling

## Overview
Global supply chains are transnational production and logistical networks that fragment manufacturing stages across multiple countries to exploit regional cost and skill advantages.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
From 'Just-in-Time' to 'Just-in-Case': For three decades following the Cold War, multinational corporations optimized supply chains for pure cost minimization using **Just-in-Time (JIT)** lean manufacturing (developed by Toyota), carrying zero inventory buffers and relying on seamless global logistics. However, systemic shocks—the 2020 COVID-19 lockdowns, the 2021 Suez Canal blockage, and geopolitical conflicts—exposed the acute fragility of hyperspecialized, single-source manufacturing hubs (especially in East Asia).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Nearshoring, Friendshoring & Strategic Decoupling: Modern industrial strategy prioritizes **resilience over pure cost efficiency**. Corporations and governments are actively pursuing: (1) **Nearshoring**: Relocating production closer to end-consumer markets (e.g., US manufacturing moving to Mexico); (2) **Friendshoring**: Restricting sourcing to trusted allied nations with shared geopolitical values; (3) **China Plus One**: Diversifying factory operations into Southeast Asia (Vietnam, Malaysia, Indonesia) and India.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Drives major capital investment shifts in semiconductors (foundry diversification), electric vehicle battery gigafactories, and pharmaceutical active ingredient production.

## Key Terminology
- **Nearshoring**: Relocating business operations or component manufacturing to a nearby country to reduce supply chain distance and logistical risk.
- **Friendshoring**: Restricting sourcing and manufacturing to geopolitical allies to insulate supply chains from geopolitical weaponization.
- **Just-in-Time (JIT)**: Inventory management strategy where components are received only as they are needed in the production process, minimizing carrying costs.
