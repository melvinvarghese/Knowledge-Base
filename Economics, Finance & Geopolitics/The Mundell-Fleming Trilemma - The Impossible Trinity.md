# The Mundell-Fleming Trilemma - The Impossible Trinity

## Overview
The Impossible Trinity (Mundell-Fleming Model) dictates that a sovereign nation cannot simultaneously maintain all three of: 1. A fixed foreign exchange rate; 2. Free capital mobility; and 3. An independent monetary policy.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Trilemma Tradeoff: A country must choose two of the three legs: 1. Fixed exchange rate + Free capital movement = Lost monetary autonomy (e.g., Eurozone member states); 2. Independent monetary policy + Free capital movement = Floating exchange rate (e.g., US, UK, Japan); 3. Fixed exchange rate + Independent monetary policy = Capital controls (e.g., China, Bretton Woods).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Asian Financial Crisis (1997): Southeast Asian economies attempted to maintain dollar pegs while opening capital accounts. When speculative capital fled, foreign exchange reserves vanished, forcing catastrophic currency devaluations.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Core architectural constraint in international macroeconomic design, central bank foreign exchange reserve management.

## Key Terminology
- **Impossible Trinity**: Trilemma in international economics stating that it is impossible to have an independent monetary policy, free capital movement, and a fixed exchange rate simultaneously.
- **Capital Controls**: Residency-based measures such as transaction taxes, limits, or prohibitions that a nation's government can use to regulate flows from capital markets.
- **Currency Peg**: Exchange rate policy in which a government fixes the exchange rate of its currency to another currency or basket.
