# The Phillips Curve - Inflation, Unemployment, and NAIRU Dynamics

## Overview
The Phillips Curve models the relationship between the rate of inflation and the unemployment rate, evolving from an empirical observation into modern expectations-augmented macroeconomic theory.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
From Short-Run Tradeoff to Long-Run Neutrality: In 1958, A.W. Phillips identified an empirical inverse correlation between nominal wage growth and unemployment in the UK. Samuelson and Solow generalized this into an inflation-unemployment tradeoff. In 1968, Milton Friedman and Edmund Phelps independently demonstrated that this tradeoff exists only in the **short run** ($\pi_t = \pi_t^e - \beta(u_t - u^*) + \epsilon_t$). In the **long run**, as workers and firms adjust their inflation expectations ($\pi_t^e = \pi_t$), unemployment converges to the **Natural Rate of Unemployment** or **NAIRU** (Non-Accelerating Inflation Rate of Unemployment), rendering the Long-Run Phillips Curve (LRPC) vertical at $u^*$.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
New Keynesian Phillips Curve: Modern central banking utilizes the New Keynesian Phillips Curve (NKPC) rooted in dynamic stochastic general equilibrium (DSGE) modeling with Calvo staggered price-setting, where current inflation depends on expected future inflation and real marginal costs: $\pi_t = \beta E_t[\pi_{t+1}] + \kappa y_t$.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Guides modern central bank dual-mandate decisions (Federal Reserve balancing maximum employment with price stability). The flattening of the Phillips Curve post-2008 has complicated interest rate policy.

## Key Terminology
- **NAIRU**: Non-Accelerating Inflation Rate of Unemployment; the specific unemployment rate below which inflation accelerates.
- **Short-Run Phillips Curve**: Inverse empirical relationship between unemployment and inflation when expectations are sticky.
- **Long-Run Phillips Curve**: Vertical line at the natural rate of unemployment indicating monetary policy neutrality over long horizons.
