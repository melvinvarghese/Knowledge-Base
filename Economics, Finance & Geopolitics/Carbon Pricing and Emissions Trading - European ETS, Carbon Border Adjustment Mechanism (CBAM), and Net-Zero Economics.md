# Carbon Pricing and Emissions Trading - European ETS, Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM), and Net-Zero Economics

## Overview
Carbon pricing is a market-based economic mechanism that places an explicit monetary cost on greenhouse gas emissions, harnessing price signals to drive cost-effective decarbonization toward net-zero targets.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
ETS Mechanics & The Social Cost of Carbon: Grounded in Arthur Pigou's theory of internalizing negative climate externalities. The **European Union Emissions Trading System (EU ETS)**, launched in 2005, is the world's premier cap-and-trade system: it sets a decreasing annual ceiling on total emissions across heavy industry, power generation, and aviation. Covered entities must surrender one tradeable EU Allowance (EUA) for every metric ton of $CO_2$ emitted, allowing the market to discover the marginal abatement cost. The theoretical optimal price is pegged to the **Social Cost of Carbon (SCC)**: the present discounted economic damage of emitting one additional ton of carbon dioxide.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Carbon Leakage & The Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM): A major risk of unilateral carbon pricing is **Carbon Leakage**: domestic industrial manufacturers relocate production to foreign jurisdictions with lax environmental rules, exporting emissions without reducing global carbon levels. In 2023, the EU enacted **CBAM**: an import tariff requiring foreign exporters of carbon-intensive goods (steel, cement, aluminum, fertilizer) to buy carbon certificates matching the EU ETS price, neutralizing competitive disadvantages and forcing trading partners to decarbonize.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Forms the economic engine of global net-zero 2050 commitments, clean hydrogen subsidies, and green steel transformation.

## Key Terminology
- **Emissions Trading System (ETS)**: A cap-and-trade market where polluters buy and sell emissions allowances under a declining statutory emissions ceiling.
- **Carbon Leakage**: The relocation of carbon-intensive manufacturing to jurisdictions with weaker climate regulations to avoid domestic carbon costs.
- **Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM)**: A border carbon tariff levied on imported goods based on their embedded emissions, designed to equalize carbon costs.
