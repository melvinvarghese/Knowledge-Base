# The Washington Consensus vs The Beijing Consensus

## Overview
The Washington Consensus and Beijing Consensus represent competing paradigms of economic development and state governance in the developing world.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Washington Consensus (John Williamson, 1989): Recommended neoliberal market-oriented policy prescriptions promoted by the IMF, World Bank, and US Treasury for crisis-wracked developing nations: fiscal discipline, public expenditure reduction, tax reform, trade liberalization, foreign direct investment deregulation, privatization of state enterprises, and secure property rights.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Beijing Consensus (Joshua Cooper Ramo, 2004): An alternative model derived from China's successful economic ascension: state capitalism, gradualist incremental reform rather than shock therapy, heavy state investment in infrastructure, selective market liberalization, technological innovation, and authoritarian political stability without Western democratic conditions.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Rival models for developing nations in Africa, Latin America, and Southeast Asia; battle for global economic governance norms.

## Key Terminology
- **Washington Consensus**: Set of ten neoliberal free-market policy prescriptions promoted by international financial institutions for developing nations.
- **Beijing Consensus**: Model of economic development characterized by authoritarian politics, state capitalism, and incremental marketization.
- **State Capitalism**: Economic system where the state undertakes commercial economic activity and owns controlling shares in major enterprises.
