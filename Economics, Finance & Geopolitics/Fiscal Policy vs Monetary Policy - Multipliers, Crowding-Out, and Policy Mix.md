# Fiscal Policy vs Monetary Policy - Multipliers, Crowding-Out, and Policy Mix

## Overview
Fiscal policy and monetary policy are the two fundamental levers used by sovereign governments and central banks to regulate macroeconomic performance and stability.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Core Mechanisms & Transmission Channels: **Fiscal policy** is executed by elected legislatures and finance ministries through taxation and government expenditure, directly altering aggregate demand via the fiscal multiplier ($k = \frac{1}{1 - MPC}$). **Monetary policy** is conducted by independent central banks manipulating short-term interest rates, reserve requirements, and balance sheet scale, transmitting through interest rate, exchange rate, asset price, and bank lending channels.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Crowding-Out & Policy Coordination: Expansionary fiscal deficits increase loanable funds demand, pushing interest rates higher and 'crowding out' private corporate investment, unless accommodated by monetary expansion. In a **liquidity trap** (where policy rates hit the Zero Lower Bound, ZLB), monetary policy loses conventional traction, making fiscal policy exceptionally potent.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
The 2020-2021 COVID-19 pandemic response exemplified unprecedented fiscal-monetary coordination, deploying trillions in direct cash transfers alongside immediate central bank asset purchase backstops.

## Key Terminology
- **Fiscal Multiplier**: Ratio of change in national output resulting from an autonomous change in government spending.
- **Crowding-Out Effect**: Phenomenon where increased government borrowing drives up interest rates, reducing private sector capital investment.
- **Zero Lower Bound (ZLB)**: Macroeconomic constraint where nominal interest rates cannot be driven significantly below zero, limiting conventional monetary easing.
