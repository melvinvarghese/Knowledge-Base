# Consumer Choice Theory - Marginal Utility, Indifference Curves, and Budget Constraints

## Overview
Consumer Choice Theory models how rational economic agents allocate scarce disposable income across competing consumption bundles to maximize subjective utility.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Foundations of Neoclassical Choice: Assumes complete, transitive, and non-satiated consumer preferences. Preferences are represented geometrically by **Indifference Curves** ($U(x_1, x_2) = C$), which are downward-sloping and convex to the origin due to the diminishing **Marginal Rate of Substitution (MRS)**: $MRS_{1,2} = -\frac{dx_2}{dx_1} = \frac{MU_1}{MU_2}$. The consumer faces a linear **Budget Constraint**: $P_1 x_1 + P_2 x_2 = I$. Constrained utility maximization occurs at the point of tangency between the budget line and the highest attainable indifference curve, yielding the equi-marginal condition: $\frac{MU_1}{P_1} = \frac{MU_2}{P_2}$.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Income and Substitution Effects: The Slutsky equation decomposes consumer response to a price change into the **Substitution Effect** (movement along the same indifference curve to buy cheaper relative goods) and the **Income Effect** (change in consumption due to altered real purchasing power), explaining upward-sloping demand for rare Giffen goods.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Provides the axiomatic microeconomic foundation for market demand curves, welfare economics, and cost-of-living indices.

## Key Terminology
- **Marginal Utility (MU)**: Additional subjective satisfaction derived from consuming one additional unit of a good or service.
- **Indifference Curve**: Graph showing combinations of two goods that provide the consumer with identical levels of utility.
- **Marginal Rate of Substitution (MRS)**: Rate at which a consumer is willing to give up one good in exchange for another while maintaining the same utility.
