# The Efficient Market Hypothesis (EMH) and Market Anomalies

## Overview
The Efficient Market Hypothesis, formulated by Eugene Fama (1970), asserts that asset prices fully reflect all available information, making it impossible to consistently achieve excess returns.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Three Forms of Market Efficiency: 1. Weak Form: Asset prices reflect all past trading history (technical analysis cannot yield excess returns); 2. Semi-Strong Form: Prices reflect all publicly available information (fundamental analysis cannot beat the market); 3. Strong Form: Prices reflect all information, public and private (insider trading cannot beat the market).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Market Anomalies & Speculative Bubbles: Critics (Robert Shiller) highlight empirical market anomalies: excess market volatility, momentum effects, value premiums, and speculative asset bubbles (Dot-com bubble, 2008 housing bubble) driven by herd behavior and irrational exuberance.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Theoretical foundation of passive low-cost index funds, ongoing active vs passive fund management debate.

## Key Terminology
- **Efficient Market Hypothesis**: Financial theory stating that asset prices reflect all available information, rendering market-beating strategies ineffective.
- **Random Walk Hypothesis**: Theory that stock market prices evolve according to a random walk and therefore cannot be predicted.
- **Irrational Exuberance**: Psychological state where investor enthusiasm drives asset prices far beyond their fundamental economic valuation.
