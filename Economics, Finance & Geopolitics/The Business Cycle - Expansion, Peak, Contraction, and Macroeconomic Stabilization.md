# The Business Cycle - Expansion, Peak, Contraction, and Macroeconomic Stabilization

## Overview
The business cycle refers to the economy-wide fluctuations in production, trade, and general economic activity over periods of several months to years around a long-term growth trend.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Phases & Driving Forces: The cycle traverses four canonical phases: **Expansion** (rising employment, consumer spending, and credit expansion), **Peak** (capacity constraints, wage pressures, and inflationary friction), **Contraction / Recession** (two consecutive quarters of declining real GDP, rising unemployment, and inventory liquidation), and **Trough** (stabilization and recovery inception). Explanatory paradigms range from Austrian credit malinvestment and Keynesian 'animal spirits' to Real Business Cycle (RBC) theory, which attributes cycles to exogenous technology shocks and dynamic general equilibrium responses.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Stabilization Policies: Countercyclical fiscal policy (automatic stabilizers like unemployment insurance; discretionary stimulus packages) and monetary policy (rate cuts, reserve requirements, forward guidance) aim to smooth output volatility, avoiding the boom-bust cycle known historically as the 'whipsaw'.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
NBER Business Cycle Dating Committee formally identifies cycle turning points in the US. Modern macroeconomic surveillance utilizes composite leading indicators (LEI), purchasing managers indices (PMI), and corporate credit spreads.

## Key Terminology
- **Recession**: A significant, widespread decline in economic activity lasting more than a few months, typically marked by contracting real GDP.
- **Automatic Stabilizers**: Government fiscal mechanisms, such as progressive income taxes and social transfers, that automatically counter cyclical trends without new legislation.
- **Animal Spirits**: Keynes' term for human emotion, confidence, and instinct that drive economic cycles and financial speculation.
