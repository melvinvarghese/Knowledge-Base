# Behavioral Nudge Theory and Public Choice Economics

## Overview
Nudge Theory and Public Choice Economics analyze government and civic policy interventions by reconciling behavioral human psychology with self-interested political incentives.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Choice Architecture & Soft Paternalism: Richard Thaler and Cass Sunstein introduced 'Nudge' theory: altering the presentation of choices (choice architecture) to steer individual decisions in predictable directions without coercively forbidding any options. Examples include default opt-out organ donation policies and automatic employee 401(k) pension enrollment.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Public Choice Economics (James Buchanan): Applies microeconomic self-interest analysis to government officials. Rejects the romantic view that politicians and bureaucrats act purely for public benevolence, demonstrating that regulatory capture, rent-seeking, and special-interest lobbying distort state interventions.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Behavioral Insights Teams in government policymaking, antitrust reform, campaign finance regulation.

## Key Terminology
- **Choice Architecture**: Design of different ways in which choices can be presented to consumers and the impact of that presentation on decision-making.
- **Public Choice Theory**: Use of economic tools to deal with traditional problems of political science, treating politicians as self-interested actors.
- **Rent-Seeking**: Attempting to obtain economic gain from others without reciprocating any benefits to society through wealth creation.
