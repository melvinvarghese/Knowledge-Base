# Sacred Geometry and Cosmic Symbolism

## Overview
From Pythagoras's musical ratios and Gothic rose windows to Islamic *girih* stars and Hindu-Buddhist *mandalas*, sacred geometry treats circles, squares, and golden proportions as visible reflections of cosmic order.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Across traditions, basic shapes carry shared symbolic grammar: the circle evokes infinite divine unity without beginning or end; the square anchors four cardinal earthly directions; and 'squaring the circle' (seen in mosque domes resting via *pendentives* on square halls, or Hindu temple *Vastu Purusha Mandala* grids) unites heaven and earth harmoniously.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
In Tibetan *Kalachakra* sand *mandalas*, monks spend days laying millions of colored sand grains into symmetric palaces only to sweep them away into flowing rivers—dramatizing impermanence (*anicca*). Meanwhile, medieval Christian cathedral masons applied *ad quadratum* geometric proportions to flood stone sanctuaries with divine light.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Sacred geometry bridges mathematics, contemplative visualization, and architectural beauty across diverse civilizations.

## Key Terminology
- **Vastu Purusha Mandala**: Sacred Hindu geometric grid orienting temple proportions to cosmic cardinal order.
- **Ad quadratum**: Medieval Gothic architectural method deriving vault heights geometrically from nested squares.
- **Sri Yantra**: Classic Hindu geometric meditation diagram of nine interlocking triangles symbolizing cosmic union.
- **Golden ratio (Phi)**: Irrational proportion (~1.618) celebrated in classical aesthetics and Renaissance proportions.
- **Sand mandala**: Intricate Tibetan Buddhist geometric artwork ritually swept away upon completion to teach impermanence.
- **Labyrinth (Chartres)**: Single-path medieval cathedral floor geometry walked slowly as a symbolic mini-pilgrimage.
