# Death, Afterlife, and Funerary Rituals

## Overview
Confronting mortality, every human culture weaves rituals of mourning and visions of what awaits beyond breath—splitting broadly between linear Abrahamic bodily resurrection/judgment and cyclical Dharmic rebirth, while funerary rites guide survivors gently through grief.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Abrahamic traditions largely envision one mortal life followed by judgment and eternal destiny: classical Judaism evolved from shadowy *Sheol* toward resurrection (*Olam HaBa*), Christianity centers on bodily resurrection and heaven/hell (with Catholic Purgatory for final purification), and Islam vividly depicts *Barzakh* and the Day of Judgment (*Yawm al-Din*) leading to *Jannah* (Paradise) or *Jahannam*.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Conversely, Hindu, Jain, and Buddhist traditions map cyclical *saṃsāra*—souls or karmic streams cycling across human, animal, heavenly, or hellish realms until *mokṣa* or *nirvāṇa*—while Tibetan *bardo* chants guide consciousness peacefully through death transitions.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Materially, funerals honor diverse values: Zoroastrian sky-exposure towers, Hindu riverbank cremations releasing ashes to the Ganges, Jewish rapid plain pine-box burial (*shiva* seven-day mourning), and Mexican *Día de los Muertos* joyful marigold altars.

## Key Terminology
- **Resurrection vs. Reincarnation**: Linear restoration of embodied personhood at end of time versus cyclical rebirth across lifetimes.
- **Purgatory**: Catholic doctrine of temporary post-death purification preparing saved souls for heaven.
- **Barzakh**: Islamic intermediate spiritual barrier-state between physical death and final Resurrection.
- **Shivah**: Seven-day Jewish home mourning period supported by visiting community meals and prayer.
- **Día de los Muertos**: Syncretic Mexican holiday welcoming deceased loved ones back joyfully with *ofrenda* altars.
- **Green / Natural burial**: Modern eco-conscious funerary practice returning unembalmed bodies directly to earth without concrete vaults.
