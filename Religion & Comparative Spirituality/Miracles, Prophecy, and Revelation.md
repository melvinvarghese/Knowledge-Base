# Miracles, Prophecy, and Revelation

## Overview
Most historical faiths claim divine communication through prophetic messengers, sacred scriptures, and miraculous signs—sparking enduring philosophical scrutiny from Spinoza and David Hume over what counts as credible testimony for extraordinary events.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
In his famous 1748 essay 'Of Miracles,' Scottish philosopher David Hume defined a miracle as a 'violation of the laws of nature' and argued no human testimony can establish a miracle unless the falsehood of the witness would be *even more* miraculous than the event itself. Conversely, theologians like Augustine and C.S. Lewis viewed miracles not as arbitrary rule-breaking, but as signs (*sēmeia*) of cosmic renewal—while modern theologians often stress symbolic spiritual meaning over forensic proof.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Similarly, 'revelation' spans *propositional* models (verbal dictation of scripture, prominent in Islam's Qur'an and fundamentalist Protestantism) and *non-propositional* encounter models (God revealing divine presence through historical liberation or Jesus's life, interpreted fallibly by human writers).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Understanding diverse views of revelation explains why literalist versus allegorical readings divide modern religious communities.

## Key Terminology
- **Miracle (Humean definition)**: Supposed divine suspension or violation of established natural laws, contested by Hume's testimony critique.
- **General vs. Special revelation**: Knowledge of God accessible through nature and conscience versus specific prophetic or scriptural disclosure.
- **Prophecy**: Speaking truth on God's behalf—encompassing social justice critique as much as future prediction.
- **Verbal plenary inspiration**: Doctrine that every word of scripture is divinely guided and free from error.
- **Theophany**: Direct visible or auditory manifestation of God to a human witness (e.g., Burning Bush).
- **Demythologization**: Rudolf Bultmann's theological method interpreting ancient miraculous cosmology existentially for modern hearers.
