# Christianity: Origins, Doctrine, and Denominations

## Overview
Christianity emerged in 1st-century Roman Judea from the life, teachings, crucifixion, and proclaimed resurrection of Jesus of Nazareth. Expanding across the Mediterranean through Paul of Tarsus and early churches, it became the world's largest religion (~2.4 billion adherents), organized around the Trinity, Incarnation, and salvation by grace.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Rooted in Second Temple Jewish prophetic expectations, the four canonical Gospels (Matthew, Mark, Luke, John) and Pauline Epistles (compiled into the New Testament alongside the Hebrew Bible as Old Testament) proclaim Jesus as the Messiah (*Christos*) and incarnate Son of God whose sacrificial death and resurrection reconcile humanity with God. Early ecumenical councils—Nicaea (325 CE) and Chalcedon (451 CE)—formulated the Nicene Creed affirming one God in three co-equal persons (Trinity: Father, Son, Holy Spirit) and Christ as fully divine and fully human.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Over two millennia, Christianity fractured into major branches: Roman Catholicism (centered on the Papacy and seven sacraments), Eastern Orthodoxy (preserving conciliar patristic liturgy), Oriental Orthodoxy (Coptic, Armenian, Ethiopian), and Protestantism (launched by the 16th-century Reformation). In the 20th–21st centuries, Pentecostalism and Charismatic Christianity surged rapidly across Latin America, Sub-Saharan Africa, and Asia.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Christian ethics, monasticism, hospitals, and universities profoundly shaped Western law, art, music, and global humanitarianism.

## Key Terminology
- **Trinity**: Core Christian doctrine of one God existing eternally in three distinct persons: Father, Son, Holy Spirit.
- **Incarnation**: Belief that the eternal divine Word (*Logos*) took full human flesh in Jesus of Nazareth.
- **Nicene Creed**: 4th-century ecumenical statement of foundational Christian orthodoxy.
- **Gospel (*Euangelion*)**: 'Good news,' referring both to the Christian message and the four canonical biographies of Jesus.
- **Sacrament**: Visible sacred ritual (e.g., Baptism, Eucharist) conveying inward divine grace.
- **Ecumenical council**: Gathering of bishops across Christian churches to settle doctrine and discipline.
