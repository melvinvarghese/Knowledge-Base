# Bioethics Across World Religions

## Overview
Breakthroughs in IVF, CRISPR gene editing, organ transplantation, and brain-death life support confront doctors and families with moral questions science alone cannot settle—making comparative religious bioethics indispensable in modern hospitals.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Major traditions bring remarkably nuanced ethical frameworks to clinics. Jewish *Pikuach Nefesh* ('saving a life' overrides nearly every Sabbath or dietary rule) strongly endorses organ donation and therapeutic research, while Islamic *fiqh* councils widely permit IVF within marriage and brain-death organ donation as life-saving charity (*sadaqah*). Catholic bioethics anchors strict protection of human life from conception alongside rejecting artificial contraception yet allowing proportionate withdrawal of 'extraordinary' futile life support.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Buddhist bioethics evaluates intentions (*cetanā*) around minimizing suffering and preserving clear dying awareness, often supporting palliative hospice care while remaining cautious toward commercialized genetics.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Hospital ethics committees routinely consult multi-faith chaplains and scholars to bridge cutting-edge medicine with families' deepest sacred values.

## Key Terminology
- **Pikuach Nefesh**: Jewish halakhic mandate that preserving human life supersedes almost all other ritual commandments.
- **Extraordinary vs. Ordinary means**: Catholic bioethical distinction permitting withdrawal of burdensome, futile medical machines.
- **Brain death**: Irreversible cessation of all brain function, accepted by most but not all religious legal authorities.
- **Moral status of the embryo**: Contested ethical threshold deciding when developing human cells carry full moral personhood.
- **Sadaqah Jariyah**: Ongoing charitable merit applied in Islamic bioethics to justify voluntary organ donation.
- **CRISPR germline ethics**: Shared interfaith caution regarding heritable human genetic enhancement versus curing disease.
