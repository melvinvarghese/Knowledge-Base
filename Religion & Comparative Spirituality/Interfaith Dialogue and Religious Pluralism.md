# Interfaith Dialogue and Religious Pluralism

## Overview
In an interconnected world where mosques, synagogues, temples, and churches share city blocks, interfaith dialogue replaces proselytizing rivalry with respectful mutual listening—asking how believers can remain deeply rooted in their own tradition while honoring truth in others.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Launched symbolically at the 1893 World's Parliament of Religions in Chicago (where Swami Vivekananda famously addressed 'Sisters and Brothers of America'), modern interfaith work accelerated after Vatican II's *Nostra Aetate* (1965) repudiated anti-Semitism and affirmed rays of truth in Hinduism, Buddhism, and Islam.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Theologian Alan Race mapped three classic stances toward religious others: *Exclusivism* (only one path saves), *Inclusivism* (one path holds fullest truth while sincere outsiders may still be saved by grace—e.g., Karl Rahner's 'anonymous Christians'), and *Pluralism* (John Hick's Kantian proposal that major faiths are distinct cultural lenses onto one infinite Real). Meanwhile, 'Scriptural Reasoning' brings Jews, Christians, and Muslims together simply to read sacred texts side-by-side without erasing real differences.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Interfaith Youth Core (Eboo Patel) and local multi-faith councils mobilize diverse congregations around shared humanitarian service, disaster relief, and anti-hate solidarity.

## Key Terminology
- **Exclusivism, Inclusivism, Pluralism**: Three theological paradigms mapping salvation and truth across rival world religions.
- **Nostra Aetate (1965)**: Landmark Vatican II declaration transforming Catholic relations with Judaism, Islam, and Eastern religions.
- **Scriptural Reasoning**: Interfaith practice gathering Jews, Christians, and Muslims to study each other's texts collaboratively.
- **Parliament of the World's Religions**: Historic global interfaith convocation launched in Chicago in 1893.
- **Comparative theology**: Francis Clooney's method deepening one's own faith through close, respectful study of another tradition's texts.
- **Ecumenism**: Movement seeking unity, mutual recognition, and cooperation among divided Christian denominations.
