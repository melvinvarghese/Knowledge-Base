# Gnosticism and Early Christian Diversity

## Overview
Before 4th-century imperial councils unified orthodox Christianity, 2nd–3rd century Mediterranean cities teemed with rival Christianities—most famously 'Gnostic' movements claiming secret liberating insight (*gnōsis*) into an imprisoned divine spark.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Known for centuries only through hostile attacks by orthodox bishops like Irenaeus, Gnosticism transformed dramatically in 1945 when Egyptian farmers unearthed the *Nag Hammadi Library*—13 Coptic codices preserving lost texts including the *Gospel of Thomas*, *Gospel of Mary*, and *Apocryphon of John*. Many Sethian and Valentinian Gnostics radically inverted Genesis: distinguishing the true unknowable Supreme God from a flawed or ignorant lower creator (*Demiurge*) who trapped sparks of divine light inside material bodies.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Salvation came not via sacrificial atonement, but awakening through secret *gnōsis* (inner spiritual knowledge) delivered by Jesus as luminous revealer.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Orthodox church fathers rejected Gnostic dualism as denying the goodness of creation and physical incarnation, yet Gnostic motifs echoed later in Manichaeism, Cathars, Carl Jung, and modern sci-fi.

## Key Terminology
- **Gnōsis**: Direct esoteric spiritual insight liberating the inner divine spark from material illusion.
- **Nag Hammadi Library**: 1945 Egyptian discovery of 4th-century Coptic manuscripts preserving diverse Gnostic and early Christian texts.
- **Demiurge**: Lower ignorant or flawed craftsman-deity blamed in Gnostic myth for creating the flawed material cosmos.
- **Gospel of Thomas**: Non-narrative Nag Hammadi collection of 114 sayings attributed to Jesus.
- **Proto-orthodoxy**: Bart Ehrman's term for the early Christian faction that eventually defined canonical orthodoxy.
- **Docetism**: Early heresy claiming Christ only *appeared* to possess a physical suffering body.
