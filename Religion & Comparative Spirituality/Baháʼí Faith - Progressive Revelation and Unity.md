# Baháʼí Faith: Progressive Revelation and Unity

## Overview
Founded in 19th-century Persia by Baháʼu'lláh (1817–1892), the Baháʼí Faith is the youngest major independent world religion (~5–8 million adherents globally), teaching the oneness of God, oneness of religion via 'progressive revelation,' and unity of humanity.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Emerging from the 1844 messianic *Bábí* movement in Shia Iran despite fierce persecution, Baháʼu'lláh proclaimed himself the promised divine educator uniting past prophetic dispensations: Abraham, Moses, Krishna, Buddha, Zoroaster, Jesus, and Muhammad each revealed eternal spiritual truths while adapting social laws to their historical era ('progressive revelation').

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Key Baháʼí principles include eliminating racial, gender, and national prejudice, harmonizing science and religion, universal education, an auxiliary international language, and abolishing clergy in favor of democratically elected nine-member Local, National, and Universal House of Justice councils (headquartered on Mount Carmel, Haifa).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Known for serene nine-sided Houses of Worship open to all faiths worldwide, Baháʼís endure ongoing persecution in Iran while championing global peace and UN civil-society initiatives.

## Key Terminology
- **Progressive revelation**: Baháʼí doctrine that major world religions represent successive chapters of one unfolding divine guidance.
- **Baháʼu'lláh**: 'Glory of God' (1817–1892), prophet-founder of the Baháʼí Faith.
- **The Báb**: 1844 Persian herald ('The Gate') whose movement preceded Baháʼu'lláh.
- **Universal House of Justice**: Elected nine-member supreme governing institution of the Baháʼí community in Haifa.
- **Harmony of science and religion**: Core tenet rejecting superstition and insisting true faith aligns with rational scientific inquiry.
- **Mashriqu'l-Adhkár**: Nine-sided Baháʼí House of Worship welcoming people of every background for silent prayer.
