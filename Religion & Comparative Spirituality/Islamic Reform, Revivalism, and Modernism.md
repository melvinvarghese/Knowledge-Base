# Islamic Reform, Revivalism, and Modernism

## Overview
Confronting 19th–20th century European colonialism and rapid scientific modernity, Muslim thinkers launched sharply divergent renewal movements—spanning liberal Islamic Modernism reclaiming rational *ijtihad*, literalist Salafism, and activist Political Islam.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Thinkers like Jamal al-Din al-Afghani, Muhammad Abduh in Cairo, and Muhammad Iqbal in South Asia championed *Islamic Modernism*: blaming stagnation on blind imitation (*taqlid*) of medieval manuals and urging fresh *ijtihad* to harmonize Islam with constitutional democracy, women's education, and modern science. Conversely, conservative *Salafi* revivalists urged stripping away medieval Sufi and cultural accretions to return strictly to the first three pious generations (*al-salaf al-ṣāliḥ*).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
In 1928 Egypt, Hassan al-Banna founded the Muslim Brotherhood ('Islam is the solution'), pioneering modern Political Islam (*Islamism*)—later radicalized in prison by Sayyid Qutb into militant rejection of secular regimes, sharply opposed by mainstream Islamic scholars and peaceful democratic Islamic parties (such as Indonesia's massive Nahdlatul Ulama).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Contemporary Muslim reformers—including Islamic feminists like Amina Wadud and Khaled Abou El Fadl—continue re-reading scripture for human rights and pluralism.

## Key Terminology
- **Islamic Modernism**: 19th–20th century reform movement reconciling Islam with constitutionalism, science, and gender equity.
- **Salafism**: Strict purist Sunni movement seeking to emulate the earliest three Muslim generations exclusively.
- **Taqlid vs. Ijtihad**: Uncritical adherence to medieval legal precedent versus fresh independent scriptural reasoning.
- **Political Islam (Islamism)**: Ideologies seeking to govern modern state law and society explicitly through Islamic principles.
- **Nahdlatul Ulama**: World's largest independent Muslim organization (Indonesia) championing pluralistic 'Islam Nusantara.'
- **Islamic feminism**: Scholarly movement retrieving egalitarian Qur'anic ethics against patriarchal legal interpretations.
