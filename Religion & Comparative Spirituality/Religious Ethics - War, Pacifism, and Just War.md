# Religious Ethics: War, Pacifism, and Just War

## Overview
Every major religious tradition wrestles with a painful moral paradox: how to honor sacred prohibitions against killing while protecting innocent communities from tyranny. Responses span strict absolute pacifism, Christian Just War criteria, Islamic *jihad* rules of restraint, and Hindu *dharma* war ethics.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Early pre-Constantinian Christians, historic Quakers, Mennonites, and Jain ascetics embraced absolute pacifism—refusing all lethal violence regardless of consequence. Once Christianity became imperial law, Augustine and Thomas Aquinas forged 'Just War Theory' (*jus ad bellum*: legitimate authority, just cause, right intention, last resort; *jus in bello*: proportionality and noncombatant immunity).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Similarly, Islamic jurisprudence distinguished greater spiritual *jihad al-nafs* (inner struggle against ego) from lesser armed *jihad*, strictly forbidding killing women, children, monks, or burning crops. In the *Bhagavad Gītā*, Krishna counsels warrior Arjuna to fight a righteous *dharma* war without personal hatred—later reinterpreted allegorically by Gandhi as nonviolent inner combat.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
These religious ethical traditions directly laid the moral foundations of modern Geneva Conventions and international humanitarian law.

## Key Terminology
- **Absolute pacifism**: Unconditional moral rejection of all lethal violence and warfare regardless of cause.
- **Just War criteria**: Moral framework limiting armed conflict to last-resort defense, legitimate authority, and civilian immunity.
- **Jihad al-nafs vs. Lesser jihad**: Islamic distinction between primary inner moral struggle and strictly regulated armed defense.
- **Noncombatant immunity**: Shared Christian, Islamic, and international-law prohibition against deliberately targeting civilians.
- **Historic Peace Churches**: Christian denominations (Quakers, Mennonites, Church of the Brethren) committed to principled pacifism.
- **Dharma-yuddha**: Ancient Indian code of honorable warfare banning poisoned weapons and attacks on surrendering foes.
