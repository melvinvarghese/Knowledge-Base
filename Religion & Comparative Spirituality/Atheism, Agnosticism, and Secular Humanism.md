# Atheism, Agnosticism, and Secular Humanism

## Overview
Atheism, agnosticism, and secular humanism represent diverse non-theistic philosophical traditions grounding morality, human dignity, and cosmic wonder without belief in deities. Tracing from ancient Indian *Cārvāka* materialists and Greek Epicureans to Enlightenment freethinkers, non-belief has a rich intellectual history.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Philosophers distinguish positive/hard atheism (explicitly denying God's existence), negative/soft atheism (lacking belief in gods), and Thomas Henry Huxley's *agnosticism* (holding divine claims currently unknowable or lacking sufficient evidence). Ancient roots include India's skeptical *Cārvāka* school, Lucretius's Epicurean atomism freeing humans from fear of vengeful gods, and Spinoza's pantheistic identification of God with Nature (*Deus sive Natura*).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
In the 20th–21st centuries, Secular Humanism (Bertrand Russell, A.C. Grayling) affirmed 'good without God' through empathy, reason, and universal human rights—contrasting with confrontational 'New Atheism' (Richard Dawkins, Christopher Hitchens) while building secular Sunday Assemblies, ethical culture societies, and humanist hospital chaplains.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Legal human rights treaties increasingly protect freedom *of* and freedom *from* religion equally, even as apostasy remains criminalized in several authoritarian states.

## Key Terminology
- **Atheism vs. Agnosticism**: Absence or denial of belief in deities versus suspending judgment on ultimate unknowability.
- **Secular Humanism**: Ethical philosophy grounding morality, reason, and human flourishing without supernatural revelation.
- **Cārvāka (Lokāyata)**: Ancient Indian materialist school rejecting Vedas, karma, and afterlife in favor of empirical perception.
- **Russell's Teapot**: Bertrand Russell's analogy showing the burden of proof rests on extraordinary unfalsifiable claims.
- **New Atheism**: Early-2000s polemical movement critiquing religious dogma directly on scientific and moral grounds.
- **Apostasy**: Formal renunciation or abandonment of a previously held religious faith.
