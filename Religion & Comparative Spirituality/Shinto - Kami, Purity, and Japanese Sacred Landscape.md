# Shinto: Kami, Purity, and Japanese Sacred Landscape

## Overview
Shinto ('Way of the Kami') is Japan's indigenous spiritual tradition, centered not on dogmatic scripture or founder, but on reverent harmony with *kami*—sacred spirits inhabiting mountains, waterfalls, ancient trees, storms, and revered ancestors.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Lacking an official canon before 8th-century *Kojiki* and *Nihon Shoki* chronicles, Shinto centers on local shrines (*jinja*) entered through vermilion *torii* gates marking the threshold from ordinary space into pure sacred ground. Worshippers rinse hands and mouth at *temizuya* water basins before clapping twice and bowing silently—prioritizing ritual purity (*harae* cleansing) over moral guilt-sin binaries, viewing impurity (*kegare*) as temporary stagnation cleared by water, salt, and seasonal *matsuri* festivals.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Historically, Shinto coexisted harmoniously with Japanese Buddhism (*shinbutsu-shūgō* syncretism) for over a millennium until 19th-century Meiji nationalism forcibly separated them to forge 'State Shinto' around imperial divinity—dismantled after 1945 into peaceful Shrine Shinto.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Today, millions of Japanese visit shrines for New Year *hatsumōde*, baby blessings, and career prayers alongside Buddhist funerals, seamlessly weaving both traditions.

## Key Terminology
- **Kami**: Sacred spirits, natural forces, or awe-inspiring presences dwelling in mountains, trees, and ancestors.
- **Torii gate**: Iconic two-post shrine gateway marking the boundary between profane everyday space and sacred ground.
- **Kegare and Harae**: Ritual impurity/stagnation and purification ceremonies restoring natural vitality.
- **Matsuri**: Vibrant seasonal shrine festivals featuring portable *mikoshi* palanquins, music, and community joy.
- **Kojiki**: 712 CE oldest Japanese chronicle recording Shinto creation myths of Amaterasu and Izanagi.
- **Shinbutsu-shūgō**: Millennium-long historical syncretism blending Japanese Shinto *kami* with Buddhist bodhisattvas.
