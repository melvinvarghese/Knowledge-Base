# Latter-day Saints (Mormonism) and Restorationism

## Overview
Emerging from New York's 1820s 'Burned-Over District' revivals through Joseph Smith Jr., the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints (LDS) represents America's most globally influential indigenous restorationist faith (~17 million members)—adding the *Book of Mormon*, living prophets, and sacred temples.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Proclaiming that ancient Christianity fell into apostasy after the Apostles, Joseph Smith published the *Book of Mormon* (1830), presented as a translated ancient record of pre-Columbian Israelite peoples visited by the resurrected Christ. Driven west across Ohio, Missouri, and Illinois—where Smith was murdered in 1844—Brigham Young led pioneers across the Plains to Utah's Salt Lake Valley.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Distinctive LDS theology affirms ongoing prophetic revelation, a non-Trinitarian Godhead of three distinct beings, pre-mortal spirit existence, eternal family sealings inside consecrated Temples, and the Word of Wisdom health code.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Abandoning 19th-century plural marriage in 1890 to secure Utah statehood, the modern LDS Church grew into a globalized, missionary-driven denomination renowned for humanitarian welfare and genealogical archives.

## Key Terminology
- **Restorationism**: 19th-century belief that original apostolic Christianity required direct divine restoration rather than mere reform.
- **Book of Mormon**: 1830 sacred scripture of Latter-day Saints standing alongside the Bible.
- **Living Prophet**: Church President revered by Latter-day Saints as receiving ongoing authoritative revelation.
- **Temple sealing**: Sacred LDS ordinance uniting marriages and families for eternity rather than 'until death.'
- **Word of Wisdom**: LDS dietary and health code abstaining from alcohol, tobacco, coffee, and tea.
- **Brigham Young**: Pioneer leader who guided Latter-day Saints to Utah and organized Deseret settlements.
