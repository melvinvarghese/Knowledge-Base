# Hinduism: Sanatana Dharma, Vedas, and Bhakti

## Overview
Hinduism—often called *Sanātana Dharma* ('eternal way') by adherents—is the world's third-largest religion (~1.2 billion people) and its oldest living major tradition. Rather than a single founder or centralized creed, it weaves Vedic ritual, Upanishadic non-dual wisdom, epic ethics, and passionate *bhakti* devotion into a vast pluralistic tapestry.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Rooted in the Indus Valley civilization and Indo-Aryan Vedic hymns (Rigveda, c. 1500–1200 BCE), early *Vedic* religion centered on fire sacrifices (*yajña*) to cosmic deities. Between 800–400 BCE, the philosophical *Upanishads* shifted inward, uniting *Brahman* (infinite ultimate reality) with *Ātman* (inner Self) and framing existence around *karma* (moral causality), *saṃsāra* (rebirth cycle), and *mokṣa* (liberation)—elaborated in popular epics *Mahābhārata* (*Bhagavad Gītā*) and *Rāmāyaṇa*.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
From the 6th century CE onward, the egalitarian *Bhakti* movement transformed Indian spirituality: poet-saints across castes and genders (Mirabai, Kabir, Andal, Tukaram) sang vernacular love poetry to Shiva, Vishnu (Krishna/Rama), or the Goddess Shakti, making personal devotion accessible without Sanskrit priests.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
In the 19th–20th centuries, figures like Swami Vivekananda and Mahatma Gandhi articulated universalist Neo-Vedanta, bringing Yoga and *ahimsa* into global culture.

## Key Terminology
- **Sanātana Dharma**: Traditional self-designation of Hinduism meaning 'eternal cosmic order or duty.'
- **Brahman and Ātman**: Ultimate infinite cosmic reality and the innermost eternal Self, identified in Advaita Vedanta.
- **Karma and Saṃsāra**: Moral law of intentional action and consequence governing the cycle of death and rebirth.
- **Mokṣa**: Ultimate spiritual liberation from the cycle of rebirth and suffering.
- **Bhakti**: Heartfelt personal devotion and loving surrender to a chosen deity (*iṣṭa-devatā*).
- **Bhagavad Gītā**: Beloved 700-verse philosophical dialogue in the *Mahābhārata* harmonizing duty, devotion, and knowledge.
