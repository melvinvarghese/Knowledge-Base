# Zoroastrianism: Dualism and Cosmic Order

## Overview
Founded in ancient Iran by the prophet Zarathustra (Zoroaster), Zoroastrianism is one of the world's oldest continuous ethical-monotheistic traditions—introducing cosmic good-versus-evil dualism, angels, final judgment, and resurrection that profoundly shaped Judaism, Christianity, and Islam.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Composed in Old Avestan (*Gathas*), Zarathustra's hymns proclaim supreme wise creator *Ahura Mazda* ('Lord of Wisdom') opposed by the destructive spirit *Angra Mainyu* (Ahriman). Humans hold radical moral free will to choose *Asha* (truth, cosmic order, righteousness) over *Druj* (deceit), summarized in the triad 'Good Thoughts, Good Words, Good Deeds' (*Humata, Hukhta, Huvarshta*). Fire and sunlight serve as pure symbols of divine light inside Fire Temples—never worshipped as idols.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
State religion of the Persian Achaemenid, Parthian, and Sasanian empires, Zoroastrians declined after the 7th-century Islamic conquest; many migrated to Gujarat, India as the *Parsis*, famed for charity and industry alongside surviving Iranian *Zartoshtis*.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Traditional *Dakhma* ('Towers of Silence') sky-burial practices avoided polluting sacred earth, water, and fire, though modern urban Parsis adapt respectfully.

## Key Terminology
- **Ahura Mazda**: Supreme wise Creator and Lord of Light in Zoroastrian theology.
- **Asha vs. Druj**: Cosmic truth, order, and righteousness opposed to deceit and chaos.
- **Gathas**: Seventeen poetic hymns attributed directly to Prophet Zarathustra in the *Avesta*.
- **Good Thoughts, Words, Deeds**: Central Zoroastrian ethical maxim guiding daily moral choice.
- **Parsis**: Descendants of Iranian Zoroastrian refugees who settled in western India after the 7th–10th centuries.
- **Frashokereti**: Final cosmic renovation when evil is vanquished and creation restored to perfection.
