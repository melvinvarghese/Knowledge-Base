# Alawite, Druze, and Yazidi Traditions

## Overview
Across Syria, Lebanon, Iraq, and Israel survive tightly knit Middle Eastern minority faiths—including the Druze, Alawites, and Yazidis—blending esoteric Neoplatonic, Islamic, and ancient Mesopotamian-Iranian heritages beneath strict initiation and communal solidarity.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Originating in 11th-century Fatimid Cairo, the *Druze* (*Muwahhidun*, 'Unitarians') closed conversion after 1043, guarding esoteric Neoplatonic-Ismaili philosophy among initiated *'Uqqāl* sages while teaching immediate soul transmigration (*taqammus*) and seven moral duties including truthfulness and mutual protection. *Alawites* (Nusayris) in coastal Syria similarly emerged from esoteric 9th-century Twelver Shia mysticism honoring Ali alongside seasonal festivals.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Distinct from Islam entirely, Northern Iraq's *Yazidis* revere transcendent God who entrusted creation to seven angels led by *Melek Taus* (the Peacock Angel)—tragically maligned by outsiders as 'devil worship' despite Yazidis viewing Melek Taus as a redeemed guardian of divine unity.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Suffering horrific 2014 ISIS genocide at Sinjar, Yazidis and Druze highlight the urgent vulnerability and resilience of ancient Middle Eastern pluralism.

## Key Terminology
- **Druze (*Muwahhidun*)**: Closed esoteric monotheistic community in Lebanon, Syria, and Israel emphasizing reincarnation and reason.
- **'Uqqāl vs. Juhhāl**: Druze internal division between initiated religious sages and uninitiated lay community members.
- **Melek Taus (Peacock Angel)**: Central benevolent angel figure in Yazidi cosmology symbolizing divine beauty and repentance.
- **Yazidis**: Endogamous Kurdish-speaking Mesopotamian minority faith synthesizing ancient Iranian and mystical motifs.
- **Taqammus**: Druze belief in immediate rebirth of human souls exclusively into new human bodies.
- **Alawites**: Esoteric Shia-rooted community centered in northwestern Syria and southern Turkey.
