# Fundamentalism and Religious Nationalism

## Overview
Far from a simple medieval leftover, modern religious fundamentalism and religious nationalism are distinctively modern reactions against rapid secularization, colonial humiliation, and cultural upheaval—selectively weaponizing sacred symbols to claim exclusive political sovereignty.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Coined in 1920s American Protestantism (*The Fundamentals*) against Darwinism and historical Bible criticism, 'fundamentalism' was analyzed comparatively across faiths by Martin Marty and Scott Appleby's landmark *Fundamentalism Project*. Rather than passive traditionalism, fundamentalists selectively adopt modern media, bureaucracies, and technology while erecting strict boundaries around supposedly inerrant fundamentals.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
When fused with state power as 'religious nationalism' (Christian nationalism, Hindu *Hindutva*, militant Buddhist nationalism, or Islamist state ideology), faith ceases to be humble devotion and becomes an exclusionary ethnic-political badge casting religious minorities as disloyal outsiders.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Countering exclusionary extremism requires amplifying internal theological voices defending pluralism, constitutional minority protections, and civic democracy.

## Key Terminology
- **Fundamentalism**: Militantly anti-secular modern religious movement asserting selective, inerrant core doctrines.
- **Religious nationalism**: Political ideology fusing national citizenship and state sovereignty with one dominant religious identity.
- **Inerrancy**: Doctrine holding sacred scripture completely free from historical, scientific, or moral error.
- **Hindutva**: Modern Hindu nationalist political ideology distinct from pluralistic spiritual Hinduism.
- **Selective modernization**: Fundamentalist embrace of modern digital media, tech, and weapons while rejecting secular pluralism.
- **Enclave culture**: Mary Douglas's term for tightly bounded communities maintaining strict purity walls against outside society.
