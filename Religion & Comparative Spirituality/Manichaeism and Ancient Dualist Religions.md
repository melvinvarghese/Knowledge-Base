# Manichaeism and Ancient Dualist Religions

## Overview
Before fading under medieval persecution, *Manichaeism*—founded by 3rd-century Persian prophet Mani—was a truly universal world religion stretching from Roman North Africa (briefly captivating young St. Augustine) all the way across the Silk Road to Tang China.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Self-consciously synthesizing Zoroaster, Buddha, and Jesus into one final universal scripture, Mani preached radical cosmic dualism between two eternal co-equal principles: Light (spirit, peace, God) and Darkness (chaotic matter, greed). After Darkness trapped fragments of Light inside the material world, salvation required vegetarian asceticism among the 'Elect' (assisted by married 'Hearers') to liberate Light particles gently back toward heaven.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Fiercely suppressed by Sasanian Zoroastrians, Roman Christians, and Tang emperors, Manichaeism survived centuries in Uyghur Central Asia and coastal Fujian (where the Cao'an shrine still stands), while inspiring later medieval European dualists like Bogomils and French Cathars.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Manichaeism's rise and extinction vividly illustrate how Silk Road empires exchanged religious ideas across Eurasia.

## Key Terminology
- **Manichaeism**: 3rd-century universal dualist world religion founded by Mani spanning Rome to China.
- **Elect vs. Hearers**: Two-tier Manichaean hierarchy separating celibate vegetarian ascetics from supportive lay followers.
- **Cosmic dualism**: Belief in two eternal, opposed cosmic forces of Light and Darkness locked in struggle.
- **Silk Road syncretism**: Cross-cultural adaptation translating Mani's teachings into Buddhist, Christian, and Zoroastrian idioms.
- **Cathars (Albigensians)**: 12th–13th century Southern French dualist movement crushed in the Albigensian Crusade.
- **Manichaean metaphor**: Modern adjective describing overly simplistic black-and-white good-versus-evil thinking.
