# Judaism: Covenant, Torah, and Halakha

## Overview
Judaism is the oldest surviving Abrahamic monotheistic tradition, emerging in the ancient Levant over three millennia ago around the covenant between God (*YHWH*) and the people of Israel. Centered on the *Torah*, ethical observance (*mitzvot*), and communal memory, Judaism transformed after the Temple's destruction into a resilient textual and household tradition.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Biblical Israelite religion centered on the Exodus liberation from Egypt, the Sinai covenant, and sacrificial worship at the Jerusalem Temple. Following the Babylonian Exile (586 BCE) and the Roman destruction of the Second Temple (70 CE), Pharisaic sages reshaped Judaism around synagogue prayer, study, and *halakha* (religious law governing diet, Sabbath rest, and ethics).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Modern Judaism spans diverse movements: Orthodox Judaism (upholding binding divine revelation of Written and Oral Torah), Conservative/Masorti Judaism (preserving *halakha* while adapting historically), Reform/Progressive Judaism (prioritizing ethical autonomy and prophetic justice), and Reconstructionist and secular cultural Judaism.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Across global history, Jewish philosophy (Philo, Maimonides, Spinoza, Abraham Joshua Heschel) and ritual life—from Passover *Seder* to *Yom Kippur*—shaped Western ethics, calendar rhythms, and interfaith theology.

## Key Terminology
- **Torah**: The Five Books of Moses (Genesis–Deuteronomy), and broadly the entirety of Jewish sacred teaching.
- **Halakha**: Collective body of Jewish religious laws derived from the Written Torah and Rabbinic Talmud.
- **Mitzvah (pl. Mitzvot)**: Sacred commandment or ethical obligation (traditionally 613 in the Torah).
- **Shabbat (Sabbath)**: Weekly day of sacred rest from Friday sunset to Saturday nightfall commemorating Creation.
- **Covenant (Brit)**: Solemn mutual commitment between God and the Jewish people.
- **Synagogue**: Local Jewish house of prayer, Torah study, and community assembly.
