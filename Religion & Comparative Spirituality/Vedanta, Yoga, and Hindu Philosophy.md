# Vedanta, Yoga, and Hindu Philosophy

## Overview
Classical Indian philosophy crystallized into six orthodox (*āstika*) schools—most enduringly *Vedānta* and *Yoga*—offering rigorous metaphysical maps of consciousness, suffering, and liberation alongside practical meditative psychology.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Drawn from the concluding Upanishads ('end of the Vedas'), Vedānta split into rival interpretations of *Brahman* and *Ātman*: Adi Shankara's *Advaita* (Non-Dual) Vedānta argued only *Brahman* is ultimately real while apparent multiplicity arises from *māyā* (cosmic illusion); Ramanuja's *Vishishtadvaita* (Qualified Non-Dualism) saw souls and world as real parts of God's body; and Madhva's *Dvaita* insisted on eternal distinction between God and individual souls.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Complementing Samkhya dualism (*Purusha* pure awareness vs. *Prakriti* evolving nature), Patanjali's *Yoga Sutras* (c. 2nd–4th century CE) defined Yoga not as gymnastics, but as 'stilling the fluctuations of the mind' (*chitta-vritti-nirodha*) through an Eight-Limbed (*Ashtanga*) path: ethical restraints (*yamas* like *ahimsa*), observances, posture (*āsana*), breath control (*prāṇāyāma*), sense withdrawal, concentration, meditation, and absorption (*samādhi*).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
While modern global Yoga emphasizes physical fitness, classical Yoga remains a holistic psychological and ethical discipline.

## Key Terminology
- **Advaita Vedānta**: Adi Shankara's non-dual philosophy affirming sole ultimate reality of *Brahman* beyond *māyā*.
- **Māyā**: Cosmic creative power or illusion masking underlying non-dual reality.
- **Patanjali's Eight Limbs**: Systematic path from ethics (*yama/niyama*) and breath (*prāṇāyāma*) to meditative *samādhi*.
- **Purusha and Prakriti**: Samkhya-Yoga dualism distinguishing silent conscious witness from dynamic material nature.
- **Samādhi**: Deep meditative absorption where subject-object separation dissolves.
- **Darśana**: Six classical Hindu philosophical 'viewpoints' (Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Samkhya, Yoga, Mimamsa, Vedanta).
