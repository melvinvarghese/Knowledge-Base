# Problem of Evil and Theodicy

## Overview
For believers and skeptics alike, the Problem of Evil poses the sharpest challenge to classical monotheism: if God is all-powerful, all-knowing, and wholly good, why do Holocausts, childhood bone cancer, and earthquakes exist?

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Formulated starkly by Epicurus and sharpened by David Hume, the 'logical' problem of evil claimed God and evil are strictly contradictory. Alvin Plantinga's 'Free Will Defense' largely answered pure logical contradiction by arguing even an omnipotent God cannot force genuinely free creatures to choose only good. Yet the 'evidential' problem of evil (William Rowe's fawn dying slowly in a forest fire) presses harder: *gratuitous* natural suffering seems deeply improbable under a loving Creator.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Theodicies attempt positive explanations: Augustine blamed moral evil on free-will Fall; Irenaeus and John Hick proposed a 'soul-making' world where struggle cultivates compassion and courage; and Process Theology (Alfred North Whitehead) reimagined God as persuasive love rather than coercive omnipotence. Meanwhile, Biblical Job and post-Holocaust Jewish theologians insist protest and lament outshine neat philosophical excuses.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Conversely, Karma-based Dharmic traditions explain uneven suffering via past-life moral causality while shifting focus toward escaping *saṃsāra*.

## Key Terminology
- **Theodicy**: Theological or philosophical attempt to reconcile God's goodness and omnipotence with the reality of evil.
- **Logical vs. Evidential evil**: Claim that God and evil are logically contradictory versus claim that vast gratuitous evil makes God improbable.
- **Free Will Defense**: Plantinga's argument that genuine moral freedom precludes God guaranteeing zero moral evil.
- **Soul-making theodicy**: Hick's Irenaean model viewing an imperfect world as necessary for cultivating mature moral character.
- **Moral vs. Natural evil**: Suffering caused by human cruelty versus suffering from earthquakes, disease, and predation.
- **Process Theology**: Theological framework viewing God as evolving alongside creation through persuasive love rather than absolute coercion.
