# Neopaganism, Wicca, and Earth-Centered Paths

## Overview
Modern Neopaganism—including Wicca, Druidry, and Heathenry—revives and creatively reimagines pre-Christian polytheism, seasonal nature cycles, and feminist goddess spirituality as decentralized, nature-revering practices.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Launched publicly in 1954 when retired British civil servant Gerald Gardner published *Witchcraft Today*, Wicca drew on ceremonial magic, Romantic folklore, and Margaret Murray's (now-discredited) witch-cult thesis to honor a Horned God and Triple Goddess across eight solar/agricultural *Wheel of the Year* Sabbats (*Samhain, Yule, Ostara, Beltane, Litha*). Later feminist 'Reclaiming' Wicca (Starhawk) and Dianic traditions intertwined environmental activism with sacred immanence.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Practicing mostly in small autonomous covens or solitary outdoor rituals guided by the Wiccan Rede ('An it harm none, do what ye will'), Neopagans emphasize direct personal experience of seasonal earth rhythms over fixed scripture.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Rapidly growing through online communities and military/university chaplaincy recognition, Neopaganism reflects modern longing for ecological re-enchantment.

## Key Terminology
- **Wheel of the Year**: Annual Neopagan cycle of eight seasonal solstice, equinox, and cross-quarter festivals.
- **Wiccan Rede**: Core ethical guideline ('An it harm none, do what ye will') emphasizing personal moral responsibility.
- **Triple Goddess**: Feminine divine archetype honoring Maiden, Mother, and Crone phases mirroring lunar cycles.
- **Immanence (Sacred)**: Viewing divine presence as woven directly inside nature and living matter rather than distant heaven.
- **Coven**: Small, autonomous egalitarian circle of Wiccan practitioners gathering for seasonal ritual.
- **Reconstructionism**: Branch of Paganism rigorously modeling historical Norse, Greek, or Celtic liturgy on primary sources.
