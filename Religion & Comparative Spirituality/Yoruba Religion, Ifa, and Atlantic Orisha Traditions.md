# Yoruba Religion, Ifa, and Atlantic Orisha Traditions

## Overview
Originating among the Yoruba of West Africa and carried across the Atlantic by enslaved Africans, Orisha spirituality unites supreme creator *Olódùmarè*, vibrant guardian *Orishas*, ancestral *Egúngún* masquerades, and the poetic *Ifá* divination corpus—thriving today in Nigeria, Cuba (*Lucumí/Santería*), Brazil (*Candomblé*), and Haiti (*Vodou*).

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
At the apex sits transcendent *Olódùmarè*, who delegates cosmic stewardship to *Orishas*—personified forces of nature and character such as *Ògún* (iron/civilization), *Ṣàngó* (thunder/justice), *Yemọja* (ocean/motherhood), and *Èṣù* (crossroads messenger). Through *Ifá* divination, *Babaláwo* and *Ìyánífá* priests cast sacred palm nuts or *ọ̀pẹ̀lẹ̀* chains to access 256 poetic *Odù* wisdom chapters guiding personal destiny (*orí*).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
During slavery, Catholic bans forced enslaved Africans in Cuba and Brazil to mask Orishas behind matching Catholic saints (such as *Changó* with St. Barbara), creating resilient syncretic traditions centered on drum-induced trance (*àṣẹ* life-force) and community healing.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Recognized by UNESCO, *Ifá* divination and global Orisha communities attract millions across Africa, the Americas, and Europe.

## Key Terminology
- **Orisha (Òrìṣà)**: Sacred divine emissaries and personified natural/ethical forces in Yoruba cosmology.
- **Olódùmarè**: Supreme transcendent Creator above the Orishas in Yoruba theology.
- **Ifá divination**: Sophisticated 256-pattern oral poetic divination system guided by *Orunmila*.
- **Àṣẹ (Axé)**: Dynamic spiritual life-force, authority, and transformative power flowing through cosmos and ritual.
- **Candomblé / Santería**: Afro-Brazilian and Afro-Cuban Orisha traditions forged under transatlantic survival.
- **Orí**: Personal spiritual 'head' or chosen destiny cultivated through character (*ìwà pẹ̀lẹ́*).
