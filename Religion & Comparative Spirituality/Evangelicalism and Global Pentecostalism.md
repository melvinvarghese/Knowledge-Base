# Evangelicalism and Global Pentecostalism

## Overview
Over the past century, Christianity's demographic center shifted decisively from Europe to the Global South—propelled largely by Evangelicalism and explosive Pentecostal/Charismatic renewal emphasizing personal conversion, spiritual gifts, and dynamic worship.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Emerging from 18th-century Great Awakenings (George Whitefield, John Wesley), historian David Bebbington defined Evangelicalism via four traits: conversionism ('born-again' personal transformation), activism (missionary outreach), biblicism (high reverence for Scripture), and crucicentrism (central focus on Christ's cross).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
In 1906, the interracial Azusa Street Revival in Los Angeles—led by Black holiness preacher William J. Seymour—ignited modern *Pentecostalism*, emphasizing 'baptism in the Holy Spirit' evidenced by speaking in tongues (*glossolalia*), divine healing, and spontaneous ecstatic worship. Later 'Charismatic' waves carried these practices into mainline Protestant and Catholic parishes worldwide.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Today, over 600 million Pentecostal and Charismatic believers anchor mega-churches and grassroots movements across Brazil, Nigeria, South Korea, and the Philippines, reshaping global politics and music.

## Key Terminology
- **Born-again conversion**: Evangelical emphasis on a conscious personal turning to Christ rather than inherited cultural membership.
- **Pentecostalism**: Renewal movement centered on Spirit-baptism, *glossolalia*, prophecy, and divine healing.
- **Glossolalia**: Ecstatic prayer or speech in unlearned 'tongues' practiced in Pentecostal worship.
- **Azusa Street Revival (1906)**: Landmark multiracial Los Angeles revival launching global modern Pentecostalism.
- **Bebbington Quadrilateral**: Four defining hallmarks of evangelicalism: conversionism, activism, biblicism, crucicentrism.
- **Prosperity gospel**: Controversial charismatic teaching linking faith and tithing directly to material wealth and health.
