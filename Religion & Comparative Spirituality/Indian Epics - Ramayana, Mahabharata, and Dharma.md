# Indian Epics: Ramayana, Mahabharata, and Dharma

## Overview
The *Rāmāyaṇa* and *Mahābhārata* are far more than ancient Sanskrit poems—they are living moral, theatrical, and philosophical encyclopedias shaping South and Southeast Asian ethical imagination for over two millennia.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Attributed to sage Vālmīki, the *Rāmāyaṇa* follows Prince Rāma's exile, Sītā's abduction by Rāvaṇa, and the loyal monkey-warrior Hanumān—idealizing *dharma* inside family, kingship, and devotion. By contrast, the monumental *Mahābhārata* (100,000+ couplets, the world's longest epic) narrates the tragic fraternal Kurukṣetra war between Pāṇḍava and Kaurava cousins, confronting agonizing gray zones where duties clash and even heroes resort to painful compromises.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Embedded inside the *Mahābhārata*, the *Bhagavad Gītā* finds warrior Arjuna paralyzed before killing kinsmen; Krishna counsels *nishkāma karma*—performing righteous duty selflessly without clinging to egoistic fruits—synthesizing action, wisdom, and devotion.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Retold across Indonesian *wayang* shadow puppets, Thai royal murals, Tamil Kamban verse, and modern television, these epics continuously anchor moral debate.

## Key Terminology
- **Rāmāyaṇa**: Classical Sanskrit epic narrating Rāma, Sītā, and Hanumān as ideals of duty and devotion.
- **Mahābhārata**: Monumental Sanskrit epic exploring tragic war, statecraft, and ambiguous *dharma*.
- **Nishkāma karma**: Gītā ethic of selfless action dedicated to duty without anxious attachment to outcomes.
- **Dharma (Epic)**: Context-sensitive cosmic, social, and moral righteousness sustaining harmony.
- **Avatāra**: Periodic earthly descent of Vishnu (such as Rama and Krishna) to restore cosmic order.
- **Itihāsa**: Traditional Indian genre designation ('thus indeed it happened') for the two great epics.
