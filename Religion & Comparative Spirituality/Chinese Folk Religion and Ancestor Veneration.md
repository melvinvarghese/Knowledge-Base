# Chinese Folk Religion and Ancestor Veneration

## Overview
Beneath formal labels of Confucianism, Daoism, and Buddhism, hundreds of millions across China, Taiwan, and Southeast Asia practice a vibrant, inclusive folk religion centered on ancestral altars, kitchen and sea deities, fortune divination, and seasonal Tomb-Sweeping.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Rather than demanding exclusive church membership, traditional Chinese religiosity operates pragmatically around harmony with family ancestors and local gods (*shén*): families burn incense and spirit paper money at home altars during *Qingming* (Tomb-Sweeping Day) and Lunar New Year, honoring ancestors as protective elders rather than distant ghosts. Neighborhood temples honor compassionate figures like *Guanyin* (Bodhisattva of Mercy), coastal sea-goddess *Mazu*, and *Guan Yu* (symbol of loyalty and commerce).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Villagers cast curved wooden *jiaobei* moon blocks or draw numbered *kau cim* fortune sticks to seek guidance on marriage, business, or health—seamlessly consulting Daoist priests for exorcisms, Buddhist monks for funerals, and Confucian ethics for family duties.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Suppressed fiercely during the Cultural Revolution, rural temple networks and ancestral halls have surged back across southern China and Taiwan as vital engines of local charity and festival cohesion.

## Key Terminology
- **Three Teachings (*Sanjiao*)**: Traditional Chinese synthesis harmonizing Confucianism, Daoism, and Buddhism as complementary paths.
- **Ancestor veneration**: Ritual honoring of deceased family elders through food offerings, incense, and grave care.
- **Qingming Festival**: Annual spring Tomb-Sweeping Day uniting families in ancestral grave maintenance.
- **Mazu**: Beloved Chinese sea goddess protecting sailors and travelers across Fujian, Taiwan, and Southeast Asia.
- **Guanyin**: East Asian Bodhisattva of Compassion (from Sanskrit Avalokiteśvara) deeply revered in folk devotion.
- **Jiaobei (Moon blocks)**: Crescent wooden divination blocks tossed in Chinese temples to receive yes/no answers.
