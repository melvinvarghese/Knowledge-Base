# Sikhism: Guru Nanak, Guru Granth Sahib, and Khalsa

## Overview
Founded in 15th-century Punjab by Guru Nanak Dev Ji, Sikhism (~25–30 million adherents) proclaims strict devotion to one Formless Creator (*Ik Onkar*), radical human equality rejecting caste and gender hierarchy, honest labor, and fearless defense of justice.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Opening his message with 'There is no Hindu, there is no Muslim'—pointing beyond divisive ritual labels toward shared divine presence—Guru Nanak established three enduring pillars: *Naam Japna* (meditative remembrance of God's Name), *Kirat Karni* (honest householder livelihood, rejecting celibate monasticism), and *Vand Chakko* (sharing generously with the needy). Nine successor human Gurus expanded this community, compiling hymns from Sikh Gurus alongside Hindu and Sufi saints into the *Adi Granth* and constructing the Golden Temple (*Harmandir Sahib*) in Amritsar with four doors welcoming all castes.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
In 1699, facing Mughal persecution, Tenth Guru Gobind Singh Ji inaugurated the *Khalsa* ('Pure/Sovereign') brotherhood—adopting the Five Ks (*Kesh* uncut hair/turban, *Kara* steel bracelet, *Kanga* comb, *Kachera* shorts, *Kirpan* ceremonial sword)—and declared the sacred scripture *Guru Granth Sahib* the eternal living Guru.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Every Gurdwara worldwide hosts *Langar*—a free communal vegetarian kitchen where strangers sit together on the floor as equals.

## Key Terminology
- **Ik Onkar**: Foundational Sikh affirmation: 'There is One Universal Creator Reality.'
- **Guru Granth Sahib**: Sacred 1,430-page poetic scripture revered since 1708 as the eternal living Guru of Sikhs.
- **Khalsa**: Initiated Sikh order created in 1699 committed to spiritual discipline, equality, and justice.
- **Langar**: Free communal kitchen in every Gurdwara serving identical vegetarian meals to all regardless of rank or faith.
- **Five Ks (Panj Kakkar)**: Five outward articles of Khalsa faith symbolizing discipline, integrity, and readiness.
- **Seva**: Selfless voluntary community service performed without expectation of reward or recognition.
