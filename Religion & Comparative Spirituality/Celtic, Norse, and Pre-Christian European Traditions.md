# Celtic, Norse, and Pre-Christian European Traditions

## Overview
Prior to Christianization, Celtic and Germanic/Norse peoples practiced rich polytheistic, nature-oriented traditions led by Druids, *godi* chieftains, and *völva* seeresses—preserved largely through medieval Icelandic Eddas, Irish manuscripts, and Roman archaeology.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Iron Age Celtic religion across Gaul and Britain revered sacred groves (*nemeton*), springs, triple-aspect goddesses (*Brigid, Morrígan*), and seasonal fire festivals (*Beltane, Samhain*), overseen by learned *Druids* serving as priests, judges, and oral poets. Farther north, Old Norse cosmology (recorded in Snorri Sturluson's *Prose Edda* and the *Poetic Edda*) envisioned nine worlds linked by world-ash *Yggdrasil*, where gods like Odin (wisdom/runes), Thor (protection), and Freyja (fertility/magic) faced fate (*wyrd/örlög*) and final *Ragnarök*.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Conversion to Christianity unfolded unevenly via syncretic diplomacy (Iceland's 1000 CE Althing compromise) and royal edicts, leaving pagan echoes in English weekday names (Tuesday–Friday) and seasonal folk customs.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Modern Reconstructionist Heathenry and Neo-Druidry revive historical liturgy creatively while scholars separate authentic archaeology from 19th-century Romantic myths.

## Key Terminology
- **Yggdrasil**: Immense sacred ash tree connecting the Nine Worlds in Norse cosmology.
- **Ragnarök**: Prophesied twilight and destruction of the Norse gods followed by cosmic renewal.
- **Druids**: Learned Celtic priestly, legal, and philosophical class officiating sacred grove rituals.
- **Samhain / Beltane**: Celtic seasonal threshold festivals marking winter's onset and summer's arrival.
- **Seiðr**: Old Norse shamanic prophetic and fate-weaving practice associated with Freyja and Odin.
- **Syncretism (Conversion)**: Blending pre-Christian seasonal dates, wells, and hero motifs into local European Christianity.
