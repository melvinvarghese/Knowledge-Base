# Religion and Ecology (Ecotheology)

## Overview
Responding to historian Lynn White Jr.'s famous 1967 charge that Western biblical 'dominion' fueled ecological crisis, theologians across every major faith launched *ecotheology*—recovering ancient sacred traditions of earth stewardship, cosmic kinship, and voluntary simplicity.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
White argued Genesis's command to 'subdue the earth' desacralized forests and rivers into mere human property. Christian ecotheologians (Thomas Berry, Sallie McFague, and Pope Francis's landmark 2015 encyclical *Laudato si'*) countered that Hebrew *radah* means loving royal care ('till and keep' the garden), viewing creation as a shared 'common home' where ecological ruin hurts the poor first.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Parallel green revivals flourished globally: Islamic *khalifa* (trusteeship) and *mizan* (cosmic balance) anchor the Islamic Declaration on Climate Change; Hindu reverence for sacred rivers and *Bhumi Devi*; Buddhist interdependence (*Pratītyasamutpāda*) and Thai 'tree-ordination' monks wrapping saffron robes around endangered rainforest giants; and Indigenous land stewardship.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Interfaith coalitions like GreenFaith now mobilize congregations globally for solar roofs, fossil-fuel divestment, and biodiversity protection.

## Key Terminology
- **Ecotheology**: Theological reflection reinterpreting sacred texts and rituals in response to planetary ecological crisis.
- **Laudato si' (2015)**: Pope Francis's influential encyclical linking climate justice, biodiversity, and care for the poor.
- **Khalifa (Stewardship)**: Islamic Qur'anic concept of humanity as accountable earthly trustees rather than absolute owners.
- **Tree ordination**: Southeast Asian Buddhist ritual blessing endangered trees with monastic robes to deter logging.
- **Lynn White thesis**: Influential 1967 essay blaming Western anthropocentric Christianity for modern ecological exploitation.
- **Integral ecology**: Holistic framework recognizing environmental crises and human poverty as one interconnected crisis.
