# Sacred Calendars, Festivals, and Liturgical Time

## Overview
Religious calendars transform flat chronological time into living sacred rhythm—anchoring annual memory through lunar new moons, agricultural solstices, fasts, and joyous festivals from Diwali and Eid to Easter and Lunar New Year.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Calendars track celestial cycles differently: the Islamic *Hijri* calendar is strictly lunar (354 days), causing Ramadan and *Eid al-Fitr* to drift smoothly through every season over a 33-year cycle; Jewish, Chinese, and Hindu calendars are *lunisolar*, inserting periodic leap months so Passover stays in spring and Lunar New Year anchors late winter; and the Christian liturgical year blends fixed solar Christmas/solstice dates with lunar-anchored Easter.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Annual festivals enact communal identity viscerally: Hindu *Diwali* ('Festival of Lights') celebrates good conquering darkness; Jewish *Passover Seder* turns dinner into an intergenerational storytelling drama of liberation; and Lent/Ramadan fasts cultivate bodily empathy for the hungry before feasting.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Shared holidays sustain cultural memory across secularized generations and anchor global multicultural calendars.

## Key Terminology
- **Lunisolar calendar**: Calendar synchronizing lunar month phases with solar seasons via periodic leap months.
- **Hijri calendar**: Purely lunar 12-month Islamic calendar dating from the 622 CE migration (*Hijra*) to Medina.
- **Liturgical year**: Annual Christian cycle tracing Advent, Christmas, Lent, Easter, and Pentecost.
- **Diwali**: Pan-Indian Festival of Lights celebrating spiritual victory of knowledge and *dharma* over darkness.
- **Eid al-Fitr / Eid al-Adha**: Two major Islamic festivals concluding Ramadan fasting and commemorating Abraham's devotion during Hajj.
- **Passover Seder**: Ritual Jewish family meal retelling the Exodus using symbolic foods like *matzah* and bitter herbs.
