# Mesoamerican and Andean Sacred Cosmologies

## Overview
Ancient Maya, Aztec, and Inca civilizations built sophisticated state religions anchored in cyclical solar-lunar calendars, sacred mountain reciprocity, and ongoing human participation in sustaining cosmic order.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Across Mesoamerica, time moved in interlocking 260-day ritual (*Tonalpohualli/Tzolk'in*) and 365-day solar cycles. In Maya *Popol Vuh* mythology and Mexica cosmology, gods sacrificed themselves to ignite the Fifth Sun—requiring continual human reciprocal bloodletting or warrior sacrifice to keep the cosmos from collapsing into darkness, balanced alongside refined flower-and-song philosophy (*in xóchitl in cuícatl*).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
In the Andes, Inca and pre-Inca peoples revered sacred landscape shrines (*huacas*), Earth-Mother *Pachamama*, mountain spirits (*Apus*), and creator *Viracocha* through reciprocal *ayni* offerings of coca leaves and *chicha* maize beer.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Following Spanish conquest, Indigenous peoples wove *Pachamama* and cyclical fiestas seamlessly into Andean and Mexican Folk Catholicism (exemplified by the Virgin of Guadalupe at Tepeyac).

## Key Terminology
- **Pachamama**: Revered Andean Earth Mother sustained through reciprocal *ayni* gratitude offerings.
- **Huaca**: Sacred Andean place, mountain peak, spring, or ancestral shrine imbued with spiritual power.
- **260-day ritual calendar**: Pan-Mesoamerican sacred count (*Tzolk'in / Tonalpohualli*) guiding naming and divination.
- **Reciprocal sacrifice**: Mesoamerican cosmic theology viewing divine nourishment as mutual debt sustaining sun and rain.
- **Popol Vuh**: K'iche' Maya sacred creation narrative recounting the Hero Twins and humanity's maize origins.
- **Syncretic Folk Catholicism**: Living blend of Indigenous sacred geography and Catholic saints across Latin America.
