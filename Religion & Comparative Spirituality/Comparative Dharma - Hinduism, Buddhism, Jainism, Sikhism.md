# Comparative Dharma: Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, Sikhism

## Overview
Born on the Indian subcontinent, Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, and Sikhism share deep family resemblances—*karma*, *saṃsāra*, *dharma*, and meditative awakening—yet diverge sharply on God, the soul, caste, and how liberation is won.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
All four Dharmic traditions diagnose ordinary ego-bound existence as trapped in cyclical repetition (*saṃsāra*) driven by intentional action (*karma*) and ignorance, pointing toward liberation (*mokṣa / nirvāṇa*). Yet their metaphysics diverge dramatically: orthodox Hinduism affirms an eternal Self (*Ātman*) and often a supreme personal or non-dual *Brahman*; Buddhism denies any permanent soul (*anātman*) or creator; Jainism posits infinite individual eternal souls (*jīvas*) without a creator; and Sikhism proclaims one personal-transcendent Creator (*Ik Onkar*) while rejecting ascetic withdrawal.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Socially, Buddhism, Jainism, and Sikhism explicitly rejected hereditary Brahmanical caste privilege and animal sacrifice, opening spiritual equality to all—though caste prejudices persisted culturally in South Asian practice.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Comparing Dharmic traditions alongside Abrahamic linear history highlights two grand paradigms of human meaning: cyclical awakening versus covenantal redemption.

## Key Terminology
- **Dharmic family**: Umbrella term for Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, and Sikhism originating in South Asia.
- **Ātman vs. Anātman**: Hindu/Jain affirmation of an eternal spiritual soul versus Buddhist teaching of no fixed self.
- **Nāstika vs. Āstika**: Classical Indian division between traditions rejecting Vedic authority (Buddhism, Jainism) and those accepting it.
- **Liberation (*Mokṣa / Nirvāṇa*)**: Ultimate release from cyclical rebirth, framed either as union with God/Brahman or extinction of craving.
- **Vegetarianism & Ahiṃsā**: Shared South Asian ethical ideals reaching strictest biological rigor in Jainism.
- **Sikh rejection of asceticism**: Guru Nanak's householder path uniting worldly work, family, and spiritual remembrance.
