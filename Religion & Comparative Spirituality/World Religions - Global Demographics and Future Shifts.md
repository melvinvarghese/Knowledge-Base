# World Religions: Global Demographics and Future Shifts

## Overview
Mapping global religious demographics reveals dramatic 21st-century shifts: Christianity and Islam together encompass over half of humanity, religious 'Nones' surge across wealthy post-industrial nations, and differential fertility and youth bulges shift religion's center of gravity decisively toward Africa and Asia.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
According to Pew Research Center projections, Christians (~2.4B) and Muslims (~1.9B) are expected to reach rough global parity near mid-century—not primarily from mass conversions, but because Muslim populations have the youngest median age and highest fertility across Sub-Saharan Africa, South Asia, and the Middle East, while European Christian populations age and secularize.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Simultaneously, Sub-Saharan Africa is projected to host over 40% of the world's Christians by 2060, transforming global Catholicism, Anglicanism, and Pentecostalism culturally. Hinduism (~1.2B) remains anchored largely in India with growing global diasporas, while Buddhism (~500M) faces demographic aging across East Asia alongside growing Western mindfulness adoption.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Understanding these demographic currents illuminates future geopolitics, urban interfaith coexistence, and shifting theological voices from the Global South.

## Key Terminology
- **Demographic transition (Religion)**: Shifts in religious shares driven by age structure, fertility rates, and secularization.
- **Global South shift**: Movement of Christianity's and Islam's demographic and leadership centers toward Africa, Asia, and Latin America.
- **Median age differential**: Gap between youthful Muslim/African Christian populations and older European/East Asian cohorts.
- **Religious switching**: Net demographic change from individuals joining or leaving traditions across lifetimes.
- **Diaspora religion**: Adaptation of Hindu, Sikh, Muslim, and Buddhist temples inside multicultural Western cities.
- **Super-diversity**: Urban neighborhoods uniting dozens of linguistic and religious traditions side-by-side.
