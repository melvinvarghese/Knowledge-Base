# Liberation Theology and Faith-Based Social Justice

## Overview
Across Christianity, Buddhism, Islam, and Judaism, 20th-century theologians challenged passive 'pie-in-the-sky' piety, arguing authentic faith demands confronting systemic poverty, racism, and dictatorship—exemplified by Latin American Liberation Theology, the Black Social Gospel, and Engaged Buddhism.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Crystallizing around Peru's Gustavo Gutiérrez (*A Theology of Liberation*, 1971) and Brazil's Leonardo Boff amid military dictatorships, Catholic Liberation Theology read Exodus and Jesus's ministry as God's 'preferential option for the poor.' Grassroots *comunidades eclesiales de base* paired Bible reflection with peasant land and union organizing—drawing Vatican caution over Marxist terminology alongside martyrs like El Salvador's Archbishop Óscar Romero.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Parallel movements surged globally: Martin Luther King Jr. fused the Black prophetic church with Gandhian nonviolence; Thich Nhat Hanh coined 'Engaged Buddhism' during the Vietnam War; and B.R. Ambedkar led millions of Dalits into socially emancipatory Navayana Buddhism.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Faith-based justice movements demonstrate how ancient prophetic scriptures continuously fuel modern anti-poverty, abolition, and human-rights struggles.

## Key Terminology
- **Preferential option for the poor**: Theological principle that God's justice prioritizes the dignity and empowerment of marginalized people.
- **Base Ecclesial Communities**: Grassroots Latin American neighborhood Bible and mutual-aid circles.
- **Black Social Gospel**: African American Christian tradition linking biblical liberation directly to civil rights and economic justice.
- **Engaged Buddhism**: Thich Nhat Hanh's movement applying Buddhist mindfulness and compassion directly to war, poverty, and ecology.
- **Prophetic tradition**: Biblical heritage (Amos, Isaiah, Micah) elevating social righteousness over empty ritual sacrifice.
- **Structural sin**: Theological concept recognizing injustice embedded inside laws and economies rather than solely private vices.
