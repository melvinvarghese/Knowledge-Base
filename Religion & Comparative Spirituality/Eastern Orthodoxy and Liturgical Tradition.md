# Eastern Orthodoxy and Liturgical Tradition

## Overview
Eastern Orthodoxy—uniting autocephalous churches across Greece, Russia, Serbia, Romania, Antioch, and Alexandria—preserves the continuous liturgical, monastic, and patristic heritage of the Byzantine East, centering salvation not on legal guilt alone, but on *theosis* (divinization).

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Parting gradually from Western Rome over papal supremacy, the *Filioque* clause in the Nicene Creed, and liturgical customs—culminating in the Great Schism of 1054—Orthodoxy governs itself via conciliar synods of equal patriarchates rather than a single infallible Pope. Its worship centers on the Divine Liturgy of St. John Chrysostom, integrating sung chant, incense, and sacred icons as 'windows into heaven' vindicated after the 8th–9th century Iconoclastic controversy.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Theologically, Orthodox soteriology emphasizes *theosis*: as St. Athanasius wrote, 'God became human so that humans might become godlike' through participation in divine uncreated energies (Gregory Palamas). Mount Athos in Greece remains a living millennium-old monastic center of hesychast contemplative prayer.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Following Ottoman rule and Soviet persecution, 20th-century Orthodox theologians like Vladimir Lossky and John Zizioulas revitalized Trinitarian and liturgical dialogue globally.

## Key Terminology
- **Theosis (Divinization)**: Orthodox understanding of salvation as progressive union with God's uncreated grace.
- **Great Schism (1054)**: Formal mutual excommunication dividing Eastern Orthodoxy and Roman Catholicism.
- **Autocephaly**: Self-governing administrative independence of national Orthodox churches within shared communion.
- **Iconostasis**: Tiered wall of sacred icons separating the altar sanctuary from the nave.
- **Hesychasm**: Eastern monastic contemplative tradition practicing the silent Jesus Prayer.
- **Filioque**: Western addition ('and the Son') to the Nicene Creed rejected by Eastern Orthodoxy.
