# Esotericism, Alchemy, and Hermetic Traditions

## Overview
Running alongside mainstream churches and mosques as a 'rejected' yet fertile undercurrent, Western Esotericism—spanning Alexandrian Hermeticism, spiritual alchemy, Renaissance Kabbalah, and modern Theosophy—shaped Renaissance art, early chemistry, and New Age spirituality.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Grounded in the late-antique *Corpus Hermeticum* ('As above, so below'), Renaissance humanists like Marsilio Ficino and Pico della Mirandola integrated Hermetic magic, Christian Kabbalah, and Neoplatonism into a universal *Prisca Theologia* ('ancient theology'). Spiritual alchemy treated laboratory transmutation of lead into gold simultaneously as inner psychological purification—an insight later mined deeply by psychologist Carl Jung.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
In the 19th century, Helena Blavatsky's *Theosophical Society* (1875) blended Western esotericism with Hindu and Buddhist concepts (*karma, reincarnation, masters*), helping introduce Asian spirituality to Western artists like Wassily Kandinsky and Piet Mondrian.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Academic pioneers like Antoine Faivre and Wouter Hanegraaff established Western Esotericism as a rigorous historical field bridging religion, art, and early science.

## Key Terminology
- **Hermeticism**: Late-antique and Renaissance philosophical-esoteric tradition attributed to legendary Hermes Trismegistus.
- **As above, so below**: Classic Hermetic maxim asserting microcosmic correspondence between human soul and macrocosmic heavens.
- **Spiritual alchemy**: Symbolic use of chemical transmutation (*nigredo, albedo, rubedo*) to map inner psychological renewal.
- **Theosophy**: 1875 esoteric synthesis founded by Helena Blavatsky blending Western occultism with Dharmic ideas.
- **Prisca Theologia**: Renaissance belief in a single primordial divine wisdom shared across Moses, Plato, and Hermes.
- **Rosicrucianism**: 17th-century European esoteric brotherhood mythos advocating universal spiritual and scientific reform.
