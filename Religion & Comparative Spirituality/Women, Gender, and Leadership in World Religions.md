# Women, Gender, and Leadership in World Religions

## Overview
Across history, women sustained daily religious households, mysticism, and charity while often being excluded from formal priesthoods and legal authority. Over the past century, feminist theologians and reformers across religions have reshaped scripture interpretation and reopened female ordination.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Early histories preserve remarkable female pioneers—Christian deaconesses like Phoebe, Buddhist *Therīgāthā* enlightened nuns, Sufi mystic Rabi'a al-Adawiyya, and Hindu Bhakti poet-saints—before patriarchal institutions steadily narrowed leadership. In the 20th–21st centuries, Reform and Conservative Judaism ordained women rabbis (beginning with Regina Jonas in 1935 and Sally Priesand in 1972), and Anglican, Lutheran, and Methodist churches opened bishoprics to women.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Simultaneously, Roman Catholicism and Eastern Orthodoxy maintain male-only priesthoods while expanding women's theological and curial leadership; Theravada Buddhists fiercely debate reviving extinct *bhikkhunī* full nun ordination lineages; and Muslim scholars like Amina Wadud and Asma Barlas challenge patriarchal *hadith* readings from inside Qur'anic egalitarianism.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Religious gender scholarship recovers overlooked female voices while honoring women's genuine agency inside conservative and progressive communities alike.

## Key Terminology
- **Ordination of women**: Granting full clerical, rabbinic, or monastic authority to women across denominations.
- **Bhikkhunī lineage**: Full Buddhist nun ordination order thriving in East Asia yet debated for revival in Theravada countries.
- **Feminist theology**: Critical theological reflection exposing patriarchal bias and retrieving liberating sacred imagery.
- **Womanist theology**: African American women's theological tradition centering Black women's survival, faith, and justice.
- **Therīgāthā**: Earliest surviving collection of poems composed by enlightened women in ancient Buddhism.
- **Female mysticism**: Historic avenue (Hildegard, Julian, Teresa, Mirabai) granting women direct spiritual authority outside clergy.
