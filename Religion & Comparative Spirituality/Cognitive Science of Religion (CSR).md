# Cognitive Science of Religion (CSR)

## Overview
Why do gods, ghosts, afterlife beliefs, and costly rituals appear independently in virtually every human culture? The Cognitive Science of Religion (CSR) applies evolutionary and developmental psychology to show how ordinary human minds naturally generate and transmit religious ideas.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Pioneered by Pascal Boyer, Justin Barrett, and Scott Atran, CSR views religious concepts partly as evolutionary byproducts of everyday cognitive modules: our Hyperactive Agency Detection Device (HADD) over-attributes invisible intentional agents to rustling bushes or sudden misfortune; childhood Theory of Mind intuitively separates minds from physical bodies ('mind-body dualism'), making disembodied spirits and afterlife survival feel effortless.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Pascal Boyer proved the most culturally contagious religious concepts are 'Minimally Counterintuitive' (MCI): ghosts or talking statues violate just *one* intuitive physics rule (walking through walls) while obeying ordinary human psychology (getting angry or lonely)—making them memorable without overwhelming working memory. Meanwhile, Ara Norenzayan's 'Big Gods' thesis showed moralizing omniscient deities solved free-riding in large anonymous cities by convincing strangers 'watched people are nice people,' reinforced by Joseph Henrich's 'Credibility Enhancing Displays' (costly fasting or celibacy proving sincere belief).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
CSR neither proves nor disproves theological truth—explaining *how* minds hold beliefs leaves philosophical questions open while illuminating humanity's universal spiritual architecture.

## Key Terminology
- **Hyperactive Agency Detection**: Evolved cognitive bias inferring hidden intentional agents behind ambiguous noises or events.
- **Minimally Counterintuitive (MCI)**: Concept violating one intuitive ontological expectation while staying mostly familiar, aiding cultural memory.
- **Big Gods hypothesis**: Norenzayan's theory that belief in all-seeing moralizing deities enabled large-scale stranger cooperation.
- **Credibility Enhancing Displays**: Costly personal sacrifices (fasting, charity, martyrdom) convincing observers a leader's faith is genuine.
- **Teleological promiscuity**: Deborah Kelemen's finding that young children naturally assume natural objects exist 'for a purpose.'
- **Intuitive dualism**: Natural human tendency to conceive mental minds as separable from physical bodies.
