# Anglicanism and the Church of England

## Overview
Born from England's 16th-century break with Rome under Henry VIII and Elizabeth I, Anglicanism charts a distinctive *via media* ('middle way') uniting Reformed theology with Catholic liturgical structure, historic bishops, and Thomas Cranmer's *Book of Common Prayer*.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Unlike continental Lutheran or Calvinist churches, the Church of England retained cathedrals, vestments, and apostolic episcopacy while adopting vernacular liturgy and Protestant justification in the *Thirty-Nine Articles*. Spanning global colonial expansion, the worldwide Anglican Communion (including the US Episcopal Church) today unites ~85+ million members across diverse Anglo-Catholic, Evangelical, and Broad Church wings.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
In the 21st century, the Communion faces sharp internal tensions between progressive Western provinces affirming LGBTQ+ marriage/ordination and conservative Global South churches (led by Nigeria, Uganda, and Kenya).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Anglican choral evensong and Cranmer's poetic prose profoundly shaped English literature and liturgical worship.

## Key Terminology
- **Via media**: Anglican 'middle way' bridging Roman Catholic liturgy and Protestant Reformation theology.
- **Book of Common Prayer**: 1549 vernacular liturgical text by Thomas Cranmer shaping English prose and worship.
- **Anglican Communion**: Worldwide fellowship of autonomous national churches in communion with the Archbishop of Canterbury.
- **Lambeth Quadrilateral**: Four core Anglican unity pillars: Scripture, Creeds, Baptism/Eucharist, and Historic Episcopate.
- **Anglo-Catholicism**: 19th-century Oxford Movement stream emphasizing sacramental continuity with early Catholic tradition.
- **Book of Homilies / 39 Articles**: Historic 16th-century Anglican doctrinal statements.
