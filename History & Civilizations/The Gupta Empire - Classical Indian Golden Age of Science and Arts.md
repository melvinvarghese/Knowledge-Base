# The Gupta Empire - Classical Indian Golden Age of Science and Arts

## Overview
The Gupta Empire (c. 320–550 CE) presided over the Classical Golden Age of India, marked by extraordinary breakthroughs in mathematics, astronomy, and literature.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Mathematical & Astronomical Breakthroughs: Indian mathematicians formalized the decimal positional numeral system and introduced the concept and operational rules of zero (*shunya*) as a full number. Aryabhata (499 CE) calculated $\pi \approx 3.1416$, proposed that the Earth rotates on its axis, and formulated correct explanations for solar and lunar eclipses.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Literature, Art, & Nalanda University: Kalidasa composed classical Sanskrit poetry (*Meghaduta*) and drama (*Shakuntala*); Ajanta cave rock-cut Buddhist frescoes and temples reached artistic zeniths. Nalanda University flourished as the world's premier residential Buddhist center of higher learning, educating thousands of international scholars.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Invention of the modern Hindu-Arabic numeral system, foundational Sanskrit drama and literature, architectural temple blueprints.

## Key Terminology
- **Aryabhata**: Classical Indian mathematician-astronomer who calculated planetary orbits and formalized trigonometric sine functions.
- **Nalanda**: Ancient residential Buddhist monastic university in Bihar, India that operated as a global center of scholarship for centuries.
- **Concept of Zero**: Mathematical operational recognition of zero as both a place-value placeholder and an independent numerical quantity.
