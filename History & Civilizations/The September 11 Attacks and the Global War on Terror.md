# The September 11 Attacks and the Global War on Terror

## Overview
The September 11, 2001 terrorist attacks by al-Qaeda killed 2,977 victims in the United States, fundamentally reorienting global international relations, security, and warfare.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Asymmetric Hijackings: Nineteen al-Qaeda suicide operatives hijacked four commercial airliners. Two planes struck the Twin Towers of the World Trade Center in New York City, causing both skyscrapers to collapse; a third struck the Pentagon in Arlington, Virginia; and the fourth (United 93) crashed in a Pennsylvania field after passengers mounted a heroic revolt against the hijackers.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Global War on Terror: President George W. Bush proclaimed the 'War on Terror', invading Afghanistan in October 2001 to overthrow the Taliban regime and dismantle al-Qaeda, followed by the controversial preemptive invasion of Iraq in 2003 based on erroneous intelligence regarding weapons of mass destruction (WMD).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Creation of the US Department of Homeland Security, passage of the USA PATRIOT Act, global drone warfare programs, long-term destabilization of the Middle East.

## Key Terminology
- **September 11 Attacks**: Series of four coordinated Islamist suicide terrorist attacks carried out by al-Qaeda against the United States in 2001.
- **Al-Qaeda**: Transnational militant Islamist organization founded in 1988 by Osama bin Laden.
- **USA PATRIOT Act**: Landmark 2001 US law dramatically expanding law enforcement and intelligence surveillance powers to combat terrorism.
