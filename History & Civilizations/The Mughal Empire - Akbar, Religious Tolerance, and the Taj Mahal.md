# The Mughal Empire - Akbar, Religious Tolerance, and the Taj Mahal

## Overview
The Mughal Empire (1526–1857), founded by Babur, governed virtually the entire Indian subcontinent, producing remarkable architectural, artistic, and administrative synthesis.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Akbar the Great & Sulh-i-Kul: Reigned 1556–1605, instituting radical religious tolerance (*Sulh-i-Kul*—universal peace), abolishing the *jizya* tax on non-Muslims, commissioning interfaith philosophical debates at the Ibadat Khana, and formulating an administrative meritocracy via the *mansabdari* ranking system.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Architectural Zenith under Shah Jahan: Mughal Indo-Islamic architecture blended Persian symmetry with Indian stone craftsmanship, culminating in Shah Jahan constructing the white marble Taj Mahal in Agra as a mausoleum for his empress Mumtaz Mahal.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Production of ~25% of 17th-century global economic GDP (textiles, spices), synthesis of Hindustani classical music and Urdu language.

## Key Terminology
- **Akbar the Great**: Third Mughal emperor who expanded the empire and pioneered universal religious tolerance and institutional reform.
- **Mansabdari System**: Administrative military grading system implemented by Akbar to organize imperial bureaucracy and troop quotas.
- **Taj Mahal**: Ivory-white marble mausoleum on the Yamuna river in Agra commissioned by Shah Jahan in 1632.
