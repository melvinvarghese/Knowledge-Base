# The Holy Roman Empire - The Golden Bull, Electors, and Decentralization

## Overview
The Holy Roman Empire (962–1806), founded by Otto the Great, was a multi-ethnic complex of hundreds of autonomous kingdoms, principalities, and free imperial cities in Central Europe.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Golden Bull of 1356: Emperor Charles IV codified the imperial constitutional structure via the Golden Bull, eliminating papal confirmation and establishing seven Prince-Electors (three archbishops and four secular princes) who held the exclusive legal right to elect the King of the Romans / Emperor.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Decentralized Federalism: Unlike centralized monarchies in France and England, the Empire was a mosaic characterized by the *Reichstag* (Imperial Diet), Imperial Circles for regional defense, and high imperial courts (*Reichskammergericht*), maintaining regional autonomy and legal stability.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Voltaire famously quipped that it was 'neither holy, nor Roman, nor an empire'; dissolved by Napoleon in 1806.

## Key Terminology
- **Golden Bull of 1356**: Constitutional decree issued by Charles IV fixing the selection of Holy Roman Emperors to seven Prince-Electors.
- **Prince-Electors**: Members of the electoral college of the Holy Roman Empire who possessed the right to elect the emperor.
- **Reichstag**: Imperial Diet of the Holy Roman Empire acting as a deliberative assembly representing imperial estates.
