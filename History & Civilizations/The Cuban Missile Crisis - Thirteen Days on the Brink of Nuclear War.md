# The Cuban Missile Crisis - Thirteen Days on the Brink of Nuclear War

## Overview
In October 1962, the Cuban Missile Crisis brought the United States and the Soviet Union to the closest point of global thermonuclear annihilation in human history.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Discovery & The Naval Quarantine: American U-2 reconnaissance aircraft photographed secret Soviet medium-range ballistic missile (MRBM) launch sites under construction in Fidel Castro's Cuba, capable of striking major U.S. cities with nuclear warheads in minutes. President John F. Kennedy rejected direct airstrikes, opting for a naval blockade ('quarantine') around Cuba.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Secret Diplomacy & Resolution: Over thirteen agonizing days, backchannel communications between Kennedy and Soviet Premier Nikita Khrushchev averted catastrophe. Khrushchev agreed to dismantle and remove the Cuban missiles; publicly, the U.S. pledged never to invade Cuba, and secretly agreed to withdraw American Jupiter nuclear missiles from Turkey.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Establishment of the direct Moscow-Washington hotline, signing of the 1963 Partial Nuclear Test Ban Treaty.

## Key Terminology
- **EXCOMM**: Executive Committee of the National Security Council assembled by President Kennedy to manage the Cuban Missile Crisis.
- **Naval Quarantine**: Naval blockade framed as an interdiction quarantine to avoid a formal legal declaration of war.
- **Jupiter Missiles**: American nuclear missiles deployed in Turkey whose secret removal resolved the standoff.
