# The Arab-Israeli Conflict - The 1948 War to the Camp David Accords

## Overview
The Arab-Israeli conflict is an enduring geopolitical and territorial struggle in the Middle East spanning wars, refugee displacements, and landmark peace treaties.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The 1948 War & The Nakba: Following the expiration of the British Mandate and the 1947 UN Partition Plan, David Ben-Gurion declared the establishment of the State of Israel in May 1948. Surrounding Arab armies invaded; Israel prevailed, securing territory while an estimated 700,000 Palestinian Arabs were expelled or fled, known as the *Nakba* ('Catastrophe').

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The 1967 Six-Day War & Camp David (1978): In a lightning preemptive strike in June 1967, Israel captured the West Bank, East Jerusalem, the Gaza Strip, the Golan Heights, and the Sinai Peninsula. The 1973 Yom Kippur War prompted diplomacy: Egyptian President Anwar Sadat and Israeli Prime Minister Menachem Begin signed the 1978 Camp David Accords brokered by Jimmy Carter, establishing peace between Israel and Egypt in exchange for returning the Sinai.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Ongoing status of Palestinian self-determination, Israeli territorial occupation, core axis of Middle Eastern international relations.

## Key Terminology
- **Nakba**: Arabic term for 'Catastrophe', referring to the mass displacement and dispossession of Palestinians during the 1948 war.
- **Six-Day War (1967)**: Brief war where Israel captured the West Bank, Gaza, Golan Heights, and Sinai Peninsula.
- **Camp David Accords**: 1978 framework agreements brokered by U.S. President Jimmy Carter leading to the 1979 Egypt-Israel Peace Treaty.
