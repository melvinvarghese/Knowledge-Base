# The American Civil War - Emancipation, Antietam, and Reconstruction

## Overview
The American Civil War (1861–1865) was fought between the United States Union and eleven secessionist Confederate slave states, ending chattel slavery and preserving the American federal republic.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Secession & Industrial Total War: Following Abraham Lincoln's 1860 election, Southern states seceded to protect chattel slavery. The war mobilized industrialized mass armies, trench networks, rifled muskets, ironclad warships (USS *Monitor* vs CSS *Virginia*), and military railways, resulting in over 750,000 casualties.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Emancipation & Reconstruction: Following the bloody Battle of Antietam (1862), Lincoln issued the Emancipation Proclamation (1863), transforming the Union war aim into the abolition of slavery. The 13th, 14th, and 15th Constitutional Amendments abolished slavery, guaranteed equal citizenship rights, and protected voting rights during the Reconstruction era.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Definitive constitutional establishment of the indivisibility of the American union, destruction of Southern plantation slave economy.

## Key Terminology
- **Emancipation Proclamation**: Presidential executive order issued by Abraham Lincoln on January 1, 1863, freeing all enslaved persons in rebel Confederate territory.
- **Battle of Gettysburg**: Decisive July 1863 three-day battle halting Robert E. Lee's invasion of the North, serving as the military turning point of the war.
- **Reconstruction Era**: Period (1865–1877) following the Civil War dedicated to reintegrating Southern states and securing civil rights for freed African Americans.
