# The Epic of Gilgamesh and Sumerian Literature

## Overview
The Epic of Gilgamesh is one of the earliest surviving literary masterpieces in human history, composed on clay tablets in Sumerian and Akkadian cuneiform.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Narrative Structure & The Quest for Immortality: Gilgamesh, King of Uruk, journeys with the wild man Enkidu. Following Enkidu's death, Gilgamesh confronts mortal anguish and travels across the Waters of Death to consult Utnapishtim, the survivor of the Great Deluge, exploring humanity's reckoning with mortality.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Flood Myth & Cross-Cultural Archetypes: The tablet XI flood narrative features striking thematic and structural parallels to the later Hebrew biblical story of Noah's Ark, evidencing shared Mesopotamian mythological substrates across ancient Near Eastern cultures.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Foundational archetype of the Hero's Journey, earliest psychological exploration of grief, friendship, and mortality in world literature.

## Key Terminology
- **Gilgamesh**: Semi-mythic historical king of the Sumerian city-state of Uruk c. 2700 BCE.
- **Utnapishtim**: Mesopotamian mythological figure granted eternal life by the gods after surviving the global flood on a colossal barge.
- **Standard Babylonian Version**: The 12-tablet canonical compilation of the epic edited by priest-scholar Sîn-lēqi-unninni c. 1200 BCE.
