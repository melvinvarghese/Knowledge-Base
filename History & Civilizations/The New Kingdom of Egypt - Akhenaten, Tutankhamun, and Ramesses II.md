# The New Kingdom of Egypt - Akhenaten, Tutankhamun, and Ramesses II

## Overview
The New Kingdom (c. 1550–1077 BCE) was the zenith of ancient Egyptian imperial power, wealth, and artistic sophistication, expanding from Nubia to the Euphrates.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Amarna Revolution: Amenhotep IV rebranded as Akhenaten, abolishing the polytheistic cult of Amun-Ra in favor of Aten (the solar disk) in history's earliest documented state-enforced quasi-monotheism, moving the capital to Amarna before his son Tutankhamun restored the traditional pantheon.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Ramesside Imperial Expansion & The Battle of Kadesh (1274 BCE): Ramesses II ('Ramesses the Great') clashed with the Hittite Empire at Kadesh in the largest recorded chariot battle in antiquity, culminating in the Egyptian-Hittite Peace Treaty—the earliest surviving written international diplomatic treaty.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Valley of the Kings rock-cut royal tombs, colossal temple architecture at Karnak and Abu Simbel, international diplomacy through the Amarna Letters.

## Key Terminology
- **Akhenaten**: Pharaoh of the Eighteenth Dynasty who instituted the radical monolatrous worship of the sun disk Aten.
- **Battle of Kadesh**: Pivotal clash between Ramesses II and the Hittite Empire in 1274 BCE leading to the first recorded peace treaty.
- **Valley of the Kings**: Necropolis on the west bank of the Nile in Thebes where New Kingdom pharaohs were buried in hidden rock tombs.
