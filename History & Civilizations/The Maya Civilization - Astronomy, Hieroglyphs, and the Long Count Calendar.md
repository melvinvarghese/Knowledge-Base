# The Maya Civilization - Astronomy, Hieroglyphs, and the Long Count Calendar

## Overview
The Maya civilization of Mesoamerica (Classic Period: 250–900 CE) developed the Western Hemisphere's only fully literate writing system and advanced astronomical mathematics.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Astronomical Precision & Mathematics: The Maya independently invented the mathematical zero (depicted as a shell glyph) and utilized a vigesimal (base-20) positional number system. Scribes tracked planetary cycles (especially Venus) and calculated solar years to an accuracy within seconds.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Long Count Calendar & Hieroglyphic Script: Conjoined the 260-day sacred *Tzolk'in* and 365-day solar *Haab'* cycles into the 52-year Calendar Round, augmented by the absolute linear Long Count calendar. Maya hieroglyphic script combined syllabic signs and logograms, carved on monumental limestone stelae and folded bark-paper codices.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Monumental stepped pyramid temples (Tikal, Palenque, Chichen Itza), raised-field wetland agriculture.

## Key Terminology
- **Long Count Calendar**: Mesoamerican astronomical calendar identifying dates by counting the number of days elapsed since August 11, 3114 BCE.
- **Tzolk'in**: 260-day sacred Mesoamerican divinatory calendar composed of 20 named day-signs paired with 13 numbers.
- **Vigesimal System**: Base-20 mathematical numeral system utilized by the Maya using bars and dots to denote quantities.
