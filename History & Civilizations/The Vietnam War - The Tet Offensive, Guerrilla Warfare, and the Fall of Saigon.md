# The Vietnam War - The Tet Offensive, Guerrilla Warfare, and the Fall of Saigon

## Overview
The Vietnam War (1955–1975) was a protracted Cold War conflict pitting communist North Vietnam (backed by the USSR and China) and the Viet Cong against South Vietnam and the United States.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Asymmetric Warfare & The Ho Chi Minh Trail: The U.S. deployed half a million troops, massive conventional firepower, and napalm/Agent Orange chemical defoliation. However, North Vietnamese and Viet Cong forces utilized asymmetric guerrilla warfare, extensive underground tunnel networks (Củ Chi), and logistical resupply along the forested Ho Chi Minh Trail.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The 1968 Tet Offensive & The Fall of Saigon: The coordinated Tet surprise attacks across 100 South Vietnamese cities shattered American domestic claims that victory was near, creating a severe 'credibility gap' and galvanizing mass anti-war protests. U.S. combat troops withdrew in 1973; Saigon fell to North Vietnamese tanks on April 30, 1975, reunifying Vietnam.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Over 3 million Vietnamese fatalities, 58,000 U.S. military deaths, syndrome of American foreign policy military hesitation ('Vietnam Syndrome').

## Key Terminology
- **Tet Offensive**: Massive coordinated surprise military campaign launched by communist forces during the Vietnamese New Year in January 1968.
- **Ho Chi Minh Trail**: Logistical network of jungle roads and paths running through Laos and Cambodia to supply communist forces in the South.
- **Viet Cong (NLF)**: National Liberation Front; communist guerrilla armed force fighting the South Vietnamese and American armies.
