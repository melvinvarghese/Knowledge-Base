# The Collapse of the Soviet Union and the End of the Cold War

## Overview
The dissolution of the Soviet Union in December 1991 abruptly ended the Cold War, peacefully disintegrating the superpower into 15 independent republics.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Gorbachev's Reforms: Facing economic stagnation and imperial overstretch, Mikhail Gorbachev came to power in 1985 introducing *Glasnost* (political openness and freedom of speech) and *Perestroika* (economic restructuring toward market decentralization). Gorbachev renounced the Brezhnev Doctrine, refusing to use Soviet military force to suppress democratic uprisings across Eastern Europe.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Revolutions of 1989 & Dissolution: In late 1989, communist regimes across Poland, Hungary, and East Germany collapsed, highlighted by the fall of the Berlin Wall in November 1989. Following a hardline communist coup attempt in Moscow in August 1991, the Belovezha Accords officially dissolved the USSR on December 26, 1991, ending the bipolar world order.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Reunification of Germany, NATO eastward expansion, emergence of United States unipolar hegemony in the 1990s.

## Key Terminology
- **Glasnost**: Policy of political openness, transparency, and freedom of expression introduced by Mikhail Gorbachev in the late 1980s.
- **Perestroika**: Soviet economic and political restructuring policy initiated by Gorbachev aimed at revitalizing the stagnant Soviet economy.
- **Belovezha Accords**: Agreement signed in December 1991 by the leaders of Russia, Ukraine, and Belarus officially dissolving the Soviet Union.
