# The Franco-Prussian War and the Unification of Germany

## Overview
The Franco-Prussian War (1870–1871), orchestrated by Prussian Chancellor Otto von Bismarck, decisively shifted the European balance of power and unified the German Empire.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Bismarck's Realpolitik & Mobilization: Bismarck provoked Emperor Napoleon III into declaring war via the manipulated Ems Dispatch. The Prussian General Staff, commanded by Helmuth von Moltke, utilized railway mobilization and Krupp steel breech-loading artillery to encircle the French imperial army at Sedan, capturing Napoleon III.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Proclamation of the German Empire: On January 18, 1871, German princes gathered in the Hall of Mirrors at Versailles to proclaim King Wilhelm I of Prussia as German Emperor (Kaiser). The Treaty of Frankfurt annexed Alsace-Lorraine and imposed a five-billion-franc indemnity on France.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Birth of the German superpower in the center of Europe, French desire for revenge (*revanche*) that catalyzed European military alliances leading to World War I.

## Key Terminology
- **Otto von Bismarck**: Prussian statesman who unified Germany through 'blood and iron' and served as the first Chancellor of the German Empire.
- **Battle of Sedan**: Decisive 1870 battle resulting in the capture of French Emperor Napoleon III and collapse of the Second French Empire.
- **Realpolitik**: System of politics or diplomacy based on practical, power-focused calculations rather than moral or ideological premises.
