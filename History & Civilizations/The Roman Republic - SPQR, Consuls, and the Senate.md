# The Roman Republic - SPQR, Consuls, and the Senate

## Overview
The Roman Republic (509–27 BCE) replaced monarchical tyranny with a complex constitutional system of mixed government balancing aristocratic senate power with popular assemblies.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Magistrates, Senate, & SPQR: Sovereign executive power (*imperium*) was divided between two annually elected Consuls equipped with mutual veto power. The Senate (*Senatus Consultum*) controlled fiscal policy and foreign diplomacy. Tribunes of the Plebs protected common citizens, possessing sacrosanct bodily immunity and absolute veto power (*veto* = 'I forbid').

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Collapse of the Republic: Territorial expansion across the Mediterranean enriched the patrician class via slave plantations (*latifundia*), impoverishing peasant farmers. The Marian military reforms created client armies loyal to warlords (Sulla, Pompey, Julius Caesar) rather than the Senate, culminating in civil wars and Caesar crossing the Rubicon in 49 BCE.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Model of checks and balances, separation of powers, and constitutional republicanism in modern political theory.

## Key Terminology
- **SPQR**: Senatus Populusque Romanus ('The Senate and People of Rome'), official motto and emblem of the Roman Republic.
- **Consul**: Highest elected political magistrate in the Roman Republic, serving joint one-year terms.
- **Tribune of the Plebs**: Roman magistrate elected to protect plebeian interests with the power of legislative veto.
