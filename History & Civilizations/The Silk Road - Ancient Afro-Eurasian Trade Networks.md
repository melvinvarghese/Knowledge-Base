# The Silk Road - Ancient Afro-Eurasian Trade Networks

## Overview
The Silk Road was an expansive network of overland and maritime trade routes connecting China, Central Asia, India, Persia, and the Mediterranean world for over 1,500 years.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Origins in the Han Dynasty: Established following diplomat Zhang Qian's missions to Central Asia (c. 130 BCE), opening commerce between Han China and Hellenistic Bactria and Parthia. Caravans carried silk, porcelain, spices, paper, jade, glassware, and Roman silver across the Taklamakan Desert and Pamir Mountains.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Cultural & Intellectual Transmission: Beyond commercial luxury goods, the Silk Road was the primary conduit for the transmission of world religions (Buddhism entering East Asia from India, Nestorian Christianity, Islam) and transformative technological innovations (papermaking, gunpowder, compass, printing).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Pre-modern proto-globalization, cross-civilizational linguistic, artistic, and scientific syncretism.

## Key Terminology
- **Silk Road**: Ancient network of Eurasian trade routes connecting the Far East to the Mediterranean Basin.
- **Caravanserai**: Roadside roadside inn network where caravan traders and merchants could rest and water pack animals.
- **Zhang Qian**: Han Dynasty envoy whose exploration of Central Asia initiated the formal establishment of the Silk Road.
