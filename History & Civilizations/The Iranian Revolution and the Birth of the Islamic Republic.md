# The Iranian Revolution and the Birth of the Islamic Republic

## Overview
The Iranian Revolution of 1979 overthrew the Western-backed Pahlavi monarchy, replacing it with an anti-imperialist theocratic Islamic Republic under Ayatollah Ruhollah Khomeini.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Grievances Against the Pahlavi Shah: Mohammad Reza Pahlavi presided over rapid Westernization (the White Revolution) and oil wealth concentration, enforced by his brutal intelligence agency SAVAK, alienating both traditional Shi'a clerics (*ulama*) and secular leftist students.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Khomeini Theocracy & The Hostage Crisis: Massive urban strikes and protests forced the Shah to flee in January 1979. Khomeini returned from exile, establishing a theocracy grounded in *Velayat-e Faqih* (Guardianship of the Islamic Jurist). Radical students stormed the U.S. Embassy in Tehran in November 1979, holding 52 Americans hostage for 444 days.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Geopolitical Shi'a-Sunni rivalry with Saudi Arabia, eight-year Iran-Iraq War (1980–1988), collapse of American Middle Eastern strategic architecture.

## Key Terminology
- **Ayatollah Khomeini**: Shi'a religious leader who led the 1979 revolution and became Iran's first Supreme Leader.
- **Velayat-e Faqih**: Governance of the Jurist; theocratic doctrine giving senior Shi'a Islamic clergy absolute ultimate political authority.
- **SAVAK**: Notorious secret police and security service of the Pahlavi monarchy in Iran.
