# The Cold War - Nuclear Deterrence, Proxy Wars, and the Iron Curtain

## Overview
The Cold War (1947–1991) was a protracted ideological, geopolitical, and economic struggle between the capitalist United States and the communist Soviet Union, fought without direct open warfare between the superpowers.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Bipolar Division & Nuclear Deterrence: Marked by Winston Churchill's 1946 'Iron Curtain' speech, dividing Europe into NATO and the Warsaw Pact. Both superpowers amassed arsenals of tens of thousands of thermonuclear weapons, producing Mutually Assured Destruction (MAD) that prevented direct conflict during flashpoints like the 1962 Cuban Missile Crisis.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Proxy Wars & Ideological Struggle: Direct conflict manifested in global proxy wars across the decolonizing Third World: the Korean War (1950–1953), the Vietnam War (1955–1975), and the Soviet-Afghan War (1979–1989), accompanied by the Space Race, global espionage (CIA vs KGB), and cultural propaganda.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Creation of modern geopolitical alliances (NATO), spaceflight exploration breakthroughs (Apollo 11), non-proliferation treaties.

## Key Terminology
- **Cold War**: Period of ideological and geopolitical tension between the United States and the Soviet Union from 1947 to 1991.
- **Mutually Assured Destruction (MAD)**: Military deterrence doctrine where full-scale nuclear use results in the complete annihilation of both attacker and defender.
- **Proxy War**: Conflict where two opposing powers use third parties as substitutes for fighting each other directly.
