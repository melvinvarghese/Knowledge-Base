# The Good Friday Agreement and the Northern Ireland Peace Process

## Overview
The Good Friday Agreement (Belfast Agreement, 1998) was a landmark peace accord that largely ended thirty years of violent sectarian conflict ('The Troubles') in Northern Ireland.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Roots of The Troubles: A three-decade conflict pitting predominantly Protestant Unionists/Loyalists (wishing to remain in the United Kingdom) against predominantly Catholic Nationalists/Republicans (wishing to join a united Ireland), characterized by paramilitary violence (Provisional IRA, UVF) and British security operations that claimed over 3,500 lives.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Constitutional Architecture & Consociational Power-Sharing: Brokered by US Senator George Mitchell and signed by British Prime Minister Tony Blair and Irish Taoiseach Bertie Ahern. Established: 1. The Principle of Consent (Northern Ireland's constitutional status can change only if a majority of its citizens vote for it); 2. Consociational power-sharing in the Northern Ireland Assembly requiring cross-community support; 3. Decommissioning of paramilitary weapons and the demilitarization of the UK-Irish border.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Regarded internationally as one of the world's most successful models of complex multi-party peace diplomacy; central consideration during post-Brexit negotiations.

## Key Terminology
- **Good Friday Agreement**: Major milestone in the Northern Ireland peace process signed on April 10, 1998, establishing power-sharing governance.
- **The Troubles**: Ethno-nationalist conflict in Northern Ireland that lasted from the late 1960s to 1998.
- **Power-Sharing**: Consociational democratic framework ensuring that both unionist and nationalist communities have a mandatory share of executive governance.
