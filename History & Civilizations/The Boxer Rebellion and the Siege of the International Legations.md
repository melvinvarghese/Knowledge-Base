# The Boxer Rebellion and the Siege of the International Legations

## Overview
The 55-day Siege of the International Legations in Beijing (June–August 1900) was the dramatic military climax of the anti-imperialist Boxer uprising in Qing China.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The 55 Days at Peking: Over 3,000 foreign diplomats, soldiers, and Chinese Christian converts were besieged inside Beijing's fortified diplomatic legation quarter by Boxer militia and Qing imperial troops under General Ronglu, enduring relentless artillery bombardments and sniper fire.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Eight-Nation Relief Expedition: In August 1900, a 20,000-strong multinational relief force (British, American, Russian, Japanese, French, German, Italian, Austro-Hungarian) marched from Tianjin, stormed Beijing's gates, broke the siege, and looted the capital, forcing Empress Dowager Cixi to flee disguised as a peasant.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Directly catalyzed the 1901 Boxer Protocol, hastening the terminal collapse of the Qing Dynasty in 1911.

## Key Terminology
- **Legation Quarter**: Diplomatic district in Beijing where foreign embassies were besieged for 55 days in the summer of 1900.
- **Ronglu**: Qing military commander who commanded imperial troops surrounding the legations while attempting to mitigate total diplomatic slaughter.
- **Boxer Indemnity**: Enormous financial indemnity of 450 million silver taels imposed on China following the relief of Beijing.
