# The Black Death and Medieval Socioeconomic Transformation

## Overview
The demographic shock of the 14th-century bubonic plague pandemic reconfigured European land-to-labor ratios, accelerating the demise of feudalism.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Labor Shortage & The High Wage Economy: Eliminating one-third to one-half of the working population abruptly shifted bargaining power from feudal landlords to agricultural laborers. Real wages doubled across Western Europe, rendering feudal serfdom unworkable and encouraging tenant farming with cash wages.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Social Unrest & Sumptuary Laws: Aristocratic elites attempted to freeze wages via statutes (e.g., English Statute of Labourers 1351) and regulate peasant clothing through sumptuary laws. These measures provoked violent peasant uprisings, including the French Jacquerie (1358) and English Peasants' Revolt (1381).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Transition toward early capitalist agrarian markets, capital intensification of agriculture, livestock farming expansion.

## Key Terminology
- **Statute of Labourers**: 1351 English law attempting to cap wages and prohibit laborers from leaving their ancestral manors.
- **Peasants' Revolt of 1381**: Major popular rebellion in England led by Wat Tyler demanding abolition of serfdom and poll taxes.
- **Sumptuary Laws**: Statutory laws attempting to regulate social class consumption and enforce dress codes according to rank.
