# The American and French Revolutions - Liberty, Rights, and Terror

## Overview
The Atlantic Revolutions of the late 18th century overthrew monarchical regimes, establishing the modern concepts of popular sovereignty and universal citizen rights.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The American Revolution (1775–1783): Thirteen British colonies revolted against imperial taxation without representation, drafting the 1776 Declaration of Independence based on Lockean natural rights. The 1787 U.S. Constitution established the world's first modern constitutional federal republic with institutional separation of powers.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The French Revolution (1789–1799): Bankrupted by wars, France erupted in revolt: the National Assembly issued the *Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen*, abolishing feudal privileges. Radicalization under the Jacobins led to the execution of Louis XVI, the Reign of Terror under Robespierre (1793–1794), and the eventual rise of Napoleon Bonaparte.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Spread of universal civil rights declarations, abolition of hereditary feudal estates, birth of modern political left-right spectrum.

## Key Terminology
- **Declaration of the Rights of Man**: Foundational French revolutionary document of 1789 establishing individual and collective universal rights.
- **Reign of Terror**: Period of intense political violence and mass executions during the French Revolution led by the Jacobins.
- **Popular Sovereignty**: Principle that the authority of a state and its government are created and sustained by the consent of its people.
