# World War I - Trench Warfare, Alliances, and the Treaty of Versailles

## Overview
World War I (1914–1918) was the first industrialized global total war, destroying four European empires and fundamentally redrawing the geopolitical landscape of the modern world.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Alliance Entanglements & Industrial Attrition: The assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand in Sarajevo triggered the alliance tripwires (Triple Entente vs Central Powers). Mobilizing 70 million soldiers, the Western Front devolved into deadlocked trench warfare dominated by defensive firepower (machine guns, heavy artillery, poison gas, barbed wire) producing catastrophic casualties at Verdun and the Somme.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Imperial Collapse & The Treaty of Versailles (1919): The war brought the collapse of the German, Russian, Austro-Hungarian, and Ottoman Empires. The punitive Treaty of Versailles imposed territorial losses, disarmament, and crushing war reparations on Germany (Article 231 'War Guilt Clause'), generating economic instability that facilitated the rise of fascism.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Creation of the League of Nations, Sykes-Picot partitioning of the Middle East, emergence of total war doctrine.

## Key Terminology
- **Trench Warfare**: Combat where armies fight from stationary, fortified trench systems protected by barbed wire and machine guns.
- **Treaty of Versailles**: 1919 peace treaty ending World War I that imposed severe financial reparations and disarmament on Germany.
- **War Guilt Clause**: Article 231 of the Versailles Treaty placing sole moral responsibility for World War I on Germany.
