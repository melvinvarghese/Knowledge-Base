# The Mexican Revolution - Zapata, Pancho Villa, and the 1917 Constitution

## Overview
The Mexican Revolution (1910–1920) was a complex ten-year civil war that overthrew the Porfiriato dictatorship, establishing pioneering constitutional agrarian and labor rights.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Overthrow of the Porfiriato: Thirty-year dictator Porfirio Díaz industrialized Mexico through foreign capital while concentrating 95% of agricultural land in elite hacienda estates. Francisco I. Madero ignited armed revolution in 1910 under the banner of electoral democracy.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Factional Warfare & Popular Leaders: Emiliano Zapata led peasant armies in Morelos demanding land redistribution (*Tierra y Libertad*—Land and Liberty) under the Plan of Ayala. Pancho Villa commanded the cavalry-heavy Division of the North. The constitutionalist faction led by Venustiano Carranza prevailed, drafting the progressive Mexican Constitution of 1917.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Pioneered early 20th-century socio-economic rights: Article 27 (state ownership of mineral/oil wealth and land reform) and Article 123 (labor protections: 8-hour workday, right to strike).

## Key Terminology
- **Plan of Ayala**: Emiliano Zapata's 1911 manifesto calling for immediate restitution and redistribution of agricultural land to peasant communities.
- **Porfiriato**: The 35-year autocratic regime of Porfirio Díaz characterized by economic modernization alongside severe social inequality.
- **Constitution of 1917**: Progressive Mexican constitution establishing seminal social, labor, and communal land (*ejido*) rights.
