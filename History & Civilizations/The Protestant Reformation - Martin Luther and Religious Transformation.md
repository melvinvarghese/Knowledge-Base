# The Protestant Reformation - Martin Luther and Religious Transformation

## Overview
The Protestant Reformation, initiated in 1517 by Martin Luther's Ninety-Five Theses, permanently fractured Western Latin Christendom and transformed European political geography.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Theological Catalyst: In 1517, German monk Martin Luther protested corrupt papal sales of indulgences in Wittenberg, articulating two core theological pillars: *Sola Fide* (justification by faith alone, rejecting salvation through papal works) and *Sola Scriptura* (the Bible as the sole religious authority, rejecting papal infallibility).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Political Consequences & Wars of Religion: Aided by the printing press, Protestant doctrines (Lutheranism, Calvinism, Anglicanism) spread rapidly. German princes leveraged the Reformation to seize monastic wealth and assert independence from the Holy Roman Emperor, precipitating over a century of devastating European religious warfare (The Thirty Years' War).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Democratization of vernacular literacy (Luther Bible), rise of state sovereignty, separation of church and secular legal jurisdiction.

## Key Terminology
- **Ninety-Five Theses**: Propositions challenging Catholic indulgence sales posted by Martin Luther in 1517.
- **Sola Fide**: Theological doctrine asserting that salvation is achieved exclusively by faith rather than good deeds.
- **Sola Scriptura**: Doctrine maintaining that Christian scriptures are the sole and final authority on faith and doctrine.
