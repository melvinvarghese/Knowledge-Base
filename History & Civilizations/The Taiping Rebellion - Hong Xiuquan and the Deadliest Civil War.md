# The Taiping Rebellion - Hong Xiuquan and the Deadliest Civil War

## Overview
The Taiping Rebellion (1850–1864) was an apocalyptic religious and agrarian civil war in Qing Dynasty China that resulted in an estimated 20 to 30 million fatalities.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Hong Xiuquan & The God Worshipping Society: Hong Xiuquan, a failed civil-service examination candidate, had hallucinatory visions convincing him he was the younger brother of Jesus Christ, sent to purge China of 'demonic' Manchu Qing rule and establish the 'Heavenly Kingdom of Great Peace' (*Taiping Tianguo*).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Radical Social Program & Brutal Suppressive Campaign: The Taipings instituted radical egalitarian policies: strict gender equality (banning foot-binding), shared communal property, and abolition of private commerce. They captured Nanjing, ruling southern China for a decade until suppressed by Qing statesman Zeng Guofan's regional Xiang Army aided by Western mercenaries.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Deadliest civil conflict in human history, fatally decentralized military power away from the Qing court to regional warlords.

## Key Terminology
- **Taiping Tianguo**: Heavenly Kingdom of Great Peace; rebel millenarian state founded by Hong Xiuquan controlling southern China.
- **Zeng Guofan**: Qing official and general who created the Xiang Army to suppress the Taiping Rebellion, preserving the Qing Dynasty.
- **Foot-Binding Abolition**: Pioneering progressive social policy implemented by Taipings decades before national abolition in China.
