# The Age of Exploration - Vasco da Gama, Columbus, and the Treaty of Tordesillas

## Overview
The Age of Exploration (15th–17th centuries) was driven by European maritime expeditions seeking direct access to Asian spice markets following Ottoman control of overland routes.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Portuguese Pioneering & The Caravel: Prince Henry the Navigator established a maritime navigation academy at Sagres. Portuguese shipwrights designed the ocean-going *caravel* utilizing lateen triangular sails to sail into the wind (tacking). Bartolomeu Dias rounded the Cape of Good Hope (1488), and Vasco da Gama reached Calicut, India (1498), establishing the maritime spice trade empire (*Estado da Índia*).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Columbus & The Treaty of Tordesillas (1494): Christopher Columbus's transatlantic voyages for Spain (1492) initiated permanent European contact with the Americas. To avert imperial conflict, Pope Alexander VI brokered the Treaty of Tordesillas, drawing a meridian 370 leagues west of Cape Verde: Spain claimed newly discovered lands to the west; Portugal claimed those to the east (including Brazil).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Birth of global maritime commerce, European colonization of the Americas, shift of economic power to Atlantic coastlines.

## Key Terminology
- **Caravel**: Small, highly maneuverable sailing ship developed by the Portuguese featuring lateen sails capable of ocean voyaging.
- **Treaty of Tordesillas**: 1494 agreement dividing newly discovered lands outside Europe between the Spanish and Portuguese Empires.
- **Vasco da Gama**: Portuguese explorer who led the first ocean voyage directly from Europe to India via the Cape of Good Hope.
