# The Cambodian Genocide - Pol Pot, the Khmer Rouge, and the Killing Fields

## Overview
The Cambodian Genocide (1975–1979) was the systematic extermination of an estimated 1.5 to 2 million people—roughly a quarter of Cambodia's population—by the extremist Maoist Khmer Rouge regime.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Year Zero & Agrarian Totalitarianism: Led by Pol Pot (Saloth Sar), the Khmer Rouge seized Phnom Penh on April 17, 1975, proclaiming 'Year Zero'. The regime forced the entire urban population into the countryside on foot, abolishing money, private property, religion, and schools in an ideological quest to create a pure, classless agrarian communist society.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Killing Fields & Tuol Sleng (S-21): The regime systematically purged intellectuals, Buddhist monks, ethnic Vietnamese and Cham minorities, and anyone wearing eyeglasses or speaking a foreign language. Over 18,000 prisoners were tortured at the Tuol Sleng interrogation center (S-21) before being murdered in mass graves ('The Killing Fields') across Cambodia.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Halted in January 1979 when the Vietnamese military invaded and overthrew the regime; trials conducted by the Extraordinary Chambers in the Courts of Cambodia (ECCC).

## Key Terminology
- **Khmer Rouge**: Extremist communist guerrilla movement that ruled Cambodia from 1975 to 1979 under the leadership of Pol Pot.
- **Year Zero**: Idea introduced by Pol Pot that all culture and traditions in a society must be completely destroyed and replaced from scratch.
- **Tuol Sleng (S-21)**: High school converted into a notorious interrogation and torture prison by the Khmer Rouge regime in Phnom Penh.
