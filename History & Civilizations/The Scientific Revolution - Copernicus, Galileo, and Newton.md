# The Scientific Revolution - Copernicus, Galileo, and Newton

## Overview
The Scientific Revolution (16th–17th centuries) replaced Aristotelian scholastic dogma with mathematical empiricism, experimental observation, and universal physical laws.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Heliocentric Paradigm Shift: Nicolaus Copernicus (*De revolutionibus*, 1543) mathematically displaced the Earth from the center of the universe. Johannes Kepler derived the three empirical laws of planetary motion, discovering that planets travel in elliptical orbits with the Sun at one focus.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Galileo's Telescope & The Newtonian Synthesis: Galileo Galilei used the telescope to observe Jupiter's moons, lunar craters, and sunspots, defending heliocentrism against the Roman Inquisition. Isaac Newton (*Principia Mathematica*, 1687) unified terrestrial and celestial mechanics under his Universal Law of Gravitation ($F = G \frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2}$) and Three Laws of Motion, inaugurating modern physics.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Establishment of the scientific method (Francis Bacon), foundation of the Royal Society and Académie des Sciences.

## Key Terminology
- **Heliocentrism**: Astronomical model positioning the Sun at the center of the solar system with Earth and planets orbiting it.
- **Principia Mathematica**: Newton's 1687 masterwork establishing the mathematical principles of classical mechanics and universal gravitation.
- **Scientific Method**: Empirical method of knowledge acquisition characterized by observation, hypothesis formation, experimentation, and revision.
