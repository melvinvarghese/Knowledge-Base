# Alexander the Great and the Hellenistic World

## Overview
Alexander the Great (356–323 BCE) of Macedon conquered the Persian Empire, creating an empire spanning three continents and unleashing the cosmopolitan Hellenistic era.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Conquest Campaigns: Tutored by Aristotle and leveraging his father Philip II's Macedonian sarissa phalanx and companion cavalry, Alexander defeated Darius III at the Granicus, Issus, and Gaugamela, marching eastward through Sogdiana and into the Punjab before his troops refused to march beyond the Hyphasis.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Hellenistic Era & Cultural Syncretism: Following Alexander's death at Babylon, his empire fractured among successor generals (*Diadochi*: Ptolemy, Seleucus, Antigonus). Greek language (*Koine*), architecture, philosophy, and science blended with Persian, Egyptian, and Indian cultures, epitomized by the Great Library of Alexandria and Greco-Buddhist Gandharan art.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Dissemination of Hellenistic scholarship across Afro-Eurasia, foundation of Alexandria, intellectual framework that later fed the Roman Empire.

## Key Terminology
- **Sarissa**: 4-to-6 meter long two-handed bronze-pointed pike used by the Macedonian infantry phalanx.
- **Diadochi**: Rival Macedonian generals, friends, and family of Alexander who divided his empire following his death in 323 BCE.
- **Koine Greek**: Common supra-regional dialect of Greek that served as the lingua franca of the Mediterranean and Middle East for centuries.
