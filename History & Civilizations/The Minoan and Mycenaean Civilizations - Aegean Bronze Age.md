# The Minoan and Mycenaean Civilizations - Aegean Bronze Age

## Overview
The Minoans of Crete and Mycenaeans of mainland Greece formed the foundational prehistoric civilizations of the Aegean Bronze Age, inspiring later Homeric epics.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Minoan Maritime Thalassocracy (c. 2000–1450 BCE): Centered at the sprawling unfortified palace of Knossos on Crete, Minoans dominated Aegean maritime trade, celebrated for polychrome frescoes, bull-leaping rituals, and the undeciphered Linear A script.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Mycenaean Militarism & Linear B (c. 1600–1100 BCE): Mycenaeans built cyclopean fortified acropolises (Mycenae, Tiryns) ruled by warlord kings (*wanax*). Deciphered by Michael Ventris in 1952, Linear B clay tablets proved their language was an archaic early form of Greek.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Archaeological origins of Greek mythology (the Minotaur Labyrinth, Trojan War legends), maritime trade circuits across the Eastern Mediterranean.

## Key Terminology
- **Knossos**: Largest Bronze Age archaeological site on Crete, serving as the ceremonial and political hub of Minoan civilization.
- **Linear B**: Syllabic script used by Mycenaean administrative scribes, deciphered as the earliest recorded Greek dialect.
- **Thalassocracy**: State with primarily maritime realm and naval dominion over sea trade routes.
