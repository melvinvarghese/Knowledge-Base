# The Song Dynasty Economic Revolution - Paper Money, Coal, and Maritime Trade

## Overview
The Song Dynasty (960–1279 CE) experienced a commercial and industrial revolution centuries ahead of early modern Europe, transforming medieval China into an economic superpower.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Monetary Innovation & Jiaozi: Exploding commercial volume outstripped copper coinage supplies. In 11th-century Sichuan, merchant guilds and later the central Song government issued *Jiaozi*—the world's first state-backed paper banknote currency, supported by reserve depositories.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Proto-Industrial Metallurgy & Maritime Expansion: Song blast furnaces burned mineral bituminous coal rather than charcoal, producing over 125,000 tons of cast and wrought iron annually by 1078 (exceeding all of Europe's iron production in 1700). Following the loss of northern land routes, the Southern Song built a state navy and commercial fleet utilizing magnetic compasses and multi-masted junks to dominate maritime trade across Southeast Asia and the Indian Ocean.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Early capitalist commercialization of agrarian markets, urbanization rates exceeding 10% of total population.

## Key Terminology
- **Jiaozi**: World's first paper-printed banknote currency developed during the Song Dynasty in China.
- **Bituminous Coal**: Mineral coal utilized by Song metalworkers to power high-temperature iron smelting blast furnaces.
- **Merchant Guilds**: Commercial associations that organized bulk shipping, wholesale warehouses, and paper credit instruments.
