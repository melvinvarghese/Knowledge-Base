# The Late Bronze Age Collapse - Sea Peoples and Systems Collapse

## Overview
Around 1200–1150 BCE, a catastrophic systems collapse simultaneously destroyed nearly every major Mediterranean civilization from Greece and Anatolia to the Levant and Egypt.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Catastrophic Urban Destruction: Within decades, the Mycenaean palaces, the Hittite Empire, the Syrian coastal city-state of Ugarit, and southern Levantine strongholds were burned to the ground and permanently abandoned, ushering in the Greek Dark Ages.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Multifactorial Drivers & The Sea Peoples: Historical and paleoclimatological evidence suggests cascading systemic failure: prolonged megadroughts, severe earthquakes ('earthquake storms'), disruptions to bronze tin/copper trade routes, and violent migratory invasions by the mysterious confederation known as the 'Sea Peoples'.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Transition from the Bronze Age to the Iron Age, democratization of metallurgy through cheaper and more abundant iron tools and weapons.

## Key Terminology
- **Late Bronze Age Collapse**: Sudden, violent cultural and economic collapse of Eastern Mediterranean civilizations c. 1200 BCE.
- **Sea Peoples**: Confederacy of seafaring raiders and migrants who invaded Anatolia, Syria, and Egypt during the Bronze Age collapse.
- **Systems Collapse**: Sociological theory where interconnected complex societies fail simultaneously due to interdependent structural vulnerabilities.
