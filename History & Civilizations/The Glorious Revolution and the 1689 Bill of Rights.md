# The Glorious Revolution and the 1689 Bill of Rights

## Overview
The Glorious Revolution (1688) peacefully overthrew Catholic King James II of England, replacing him with William III of Orange and Mary II and establishing parliamentary constitutional monarchy.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Bloodless Coup & Invitation to William: Fearing a permanent Catholic Stuart dynasty, seven English politicians ('the Immortal Seven') invited Dutch Stadtholder William of Orange to invade England with an armada. James II fled to France, achieving a virtually bloodless transfer of royal power in England.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The 1689 English Bill of Rights: Parliament passed the landmark Bill of Rights, permanently ending the monarch's claim to divine right and absolute power. It established that the Crown cannot suspend laws, levy taxes, or maintain a standing army without Parliamentary consent, guaranteeing free speech in Parliament, regular elections, and trial by jury.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Direct philosophical inspiration for John Locke's *Second Treatise of Government*, architectural template for the United States Constitution.

## Key Terminology
- **Glorious Revolution**: 1688 events in England that resulted in the deposition of James II and the accession of William III and Mary II.
- **English Bill of Rights**: 1689 constitutional statute establishing parliamentary supremacy, limits on royal power, and individual civil liberties.
- **Constitutional Monarchy**: System of government in which a monarch shares power with a constitutionally organized government such as a parliament.
