# The Islamic Golden Age - House of Wisdom and Scientific Translation

## Overview
The Islamic Golden Age (8th–14th centuries CE), centered in Abbasid Baghdad, was an intellectual flourishing where Muslim, Christian, and Jewish scholars translated, synthesized, and expanded global science.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The House of Wisdom (*Bayt al-Hikma*): Founded by Caliph Harun al-Rashid and expanded by al-Ma'mun, the Translation Movement translated Greek, Persian, and Indian scientific, mathematical, and philosophical manuscripts (Aristotle, Euclid, Galen) into Arabic.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Pioneering Scientific Advances: Al-Khwarizmi developed algebra (*al-jabr*) and introduced Hindu-Arabic numerals and decimals to the West. Ibn al-Haytham (Alhazen) established experimental optics and the scientific method. Ibn Sina (Avicenna) authored *The Canon of Medicine*, the standard medical textbook in Europe and the Middle East for 600 years.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Direct foundation of modern algebra, experimental optics, medical diagnostics, and the European Renaissance.

## Key Terminology
- **House of Wisdom**: Major intellectual center and library in Abbasid Baghdad during the Islamic Golden Age.
- **Al-Khwarizmi**: 9th-century mathematician who formalized algebra and the decimal positional numerical system.
- **Ibn Sina (Avicenna)**: Polymath physician whose 'Canon of Medicine' served as the medical authority for centuries.
