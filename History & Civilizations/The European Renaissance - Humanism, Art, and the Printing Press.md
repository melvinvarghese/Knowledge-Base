# The European Renaissance - Humanism, Art, and the Printing Press

## Overview
The Renaissance (14th–17th centuries) marked the cultural, artistic, and intellectual rebirth of Western Europe, bridging the Middle Ages with modernity through Renaissance Humanism.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Renaissance Humanism & The Classical Revival: Sparked in Italian city-states (Florence) by scholars like Petrarch, humanism emphasized the study of classical Greek and Roman literature, rhetoric, and moral philosophy (*studia humanitatis*), viewing human intellectual capacity as divine.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Technological Catalyst - Gutenberg's Printing Press: Around 1440, Johannes Gutenberg developed movable metal type, oil-based ink, and mechanical press integration in Mainz. By democratizing book production, the press shattered clerical monopolies on knowledge, enabling rapid dissemination of scientific and religious ideas.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Scientific revolution seeds, linear artistic perspective (Brunelleschi, Da Vinci, Michelangelo), vernacular literature democratization.

## Key Terminology
- **Humanism**: Renaissance intellectual movement emphasizing classical learning, individualism, and human agency.
- **Movable Type**: Printing technology utilizing individually cast metal letterforms invented in Europe by Johannes Gutenberg c. 1440.
- **Linear Perspective**: Geometric drawing method developed in the Renaissance creating convincing spatial depth on flat surfaces.
