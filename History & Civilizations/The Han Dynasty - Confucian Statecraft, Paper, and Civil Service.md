# The Han Dynasty - Confucian Statecraft, Paper, and Civil Service

## Overview
The Han Dynasty (202 BCE–220 CE) consolidated Chinese imperial identity, establishing Confucianism as state orthodoxy and birthing transformative technological inventions.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Confucian Synthesis & The Imperial Examination: Emperor Wu (Han Wudi) synthesized Qin administrative centralization with Confucian moral philosophy, establishing the Imperial Academy to train civil servants based on mastery of the Confucian Classics, laying the foundation for meritocratic bureaucracy.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Technological Inventions: Cai Lun perfected the manufacture of paper from mulberry bark and rags (c. 105 CE); Han inventors created the magnetic compass, blast furnaces for cast iron, chain pump irrigation, and the world's first seismoscope (Zhang Heng, 132 CE).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Ethnic and national self-identification of China's majority as 'Han', institutional model of Chinese imperial governance for two millennia.

## Key Terminology
- **Han Wudi**: Seventh emperor of the Han Dynasty who expanded imperial territory and established Confucianism as the state ideology.
- **Cai Lun**: Imperial Han court official credited with standardizing papermaking technology using plant fibers and rags.
- **Imperial Academy (Taixue)**: Ancient Chinese university founded to train administrative officials in the Confucian classics.
