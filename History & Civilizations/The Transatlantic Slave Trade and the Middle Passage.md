# The Transatlantic Slave Trade and the Middle Passage

## Overview
The Transatlantic Slave Trade (16th–19th centuries) forcibly abducted and transported an estimated 12.5 million enslaved Africans to the Americas under horrific conditions.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Triangular Trade: European merchant ships sailed to West Africa carrying manufactured goods (guns, textiles, metalware) traded for enslaved persons; the notorious 'Middle Passage' transported captives across the Atlantic to work plantation cash crops (sugar, cotton, tobacco, coffee); and the raw commodities were shipped back to Europe for processing.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Horrors of the Middle Passage & Economic Impact: Enslaved people were shackled in suffocating, disease-ridden ship holds where roughly 15% (nearly 2 million individuals) perished during transit. The coerced labor of enslaved Africans generated colossal capital accumulations that fueled Western European financial institutions and early industrialization.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Demographic and political destabilization of West African kingdoms, institutionalization of anti-Black racial caste systems across the Americas.

## Key Terminology
- **Middle Passage**: Forced ocean crossing of slave ships carrying abducted Africans from West Africa to the Caribbean and Americas.
- **Triangular Trade**: Three-legged transatlantic commercial trade network linking Europe, West Africa, and the American colonies.
- **Plantation Economy**: Economic system reliant on agricultural mass production of export crops powered by enslaved chattel labor.
