# The Tang and Song Dynasties - Champa Rice, Gunpowder, and Printing

## Overview
The Tang (618–907 CE) and Song (960–1279 CE) dynasties were China's Golden Age of cosmopolitan culture, economic proto-capitalism, and technological revolution.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Tang Cosmopolitanism & Poetry: Tang Chang'an was the world's largest metropolis (>1 million residents), welcoming Persian, Arab, and Central Asian merchants along the Silk Road. Li Bai and Du Fu brought classical Chinese poetry to its artistic peak.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Song Technological & Economic Revolution: Introduction of drought-resistant, fast-ripening Champa rice from Vietnam triggered a population explosion. Song China developed the Four Great Inventions: Woodblock/Movable-Type Printing (Bi Sheng), Gunpowder weaponry, and the Mariner's Magnetic Compass. The Song state issued *Jiaozi*, the world's first paper money, accompanied by thriving joint-stock merchant enterprises.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Proto-industrial iron smelting, commercialization of agriculture, establishment of Neo-Confucian philosophy under Zhu Xi.

## Key Terminology
- **Champa Rice**: Quick-maturing, drought-resistant rice variety that allowed double-cropping and fueled Song China's population boom.
- **Jiaozi**: First paper banknote currency in world history, developed in 11th-century Song Dynasty Sichuan.
- **Four Great Inventions**: Historic Chinese inventions: paper making, printing, gunpowder, and the compass that transformed global history.
