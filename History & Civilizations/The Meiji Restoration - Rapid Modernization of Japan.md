# The Meiji Restoration - Rapid Modernization of Japan

## Overview
The Meiji Restoration (1868) overthrew over 250 years of Tokugawa shogunal isolation, transforming Japan within four decades into a modern industrial and military global power.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The End of Tokugawa Isolation (*Sakoku*): The arrival of American Commodore Matthew Perry's Black Ships in 1853 forced Japan to sign unequal trade treaties, sparking internal crisis. In 1868, samurai reformers overthrew the Tokugawa Shogunate, restoring nominal executive authority to teenage Emperor Meiji under the slogan *Fukoku Kyōhei* ('Enrich the Country, Strengthen the Armed Forces').

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Wholesale Modernization & Institutional Reform: The Meiji regime abolished the feudal four-tier class system, disarmed the samurai class, introduced universal conscription, built national railway and telegraph networks, established compulsory public education, and promulgated the 1889 Meiji Constitution modeled on Prussia.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Defeat of imperial Russia in the Russo-Japanese War (1904–1905), model of rapid state-directed industrial modernization.

## Key Terminology
- **Meiji Restoration**: Political transformation of 1868 that restored imperial rule and catalyzed the rapid modernization and industrialization of Japan.
- **Fukoku Kyohei**: National slogan of Meiji Japan: 'Enrich the country, strengthen the military'.
- **Samurai Abolition**: Dismantling of the hereditary samurai warrior class in favor of a national conscript army.
