# The Greco-Persian Wars - Marathon, Thermopylae, and Salamis

## Overview
The Greco-Persian Wars (499–449 BCE) pitted the mighty Achaemenid Empire against disparate Greek city-states fighting to preserve their independence.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
First Invasion & The Battle of Marathon (490 BCE): Following the Ionian Revolt, Darius I invaded mainland Greece. Athenian hoplite phalanxes executed a double-envelopment charge at Marathon, routing a superior Persian force without Spartan assistance.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Second Invasion: Thermopylae and Salamis (480 BCE): Xerxes I launched a massive land-sea campaign. King Leonidas and 300 Spartans mounted a sacrificial holding action at the pass of Thermopylae. Themistocles' naval strategy then lured the Persian fleet into the narrow straits of Salamis, where maneuverable Athenian triremes shattered Persian naval hegemony.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Preservation of nascent Western democracy, catalyst for the Classical Athenian Golden Age and development of historical writing by Herodotus.

## Key Terminology
- **Hoplite Phalanx**: Tightly packed formation of armored Greek infantry armed with long spears (*dory*) and overlapping round shields (*hoplon*).
- **Trireme**: Ancient Hellenic warship powered by three stacked tiers of oarsmen designed for high-speed naval ramming.
- **Themistocles**: Athenian statesman and admiral who championed naval expansion and orchestrated victory at the Battle of Salamis.
