# The Songhai Empire - Askia the Great and the Timbuktu Scholastic Center

## Overview
The Songhai Empire (15th–16th centuries) was the largest pre-colonial empire in West African history, dominating the trans-Saharan gold, salt, and manuscript trade.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Imperial Expansion & Administrative Centralization: Founded by Sonni Ali Ber and consolidated by Askia Muhammad I ('Askia the Great'), the empire established a centralized bureaucracy, professional navy on the Niger River, and unified weights and measures across West Africa.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Timbuktu as a Global Center of Scholarship: The university of Sankore in Timbuktu housed over 25,000 international students and dozens of private libraries containing hundreds of thousands of handwritten Arabic manuscripts on astronomy, mathematics, medicine, jurisprudence, and philosophy.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Fell in 1591 to Moroccan invaders utilizing gunpowder arquebuses at the Battle of Tondibi, fracturing the Western Sahel.

## Key Terminology
- **Askia Muhammad**: Ruler of the Songhai Empire who institutionalized Islamic law and turned Timbuktu into a major scholastic center.
- **Sankore University**: Ancient educational institution and mosque in Timbuktu famous for manuscript preservation and Islamic scholarship.
- **Trans-Saharan Trade**: Trade routes across the Sahara Desert exchanging gold, salt, ivory, and manuscripts between West Africa and the Mediterranean.
