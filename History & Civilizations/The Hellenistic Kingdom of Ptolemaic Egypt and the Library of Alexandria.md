# The Hellenistic Kingdom of Ptolemaic Egypt and the Library of Alexandria

## Overview
Ptolemaic Egypt (305–30 BCE), founded by Alexander's Macedonian general Ptolemy I Soter, merged Hellenistic Greek administrative culture with ancient Egyptian pharaonic traditions.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Dynastic Statecraft & Alexandria: The Ptolemies declared themselves pharaohs, ruling from the Mediterranean metropolis of Alexandria. Alexandria became the financial and intellectual capital of the Mediterranean, home to the Pharos Lighthouse (one of the Seven Wonders) and the great royal Mouseion.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Great Library of Alexandria: Directed by scholars like Eratosthenes (who calculated Earth's circumference) and Aristarchus of Samos (early heliocentric model), the Library sought to collect all human knowledge, amassing an estimated 400,000 papyrus scrolls through mandatory confiscation and copying of books on ships docking in the harbor.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Hellenistic scientific golden age, synthesis of Greek mathematics (Euclid) and Egyptian astronomy, ended with Cleopatra VII in 30 BCE.

## Key Terminology
- **Ptolemy I Soter**: Macedonian general under Alexander the Great who founded the Ptolemaic Dynasty in Egypt.
- **Mouseion**: Royal research institution in Alexandria that included the famous Great Library.
- **Cleopatra VII**: Last active ruler of the Ptolemaic Kingdom whose death marked the absorption of Egypt into the Roman Empire.
