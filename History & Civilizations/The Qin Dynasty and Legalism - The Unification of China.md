# The Qin Dynasty and Legalism - The Unification of China

## Overview
The Qin Dynasty (221–206 BCE), led by Qin Shi Huang, unified China for the first time, ending the Warring States period through iron military discipline and Legalist philosophy.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Legalist Philosophy (*Fajia*): Rejecting Confucian ideals of moral benevolence, Legalist statesmen (Shang Yang, Han Feizi) asserted that order requires absolute state authority, strict codified laws, and ruthless punitive incentives (*Fa*, *Shu*, *Shi*).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Imperial Standardization & Monumental Works: Qin Shi Huang abolished feudal estates, dividing China into 36 centralized commanderies. He instituted absolute standardization across weights, measures, bronze currency, cart axle widths, and the Chinese script (*Xiaozhuan*). He linked regional border defenses into the early Great Wall and was entombed with the life-sized Terracotta Army.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Established the bureaucratic imperial state model that governed China across successive dynasties for over two millennia.

## Key Terminology
- **Qin Shi Huang**: First Emperor of unified China who founded the Qin Dynasty in 221 BCE.
- **Legalism**: Philosophical doctrine advocating rigorous statutory law and total centralized state control.
- **Terracotta Army**: Collection of thousands of life-sized terracotta soldiers buried with Qin Shi Huang to protect him in the afterlife.
