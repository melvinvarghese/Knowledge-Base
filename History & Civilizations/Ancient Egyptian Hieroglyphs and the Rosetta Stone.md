# Ancient Egyptian Hieroglyphs and the Rosetta Stone

## Overview
Egyptian Hieroglyphs were a complex formal script combining logographic, syllabic, and alphabetic elements, deciphered in 1822 via the discovery of the multilingual Rosetta Stone.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Script Evolution: Used continuously from c. 3200 BCE through the Roman Empire for monumental temple inscriptions and sacred texts, accompanied by cursive daily variants (hieratic and demotic scripts). Following Christianization, knowledge of the script vanished for 1,400 years.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Rosetta Stone & Champollion's Breakthrough: Unearthed near Rashid (Rosetta) in 1799 by French soldiers during Napoleon's Egyptian expedition, the granodiorite stele bore a decree of Ptolemy V (196 BCE) inscribed in three parallel scripts: Ancient Greek, Demotic Egyptian, and Hieroglyphic Egyptian. Jean-François Champollion deciphered the phonetic cartouches of royal names (Ptolemy and Cleopatra), unlocking millennia of Egyptian dynastic history.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Foundational decipherment that established the academic discipline of modern Egyptology.

## Key Terminology
- **Hieroglyphs**: Formal writing system used by ancient Egyptians combining logographic and alphabetic symbols.
- **Rosetta Stone**: Granodiorite stele bearing decrees in three scripts that enabled the modern decipherment of Egyptian hieroglyphs.
- **Cartouche**: Oval loop enclosing royal Egyptian names written in hieroglyphs, indicating pharaonic status.
