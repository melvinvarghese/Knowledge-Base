# The Spanish Civil War - Fascism, Republicanism, and Guernica

## Overview
The Spanish Civil War (1936–1939) pitted the democratic Second Spanish Republic against General Francisco Franco's fascist Nationalist military coup, serving as a rehearsal for World War II.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Polarization & Foreign Intervention: Following the 1936 election of the leftist Popular Front, conservative generals staged a coup. The conflict polarized the world: Nazi Germany and Fascist Italy provided troops, tanks, and combat aircraft to Franco; the Soviet Union and international volunteer brigades (International Brigades) backed the Republic.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Bombing of Guernica: On April 26, 1937, the German Luftwaffe's Condor Legion terror-bombed the Basque town of Guernica, testing carpet bombing techniques on civilians and inspiring Pablo Picasso's monumental anti-war painting *Guernica*.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Establishment of Francisco Franco's authoritarian dictatorship lasting until 1975, prelude to Axis total-war blitzkrieg tactics in World War II.

## Key Terminology
- **Guernica**: Basque town obliterated by Nazi aerial bombing in 1937, immortalized in Pablo Picasso's iconic cubist masterpiece.
- **International Brigades**: Paramilitary volunteer units composed of foreign antifascist socialists and liberals who traveled to Spain to defend the Republic.
- **Popular Front**: Electoral coalition of left-wing and centrist political parties formed in 1936 to govern the Second Spanish Republic.
