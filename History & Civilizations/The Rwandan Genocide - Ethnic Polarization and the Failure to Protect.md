# The Rwandan Genocide - Ethnic Polarization and the Failure to Protect

## Overview
The 1994 Rwandan Genocide saw the systematic slaughter of an estimated 800,000 Tutsi and moderate Hutu within roughly 100 days by extremist Hutu militias.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Colonial Identity Construction & Polarization: Belgian colonial administrators had institutionalized rigid ethnic identity cards favoring minority Tutsis over majority Hutus. Following independence in 1962, Hutu regimes assumed power amid recurrent ethnic violence.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The 100-Day Extermination & International Inaction: When the plane carrying President Juvénal Habyarimana was shot down on April 6, 1994, Hutu extremist leaders mobilized the military, presidential guard, and youth militias (*Interahamwe*). Armed with machetes and directed by hate radio (RTLM), killers executed a pre-planned genocide. The UN Security Council withdrew peacekeepers (UNAMIR), failing to intervene until the Tutsi-led Rwandan Patriotic Front (RPF) took military control.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Catalyst for the doctrine of 'Responsibility to Protect' (R2P), establishment of the International Criminal Tribunal for Rwanda (ICTR) and local *Gacaca* community courts.

## Key Terminology
- **Interahamwe**: Extremist Hutu paramilitary youth organization that carried out the bulk of the 1994 genocide massacres.
- **UNAMIR**: United Nations Assistance Mission for Rwanda whose peacekeeping mandate was restricted during the genocide.
- **Responsibility to Protect (R2P)**: Global political commitment endorsed by the UN asserting the international duty to prevent genocide and ethnic cleansing.
