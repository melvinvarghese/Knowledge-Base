# The Phoenicians - Purple Dye, Maritime Thalassocracy, and the Alphabet

## Overview
The Phoenicians were a Canaanite maritime trading civilization centered in modern Lebanon (Tyre, Sidon, Byblos) whose merchant network spanned the Mediterranean.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Maritime Navigation & Tyrian Purple: Renowned as the ancient world's master shipbuilders, constructing cedar-hulled *hippoi* trading ships with double rudders. They manufactured Tyrian purple dye—extracted through labor-intensive fermentation of millions of predatory *Murex* sea snails—which became the universal Mediterranean symbol of imperial royalty.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Phoenician Alphabet: To facilitate rapid commercial accounting, Phoenician scribes developed the world's first widespread phonemic alphabet (c. 1050 BCE). Replacing cumbersome cuneiform and hieroglyphic scripts with 22 consonant letters, it was adopted and adapted by the Greeks, directly ancestral to all modern Western alphabets.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Foundation of Carthage, circumnavigation of Africa (recorded by Herodotus), democratization of written literacy.

## Key Terminology
- **Phoenician Alphabet**: Ancient 22-letter consonantal abjad alphabet that is the ancestor of almost all modern phonetic alphabets.
- **Tyrian Purple**: Extremely rare and expensive natural purple dye extracted from murex snails by Phoenician craftsmen in Tyre.
- **Carthage**: Major Phoenician colony founded on the North African coast in 814 BCE that grew into a powerful maritime empire.
