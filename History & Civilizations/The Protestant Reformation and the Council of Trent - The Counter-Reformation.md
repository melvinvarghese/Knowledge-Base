# The Protestant Reformation and the Council of Trent - The Counter-Reformation

## Overview
The Council of Trent (1545–1563) was the 19th ecumenical council of the Roman Catholic Church, serving as the theological and institutional engine of the Catholic Counter-Reformation.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Doctrinal Affirmations: Convoked by Pope Paul III in northern Italy to formulate a systematic response to Protestantism. The council reaffirmed traditional Catholic doctrines: the equal authority of Sacred Scripture and unwritten Church Tradition, the Latin Vulgate as the canonical Bible, the seven sacraments, and justification by faith working through love (rejecting *Sola Fide*).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Institutional Reforms & The Jesuits: Abolished corrupt sales of indulgences, mandated rigorous university seminaries to educate parish priests, and enforced episcopal residency rules. The newly chartered **Society of Jesus (Jesuits)**, founded by Ignatius of Loyola, served as the Pope's intellectual vanguard, establishing colleges across Europe and global missionary outposts in the Americas and Asia.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Inaugurated the theatrical, emotional Baroque art movement to inspire popular Catholic devotion; defined modern Catholicism until Vatican II.

## Key Terminology
- **Council of Trent**: Ecumenical Catholic council held between 1545 and 1563 that reformed Church discipline and condemned Protestant doctrines.
- **Jesuits**: The Society of Jesus; religious order of the Catholic Church founded by Ignatius of Loyola emphasizing intellectual mission and papal loyalty.
- **Vulgate**: Late 4th-century Latin translation of the Bible authored by Saint Jerome, affirmed as the official Catholic Bible at Trent.
