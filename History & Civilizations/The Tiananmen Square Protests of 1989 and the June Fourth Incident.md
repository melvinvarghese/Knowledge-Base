# The Tiananmen Square Protests of 1989 and the June Fourth Incident

## Overview
The 1989 Tiananmen Square Protests were student-led pro-democracy demonstrations in Beijing that were violently suppressed by the People's Liberation Army on June 4, 1989.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Origins & The Goddess of Democracy: Sparked in April 1989 following the death of reformist CCP leader Hu Yaobang. University students and workers occupied Tiananmen Square for seven weeks, demanding freedom of speech, freedom of the press, democratic reform, and government accountability against corruption. Protesters erected a 10-meter papier-mâché statue: the 'Goddess of Democracy'.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Military Crackdown & Tank Man: On the night of June 3–4, 1989, the Chinese leadership ordered the military to clear the square with tanks and live infantry fire, killing an estimated several hundred to thousands of unarmed civilians. The iconic photograph of an unidentified lone protester ('Tank Man') standing in front of a column of Type 59 tanks became the universal global symbol of defiance against authoritarianism.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
International diplomatic condemnation, imposition of Western arms embargoes, intensified domestic state censorship across modern China.

## Key Terminology
- **Tiananmen Square**: Large city square in the center of Beijing that served as the focal point of the 1989 pro-democracy protests.
- **Tank Man**: Iconic unidentified demonstrator who stood directly in front of a column of Chinese military tanks on June 5, 1989.
- **Goddess of Democracy**: 10-meter statue constructed by student protesters in Tiananmen Square modeled on the Statue of Liberty.
