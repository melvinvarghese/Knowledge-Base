# The Crusades - Holy Wars, Levant States, and Cultural Exchange

## Overview
The Crusades (1095–1291) were a series of papacy-sanctioned military expeditions by Western European Christians to reclaim Jerusalem and the Holy Land from Muslim rule.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
First Crusade & The Outremer States: Initiated by Pope Urban II's 1095 speech at Clermont (*Deus Vult*). The First Crusade captured Jerusalem in 1099, establishing the feudal Crusader States (Kingdom of Jerusalem, County of Edessa, Principality of Antioch, County of Tripoli).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Saladin & Later Crusades: Sultan Saladin recaptured Jerusalem in 1187 following the Battle of Hattin. The disastrous Fourth Crusade (1204) was diverted by Venetian mercantile interests to sack Christian Constantinople, fatally weakening the Byzantine Empire.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Commercial rise of Italian maritime merchant republics (Venice, Genoa), transfer of Arab medical, philosophical, and architectural knowledge to Europe.

## Key Terminology
- **Outremer**: French term for 'overseas', designating the four feudal Crusader States established in the Levant.
- **Saladin**: First Sultan of Egypt and Syria who united Muslim forces and recaptured Jerusalem in 1187.
- **Sack of Constantinople (1204)**: Catastrophic event during the Fourth Crusade where Western crusaders assaulted and looted the Byzantine capital.
