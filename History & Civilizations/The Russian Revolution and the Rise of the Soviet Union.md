# The Russian Revolution and the Rise of the Soviet Union

## Overview
The Russian Revolution of 1917 dismantled the 300-year Romanov autocracy and established the Soviet Union, the world's first socialist state.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
February & October Revolutions: Exhausted by military disaster in World War I, food riots forced Tsar Nicholas II to abdicate in the February 1917 Revolution. The subsequent liberal Provisional Government continued the unpopular war, creating dual power with the Petrograd Soviet. In October 1917, Vladimir Lenin's Bolsheviks launched an armed insurrection under the slogan 'Peace, Land, and Bread', seizing power.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Civil War & The USSR: The Bolshevik Red Army defeated counter-revolutionary White forces in a brutal civil war (1917–1922). Lenin established the Union of Soviet Socialist Republics (USSR) in 1922, transitioning from War Communism to the market-tolerant New Economic Policy (NEP) before Joseph Stalin seized power, enforcing forced agricultural collectivization and rapid five-year industrialization plans.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Global rise of Marxist-Leninist communist movements, bipolar ideological division of the 20th century.

## Key Terminology
- **Bolsheviks**: Radical Marxist faction led by Vladimir Lenin that seized state power during the October 1917 Revolution.
- **Petrograd Soviet**: Council of workers' and soldiers' deputies exercising dual executive authority in Russia in 1917.
- **Collectivization**: Stalinist policy forcibly consolidating individual peasant agricultural farms into large state-controlled collective farms.
