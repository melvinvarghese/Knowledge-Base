# Indus Valley Civilization - Harappa and Mohenjo-daro Urban Planning

## Overview
The Indus Valley Civilization (c. 2600–1900 BCE) was a Bronze Age civilization across modern Pakistan and northwest India celebrated for unprecedented urban engineering and sanitation systems.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Grid Urban Planning & Architecture: Major metropolitan centers (Mohenjo-daro, Harappa, Dholavira) were laid out on precise cardinal grid plans with fired mud-brick architecture of standardized dimensional ratios (1:2:4). Cities featured distinct fortified citadels and lower residential sectors.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Advanced Hydraulic Engineering: Possessed the ancient world's most sophisticated civic water management: private household bathrooms connected to covered street drainage sewers, public soak pits, and monumental structures like the Great Bath of Mohenjo-daro.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Early development of standardized weights and measures, maritime trade networks with Mesopotamia (Meluhha).

## Key Terminology
- **Indus Valley Civilization**: Bronze Age civilization in the Indus river basin renowned for standardized urban planning.
- **Mohenjo-daro**: Major Indus metropolis featuring the Great Bath and advanced municipal drainage networks.
- **Standardized Brick Ratio**: Rigid architectural brick manufacturing ratio of 1:2:4 widespread across all Indus settlements.
