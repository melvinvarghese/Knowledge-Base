# The Haitian Revolution - Toussaint Louverture and the Only Successful Slave Revolt

## Overview
The Haitian Revolution (1791–1804) in Saint-Domingue was the only successful revolt by enslaved people in human history, establishing the world's first Black republic.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Uprising in the Sugar Colony: Saint-Domingue was the richest colony on earth, producing 40% of the world's sugar through brutal chattel slavery. In August 1791, a coordinated insurrection erupted following a Vodou ceremony at Bois Caïman led by Dutty Boukman.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Toussaint Louverture & Independence: Former slave Toussaint Louverture organized rebel forces into a disciplined army, defeating Spanish, British, and French colonial forces. Following Napoleon's attempt to reinstate slavery (1802) and Louverture's capture, Jean-Jacques Dessalines defeated French general Rochambeau at the Battle of Vertières, declaring independent Haiti on January 1, 1804.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Shattered notions of white racial supremacy, terrified Atlantic slaveholders, forced Napoleon to sell the Louisiana Territory to the United States.

## Key Terminology
- **Toussaint Louverture**: Brilliant military general and former slave who led the Haitian Revolution toward autonomy and emancipation.
- **Bois Caïman**: Religious Vodou ceremony in August 1791 that catalyzed the mass slave insurrection across Saint-Domingue.
- **Battle of Vertières**: Decisive 1803 battle where Haitian revolutionary forces crushed Napoleon's expeditionary army, securing independence.
