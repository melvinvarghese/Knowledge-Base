# The British Empire - The East India Company and the Raj

## Overview
The British Empire became the largest empire in human history ('the empire on which the sun never sets'), pivoting upon its imperial dominion over the Indian subcontinent.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Corporate Rule of the English East India Company (EIC): Founded in 1600 as a joint-stock mercantile venture, the EIC acquired territorial control following the Battle of Plassey (1757) and the grant of the *Diwani* (tax revenue rights) of Bengal, commanding a private mercenary army of over 250,000 native Indian soldiers (*sepoys*).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The 1857 Rebellion & The British Raj: Greased cartridge grievances triggered the widespread Indian Rebellion of 1857. In 1858, the British Parliament abolished the EIC and transferred direct imperial governance to the British Crown, establishing the British Raj under a Viceroy until partition and independence in 1947.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Global diffusion of the English language, common law jurisprudence, parliamentary systems, and railway infrastructure, offset by devastating famines and deindustrialization.

## Key Terminology
- **East India Company (EIC)**: British chartered joint-stock trading enterprise that achieved corporate military rule over India.
- **Sepoy Mutiny (1857)**: Major rebellion across northern and central India against British East India Company rule.
- **British Raj**: Period of direct British Crown governance over the Indian subcontinent from 1858 to 1947.
