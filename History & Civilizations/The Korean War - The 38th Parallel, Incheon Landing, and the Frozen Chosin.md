# The Korean War - The 38th Parallel, Incheon Landing, and the Frozen Chosin

## Overview
The Korean War (1950–1953) was the first major military armed conflict of the Cold War, solidifying the ideological division of the Korean peninsula along the 38th Parallel.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Invasion & The Incheon Amphibious Landing: On June 25, 1950, Kim Il-sung's North Korean forces invaded South Korea, pushing UN/US forces into the Pusan Perimeter. General Douglas MacArthur orchestrated the daring amphibious landing at Incheon behind enemy lines, recapturing Seoul and routing North Korean armies back toward the Yalu River.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Chinese Intervention & The Frozen Chosin: In late 1950, Chairman Mao Zedong dispatched hundreds of thousands of Chinese People's Volunteer Army troops across the Yalu River in surprise mass night attacks. At the Battle of Chosin Reservoir, surrounded US Marines fought through sub-zero blizzards to execute a breakout to the sea.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Armistice agreement of July 1953 creating the 4-kilometer-wide Korean Demilitarized Zone (DMZ), establishing the ongoing permanent division between democratic South Korea and authoritarian North Korea.

## Key Terminology
- **38th Parallel**: Latitude line chosen in 1945 as the boundary dividing the Soviet and American occupation zones in Korea.
- **Incheon Landing**: Amphibious invasion and battle of the Korean War resulting in a decisive victory and strategic reversal in favor of the UN.
- **Korean Demilitarized Zone (DMZ)**: Heavily fortified border barrier dividing the Korean Peninsula roughly along the 38th parallel.
