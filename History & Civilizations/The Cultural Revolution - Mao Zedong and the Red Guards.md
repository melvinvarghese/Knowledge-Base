# The Cultural Revolution - Mao Zedong and the Red Guards

## Overview
The Great Proletarian Cultural Revolution (1966–1976) was a sociopolitical campaign launched by Mao Zedong to purge 'revisionist' elements from the Chinese Communist Party and reassert ideological orthodoxy.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Mobilization of the Red Guards & The Four Olds: Mao bypassed the formal party apparatus, rallying millions of radicalized high school and university students into 'Red Guards' clutching Mao's *Little Red Book*. Red Guards attacked party cadres, intellectuals, and religious institutions, purging the 'Four Olds': Old Customs, Old Culture, Old Habits, and Old Ideas.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Anarchy & Economic Paralysis: Factional fighting between Red Guard groups escalated into armed street warfare, forcing the People's Liberation Army under Lin Biao to intervene. Intellectuals and urban youths were sent down to the countryside (*Xiaxiang*) for forced agrarian re-education. Tens of millions suffered persecution, humiliation, or death.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Paralysis of Chinese higher education, economic regression, and the subsequent rise of pragmatic reformer Deng Xiaoping.

## Key Terminology
- **Red Guards**: Militant youth movement mobilized by Mao Zedong in 1966 to root out capitalist roaders and revisionists.
- **Little Red Book**: Quotations from Chairman Mao Zedong, widely printed and carried as a compulsory ideological symbol during the Cultural Revolution.
- **Deng Xiaoping**: Pragmatic CCP leader who was purged during the Cultural Revolution and later orchestrated China's post-Mao economic reforms.
