# The Ottoman Empire - Mehmed II, Suleiman the Magnificent, and the Devshirme

## Overview
The Ottoman Empire (1299–1922) stood as the preeminent geopolitical and military power at the crossroads of Europe, Asia, and Africa for six centuries.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Conquest of Constantinople (1453): Sultan Mehmed II ('The Conqueror') deployed massive bronze siege cannons and rolled warships overland into the Golden Horn to conquer Byzantine Constantinople, rebranding it as Kostantiniyye (Istanbul) and declaring himself Roman Caesar (*Kayser-i Rûm*).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Administrative Institutions & Suleiman's Zenith: Reached its imperial peak under Suleiman I ('The Magnificent' or 'The Lawgiver', r. 1520–1566), who harmonized secular statutory law (*kanun*) with Islamic Sharia. The *Devshirme* system conscripted Christian youth to be educated as imperial administrators and elite Janissary infantry; the *Millet* system granted autonomous legal jurisdiction to religious minorities (Jews, Greek Orthodox, Armenians).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Dominance of Mediterranean and Black Sea trade, architectural masterpieces of Mimar Sinan (Süleymaniye Mosque).

## Key Terminology
- **Janissaries**: Elite Ottoman infantry corps recruited through the devshirme system, serving as the Sultan's household troops.
- **Devshirme**: Ottoman practice of conscripting non-Muslim youth into imperial administrative and military service.
- **Millet System**: Autonomous self-governing communal framework allowing religious minorities to administer their own internal laws.
