# The Unification of Italy - Cavour, Garibaldi, and the Risorgimento

## Overview
The Risorgimento ('Resurgence') was the 19th-century political and social movement that consolidated disparate states of the Italian peninsula into the single Kingdom of Italy.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Diplomacy & Guerrilla Warfare: Camillo Benso, Count of Cavour, prime minister of the Kingdom of Piedmont-Sardinia, deployed pragmatic diplomacy and an alliance with Napoleon III of France to defeat the Austrian Empire in 1859. Concurrently, revolutionary Giuseppe Garibaldi launched the 'Expedition of the Thousand' (the Redshirts) in 1860, conquering the Kingdom of the Two Sicilies.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Proclamation of the Kingdom & The Roman Question: In 1861, the Kingdom of Italy was proclaimed under King Victor Emmanuel II. Rome was annexed in 1870 following the withdrawal of the French garrison during the Franco-Prussian War, ending over a millennium of papal temporal rule.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Transformation of southern European geopolitics, model of 19th-century liberal nationalism.

## Key Terminology
- **Risorgimento**: 19th-century political movement that unified the fractured states of the Italian peninsula into modern Italy.
- **Giuseppe Garibaldi**: Italian general, patriot, and republican whose military campaigns in the south led directly to Italian unification.
- **Count Cavour**: Piedmontese statesman whose diplomatic alliances orchestrated the political framework of Italian unification.
