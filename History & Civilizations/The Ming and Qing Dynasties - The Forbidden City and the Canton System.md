# The Ming and Qing Dynasties - The Forbidden City and the Canton System

## Overview
The Ming (1368–1644) and Qing (1644–1912) dynasties represented the final two imperial eras of China, marked by architectural grandeur, demographic growth, and rising Western contact.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Ming Restoration & Zheng He's Treasure Fleets: The Ming overthrew the Mongol Yuan dynasty, rebuilding the stone Great Wall and constructing the monumental imperial palace—the Forbidden City in Beijing. Admiral Zheng He led seven epic naval expeditions with colossal multi-decked treasure ships across the Indian Ocean to Arabia and East Africa (1405–1433) before the court halted maritime exploration.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Manchu Qing Dynasty & The Canton System: The Jurchen/Manchu established the Qing dynasty, expanding imperial borders to include Tibet, Xinjiang, and Mongolia. The court restricted all Western maritime trade to Guangzhou (Canton) under the strict Canton System, requiring foreign merchants to trade exclusively with authorized Chinese merchant guilds (*Cohong*).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Massive silver inflows into China from New World Spanish mines, trade imbalances that eventually prompted the British to introduce illicit opium.

## Key Terminology
- **Forbidden City**: Imperial palace complex in Beijing serving as the political and ceremonial seat of Chinese emperors from 1420 to 1912.
- **Zheng He**: Ming admiral and diplomat who commanded seven colossal naval treasure fleets across the Indian Ocean.
- **Canton System**: Strict trade policy from 1757 to 1842 restricting Western maritime commerce exclusively to the southern port of Canton.
