# The Sasanian Empire - Zoroastrian Statecraft and Roman Rivalry

## Overview
The Sasanian Empire (224–651 CE) was the last pre-Islamic Persian empire, serving as a global imperial superpower and cultural peer rival to the Roman and Byzantine Empires.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Zoroastrian State Orthodoxy & Royal Ideology: Founded by Ardashir I, the Sasanians legitimized their rule through orthodox Zoroastrianism, with fire temples (*Atash Behram*) maintained across the empire. The Shahanshah ('King of Kings') governed an administrative bureaucracy supported by heavy armored cataphract cavalry.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Byzantine-Sasanian Wars: Under Khosrow I ('Anushirvan the Just') and Khosrow II, Sasanians built the monumental vaulted palace of Ctesiphon (Taq Kasra) and contested Mesopotamia, Armenia, and Syria. The climactic 602–628 war with Byzantine Emperor Heraclius exhausted both empires, leaving them vulnerable to the rapid 7th-century Arab-Muslim conquests.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Architectural mastery of parabolic masonry vaults, major influence on Islamic art, administration, and chivalric codes.

## Key Terminology
- **Shahanshah**: Ancient Persian royal title meaning 'King of Kings' representing universal imperial sovereignty.
- **Cataphract**: Heavily armored cavalry soldier where both rider and horse were clad in metal scale or chainmail armor.
- **Taq Kasra**: Monumental Sasanian brick palace vault in Ctesiphon, the largest single-span unreinforced brick vault in the world.
