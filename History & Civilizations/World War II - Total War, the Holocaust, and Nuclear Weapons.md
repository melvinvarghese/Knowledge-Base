# World War II - Total War, the Holocaust, and Nuclear Weapons

## Overview
World War II (1939–1945) was the deadliest conflict in human history, resulting in 70–85 million fatalities, the systematic Nazi Holocaust, and the dawn of the nuclear era.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Global Theaters & Total War Mobilization: Triggered by Nazi Germany's invasion of Poland in 1939 and Imperial Japan's expansion across Asia, pitting the Allied powers (USA, USSR, UK, China) against the Axis powers (Germany, Japan, Italy). Turning points at Stalingrad (1942–1943), Midway (1942), and the Normandy D-Day landings (1944) crushed Axis militaries through industrial mobilization.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Holocaust & The Atomic Bomb: Nazi Germany industrialized mass murder, systematically exterminating 6 million European Jews and millions of others in concentration and death camps. The war culminated in August 1945 with the U.S. atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki, inaugurating the nuclear age.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Creation of the United Nations (UN), Nuremberg war crimes principles, collapse of European global colonial empires, rise of American-Soviet superpowers.

## Key Terminology
- **The Holocaust**: State-sponsored, systematic genocide of six million European Jews by the Nazi regime and its collaborators.
- **Total War**: Military conflict where belligerents engage in complete civilian and industrial economic mobilization.
- **Manhattan Project**: Secret Allied research and development project that produced the first operational atomic weapons.
