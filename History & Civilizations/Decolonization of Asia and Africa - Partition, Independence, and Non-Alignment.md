# Decolonization of Asia and Africa - Partition, Independence, and Non-Alignment

## Overview
Decolonization (1945–1975) dismantled centuries-old European colonial empires across Asia and Africa, redrawing the political map of the world and birthing dozens of sovereign independent nations.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Indian Independence & Partition (1947): Mahatma Gandhi's mass non-violent civil disobedience (*Satyagraha*) and the Indian National Congress forced British withdrawal. However, communal tensions led to the catastrophic Partition of British India into India and Pakistan, displacing 15 million people and killing over one million.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
African Liberation & The Non-Aligned Movement: Independence swept Africa through peaceful transitions (Ghana under Kwame Nkrumah in 1957) and violent liberation wars (Algerian War of Independence, Kenyan Mau Mau rebellion). Leaders formed the Non-Aligned Movement (Bandung Conference, 1955) to resist being subordinated as satellites of either the U.S. or Soviet blocs during the Cold War.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Universalization of nation-state sovereignty, creation of post-colonial international development paradigms, linguistic and cultural reclamation.

## Key Terminology
- **Decolonization**: Historical process whereby colonial powers relinquished political control over dependent foreign territories.
- **Partition of India**: 1947 division of British India into two independent dominions: Hindu-majority India and Muslim-majority Pakistan.
- **Non-Aligned Movement**: Forum of developing states not formally aligned with or against any major Cold War superpower bloc.
