# The Marshall Plan and the Post-War Economic Reconstruction of Europe

## Overview
The Marshall Plan (officially the European Recovery Program, 1948–1951) was an American foreign assistance initiative that provided over $13 billion to rebuild war-torn Western European economies.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Strategic Rationale & Implementation: Announced by US Secretary of State George C. Marshall, the plan aimed to prevent hunger, poverty, and despair from driving Western European democracies (particularly France and Italy) into the sphere of Soviet communist influence. Aid was distributed as direct grants and commodities (food, fuel, industrial machinery), requiring European nations to coordinate cross-border economic planning through the Organization for European Economic Co-operation (OEEC).

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Economic Miracle (*Wirtschaftswunder*): Marshall Plan aid catalyzed rapid industrial modernization, removed internal trade barriers, and restored financial liquidity, sparking unprecedented GDP growth across Western Europe.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Divided Europe into institutional capitalist and communist blocs (Stalin rejected aid for Eastern Europe, creating COMECON), institutional precursor to European economic integration.

## Key Terminology
- **Marshall Plan**: American initiative passed in 1948 providing foreign aid to Western Europe to foster economic recovery after World War II.
- **OEEC**: Organization for European Economic Co-operation, established in 1948 to administer American and Canadian Marshall Plan aid.
- **Wirtschaftswunder**: German term ('Economic Miracle') describing the rapid post-WWII reconstruction and industrial growth of West Germany.
