# The Industrial Revolution - Steam, Factories, and Urbanization

## Overview
The Industrial Revolution (c. 1760–1840) transformed agrarian human societies into mechanized industrial powerhouses through fossil-fueled steam engines and factory systems.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Steam Engine & Textile Mechanization: Originated in Britain due to rich coal deposits, patent protection, and naval trade access. James Watt's improved condenser steam engine (1769) decoupled manufacturing from seasonal water power. Mechanized spinning jennies and power looms drove mass textile production.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Urbanization & Social Transformation: Populations migrated from rural villages into densely packed industrial factory cities (Manchester, Birmingham), creating an urban industrial proletariat. Harsh working conditions, child labor, and extreme inequality birthed labor unions, public health sanitation acts, and Karl Marx's critique of industrial capitalism.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Shift to fossil-energy economic growth, global transport networks (railways, steamships), rise of industrial working class movements.

## Key Terminology
- **Industrial Revolution**: Transition from manual artisanal production to mechanized factory manufacturing powered by steam and fossil fuels.
- **James Watt Steam Engine**: Efficient steam engine with separate condenser that provided continuous rotational mechanical power to factories.
- **Proletariat**: Industrial working class possessing no capital of their own and selling their labor power for wages.
