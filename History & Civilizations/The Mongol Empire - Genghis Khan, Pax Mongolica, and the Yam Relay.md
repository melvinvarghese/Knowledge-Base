# The Mongol Empire - Genghis Khan, Pax Mongolica, and the Yam Relay

## Overview
The Mongol Empire (13th–14th centuries), forged by Genghis Khan, became the largest contiguous land empire in human history, stretching from the Sea of Japan to Central Europe.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Military Meritocracy & Organization: Temüjin (Genghis Khan) united the nomadic steppe tribes in 1206, dismantling tribal aristocracies and structuring armies into decimal units (*arban*, *jagun*, *mingghan*, *tumen* of 10,000). Master horse archery, siege engineering assimilated from Chinese and Persian engineers, and psychological warfare produced rapid conquests.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Pax Mongolica & The Yam Communication Relay: The Mongol peace created unprecedented transcontinental trade, protected by the *Yassa* legal code. The *Yam* postal relay network operated thousands of relay stations with horses, allowing riders bearing bronze passports (*paiza*) to cross Eurasia in weeks.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Global commercial integration, trans-Eurasian exchange of gunpowder and printing, but facilitated the spread of the Black Death.

## Key Terminology
- **Pax Mongolica**: Period of transcontinental peace and security across Eurasia under Mongol imperial administration.
- **Yam**: Imperial horse messenger relay service operating across the vast Mongol Empire.
- **Genghis Khan**: Founder and first Khagan of the Mongol Empire who unified the nomadic steppe tribes.
