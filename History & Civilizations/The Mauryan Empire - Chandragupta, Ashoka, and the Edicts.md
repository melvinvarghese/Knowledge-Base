# The Mauryan Empire - Chandragupta, Ashoka, and the Edicts

## Overview
The Mauryan Empire (322–185 BCE) was the first pan-Indian subcontinent empire, reaching its ethical and philosophical peak under Emperor Ashoka.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Founding & Kautilya's Arthashastra: Founded by Chandragupta Maurya with his advisor Chanakya (Kautilya), author of the *Arthashastra*—a pioneering treatise on statecraft, espionage, economic management, and political realism preceding Machiavelli by millennia.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Ashoka the Great & The Kalinga War: Following the horrific bloodshed of the Kalinga War (c. 261 BCE), Ashoka underwent a profound remorse-driven conversion to Buddhism. He renounced military conquest (*digvijaya*) for moral victory (*dharmavijaya*), carving the Edicts of Ashoka across rock faces and polished sandstone pillars advocating religious tolerance, animal welfare, and non-violence (*ahimsa*).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Spread of Buddhism across East Asia and Sri Lanka, the Lion Capital of Ashoka adopted as the modern national emblem of India.

## Key Terminology
- **Arthashastra**: Ancient Indian Sanskrit treatise on statecraft, economic policy, and military strategy attributed to Kautilya.
- **Edicts of Ashoka**: Collection of 33 inscriptions on pillars, boulders, and cave walls proclaiming Ashoka's moral and humanitarian governance.
- **Dharma (Dhamma)**: Moral duty, righteousness, and cosmic law championed by Ashoka as the ethical basis of state governance.
