# The Suez Crisis of 1956 and the Decline of British Imperial Hegemony

## Overview
The 1956 Suez Crisis was a pivotal geopolitical confrontation in the Middle East that marked the definitive end of Britain and France as front-rank global imperial superpowers.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Nationalization & The Sèvres Collusion: In July 1956, Egyptian President Gamal Abdel Nasser nationalized the strategic Suez Canal Company to finance the Aswan High Dam following Western funding withdrawal. In response, Britain, France, and Israel secretly met at Sèvres, concocting a pretext: Israel would invade the Sinai Peninsula, and Britain and France would intervene as 'peacekeepers' to seize control of the canal.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Superpower Ultimatum & Imperial Humiliation: President Dwight D. Eisenhower was outraged by the uncoordinated colonial aggression during the ongoing Hungarian Revolution. The US threatened to crash the British pound sterling through IMF debt manipulation, while the Soviet Union threatened rocket strikes on London and Paris. Humiliated, Britain and France withdrew their troops.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Demonstrated that European colonial powers could no longer act independently of American geopolitical consent; elevated Nasser to pan-Arab hero.

## Key Terminology
- **Suez Canal**: Artificial sea-level waterway in Egypt connecting the Mediterranean Sea to the Red Sea, nationalized in 1956.
- **Protocol of Sèvres**: Secret diplomatic agreement between France, the United Kingdom, and Israel to coordinate military action against Egypt.
- **Pan-Arabism**: Ideology espousing the unification of the countries of North Africa and Western Asia from the Atlantic Ocean to the Arabian Sea.
