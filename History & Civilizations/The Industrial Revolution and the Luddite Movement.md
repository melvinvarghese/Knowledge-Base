# The Industrial Revolution and the Luddite Movement

## Overview
The Luddite uprising (1811–1816) was a militant labor protest movement by English textile artisans who organized nocturnal raids to smash automated industrial machinery.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Grievance of Skilled Artisans: Popularly mischaracterized as mindless technophobia, the Luddites (named after mythical leader General Ned Ludd) were highly skilled hand-loom weavers and cloth dressers. Their grievance was not the machines per se, but the 'fraudulent and deceitful manner' in which factory capitalists used automated wide-frame looms to produce shoddy goods, slash wages, eliminate apprentice protections, and force workers into dangerous factory wage-labor.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
State Suppression & The Frame Breaking Act: Luddites organized secret oath-bound societies, launching targeted nocturnal hammer raids against factories across Nottinghamshire and Yorkshire. The British government responded ruthlessly: deploying 12,000 soldiers (more troops than Wellington had in the Iberian Peninsula), passing the 1812 Frame Breaking Act making machine-smashing a capital crime punishable by hanging, and exiling leaders to Australian penal colonies.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Pioneering historical milestone in industrial working-class resistance, modern debates over technological unemployment and automation.

## Key Terminology
- **Luddite**: 19th-century English textile worker who destroyed machinery in protest against wage cuts and de-skilling of labor.
- **Frame Breaking Act 1812**: Act of the British Parliament that made the destruction of mechanized industrial stocking frames a capital offense.
- **Artisanal Guild**: Traditional trade association of craftsmen controlling the practice of their craft and apprentice wages in an area.
