# The Peace of Westphalia and the Birth of the Sovereign Nation-State

## Overview
The Peace of Westphalia (1648) ended the Thirty Years' War, establishing the foundational principles of modern international law and Westphalian state sovereignty.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Thirty Years' War (1618–1648): A catastrophic conflict primarily fought in the Holy Roman Empire, blending religious tensions with geopolitical power struggles (Bourbon France vs Habsburg Austria/Spain) that killed an estimated 20–40% of the German population.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Westphalian Paradigm: The treaties of Münster and Osnabrück recognized that each sovereign ruler holds absolute territorial authority (*cuius regio, eius religio*—whose realm, his religion) and established the doctrine of non-intervention in internal domestic affairs between sovereign states, replacing medieval papal and imperial hegemony with a balance of power among sovereign equals.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Foundation of the modern international system, diplomatic immunity protocols, legal sovereignty of nation-states.

## Key Terminology
- **Peace of Westphalia**: Pair of peace treaties signed in 1648 establishing the framework for modern sovereign nation-states.
- **Westphalian Sovereignty**: Principle in international law that each nation-state has exclusive sovereignty over its territory.
- **Thirty Years' War**: Devastating pan-European conflict fought over religion and continental geopolitical hegemony.
