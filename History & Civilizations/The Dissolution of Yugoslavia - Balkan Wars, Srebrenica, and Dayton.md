# The Dissolution of Yugoslavia - Balkan Wars, Srebrenica, and Dayton

## Overview
The breakup of the Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia in the 1990s triggered the deadliest military conflicts on European soil since World War II.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Nationalist Fracturing: Following the death of long-serving leader Josip Broz Tito in 1980 and the collapse of communism, Serbian leader Slobodan Milošević exploited ethnic nationalism. Slovenia and Croatia declared independence in 1991, followed by multi-ethnic Bosnia and Herzegovina in 1992, sparking violent war.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Ethnic Cleansing & The Srebrenica Genocide: Bosnian Serb forces backed by Belgrade engaged in systemic 'ethnic cleansing' of Bosniak Muslims and Croats. The nearly four-year Siege of Sarajevo terrorized civilians; in July 1995, Bosnian Serb forces overran the UN-declared 'safe area' of Srebrenica, methodically massacring over 8,000 Bosniak men and boys.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
NATO military intervention, signing of the 1995 Dayton Accords creating a fractured bi-entity Bosnian state, trials at the International Criminal Tribunal for the former Yugoslavia (ICTY).

## Key Terminology
- **Srebrenica Massacre**: July 1995 genocide of more than 8,000 Bosniak Muslim men and boys by the Army of Republika Srpska.
- **Dayton Accords**: 1995 peace agreement negotiated in Dayton, Ohio ending the Bosnian War and establishing a power-sharing state.
- **ICTY**: International Criminal Tribunal for the former Yugoslavia established by the UN to prosecute war crimes.
