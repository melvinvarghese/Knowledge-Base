# The Cuban Revolution - Fidel Castro, Che Guevara, and the 26th of July Movement

## Overview
The Cuban Revolution (1953–1959) was an armed political revolution that overthrew the US-backed authoritarian regime of Fulgencio Batista, establishing a socialist state 90 miles from Florida.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Moncada, Granma, & Sierra Maestra Guerrilla Warfare: Began on July 26, 1953, with a failed assault on the Moncada Barracks, where Fidel Castro delivered his famous defense speech: 'History will absolve me.' In December 1956, Castro, his brother Raúl, and Argentine doctor Ernesto 'Che' Guevara landed from the yacht *Granma* in eastern Cuba, waging asymmetric guerrilla warfare from the rugged Sierra Maestra mountains.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Triumph & Geopolitical Realignment: Following Che Guevara's decisive victory at the Battle of Santa Clara, Batista fled on New Year's Eve 1958. Castro entered Havana, nationalized American agricultural plantations and oil refineries, and aligned Cuba with the Soviet Union following the failed CIA-sponsored Bay of Pigs invasion in 1961.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Global focal point of the Cold War, model for Latin American guerrilla liberation movements (Foco theory), decades of US trade embargo.

## Key Terminology
- **26th of July Movement**: Revolutionary movement created and led by Fidel Castro that overthrew the Fulgencio Batista dictatorship in Cuba.
- **Foco Theory**: Revolutionary military doctrine formalizing guerrilla warfare as a catalyst for mass popular revolution.
- **Bay of Pigs**: Failed 1961 military invasion of Cuba undertaken by a CIA-sponsored paramilitary group of Cuban exiles.
