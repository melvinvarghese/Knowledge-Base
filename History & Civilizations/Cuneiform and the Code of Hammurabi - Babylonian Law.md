# Cuneiform and the Code of Hammurabi - Babylonian Law

## Overview
The Code of Hammurabi (c. 1755 BCE) is one of the oldest deciphered legal codes in human history, inscribed on a diorite stele in Babylonian cuneiform.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Origins in Mesopotamia: King Hammurabi of the First Babylonian Dynasty unified Mesopotamia through conquest and administrative centralization, commissioning the 2.25-meter basalt stele depicting Hammurabi receiving laws directly from Shamash, the god of justice.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Lex Talionis & Social Hierarchy: The code codifies 282 legal rulings covering property rights, commerce, and violent crimes under the principle of *lex talionis* ('an eye for an eye'). However, penalties were strictly stratified by social class: offenses against patricians (*awilu*) resulted in physical retribution, while offenses against commoners (*mushkenu*) or enslaved people (*wardu*) incurred financial fines.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Foundational milestone in the codification of statutory law, presumption of evidence, and contract formalization.

## Key Terminology
- **Code of Hammurabi**: Babylonian legal text inscribed c. 1755 BCE comprising 282 statutory decrees.
- **Cuneiform**: Wedge-shaped script developed in ancient Sumer, imprinted with reed styluses on clay tablets.
- **Lex Talionis**: Law of retaliation whereby punishment corresponds in kind and degree to the crime committed.
