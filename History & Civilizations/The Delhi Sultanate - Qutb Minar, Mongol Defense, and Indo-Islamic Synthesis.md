# The Delhi Sultanate - Qutb Minar, Mongol Defense, and Indo-Islamic Synthesis

## Overview
The Delhi Sultanate (1206–1526) was an Islamic empire spanning five successive dynasties (Mamluk, Khalji, Tughlaq, Sayyid, Lodi) that ruled northern India from Delhi.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Founding & Architecture: Founded by Qutb ud-Din Aibak, the Sultanate synthesized Persian and Indian architectural traditions, exemplified by the monumental 73-meter brick Qutb Minar and Alai Darwaza in Delhi.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Mongol Repulsion & Khalji Reforms: Under Alauddin Khalji (r. 1296–1316), the Sultanate successfully repelled multiple massive Mongol invasions through aggressive military fortification, market price controls, and agrarian revenue reform, protecting India from the devastation suffered by Persia and Mesopotamia.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Emergence of Hindustani music, evolution of the Urdu language, introduction of mechanical innovations (the spinning wheel, paper technology).

## Key Terminology
- **Qutb Minar**: 73-meter victory minaret in Delhi founded in 1199, an iconic exemplar of early Indo-Islamic architecture.
- **Alauddin Khalji**: Powerful Sultan of Delhi who instituted rigorous market reforms and successfully defeated successive Mongol invasions.
- **Jizya**: Per-capita tax historically levied on non-Muslim subjects (*dhimmis*) residing in Muslim lands under Islamic law.
