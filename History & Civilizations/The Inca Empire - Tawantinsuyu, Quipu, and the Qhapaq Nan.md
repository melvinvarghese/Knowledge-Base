# The Inca Empire - Tawantinsuyu, Quipu, and the Qhapaq Nan

## Overview
The Inca Empire (*Tawantinsuyu*, 'The Four United Regions') was the largest empire in pre-Columbian America, spanning 4,000 kilometers along the spine of the Andes.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Mountain Engineering & The Qhapaq Ñan: Engineered 40,000 km of paved roads across vertical Andean terrain, featuring stone-paved stairs, tunnels cut through granite mountains, and woven grass suspension bridges (*q'eswachaka*). Imperial runners (*chasquis*) carried messages hundreds of kilometers per day.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Quipu Data Accounting & The Mita System: Without an alphabetic script, the Inca recorded census data, taxes, and grain reserves using *quipu*—complex knotted colored cords encoding decimal numbers. Economic production operated without money or commercial markets, structured through reciprocal community labor obligations (*mita*) and imperial state storehouses.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Precision earthquake-resistant cyclopean stone architecture (Machu Picchu, Sacsayhuamán), agricultural mountain terrace farming.

## Key Terminology
- **Quipu**: Andean recording device made of knotted strings used to record statistical, fiscal, and narrative information.
- **Mita**: Mandatory public labor service tribute required of subjects in the Inca Empire to construct roads, temples, and terraces.
- **Qhapaq Ñan**: Vast Inca road network spanning over 40,000 kilometers facilitating communication and imperial troop mobilization.
