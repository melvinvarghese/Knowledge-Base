# The Columbian Exchange - Biological and Agricultural Global Integration

## Overview
The Columbian Exchange was the massive, unprecedented transfer of plants, animals, culture, populations, and communicable diseases between the Old and New Worlds following 1492.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Demographic Catastrophe: Indigenous American populations had lived isolated from Old World zoonotic diseases for millennia and lacked acquired immunity. Smallpox, measles, typhus, and influenza decimated indigenous populations by an estimated 80–90% within a century, causing the largest demographic collapse in human history.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Agricultural Revolution & Flora Transfers: New World staple crops—potatoes, maize, cassava, sweet potatoes, tomatoes, chilies—transformed Old World agriculture, dramatically increasing caloric yields and powering population booms in Europe, Africa, and China. In exchange, the Old World introduced horses, cattle, pigs, sheep, wheat, sugar cane, and coffee to the Americas.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Ecological reorganization of global biomes, fuel for the transatlantic slave trade driven by plantation sugar monoculture.

## Key Terminology
- **Columbian Exchange**: Transcontinental exchange of crops, pathogens, animals, and human populations between Afro-Eurasia and the Americas.
- **Zoonotic Disease**: Infectious disease transmitted from non-human animals to humans, causing fatal virgin-soil epidemics in the Americas.
- **Monoculture Plantation**: Agricultural economic model cultivating a single export crop (e.g., sugar, tobacco) utilizing coerced labor.
