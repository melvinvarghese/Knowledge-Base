# The Battle of Stalingrad - Urban Attrition and the Turning Point of WWII

## Overview
The Battle of Stalingrad (August 1942 – February 1943) was the bloodiest single battle in human military history, resulting in nearly two million casualties and permanently turning the tide of World War II against Nazi Germany.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Rattenkrieg & Close-Quarters Combat: The German Sixth Army under General Friedrich Paulus pushed into Stalingrad on the Volga River. The Soviet 62nd Army under General Vasily Chuikov adopted the tactic of 'hugging the enemy'—fighting at point-blank range among city ruins, basements, and factories (the Red October and Tractor factories), neutralizing the German Luftwaffe's aerial superiority.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Operation Uranus & The Encirclement: On November 19, 1942, Soviet Generals Georgy Zhukov and Aleksandr Vasilevsky launched **Operation Uranus**—a massive pincer counter-offensive that shattered vulnerable Romanian and Italian flank armies, encircling 300,000 German soldiers in a pocket (*Kessel*). In February 1943, Paulus surrendered, shattering the myth of German Wehrmacht invincibility.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Strategic turning point on the Eastern Front, initiating the Soviet Red Army's unbroken westward advance toward Berlin.

## Key Terminology
- **Operation Uranus**: Soviet strategic pincer counter-offensive in November 1942 that encircled the German Sixth Army at Stalingrad.
- **Rattenkrieg**: German military slang ('Rat War') describing the brutal, room-to-room urban combat fought in the ruins of Stalingrad.
- **Friedrich Paulus**: Field Marshal commanding the German Sixth Army who was captured at Stalingrad following Hitler's refusal to permit retreat.
