# The Punic Wars - Rome vs Carthage and Hannibal Barca

## Overview
The three Punic Wars (264–146 BCE) between the Roman Republic and the maritime Carthaginian Empire determined mastery of the Mediterranean world.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
First Punic War & The Corvus: Rome built its first major fleet to contest Carthage's maritime empire in Sicily, inventing the *corvus* (boarding bridge) to turn naval engagements into infantry clashes.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Second Punic War & Hannibal's Genius (218–201 BCE): Carthaginian general Hannibal Barca led an army with war elephants across the Alps into Italy, executing history's classic double-envelopment annihilation of eight Roman legions at Cannae (216 BCE). Rome survived via Quintus Fabius Maximus's attrition strategy until Scipio Africanus defeated Hannibal at Zama in 202 BCE.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Third Punic War (149–146 BCE): Ended with the total destruction of Carthage, solidifying Rome's unchallenged hegemony over the Mediterranean (*Mare Nostrum*).

## Key Terminology
- **Battle of Cannae**: Tactical masterpiece in 216 BCE where Hannibal's outnumbered army encircled and destroyed the Roman legions.
- **Fabian Strategy**: Military strategy of avoiding decisive pitched battles in favor of wearing down an opponent through attrition and skirmishes.
- **Scipio Africanus**: Roman general and statesman who defeated Hannibal Barca at the Battle of Zama in 202 BCE.
