# The Protestant Reformation and the German Peasants' War

## Overview
The German Peasants' War (1524–1525) was Europe's largest mass popular uprising prior to the French Revolution, blending Protestant religious reform with radical peasant economic demands.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Twelve Articles of the Black Forest: In 1525, peasant confederations published the *Twelve Articles*—the first modern statement of human and economic rights in Europe. Drawing on Luther's 'priesthood of all believers', peasants demanded the abolition of serfdom, reduction of feudal labor tributes, election of local pastors, and restoration of communal fishing and forestry rights.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Thomas Müntzer & Luther's Harsh Condemnation: Radical theologian Thomas Müntzer led peasant armies under an apocalyptic banner of Christian communism (*omnia sunt communia*—all things in common). Shocked by social anarchy, Martin Luther vehemently rejected social revolution, publishing *Against the Murderous, Thieving Hordes of Peasants*, urging princes to violently crush the revolt.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Decisive defeat of the peasant armies at Frankenhausen, cementing aristocratic control over the Lutheran Reformation.

## Key Terminology
- **German Peasants' War**: Mass popular social uprising in 1524–1525 across German-speaking Central Europe inspired by Protestant theology.
- **Twelve Articles**: Peasant manifesto articulating economic, labor, and religious grievances based on Scripture in 1525.
- **Thomas Müntzer**: Radical German Protestant reformer who championed peasant revolution and communal ownership of property.
