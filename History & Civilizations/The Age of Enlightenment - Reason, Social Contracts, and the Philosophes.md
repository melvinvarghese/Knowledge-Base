# The Age of Enlightenment - Reason, Social Contracts, and the Philosophes

## Overview
The Age of Enlightenment (18th century) was an intellectual movement that championed empirical reason, scientific skepticism, and individual liberty over dogma and absolute monarchy.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Philosophes & The Republic of Letters: French thinkers (Voltaire, Montesquieu, Diderot) and Scottish intellectuals (David Hume, Adam Smith) disseminated ideas through salons and Diderot's monumental *Encyclopédie*, advocating secular tolerance, freedom of speech, and the separation of powers.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Social Contract Theory: Political philosophers re-imagined the state as a contractual arrangement among free individuals: Thomas Hobbes argued for absolute sovereignty to escape the brutish state of nature; John Locke argued that government exists to protect natural rights (life, liberty, property); and Jean-Jacques Rousseau argued that legitimate political authority derives from the 'General Will'.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Intellectual foundation of modern constitutional democracy, human rights declarations, modern free-market economics.

## Key Terminology
- **Enlightenment**: Intellectual movement advocating empirical reason, tolerance, and human rights over superstition and absolute rule.
- **Social Contract**: Political philosophy concept where individuals implicitly surrender some freedoms in exchange for state protection of rights.
- **Separation of Powers**: Montesquieu's doctrine distributing legislative, executive, and judicial powers into separate independent branches.
