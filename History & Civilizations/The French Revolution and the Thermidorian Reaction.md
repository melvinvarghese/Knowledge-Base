# The French Revolution and the Thermidorian Reaction

## Overview
The Thermidorian Reaction (July 1794) was the parliamentary coup that overthrew Maximilien Robespierre and dismantled the radical Jacobin Reign of Terror during the French Revolution.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Fall of Robespierre (9 Thermidor Year II): Exhausted by the escalating guillotine executions under the Law of 22 Prairial, members of the National Convention united in fear for their own lives. On 9 Thermidor (July 27, 1794), deputies shouted Robespierre down in the Convention, arresting him and guillotining him and 21 allies the following day.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Dismantling the Jacobin State & The Directory: The Thermidorian regime abolished the Paris Commune, closed the radical Jacobin Club, repealed maximum price controls on food (the Law of the Maximum), and unleashed the reactionary 'White Terror' against former Jacobins. The 1795 Constitution established the conservative **French Directory** (a five-member executive branch), which struggled with economic inflation until Napoleon Bonaparte's coup d'état of 18 Brumaire (1799).

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Classic political science model for the moderation and exhaustion phase of radical revolutions (Crane Brinton's *Anatomy of Revolution*).

## Key Terminology
- **Thermidorian Reaction**: Coup d'état in the French Revolution resulting in the fall of Maximilien Robespierre and the end of the Reign of Terror.
- **French Directory**: Five-member executive committee that governed France from November 1795 until overthrown by Napoleon in 1799.
- **White Terror**: Period of violent retribution in 1794–1795 carried out by royalists and conservatives against Jacobin militants.
