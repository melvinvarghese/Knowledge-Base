# The Black Death - Bubonic Plague and European Social Transformation

## Overview
The Black Death (1346–1353) was the deadliest pandemic in human history, killing an estimated 30–60% of the European population and transforming medieval socioeconomic structures.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Etiology & Spread: Caused by the bacterium *Yersinia pestis*, transmitted primarily by fleas on black rats (*Xenopsylla cheopis*) along Mongol trade routes and Genoese merchant ships across the Black Sea and Mediterranean ports.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Economic & Social Transformation: The catastrophic labor shortage shattered feudal serfdom: surviving peasants demanded higher wages and freedom of movement, precipitating the decline of the manorial economy. Religious disillusionment weakened Church authority, catalyzing social revolts (1381 English Peasants' Revolt) and paving the way for the Renaissance.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Collapse of feudal serfdom, rise of agricultural wage labor, medical public health quarantine protocols (Venetian 40-day *quarantena*).

## Key Terminology
- **Black Death**: Devastating plague pandemic that decimated Eurasian populations between 1346 and 1353.
- **Yersinia pestis**: Gram-negative, rod-shaped coccobacillus bacterium causing bubonic, septicemic, and pneumonic plague.
- **Quarantine**: Public health measure originating from the Venetian 40-day isolation period (*quaranta giorni*) for arriving ships.
