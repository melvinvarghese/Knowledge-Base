# The Scramble for Africa and European Imperialism

## Overview
The Scramble for Africa (1881–1914) was the rapid invasion, partition, and colonization of the African continent by European imperial powers, formalised at the Berlin Conference.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Berlin Conference (1884–1885): Organized by German Chancellor Otto von Bismarck, 14 European nations gathered in Berlin to carve up the continent without a single African representative present, establishing the principle of 'effective occupation' to claim territory.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Technology & Exploitation: Enabled by Industrial Revolution technologies (quinine malaria prophylaxis, steam gunboats, Maxim machine guns), European territorial control exploded from 10% in 1870 to 90% by 1914. Extraction of rubber, ivory, copper, and diamonds was accompanied by violent atrocities, notably King Leopold II's brutal regime in the Congo Free State.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Arbitrary geometric colonial borders that fractured ethnic groups, destruction of indigenous economies, legacy of post-colonial conflicts.

## Key Terminology
- **Scramble for Africa**: Aggressive partition and conquest of African territory by European colonial powers between 1881 and 1914.
- **Berlin Conference**: Diplomatic conference in 1884–1885 establishing rules for European colonization and trade in Africa.
- **Effective Occupation**: Colonial doctrine requiring European states to establish real administrative control to legitimate territorial claims.
