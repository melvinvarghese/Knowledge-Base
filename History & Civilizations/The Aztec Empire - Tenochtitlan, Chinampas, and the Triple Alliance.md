# The Aztec Empire - Tenochtitlan, Chinampas, and the Triple Alliance

## Overview
The Aztec Empire (Triple Alliance: Tenochtitlan, Texcoco, Tlacopan) dominated central Mexico from 1428 until the Spanish conquest of 1521.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
The Island Metropolis of Tenochtitlan: Built on an island in Lake Texcoco, Tenochtitlan was a marvel of hydraulic urban engineering, connected to the mainland by stone causeways with removable bridges, freshwater aqueducts from Chapultepec, and flood-control dikes.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Chinampa Agriculture & Imperial Hegemony: High population densities were sustained by *chinampas* ('floating gardens')—hyper-fertile artificial agricultural plots built of lake mud and reeds yielding up to seven crops annually. Aztec imperial power extracted tributary goods (cacao, textiles, obsidian, jade) enforced by religious cosmology linking human sacrificial debt to the nourishment of the sun god Huitzilopochtli.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Fell to Hernán Cortés in 1521 aided by smallpox epidemics and tens of thousands of indigenous Tlaxcalan and regional allies revolting against Aztec tribute.

## Key Terminology
- **Chinampas**: Mesoamerican agricultural technique using narrow artificial islands constructed in shallow lake waters to achieve intensive crop yields.
- **Tenochtitlan**: Island capital city of the Aztec Empire founded in 1325, now the site of modern Mexico City.
- **Triple Alliance**: Confederation formed in 1428 between Tenochtitlan, Texcoco, and Tlacopan that established the Aztec Empire.
