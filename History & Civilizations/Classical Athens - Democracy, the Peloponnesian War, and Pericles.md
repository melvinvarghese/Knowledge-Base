# Classical Athens - Democracy, the Peloponnesian War, and Pericles

## Overview
Classical Athens (5th century BCE) birthed direct democratic governance, dramatic theater, and monumental philosophy during its Golden Age under Pericles.

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Direct Democracy (*Demokratia*): Instituted by Cleisthenes (508 BCE), sovereign authority rested in the *Ekklesia* (popular assembly of adult male citizens), while the *Boule* (Council of 500) set legislative agendas chosen by lottery (sortition) rather than election to prevent oligarchy.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
The Peloponnesian War (431–404 BCE): Conflict with Sparta and the Peloponnesian League triggered by Athenian imperial overreach via the Delian League. Documented by Thucydides, the catastrophic war resulted in the destruction of the Athenian fleet at Syracuse and Sparta's eventual victory, shattering Classical Greek primacy.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Foundational model of democratic participatory governance, Western literary drama, and political historiography.

## Key Terminology
- **Direct Democracy**: System of government where citizens vote directly on policy initiatives rather than electing representatives.
- **Sortition**: Selection of political public officials by random lottery, central to Athenian democratic equality.
- **Peloponnesian War**: Devastating conflict between Athens and Sparta ending the golden age of classical Greek city-states.
