# The Byzantine Empire - Constantinople, Justinian, and Corpus Juris Civilis

## Overview
The Byzantine Empire (Eastern Roman Empire) preserved and transformed Roman governance, Greek scholarship, and Christian theology from its capital at Constantinople for over a thousand years (330–1453 CE).

## Core Mechanics & Foundations
Justinian I & Legal Codification: Reigning 527–565 CE, Justinian reconquered Italy and North Africa and built the monumental Hagia Sophia. His crowning achievement was the *Corpus Juris Civilis* (Code of Justinian)—a systematic codification of a millennium of Roman civil jurisprudence that forms the basis of modern European continental legal systems.

## Modern Applications & Advanced Significance
Constantinople's Strategic Bastion: Protected by the massive triple Theodosian Walls and naval Greek Fire, Constantinople stood as the defensive bulwark shielding Europe against successive Persian, Arab, and nomadic invasions until its fall to the Ottoman Empire in 1453.

## Real-World Impact & Systems
Preservation of Classical Greek and Roman philosophical manuscripts, East-West Christian Schism (1054), Eastern Orthodox culture.

## Key Terminology
- **Byzantine Empire**: Eastern continuation of the Roman Empire based in Constantinople throughout the Middle Ages.
- **Corpus Juris Civilis**: Fundamental body of civil law codified under Emperor Justinian I.
- **Greek Fire**: Incendiary chemical naval weapon used by the Byzantine navy that could burn on water.
